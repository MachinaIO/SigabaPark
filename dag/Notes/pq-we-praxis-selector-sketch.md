[Praxis (Tse, Vanjani and Vesely), ePrint 2026/2971](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2971), Sections 2.3, 4, 5.3–5.4 and 6.1–6.3, gives a reason to separate constraint compression from the cryptographic mechanism that hides the compressed constraints. Its QAP uses products of short factorizations; combining *hidden* factors uses a pairing. The fixed-gap search is a bounded discrete logarithm in the target group. Theorem 5.9 is an extraction statement in a bilinear generic-group and random-oracle model. Module-SIS controls one alternative in its conclusion. The concrete 7.88 GiB comparison uses 100-bit security, BN254 and an honestly set up Groth16 verifier. The reported decryption count is an average given a proof. This is a different relation and cost model from a tape-machine bound for the 128-bit CircuitSAT target. In particular the generic-group restriction does not establish hardness against quantum computation on concrete group encodings.

Two details matter when trying to transfer the construction. First, Definition 4.1 requires Boolean witnesses over a large prime field. Solvability of affine constraints over that field after dropping Booleanity can give a wrong answer: x+y=3 has field solutions modulo 5 and no solution in bits. Our linear consistency verifier works over GF(2), where every field element is a bit. Second, Section 6.1 explicitly allows accepting proofs of a false public input to exist. Extractable WE plus knowledge soundness is what lets that application use a proof verifier. Ordinary false-statement WE applied to the language “an accepting proof exists” would have no security obligation on such an input. Using a post-quantum argument with computational soundness alone does not close this gap.

Here are two alternatives that operate on public data before single-input lattice ABE. They use the exact witness transformation that orients each nonlinear NAND, records selected operands in a basis of t rows, and verifies the remaining constraints by GF(2) elimination. The source for the outer construction is [Brakerski et al., Section 3.2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/874.pdf). Neither alternative uses a group encoding.

## Optimize the number of encrypted entries

Suppose each nonlinear gate reads two distinct independent coordinate labels (possibly with affine constants). All operands must be drawn from the same independent family of labels, which may include earlier gate outputs in the joint linear system. The three possible selector rows are the unit vectors for its endpoints and their sum. Over GF(2) these are precisely the nonzero vectors supported on the two endpoints. The exact minimum certificate dimension over this choice of orientations is the minimum vertex cover size of the operand graph.

The theorem selector_support_cover proves the underlying support statement over any field and for arbitrary support sets. Given a matrix of admissible rows of rank d, choose d independent columns that span its column space. Each nonzero row must appear nonzero in at least one of those columns. Thus their indices cover every support. Conversely for a cover choose one covered coordinate per row and put a one there. The rank is at most the cover size. This argument is about row orientation on one representation. Arbitrary nonlinear changes of verifier are outside the optimality claim.

For bipartite operand graphs this gives a polynomial-time optimal orientation by maximum matching and the alternating-path minimum cover construction. For general graphs, even the orientation optimum on independent variable pairs contains minimum vertex cover. A maximal matching gives a polynomial lower bound and its endpoints give a cover of at most twice that size. For dependent affine operands, a cover of the graph on distinct linear forms still gives an upper bound; the equality uses independence. Constant, identical and complementary operands can be simplified first.

For example take edges (0,3),(0,4),(1,3),(1,4),(2,3) in this order. The frequency-based greedy rule (prefer a row in span, then highest global frequency, then the first operand) uses three independent bits. Vertices 3 and 4 cover every edge and the disjoint edges (0,3),(1,4) certify optimality. Over 25 disjoint copies this changes the balanced split from 2^37 keys and 2^38 encrypted attributes to 2^25 of each. These are table counts. An exhaustive enumeration of all 1,099 labelled simple graphs with one through five vertices compared minimum cover size against the minimum rank across all three choices per edge. All matched.

There is also a concrete obstruction to uniform savings. For cyclic i in 0,...,127, generate y_i=NAND(x_i,x_(i+1)) and then z_i=NAND(x_i,y_i); XOR all z_i. This fits in 4096 raw NANDs using four NANDs per XOR and unused padding. In joint input/gate-output coordinates the 128 pairs (x_i,y_i) form a matching. The set of x_i covers these gates and the y-producing gates. Hence the orientation optimum on this graph is exactly 128, even though other algebraic rewrites could improve this particular function.

## Compress the public verifier with one shared random projection

For k surviving nonlinear NANDs, fixing the t-bit certificate and expanding the internal wires in gate order gives M_z x = b_z on the original n bit variables with m=k+1 rows (the k selector equalities and the required output bit). On a false statement every residual coset b_z+image(M_z) excludes zero. Its size is 2^rank(M_z). Let r bound these ranks, for example r=n.

Enc samples one uniform binary Toeplitz projection H with s rows and m columns, records its m+s-1 diagonal bits in the public ciphertext, and makes the ABE policy test consistency of H M_z x = H b_z. The setup can be fresh per encryption. Both the key issuer and the decryptor reconstruct the same public policy. Every original satisfying witness remains accepted for every H. Over the public choice of H the probability that *any* certificate is accepted on a false circuit is at most min(1,2^(t+r-s)). For any nonzero residual v let j be its first nonzero coordinate. In output row i the distinguished term is diagonal bit i-j; all other nonzero terms use strictly smaller diagonal indices. The s output equations are triangular in the s distinguished bits, hence Hv is uniform. The shared_sketch_bad_seeds theorem gives the union count with no independence between certificates. This is statistical soundness against existentially chosen witnesses after H is public. A per-certificate soundness bound alone would not suffice for ordinary WE.

With n=128, t=64, k=4096 and a 140-bit bad-seed budget, s=332 suffices under the conservative bound r≤128. This conditional size calculation reduces the elimination input from 4097 equations to 332 with a 4428-bit public seed. It retains the work to compute the m residual rows; forming their sketches, recompiling the policy, the seed read cost, and the ABE depth/noise may outweigh the reduction. Skip sketching when s≥m. On a bad seed the adversary's conditional acceptance-probability difference is at most one, so the unconditional distinguishing advantage increases by at most the bad-seed probability over a uniform good-seed bound. Budget this before squaring advantage in Sigaba's gain metric. The Lean results cover the combinatorial support equivalence and finite union count. The Toeplitz proof above is mathematical; a complete tape implementation and end-to-end LWE parameters remain open.
