# Marked moments for a fine Construction A mesh

Write $m$ for the Euclidean dimension and $n$ for the number of parity checks. All volumes and sieve radii below are in the coordinates of $L=s^{-1}\Lambda_q^\perp(A)$, where $s=q^{n/m}$. Take $n=\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor$ and any prime between $\exp(m^8)$ and $2\lceil\exp(m^8)\rceil$ (Bertrand). Entries of A are independent uniform residues. For sufficiently large m, **S1, S2 and S3′ hold simultaneously with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(m)}$** at the radii in §3.1 of the companion sieve note, with its dimension replaced by m. The proof also transfers the two inequalities of [Laarhoven, Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13635v1#S5.SS1), on their respective domains, up to an additive $e^{-m^2}$. We use the Euclidean diagram estimates from that proof (see also g1_audit.md). No distributional comparison of lattice measures is assumed.

## A quantitative integration lemma

Use $k\le C\log m$ vector slots and a coordinate box of radius $B=\exp(O(\log^2 m))$, enlarged to at least 2. Consider a bounded workload F supported in this box. Its factors can include ball and shell indicators on individual slots and their differences, with radii at most B. Allow $O(k)$ such tests. Off their boundary spheres assume the remaining product has magnitude and within-piece Lipschitz constant at most $E=\exp(O(m\log^4 m))$ in the maximum coordinate metric. Here within-piece means a difference bound for two points with the same test outcomes. Include individual slot box tests if necessary. Sum over nonzero slots. Then the q-ary expected workload differs by at most $e^{-2m^2}$ from the following real diagram sum, uniformly for fixed implied constants.

For every rational subspace $W\subset\mathbb Q^k$ of dimension $r>0$ not in any coordinate hyperplane, select r coordinate pivots I with maximum absolute Plücker coordinate, breaking ties lexicographically. Write $T:\mathbb R^r\to W_{\mathbb R}$ for reconstruction from those coordinates and $P=\{u\in\mathbb Z^r:Tu\in\mathbb Z^k\}$, of index D. The summand is
$$
 D^{-m}\int F(Tu_1,\ldots,Tu_m)\,du_1\cdots du_m ,
$$
where the $u_j$ index spatial coordinates. This is exactly the Rogers diagram *functional* in pivot coordinates. Indeed, changing pivots by a matrix M maps the projected lattice onto the other projected lattice, changing its index by $|\det M|$ and the integral's coordinate element by $|\det M|^m$. In echelon pivots, integrality is the kernel of the denominator congruence, giving the usual coefficient. One subspace gives one reduced rational echelon matrix. Thus results proved by bounding this sum apply without a Haar averaging identity.

**Proof.** A supported integer tuple has slot lengths at most $s\sqrt m B$, and $(s\sqrt m B)^k<q$ since $kn/m=o(1)$. Q1 and Q2 give weight $s^{-mr}$ for row space W (take the spatial coordinate vectors as rows). Its exact sum runs over $m$ elements of P spanning $\mathbb Q^r$, divided by s before evaluation. Entries of T have magnitude at most one by the maximum minor rule. They belong to $D^{-1}\mathbb Z$ since $D\mathbb Z^r\subset P$. Hence in a dyadic bin $d\le D<2d$ there are at most $2^k(2d)(4d+1)^{k^2}$ spaces, by enumerating I, D and the numerators.

Discard $D>H=\lceil e^{m^2}\rceil$. For the integral the absolute cost per space is at most $E(2B)^{rm}D^{-m}$. For the discrete sum, a contributing tuple supplies r independent points of P in the box of radius sB. Their parallelepiped has coordinate diameter at most $2rsB$. They generate a sublattice of determinant V; in each of its V/D cosets the translates of its fundamental cell around box points fit in a box of side $2(r+1)sB$ and have disjoint interiors. Thus the box contains at most $(2(r+1)sB)^r/D$ points of P, even if only one contributing tuple exists. Raising to m and multiplying by $Es^{-rm}$ proves the discrete tail estimate. The bin series from H decays with ratio $2^{-m+k^2+1}$; both tails together are $\exp(-m^3+o(m^3))$. The spanning restriction is essential here.

For $D\le H$, first include nonspanning tuples in the discrete sum. Their first r rows have vanishing determinant, a nonzero degree-r polynomial in the rm entries. On the integer grid in the pivot box this costs at most $Er(2sB+1)^{rm-1}s^{-rm}$, rounding the grid cardinality down as needed. The grid zero bound $rN^{rm-1}$ follows by induction in the number of variables, splitting according to vanishing of the leading coefficient and using the univariate degree bound. This overcounts by ignoring congruences.

Split P into residue classes modulo D; there are $D^{r-1}$ classes. For each product class use cubes of side (D/s) anchored at its sample points. The normalized sum is $D^{-m}$ times the average of the cube quadratures. A moved slot changes by at most (rD/s) per coordinate. Away from test crossings the integrated error per quadrature is bounded by (EkrD/s) times the enlarged pivot box volume $(2B+2)^{rm}$ (enlarge E by a polynomial in m for norm conversions). For a crossing of a squared norm test, its squared norm lies within $\eta=O(mkr(B+1)H/s)$ of the threshold on the relevant cube. A slot or difference identically zero on W gives a constant test and can be omitted. Otherwise a nonzero reconstruction coefficient has magnitude at least $1/D$. Holding all but one associated pivot coordinate fixed leaves a quadratic $(at+b)^2+c$ with $|a|\ge1/D$. The length cut out by a squared-norm window of radius $\eta$ is at most $4D\sqrt{2\eta}$, including at tangency. Multiply by $(2B+2)^{rm-1}$, the number of tests and $2E$. Coordinate box faces obey the same estimate or the elementary linear slice bound. Only cubes meeting the support matter. This proof also handles coincident sphere constraints. Multiplying these errors by the number of spaces through H costs $\exp(O(k^2\log H))$; all small-index errors together are $s^{-1/2}\exp(O(m^2\log^2 m+m\log^4 m))$. Since $\log s\asymp m^{15/2}$, the asserted error follows.

## Application to the sieve

For the centered moments expand the even power before applying the lemma. Each raw summand uses the mark and at most 2h occurrence slots. Repeated lattice points are allowed. The factors other than shell tests are the continuum centers and the weights in Proposition 5.1. Here are details checking their regularity. On any compact separation interval inside (0,2), the two-distance formula in qary.md gives $K_R(d)\ge R^m e^{-O(m\log m)}$: restrict both distances to the middle half of their windows, where the transverse radius is at least a fixed multiple of R; the unit transverse sphere area is at least $e^{-O(m\log m)}$ by the ball volume formula. Volumes are at most $(2B+2)^m$. Translation through length t changes a ball indicator in L1 by at most twice its perpendicular projection volume times t (slice into intervals along the translation). Summing two such bounds applies to a shell. This bounds the Lipschitz constants of K and the endpoint center (a convolution with a bounded weight); the infimum defining the numerator of the weight is constant for the given band. Division by the preceding lower bound squared controls the weight on its band, with its jump handled by the shell test. Since $R\asymp R^*$ or larger up to the specified factor, all individual bounds are $\exp(O(m\log^2 m))$. Products of logarithmically many factors satisfy the lemma by telescoping. The binomial coefficient sum is $2^{2h}$. This proves the asserted additive transfer, preserving the partition cancellations, Gram penalties and endpoint gap of Proposition 5.1. It controls the **integrated** marked moment; a separate relative quadrature estimate for each near-degenerate diagram would be stronger than required.

For ordinary integer fibres at the separations used below there is also a uniform relative estimate. In physical coordinates round to the nearest integer with displacement at most $\sqrt m/2$. Sandwich the count between two-distance volumes with both windows narrowed or widened by that amount. On compact sets of nondegenerate central triangles the coarea density is proportional to $ab h^{m-3}/D_0$ with separation $D_0$ and transverse radius h. Differentiating the triangle area formula gives logarithmic derivatives $O(m/(sR))$ in either distance since the triangle inequalities have slack $\Omega(sR)$. Rescaling both windows about their respective midpoints changes their area by $1+O(m^{5/2}/(sR))$ and the density by $\exp(O(m^{3/2}/(sR)))$. This includes unequal endpoint windows and every orientation.

* **S1.** Transfer the first two shell moments. In the continuum rank-one sum write the two slots as multiples a,b of a common vector with coprime signed integers (up to common sign). For $j=\max(|a|,|b|)$ there are at most $8j$ choices and the integral is at most $V_R/j^m$ by the larger multiple's shell test. The full-rank term is $V_R^2$. Chebyshev thus costs $O(1/V_R)$ including the negligible quadrature error even after multiplying by $V_R$. At all sieve radii $V_R\ge(4/3)^{m/2}e^{o(m)}$.
* **S2.** Use the marked-tail argument of §3.10 Step 2 with $h=\lceil\log m\rceil$. On the sieve bands the weight is $1+O(1/m)$ and the endpoint center is within relative $O(1/m)$ of its shell average (the same coarea formula, differentiating in separation, gives log derivative $O(m/R)$; for the endpoint keep the radial weight inside the two-distance integral). Double counting volumes identifies the unweighted average with $\gamma^m\beta_R(1+O(1/m))$. Thus a violation of the stated windows forces relative deviation at least a fixed positive constant. For $\gamma=(\ell-1)/\ell$, $\ell=\lceil\log m\rceil$, a nonzero low-parent residual has magnitude at least $1/\ell^2$; zero forces the trivial coefficients since the tested longitudinal integer has bounded magnitude. At the smallest radius the partition exponent divided by m is at most $\tfrac12\log(4/3)-1/3+o(1)$, and the correction exponents are at most $\log(8/9),\tfrac12\log(e/3),-\tfrac12\log3$, plus o(1), by the continuum volume computation of Step 2. Increasing radius scales both population and degree by the same power, improving the partition term. The additive integration error divided by the threshold moment is negligible since the minimum continuum degree grows as $\exp(\Omega(m/\ell))$. Sum over the two kinds of marks and $O(\log^3 m)$ steps.
* **S3′.** Remove all nonzero marks of norm below $R^*/2$ by the order-one lemma and Markov, at cost $2^{-m}+O(e^{-2m^2})$ with the unit-ball-volume definition of $R^*$ (a polynomial volume normalization only changes the subexponential factor). Cover the remaining radii through $r_0$ by $O(m^2)$ touching relative-width $m^{-2}$ shells, extending the last one by at most a relative $O(m^{-2})$. Their separation ratios to the terminal radius stay in a compact subinterval of (0,2). On each shell the target inequality with h=1 bounds the expected number of uncovered marks by $O(Q/m_*)+e^{-m^2}/m_*^2$: each contributes $m_*^2$. Here $Q\le e^{O(m/\ell)}$, and $m_*\ge(13/12)^{m/2}e^{-O(m/\ell)}$ by the two-distance formula: throughout the middle half windows $h^2\ge R^2(1-r_0^2/(4R^2)-O(m^{-2}))$; the two window lengths and the ratio of adjacent unit sphere areas cost only polynomial factors relative to the shell volume. The product of the limiting squared scales is $(4/3)(1-3/16)=13/12$. Markov and summation finish. Removing the inner ball avoids projecting onto arbitrarily short axes.

## Algorithmic and formal scope

The same variance argument on a ball of radius $r_0$ proves S4 with failure $e^{-\Omega(m/\log m)}$. Rank failure costs at most $(q^n-1)q^{-m}$ by a left-null-vector union bound. Conditional on full rank the dual normalized lattice is $(s/q)$ times the integer lifts of a uniform n-dimensional residue subspace. Each nonzero residue occurs with probability $(q^n-1)/(q^m-1)$. In a normalized ball of radius $R^*/2$ the physical radius is less than q, and rounding cubes bounds its expected nonzero population by $(1+o(1))2^{-m}$ up to the normalization polynomial. Indeed the relative inflation from the cube radius is $1+O(\sqrt m s/(qR^*))$ before taking the mth power. This supplies the dual minimum input to the transference/basis-reduction step S5 cited in §3.1. The algorithmic layer of the companion note then gives exact SVP with exhaustive searches in $(4/3)^{m+o(m)}$ time, and with its Hypothesis P in $(3/2)^{m/2+o(m)}$ time under its computational model. For Lemma H, the cap-intersection probability is Lipschitz on these shells. Rotating one cap axis through angle $t$ changes its indicator with probability at most $t/\pi$: condition on the length of the projection into the rotation plane, where the admitted azimuths form an interval and the azimuth is uniform. Moving either normalized axis therefore satisfies the workload lemma on the shell. The continuum full-rank term is the product of shell volumes times the squared cap probability by conditioning on the filter direction. The coincident slot contributes at most one shell volume times the cap probability, and antipodes have zero intersection. For distinct absolute primitive multiples, eligibility forces the larger multiplier $j\gg\ell$, because the two radii differ by at most a relative $O(1/\ell)$ and the multipliers differ by at least one. Equal absolute multiples in distinct shells are ineligible. The remaining sum is bounded by $V_R C_m\sum_{j\gg\ell}8j^{1-m}$, where $C_m$ is one cap's probability (use the larger of the two shell volumes). This is negligible compared with $V_R V_{R'} C_m^2$ throughout the sieve. This weaker remainder suffices for the forward and reverse work estimates and transfers with the same absolute integration error. Neither running-time exponent is per parity check. Sharper bit-model guarantees, polynomial moduli, and aspect ratio two remain open here.
