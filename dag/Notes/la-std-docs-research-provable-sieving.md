Research note, 2026-11-21. This is the pen-and-paper step: no Lean or Python code is touched.

**Question.** Is there an algorithm that finds a shortest nonzero vector of a random lattice in time
`2^{cn+o(n)}`, with `c` close to `0.2925 = ½·log₂(3/2)`? It must succeed with probability `1 − o(1)`
over the lattice and its own coins, without the random-list heuristic (B). Here "random" means
Haar-random in `X_n = SL_n(R)/SL_n(Z)`; random `q`-ary lattices are the second target.

Notation: `R* = vol(B_1)^{-1/n} = (1+o(1))·√(n/(2πe))` is the radius of the ball of volume 1, so
`λ₁(L) ≈ R*` for a typical `L`. `N := (4/3)^{n/2} = 2^{0.2075n}`, `ℓ := ⌈ln n⌉` and `γ := 1 − 1/ℓ`.
"Quenched" means a statement about every point of one sampled lattice at once. "Annealed" means a
statement averaged over lattices.

## 0. Verdict in brief

1. **The target has a claimed solution, two months old.** Doulgerakis and Laarhoven,
   *Smooth Sailing through Spherical Shells: Provable Random-Lattice Sieving in Time 2^{0.292n}*,
   arXiv:2609.37482 (26 Sep 2026) [DL26]. For `L` Haar-random, it solves exact SVP in time
   `(3/2)^{n/2+o(n)}` and space `(4/3)^{n/2+o(n)}`, with probability `1 − e^{−Ω(n/log n)}` over `L` and
   the coins. It also returns the complete catalogue `L ∩ B(√(4/3)·R*·(1−o(1)))`, and it gives
   random-target CVP and batched discrete Gaussian sampling at the same cost.
2. **[DL26] follows the parent's route (i)–(iii) almost exactly, with two changes.** The invariant is
   "each list is i.i.d. uniform on the lattice points of a thin shell", not "the list is all of
   `S_r`". The adaptive dependence is removed exactly by a frozen center bank and two rejection
   steps.
3. **One deep input remains: quenched lens counts.** For every lattice point `u` of a complete shell
   at radius down to `√(4/3)·e^{1/ℓ}·R*`, the number of lattice points at the right distance from `u`
   must be within a constant factor of the lens volume. This must hold for about `2^{0.2n}` points
   at once. [DL26] takes it from Laarhoven, *Spherical statistics and phase transitions in
   high-dimensional random lattices*, arXiv:2609.13635 (12 Sep 2026) [Laa26]. [Laa26] proves it with
   Rogers' formula at moment order about `log n` (§3.10). [DL26] also uses a coverage statement from
   [Laa26]. For exact SVP alone (not the full catalogue), that statement can be replaced by a
   classical third-moment argument (Lemma S3′, new here). SVP then depends on [Laa26] only through
   the pointwise degree bounds (S2, Gap G1).
4. **Trust level.** Neither paper is refereed. By their own disclosures, both were written largely
   with AI assistance: [Laa26] says most of its content was generated by ChatGPT models, and [DL26]
   used ChatGPT to audit and develop proofs. [Laa26] reports a Lean 4 formalization of its main
   theorems. That formalization takes the Siegel and Rogers mean-value identities and the
   spherical Gram density as assumptions, and I could not find where the code is published. I found
   no published critique, erratum or second arXiv version of either paper as of today.
5. **What I checked (§3).**
   * I reconstructed the algorithmic layer of [DL26] with complete proofs: the exact cancellation,
     the conditional-independence induction, bank goodness via Chernoff bounds, the acceptance rate
     `1/12`, the parameter schedule, initialization and the catalogue. The cancellation identity
     was also checked by exact rational computation, and the bookkeeping numerically (Appendix).
   * Given the static hypotheses, correctness plus a `2^{0.415n+o(n)}` time bound follow with
     exhaustive pair search.
   * The `0.2925` time bound needs, on top of the hypotheses already used for correctness, only an
     annealed two-point Rogers estimate, Markov's inequality, and a product-code nearest-neighbour
     lemma (proof in §3.9).
   * I checked the margins of the union bound behind [Laa26]'s quenched theorem (its Thm 6.1).
   * New here: Lemma S3′ (§3.10). It covers the shortest vectors using only Rogers' formula at
     `k ≤ 3` and Chebyshev's inequality, which removes the coverage half of the deep input for SVP.
6. **What I did not verify.**
   * [Laa26] Propositions 5.1–5.2: a growing-order Rogers moment bound, proved through about 25 pages
     of diagram classification.
   * The product-code nearest-neighbour lemma of Gao–Feng–Hu [GFH26b].
   * The CVP and discrete Gaussian sampling appendices of [DL26].

   For exact SVP, the whole result reduces to one explicit inequality, Gap G1 in §3.10. The
   `0.2925` running time also needs the search lemma P.
7. **Assessment.** The architecture is sound and the exponents check. The result is *plausible and
   probably correct*, but should be cited as "claimed (2026 preprints), pending independent
   verification". The parent's worry was "exponentially many vectors versus the regime where
   concentration is known". That is exactly where the new input enters, and §3.10 explains why
   annealed (fixed-order, averaged) statistics cannot replace it.
8. **Consequences for Sigaba-Neo.** This does **not** turn the estimator's sieving cost into a
   theorem about LWE attacks, for three reasons:
   * BKZ calls the sieve on projected blocks of a reduced basis. These blocks are not Haar-random.
   * [DL26] works with exact real arithmetic on a real basis. A Turing machine needs a finite
     encoding, and no such model is treated.
   * LWE lattices are `q`-ary with a planted short vector.

   What it does give, if verified, is a proven upper bound of `2^{0.2925n+o(n)}` on average-case SVP:
   a candidate `Assumption.Attack`-style statement for random lattices, not for LWE.

## 2. Target statements

**Model.** Real RAM with exact arithmetic, as in [DL26]. The input is any basis of `L`, where `L` is
drawn from the normalized Haar measure on `X_n`. Probabilities are over `L` and the algorithm's coins.

**T0 (main target; [DL26, Thm 1.1, Cor 1.2, Thm 7.3]).** For all large `n` there is a randomized
algorithm `A_n` that outputs `v ∈ L` with `‖v‖ = λ₁(L)`, and the full catalogue `L ∩ B(R_cat)` with
`R_cat = (√(4/3) − Θ(ℓ^{−2}))·R*`. The guarantees:

* success probability `1 − e^{−Ω(n/log n)}`;
* every execution runs in time at most `(3/2)^{n/2}·e^{O(n log log n / log n)} = 2^{0.2925n+o(n)}`;
* space at most `(4/3)^{n/2}·e^{O(n/log n)} = 2^{0.2075n+o(n)}`.

**T1 (weaker: correctness only).** Same as T0, with time `(4/3)^{n+o(n)} = 2^{0.415n+o(n)}`.
Exhaustive pair search replaces nearest-neighbour search. This needs only the static hypotheses
S1–S5 of §3.1 and no search-structure lemma. It is proved in §3.8, conditional on S2 and S3. For
exact SVP alone, S2 suffices, using Lemma S3′.

**T2 (static saturation theorem; [Laa26, Thm 6.1, Cors 6.5, 6.7]).** This is a purely
geometric statement with no algorithm: hypotheses S1–S3 of §3.1 hold with probability
`1 − e^{−Ω(n/log n)}`. It is the real content and the only deep step.

**T3 (any fixed `ε > 0`; proposed here, not proved).** Time `2^{(0.2925+ε)n}`. The quenched
statistics are needed only at radii `≥ c(ε)·R*`, with `c(ε) > √(4/3)` fixed and moment order
`O_ε(1)`.

* Design change: stop the shrinking tree at radius `c·R*`, where shells have `c^n ≫ N` points. Grow
  the right spine of the tree to `c^n·2^{o(n)}` movers, so the two terminal lists cover the terminal
  shell. Then extract the catalogue up to `γ·c·R*`.
* Cost: about `c^n·(9/8)^{n/2}` for the spine (each query against a bank of size `≈ N`), plus about
  `c^{2n}·(3/4)^n·(3/2)^{n/2}` for the final pair search. Both tend to `(3/2)^{n/2}` as `c ↓ √(4/3)`.
* What it avoids: the near-critical window, where lens counts are only `e^{Θ(n/ℓ)}` and order
  `Θ(log n)` is needed. With `c` fixed, all relevant lens counts are `e^{Ω_ε(n)}`.
* What it does not avoid: the bound on non-partition diagrams below `(3/4)^{n/2}` (Gap G1 in
  §3.10). [Laa26, Thm 7.1] states the fixed-`c` analogue of T2 at fixed order `h > log c/log(c√3/2)`.

**T4 (random `q`-ary lattices).** The analogue of T0 or T3 for `Λ_q^⊥(A) = {x ∈ Z^m : Ax ≡ 0 mod q}`,
with `A` uniform in `Z_q^{n×m}`, `q` prime, `m − n` large and `q ≫ GH(Λ)`. §4 gives the exact
mean-value formula and the remaining lemmas. Open.

**T5 (bit model).** T0 for a Turing machine, given finite-precision input. Examples: rational
approximations of a Haar basis, or Hecke-point integer lattices [GM03, mem] with a quantitative
equidistribution rate. Open, and not discussed in [DL26].

## 3. The proof: a checked skeleton, conditional on explicit static lemmas

This section reconstructs [DL26]. The proofs given here are complete; each is my own write-up of
the argument and is labelled as proved here or cited. The deep lattice inputs are isolated as
hypotheses S2 and S3.

### 3.1 Parameters, counts and static hypotheses

**Shells.**

* `w := n^{−2}` and `S_R := {x : R(1−w) ≤ ‖x‖ ≤ R(1+w)}`.
* `V_R := vol(S_R)`. Exact scaling gives `V_{γR} = γ^n·V_R`.
* `U_R := L ∩ S_R`.

**Radii.**

* `R_m := √(4/3)·e^{1/ℓ}·R*` and `R_i := γ^{i−m}·R_m`.
* `R_0 = e^{Θ(log² n)}·R*` is chosen with `R_0 = s_n·√(n/(2π))`, so that the Gaussian width `s_n`
  of §3.7 is large enough. Hence `m = O(log³ n)`.
* `R_cat := γ·R_m = √(4/3)·(1 − 1/(2ℓ²) + O(ℓ^{−3}))·R*`.

**List sizes.** `M_0 := ⌈N·e^{2n/ℓ}⌉` and `M_{i+1} := ⌊M_i/16⌋`, so `M_i = N·e^{2n/ℓ − O(log³ n)}`.

**Lens volumes.** For one step `R → γR`, set `β_R := vol(S_R ∩ (S_R + γR·e₁))` and
`α_R := γ^n·β_R = (V_{γR}/V_R)·β_R`. The spherical-cap computation gives
`ln(β_R/V_R) = (n/2)·ln(1 − γ²/4) + O(log n)`.

**Lattice counts.** For `u ∈ U_R` and `d ∈ U_{γR}`:

* `a_L(u) := #{c ∈ U_R : u − c ∈ U_{γR}}` counts the centers eligible for a mover;
* `b_L(d) := #{c ∈ U_R : d + c ∈ U_R}` counts the representations of a difference.

Counting pairs `(u, c)` two ways gives `Σ_u a_L(u) = Σ_d b_L(d)`.

**Static hypotheses on `L`.** They concern only the predetermined radii above.

| | Statement | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

S1, S4 and S5 fail with probability `e^{−Ω(n/log n)}`. [Laa26] claims that S2 fails with
probability `e^{−Ω(n)}` and S3 with probability `e^{−Ω(n/log n)}`.

**Lemma S1 (shell populations; proved here).** For `n ≥ 3` and any radius `R` with `V_R → ∞`,
`Var_L |U_R| ≤ 2V_R·(1 + o(1))`. Hence `P[ abs(|U_R| − V_R) > θ·V_R ] ≤ 3/(θ²·V_R)`.

*Proof.* Siegel gives `E|U_R| = V_R`. Rogers' formula for `k = 2`, in the form of [Laa26, Prop 4.1]
with `r = 1`, `D = (q, p)`, `gcd(p, q) = 1` and `c_D = q^{−n}`, reads
`E[|U_R|²] = V_R² + Σ_{q≥1} Σ_{p≠0, gcd(p,q)=1} q^{−n} ∫ 1_{S_R}(x)·1_{S_R}(px/q) dx`.

* A nonzero integrand needs `|p/q| ∈ [1−3w, 1+3w]`.
* If `q < 1/(3w) = n²/3`, then `||p| − q| ≤ 3wq < 1`, which forces `p = ±q` and so `q = 1`. These two
  terms (`y = ±x`) contribute exactly `2V_R`.
* If `q ≥ n²/3`, there are at most `2(6wq + 1)` values of `p`, and the terms total at most
  `V_R·Σ_{q≥n²/3} 2(6wq+1)·q^{−n} = V_R·n^{−Ω(n)}`.

Chebyshev's inequality finishes the proof. ∎

The smallest shell is `U_{R_m}`, with `V_{R_m} = N·e^{n/ℓ}·n^{O(1)}`. Over the `m + 1` radii, the
failure probability is `e^{−Ω(n)}`.

**Lemma S4 (proved here).** `P[λ₁ < (1−1/ℓ)·R*] ≤ (1−1/ℓ)^n` by Siegel and Markov. For the other
side, take the ball `B` of volume `V = (1+1/ℓ)^n` and the same `k = 2` computation. For a ball, the
`q ≥ 2` terms add `O(V·2^{−n}·n)`, so `Var #(L ∩ B ∖ 0) ≤ 3V`, and Chebyshev gives
`P[L ∩ B = {0}] ≤ 3/V`. Both bounds are `e^{−Ω(n/log n)}`. ∎

### 3.2 The algorithm ([DL26, §3, Alg. 1], restated)

**Tree.** Use a binary tree of lists `L_{i,j}` with `|L_{i,j}| = M_i`, for `0 ≤ i ≤ m` and
`0 ≤ j < 2^{m+1−i}`. The leaves `L_{0,j}` are filled by §3.7. For `i < m`, merge
`(L_{i,2j}, L_{i,2j+1}) ↦ L_{i+1,j}`. The **left** list is frozen as the bank `C = (c_1,…,c_M)`, and
the **right** list supplies the movers. All randomness is disjoint across nodes.

**Thresholds.** `a_max := 3Mα_R/V_R`, `b_min := Mβ_R/(4V_R)` and `b_max := 3Mβ_R/V_R`, where
`M = M_i`.

**Merge.** For each mover `u`, with fresh coins:

1. Compute `a := a_C(u) := #{j : u − c_j ∈ U_{γR}}`, counting indices. If `a = 0` or `a > a_max`,
   output `⊥`.
2. With probability `a/a_max`, continue. Otherwise output `⊥`.
3. Pick an eligible index `j` uniformly at random and set `d := u − c_j`.
4. Compute `b := b_C(d) := #{j' : d + c_{j'} ∈ U_R}`. If `b < b_min` or `b > b_max`, output `⊥`.
5. Output `d` with probability `b_min/b`. Otherwise output `⊥`.

Keep the first `M_{i+1}` outputs that are not `⊥`, and abort if there are fewer.

**Final step.** Report every pair `(x, y) ∈ L_{m,1} × L_{m,0}` with `0 < ‖x − y‖ ≤ R_cat`.
Deduplicate, then output the catalogue and its shortest element.

### 3.3 Lemma A: exact cancellation (proved here)

**Lemma A.** Let `U, T ⊂ R^n` be finite, and let `C ∈ U^M` be an indexed multiset. Assume
`0 < b_min ≤ b_C(d) ≤ b_max` for every `d ∈ T`, and `a_C(u) ≤ a_max` for every `u ∈ U`. Run steps
1–5, with `U_R` and `U_{γR}` replaced by `U` and `T`, on a mover `u ~ Unif(U)`. Then for every
`d ∈ T`,

```
P[output = d | C] = b_min / (a_max·|U|).
```

So the output is not `⊥` with probability `p = |T|·b_min/(a_max·|U|)`, and given that, it is
`Unif(T)`. Neither law depends on `C`.

*Proof.* Fix `d ∈ T`. The output `d` arises only from a pair `(u, j)` with `u = d + c_j`, which
requires `d + c_j ∈ U`. There are exactly `b_C(d)` such indices `j`. For each of them:

* `P[u = d + c_j] = 1/|U|`;
* step 1 passes, because `1 ≤ a_C(u) ≤ a_max`;
* step 2 passes with probability `a_C(u)/a_max`;
* step 3 picks `j` with probability `1/a_C(u)`;
* step 4 passes by hypothesis;
* step 5 outputs `d` with probability `b_min/b_C(d)`.

Different `j` give disjoint events. Summing over the `b_C(d)` indices:
`b_C(d)·(1/|U|)·(1/a_max)·(b_min/b_C(d)) = b_min/(a_max·|U|)`. ∎

**Remark A′ (one-sided robustness).** Without the hypotheses, the same computation gives

```
P[d | C] = b_min/(a_max·|U|) · #{j : d + c_j ∈ U, a_C(d + c_j) ≤ a_max}/b_C(d) · 1[b_min ≤ b_C(d) ≤ b_max]
         ≤ b_min/(a_max·|U|).
```

The output law is therefore always dominated by the uniform law, whatever the lattice. Only the
lower bound needs S2. With movers drawn from `ν ≤ K·Unif(U)`, the bound becomes
`P[d | C] ≤ K·b_min/(a_max·|U|)`. Lemma I and §3.10 use this.

The identity was checked in exact rational arithmetic on a toy shell in `Z³` (Appendix): the output
law was exactly uniform for three random banks.

### 3.4 Lemma B: a sampled bank inherits S2 (proved here)

**Lemma B.** Assume S1 and S2 at step `R → γR`, and let `C ~ Unif(U_R)^{⊗M}` with `M = M_i`. Then

```
P[ a_C(u) ≤ a_max ∀u ∈ U_R  and  b_min ≤ b_C(d) ≤ b_max ∀d ∈ U_{γR} ]  ≥  1 − exp(−e^{(4/3)n/ℓ − O(n/ℓ² + log³ n)}).
```

*Proof.* `a_C(u) ~ Bin(M, a_L(u)/|U_R|)`. By S1 and S2 its mean is at most
`2Mα_R/(0.99·V_R) ≤ 2.03·Mα_R/V_R`, while `a_max = 3Mα_R/V_R`. Chernoff's upper tail with
`δ ≈ 0.478` gives failure at most `exp(−0.07·Mα_R/V_R)`.

`b_C(d) ~ Bin(M, b_L(d)/|U_R|)`, with mean in `[0.330, 2.03]·Mβ_R/V_R`. The thresholds are
`0.25·Mβ_R/V_R` and `3·Mβ_R/V_R`, so the lower tail (`δ ≈ 0.24`) and upper tail each fail with
probability at most `exp(−0.009·Mβ_R/V_R)`.

Since `β_R ≥ α_R`, every point fails with probability at most `exp(−Mα_R/(120·V_R))`. Take a union
bound over the at most `3V_R` points. Here `ln V_R ≤ n·ln(R_0/R*) + O(log n) = O(n log² n)`, while

```
ln(Mα_R/V_R) = ln M + n ln γ + (n/2)·ln(1 − γ²/4) + O(log n) = (4/3)·n/ℓ + O(n/ℓ² + log³ n).
```

To see the last step, expand `1 − γ²/4 = (3/4)·(1 + 2/(3ℓ) − 1/(3ℓ²))`, so that
`(n/2)·ln(4/3) + (n/2)·ln(1 − γ²/4) = n/(3ℓ) + O(n/ℓ²)`. Also `n ln γ = −n/ℓ + O(n/ℓ²)` and
`ln M = (n/2)·ln(4/3) + 2n/ℓ − O(log³ n)`. The Appendix checks these numerically. ∎

### 3.5 Lemma C: constant acceptance rate (proved here)

On S1 and a good bank, the success probability of one mover is

```
p = |U_{γR}|·b_min / (|U_R|·a_max) = ((1+θ_{γR})/(1+θ_R)) · V_{γR}·β_R / (12·V_R·α_R) = (1+θ_{γR}) / (12·(1+θ_R)).
```

The last equality uses `V_{γR} = γ^n V_R` and `α_R = γ^n β_R`. So `p ∈ [1/12.25, 1/11.76]`.
Successes are `Bin(M_i, p)`, so `P[fewer than M_i/16 successes] ≤ e^{−M_i/500}`. ∎

### 3.6 Theorem D: the i.i.d.-uniform invariant (proved here)

**Theorem D.** Fix `L` satisfying S1 and S2. Assume the leaves are independent with laws
`Unif(U_{R_0})^{⊗M_0}`. Process the merges in a fixed bottom-up order. Let `E_t` be the event that
each of the first `t` merges had a good bank and at least `M_{i+1}` successes.

* Conditional on `E_t`, the lists not yet consumed are independent, and a list at level `i` has law
  `Unif(U_{R_i})^{⊗M_i}`.
* `P[E^c] ≤ 2^{m+2}·(exp(−e^{Ω(n/ℓ)}) + e^{−M_m/500}) = exp(−e^{Ω(n/ℓ)})`, where
  `2^{m+2} = 2^{O(log³ n)}`.

*Proof.* Induction on `t`. Let merge `t + 1` act on the bank `C`, the movers `Mv` and fresh coins
`ρ`. Let `G` be the event that the bank is good, a function of `C`. Let `H` be the event of enough
successes, a function of `(C, Mv, ρ)`.

* By induction, conditional on `E_t`, the triple `(C, Mv, ρ)` is independent of the other available
  lists, and `C` and `Mv` are i.i.d. uniform.
* Fix a good bank value `c`. By Lemma A, the trial outcomes `Y_1,…,Y_{M_i}` are i.i.d., equal to `⊥`
  with probability `1 − p` and uniform on `U_{γR}` otherwise. Neither `p` nor the uniform law
  depends on `c`.
* Given the success pattern `s ∈ {0,1}^{M_i}`, the values at the success positions are i.i.d.
  uniform. So, conditional on `H` (at least `M_{i+1}` successes), the first `M_{i+1}` values have law
  `Unif(U_{γR})^{⊗M_{i+1}}`, independently of the pattern.
* This conditional law is the same for every good `c`. Integrating over `c ∈ G` therefore leaves the
  output law unchanged, and the output is independent of `C`.
* `G` and `H` are events of `(C, Mv, ρ)`. Conditioning on them leaves the other lists independent
  with their laws, and the output independent of those lists.
* Set `E_{t+1} := E_t ∩ G ∩ H`. Lemmas B and C bound `P[G^c | E_t]` and `P[H^c | E_t, G]`. Sum over
  at most `2^{m+1}` merges. ∎

This is the step that removes the adaptive dependence that heuristic (B) hides. Each mover is used
at most once. The bank is frozen and discarded after its merge. The double rejection makes the
output law the same for every good bank, so conditioning on the bank's history introduces no bias.

### 3.7 Lemma E: initialization (key identity proved here; sampler cited)

On S5, take `s_n` with `R_0 = s_n·√(n/(2π))` and `s_n ≥ e^{(K+1) log² n}·R*`.

1. **Smoothing.** `s_n ≥ η_ε(L)` for `ε = e^{−8n}`, because `η_ε ≤ λ_n·√(ln(2n(1+1/ε))/π)` and
   `λ_n ≤ max‖b_i‖` [GPV08]. For unimodular `L`, Poisson summation then gives
   `ρ_s(L − t) = s^n·(1 + O(ε))` uniformly in `t`.
2. **Sampler.** The GPV sampler is within total variation `e^{−6n}` of `D_{L,s}` and runs in
   polynomial time [GPV08], [DL26, Lemma 4.2] (cited).
3. **Radial rejection (proved here).** For an ideal sample `x ~ D_{L,s}`, keep `x` only if
   `x ∈ S_{R_0}`. Then accept it with probability `p₀(x) = exp(π(‖x‖² − R_+²)/s²) ≤ 1`, where
   `R_+ = R_0(1+w)`. This gives `P[accept and output x] = e^{−πR_+²/s²}/ρ_s(L)` for every
   `x ∈ U_{R_0}`, which is exactly uniform. One attempt succeeds with probability
   `|U_{R_0}|·e^{−πR_+²/s²}/ρ_s(L)`. Its factors are:
   * `V_{R_0} = κ_n R_0^n·(2/n)·(1 + O(1/n))`;
   * `e^{−πR_+²/s²} = e^{−n/2}·(1 + O(1/n))`;
   * `κ_n R_0^n/s^n = (n/2)^{n/2}/Γ(n/2 + 1) = e^{n/2}·(πn)^{−1/2}·(1 + O(1/n))`, by Stirling.

   So the success probability is `Θ(n^{−3/2})`. Capping at `n³` attempts fails with probability
   `e^{−Θ(n^{3/2})}`.
4. **Transfer.** Maximally couple all `N·2^{o(n)}` leaf samples with ideal ones. The total
   variation error is `e^{−Ω(n)}`, and every continuation inherits this bound [DL26, Lemma 4.5].
   The proof: run the same coins after the coupling.

**Why S5 needs much better than `2^{o(n)}` quality.** The tree has `2^{m+1}` lists, the list sizes
carry a factor `16^m`, and `m = ln(R_0/R_m)/(−ln γ) ≈ ℓ·ln(R_0/R*)`. One needs `m = o(n)`, so
`R_0/R* = e^{o(n/log n)}`. [DL26] takes `e^{O(log² n)}`, from block reduction with block size
`n/log n`.

Reusing one frozen bank across all merges of a level preserves independence, because by Lemma A the
output law does not depend on the bank. It does not remove the blow-up, though: the mover side
still doubles at each level.

### 3.8 Lemma F and Theorem G: correctness in time `2^{0.415n}` (proved here, given S2 and S3)

**Lemma F.** On S1–S4 and `E`, the terminal lists `L_{m,0}` and `L_{m,1}` are independent and
i.i.d. uniform on `U_{R_m}`, with `M_m/|U_{R_m}| ≥ e^{n/ℓ − O(log³ n)}`. Hence
`P[some point of U_{R_m} is missing from either list] ≤ 2|U_{R_m}|·e^{−M_m/|U_{R_m}|} = exp(−e^{Ω(n/ℓ)})`.

When no point is missing, S3 writes every nonzero `v ∈ L ∩ B(R_cat)` as `x − y` with
`x, y ∈ U_{R_m}`, both present in both lists. A shortest vector has norm at most
`(1+1/ℓ)·R* < R_cat` by S4. ∎

**Theorem G (T1, conditional on S2, and on S3 for the catalogue).** Suppose S2 and S3 hold with
probability `1 − δ_n`. Run the algorithm with exhaustive searches:

* each mover scans the bank;
* each proposal scans the bank;
* the final step scans all `M_m²` pairs.

It returns the catalogue `L ∩ B(R_cat)`, and so a shortest vector, with probability at least
`1 − δ_n − e^{−Ω(n/log n)}`. It runs in time `2^{O(log³ n)}·M_0²·poly(n) = 2^{0.415n+o(n)}` and space
`N·e^{O(n/log n)}`.

*Proof.* Combine Lemmas S1, S4, the cited S5, Lemma E, Theorem D and Lemma F. The time bound
comes from at most `2^{m+1}` merges of `O(M_i²)` comparisons each, plus the final scan. ∎

For **exact SVP alone**, S3 can be dropped: Lemma S3′ (§3.10) gives the conclusion with
probability at least `1 − δ'_n − e^{−Ω(n/log n)}`, where `δ'_n` is the failure probability of S2
alone.

### 3.9 Time `2^{0.2925n}`: only annealed statistics are needed (proved here, modulo the search lemma P)

**Filters.** For unit vectors at angle `θ` and `z` uniform on `S^{n−1}`, let
`C_n := P_z[⟨z, x̂⟩ ≥ 1/2]` and `W_n(θ) := P_z[⟨z, x̂⟩ ≥ 1/2, ⟨z, ŷ⟩ ≥ 1/2]`. BDGL's estimates
[BDGL16, Lemmas 2.1–2.2], as restated in [DL26, (51)], give `C_n^{−1} = (4/3)^{n/2+o(n)}` and
`W_n(π/3)^{−1} = (3/2)^{n/2+o(n)}`. Write `K(x, y) := W_n(∠(x, y))` and
`N_f := W_n(π/3)^{−1}·2^{o(n)}` for the number of filters.

**Angles the search must cover.**

* Forward pairs `(u, c)` with `u − c ∈ S_{γR}`: `cos ∠ = 1 − γ²/2 + O(w) ≈ 1/2 + 1/ℓ`.
* Reverse pairs `(−d, c')` with `d + c' ∈ S_R`: `cos ∠ = γ/2 + O(w) ≈ 1/2 − 1/(2ℓ)`.
* Final pairs: `cos ∠ ∈ [1/2 + 1/ℓ, 5/8 + o(1)]`.

All lie inside `(2/5, 2/3)`.

**Hypothesis P (product-code search; [GFH26b, Thm 4.2, Alg. 1, Prop 4.3, Cor 4.4] via [DL26,
App. A]; not checked by me).** There is a randomized structure built from random product codes
with three properties:

* **P1.** Each fixed pair whose cosine lies in the window is reported except with probability
  `e^{−Cn}`, for any constant `C`, at a cost factor `O_C(n)`.
* **P2.** A fixed pair `(x, y)` becomes a candidate with probability at most
  `2^{o(n)}·N_f·K(x, y)`. Inserting or querying one vector costs at most `2^{o(n)}·(1 + N_f·C_n)`.
* **P3.** A Cartesian-subcode traversal keeps the space at `N·2^{o(n)}`.

**Lemma H (annealed pair statistic; proved here).** Take `R' ∈ {R, γR}`. Then

```
E_L Σ_{x ∈ U_R, y ∈ U_{R'}} K(x, y)  ≤  V_R·V_{R'}·C_n²  +  1[R' = R]·V_R·C_n  +  V_R·n^{−Ω(n)}.
```

*Proof.* Apply Rogers' `k = 2` formula as in Lemma S1, with `f(x, y) = 1_{S_R}(x)·1_{S_{R'}}(y)·K(x, y)`.

* **Main term.** The directions of independent uniform points of two shells are independent and
  uniform on the sphere. By Fubini over `z`, `E_{x,y} P_z[·] = E_z[P_x(·)·P_y(·)] = C_n²` exactly.
* **Rank-one terms `y = (p/q)·x`.** `p/q = 1` (only when `R' = R`) gives `K = C_n`. `p/q = −1` gives
  `K = 0`, since a cap at threshold `1/2` never contains antipodal points. Every other `p/q` needs
  `q ≥ n²/3`, or `q ≥ ℓ − 1` when `R' = γR` (note `γ = (ℓ−1)/ℓ`). Each such term has weight at most
  `q^{−n}`. ∎

By Markov's inequality, with `h_n := n·log log n/log n`, the bound holds up to a factor `e^{h_n}`
for all `O(log³ n)` shell pairs at once, except on a set of lattices of measure `e^{−Ω(h_n)}`.

**Forward work at one merge.** Conditional on `(L, E_t)`, the bank and the movers are independent
and i.i.d. uniform (Theorem D). So the expected work is at most

```
2^{o(n)}·N_f·( M·C_n + M²·(1/|U_R|²)·Σ_{x,y∈U_R} K(x,y) )  ≤  2^{o(n)}·N_f·( M·C_n + M²·e^{h_n}·C_n² )  =  N_f·2^{o(n)}.
```

The inequality uses Lemma H and S1. The last step uses `M·C_n = 2^{o(n)}`.

**Lemma I (reverse queries; proved here).** A reverse query `−d` is built from the bank itself. Let
`κ(d, c')` be the probability over the filters that `−d` and `c'` collide; by P2,
`κ ≤ 2^{o(n)}·N_f·K`. By Remark A′, conditional on `C`,
`P[(u, j) proposed | C] ≤ (1/(|U_R|·a_max))·1[u − c_j ∈ U_{γR}]`. Averaging over an i.i.d. uniform
bank, the expected number of collisions with indices `j' ≠ j`, per mover, is at most

```
(M² / (|U_R|³·a_max)) · Σ_{d ∈ U_{γR}} b_L(d) · Σ_{c' ∈ U_R} κ(d, c').
```

The index `j' = j` adds at most one collision.

Now bound each factor. S2 gives `b_L(d) ≤ 2β_R`. Lemma H with `R' = γR` and Markov bound the double
sum by `e^{h_n}·V_{γR}·V_R·C_n²·2^{o(n)}·N_f`. Multiply by the `M` movers. Using
`a_max = 3Mα_R/V_R` and `β_R·V_{γR} = α_R·V_R`, the total is

```
(2/3)·M²·N_f·C_n²·e^{h_n}·2^{o(n)} = N_f·2^{o(n)}.
```

This also holds on the event that the bank is good, because the quantity is nonnegative. ∎

**Theorem J (T0 time bound, modulo P, S2 and S3).** On a set of lattices of measure
`1 − e^{−Ω(n/log n)}`, the conditional expected total work is `N_f·2^{o(n)} = (3/2)^{n/2+o(n)}`. This
sums the forward and reverse work over `2^{O(log³ n)}` merges, plus the final search between two
lists of size `M_m`, which Lemma H with `R' = R = R_m` bounds the same way. Apply Markov's
inequality to the coins, and abort when the work exceeds `(3/2)^{n/2}·e^{2h_n}`.

**Correctness of the reported counts.** The counts `a_C(u)` and `b_C(d)` must be **exact**: one
missed pair breaks the exact uniformity. By P1 with a large constant `C`, applied to the at most
`2^{0.3n}` required pairs, the reports are exact except with probability `e^{−Ω(n)}`. Transfer as
in Lemma E(4). ∎

**Consequence.** The `0.2925` part needs no quenched statistics beyond the upper bound
`b_L ≤ 2β_R`, which S2 already supplies. Everything else is Rogers at `k = 2`, Markov, and the
product-code lemma P.

### 3.10 The deep input: quenched lens counts (Gap G1)

This is where the parent's concern applies: `2^{0.2n}` points against the regime where
concentration is known. [Laa26] obtains S2 in two steps.

**Step 1: high moments (Gap G1; [Laa26, Prop 5.1], quoted).** Fix a compact `J ⋐ (0, 2)` and
constants `c₀, K, H`. The bound must hold uniformly for:

* `α ∈ J`;
* `c₀ ≤ R/R* ≤ e^{K log² n}`;
* `1 ≤ h ≤ H log n`.

**Notation.**

* `T = S_{αR}`, `P = vol(S_R)` and `Q = vol(T)`.
* `K_R(d) := vol(S_R ∩ (S_R + d))` is the lens volume for a difference `d`.
* `m* := inf_{d ∈ T} K_R(d)`, and `ω(d) := m*/K_R(d)` flattens the lens volume across the thin
  shell, so that `ω(d)·r_{L,R}(d)` has continuum mean `m*`.
* `r_{L,R}(d) := #{x ∈ L ∩ S_R : x − d ∈ L ∩ S_R}`.
* `D^ω_{L,R,α}(x) := Σ_{d ∈ L∩T} 1_{S_R}(x − d)·ω(d)` is the weighted endpoint degree, with continuum
  value `a^ω_R(x)`; `a* := sup_{x ∈ S_R} a^ω_R(x)`.

The bound is

```
E_L Σ_{d∈L∩T} |ω(d)·r_{L,R}(d) − m*|^{2h}
   ≤ Q·e^{O(h log 2h)}·m*·max(1,m*)^{h−1}  +  Q·e^{O(h log n)}·max(1,m*)^{2h−1}·Ξ_n,
Ξ_n := (16/27 + ε_n)^{n/2} + (e/4 + ε_n)^{n/2} + 2^{−n}.
```

The same holds for the endpoint degrees `D^ω` (with `P` and `a*` in place of `Q` and `m*`), under
an extra "low-parent gap" condition. [Laa26, Prop 5.2] is the random-shift analogue.

The proof expands the `2h`-th power and applies Rogers' formula at order `k ≤ 2h + 1 = O(log n)`,
which satisfies `k ≤ n − 1` and `k² ≤ 4(n − 3)`. It classifies the integer relation matrices `D`:

* **Partition terms** (coincidences, `±` pairings, the involution `x ↦ d − x`) give the first term.
* **Non-partition terms** pay a Gram-determinant penalty. A signed sum of `t ≥ 3` unit vectors that
  is again a unit vector forces `det G ≤ (1/t)(1 + 1/t)^{t−1} ≤ 16/27`, with equality at `t = 3`. I
  re-derived this (Appendix). A coefficient vector of norm at least 2 forces `λ_min(G) ≤ 1/4`, so
  `det G ≤ λ e^{1−λ} ≤ e/4`. Denominators `q ≥ 2` cost `c_D ≤ q^{−n}`, which gives `2^{−n}`.
* The matrix counts `e^{O(h² log n)}` are subexponential.

**Step 2: Markov's inequality and a union bound ([Laa26, Thm 6.1]; arithmetic checked).** Take
`h = ℓ` and the worst radius `R = R_ℓ = √(4/3)·e^{1/ℓ}·R*`. "Some endpoint is off by more than a
factor `3/2`" forces the moment sum to be at least `2^{−2h}·(ā_R)^{2h}`. Markov's inequality then
bounds the failure probability by

```
P·e^{O(h log h)}·ā^{−h}  +  (P/ā)·e^{O(h² log n)}·Ξ_n ,    with  P/ā = (4/3)^{n/2}·e^{2n/(3ℓ)+o(n/ℓ)},  h·ln ā = n/3 + O(n/ℓ).
```

The exponents per `n` are:

| Term | Exponent | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| partition term | `½ ln(4/3) − 1/3` | `−0.1895` |
| `16/27` | `½ ln((4/3)·(16/27)) = ln(8/9)` | `−0.1178` |
| `e/4` | `½ ln(e/3)` | `−0.0493` |
| `2^{−n}` | `−½ ln 3` | `−0.5493` |

All are negative. Scaling `R ↦ uR` multiplies `P` and `ā` by `u^n`, so the non-partition prefactor
`P/ā` does not change and the partition term only improves: the bound is uniform in `R`. The target
counts `r_L` have the extra favourable factor `e^{−4n/(3ℓ)}`. I recomputed all of these numbers
(Appendix).

**The sharp requirement.** The union bound covers `≈ (4/3)^{n/2}` points, and each non-partition
term loses one factor `m*`. So every **non-partition penalty base must be below `3/4`**, uniformly
in `h ≤ H log n`.

* The published bases leave slack: `e/4 ≈ 0.680` and `16/27 ≈ 0.593`, against `0.75`.
* The relations with **two** free parents, such as `x₃ = x₁ + x₂ − d` in the fibre of `d`, need
  their two parents at 120° to each other. They therefore pay exactly `(sin² 120°)^{n/2} = (3/4)^{n/2}`,
  which is **no margin at all** in the bound shape above.
* [Laa26] handles these separately, through a "low-parent gap" `Δ_lp ≥ C·n^{−2}` and a "Diophantine
  nonresonance" lemma (its Lemmas 5.10 and 5.16).
* My own count suggests they are harmless after centering. Each such relation spends three moment
  slots on two free vectors, so `r` of them gain a factor `((3/4)^{n/2}/m*)^r` against the partition
  scale. This is a plausibility check only. **This is the first place an independent verifier
  should look.**

**Gap G1 (exact statement).** [Laa26] Proposition 5.1, i.e. eq. (5.2) and, for endpoints, (5.3).
It must hold with `Ξ_n ≤ (3/4 − η)^{n/2}` for some fixed `η > 0`, uniformly for `α` in a
neighbourhood of `γ` and of `1`, for `R_ℓ ≤ R ≤ e^{K log² n}·R*`, and for `h ≤ 2⌈ln n⌉`. With G1:

* S2 follows by Step 2, which I checked;
* S3 follows by [Laa26, Prop 6.6, Cor 6.7], with the same moment bounds on a ball of target radii
  (not checked line by line);
* T0 follows by §§3.3–3.9.

**Why annealed or fixed-order averages cannot replace G1 for this design.** Exactness needs
`b_C(d) ≥ b_min` for **every** `d`. Suppose one has only "all but a small fraction of points are
regular".

* Remark A′ still gives upper domination `μ_i ≤ K_i·Unif`, with no regularity assumption. Here
  `K_{i+1} = K_i·p_unif/p_i`, where `p_i` is the actual success rate and `p_unif` is its value in
  Lemma C. If `p_i ≥ p_unif/2` at every level, which is an averaged statement, the loss is
  `2^{O(log³ n)} = 2^{o(n)}` overall. That is acceptable.
* Lower bounds fail on a deficient set `D_L`, a set that depends on `L`. The final step needs a
  specific vector `s = s(L)` to have representations outside the deficient sets of every level.
  Since `s` and `D_L` are both functions of `L`, averaging cannot decouple them.
* Cauchy–Schwarz gives `Σ_{s short} |Rep(s) ∩ D| ≤ (|D|·Σ_y deg_short(y)²)^{1/2}`. That is useless
  unless `|D| ≲ e^{O(n/ℓ)}`, whereas annealed bounds only give `|D| ≤ e^{−Ω(n/ℓ)}·|U| ≈ e^{−Ω(n/ℓ)}·N`.

The parent's alternative invariant, "the list is all of `S_r`", has the same problem: it is
literally a quenched coverage statement of type S3 at every level. A design with only annealed
inputs would need a new idea to decorrelate `s` from the deficient sets, for example re-randomizing
the lattice through random sublattices or superlattices, as in [GFH26a, Hha26b].

**One part is annealed after all: for SVP, S3 can be replaced by a classical lemma.** The full
catalogue needs S3 down to `R_cat ≈ √(4/3)·R*`, where lens volumes are only `e^{Θ(n/ℓ)}`. A
shortest vector, however, has norm at most `r₀ := (1+1/ℓ)·R*` by S4, and at that length the lens
volumes are exponentially large.

**Lemma S3′ (proved here, given Rogers' formula at `k ≤ 3`).** For `v ∈ L` put

* `b_m(v) := #{x ∈ U_{R_m} : x − v ∈ U_{R_m}}`;
* `μ(v) := vol(S_{R_m} ∩ (S_{R_m} + v))`.

Then `E_L #{v ∈ L : 0 < ‖v‖ ≤ r₀, b_m(v) = 0} ≤ e^{−(n/2)·ln(13/12) + O(n/ℓ)} = e^{−0.040n+o(n)}`.
So with probability `1 − e^{−Ω(n)}`, every lattice vector of norm at most `r₀`, and in particular
every shortest vector, is a difference of two points of `U_{R_m}`.

*Proof.* Use `1[b_m(v) = 0] ≤ (b_m(v) − μ(v))²/μ(v)²`, sum over `0 < ‖v‖ ≤ r₀`, take expectations,
and expand the square.

* **Constant term.** Siegel gives `E Σ_v 1 = vol(B(r₀))`.
* **Cross term.** Rogers at `k = 2` gives `E Σ_v b_m(v)/μ(v) = vol(B(r₀))`. A rank-one term
  `x = (p/q)·v` would need `|p/q| ≈ |p/q − 1|`, i.e. `p/q ≈ 1/2` and so `‖v‖ ≈ 2R_m > r₀`; none occur.
* **Square term.** Rogers at `k = 3` gives `E Σ_v b_m(v)²/μ(v)² = vol(B(r₀)) + 2∫_{B(r₀)} dv/μ(v) + negligible`.
  To see this, fix `x` in the fibre of `v`. Then `‖x‖ = ‖x − v‖ = R_m` and `⟨x, v⟩ = ‖v‖²/2`. A
  rank-two relation `x' = a·x + b·v` keeps `x'` in the fibre only if
  `‖ax + bv‖ = ‖ax + (b−1)v‖ = R_m`. Subtracting the two equations gives `a = 1 − 2b`, and then
  `b(b−1)·(4R_m² − ‖v‖²) = 0`. Since `‖v‖ < 2R_m`, this forces `(a, b) ∈ {(1, 0), (−1, 1)}`:
  `x' = x`, or the forced pairing `x' = v − x`. Each contributes `∫ dv/μ(v)`. With the thin-shell
  slack `w`, any other rational `(a, b)` needs a denominator `q ≳ n²`, whose weight is
  `c_D ≤ q^{−n}`. Rank-one terms are infeasible as above.

Hence the expectation is at most `(2 + o(1))·vol(B(r₀))/min_{‖v‖≤r₀} μ(v)`. Here
`vol(B(r₀)) = e^{n/ℓ + O(n/ℓ²)}`, and
`min μ ≥ V_{R_m}·(1 − r₀²/(4R_m²))^{n/2}·n^{−O(1)} = (4/3 · 13/16)^{n/2}·e^{O(n/ℓ)} = (13/12)^{n/2}·e^{O(n/ℓ)}`. ∎

**Consequence.** For exact SVP, G2 is not needed. Run the final search only for differences of norm
at most `r₀`, i.e. cosine at least `5/8 − o(1)`. Then Lemma F holds with S3′ in place of S3. The
deep dependence of the SVP result on [Laa26] reduces to **S2 alone**: Gap G1. G2 is still needed for
the full catalogue up to `√(4/3)·R*`, and hence for the dual-attack and discrete-Gaussian
applications.

## 4. Second target: random `q`-ary lattices

**Setting.** `Λ = Λ_q^⊥(A) = {x ∈ Z^m : Ax ≡ 0 (mod q)}`, with `q` prime and `A` uniform in
`Z_q^{n×m}`. Then `det Λ = q^n` (when `A` has full rank), and the Gaussian-heuristic radius is
`GH ≈ √(m/(2πe))·q^{n/m}`. `Λ_q(A)` is the dual, up to scaling by `q`. I found no provable-sieving
result for this distribution. [DL26] and [Laa26] cover Haar lattices only, and [Laa26, §1.8]
excludes "structured modular lattices" explicitly.

**Lemma Q1 (exact `q`-ary mean-value formula; proved here).** For every `F : (Z^m)^k → [0, ∞)`,

```
E_A Σ_{x_1,…,x_k ∈ Λ} F(x_1,…,x_k) = Σ_{x ∈ (Z^m)^k} F(x) · q^{−n·rk_{F_q}(x̄_1,…,x̄_k)}.
```

*Proof.* For fixed `x`, the event "`Ax_i ≡ 0` for all `i`" says that each of the `n` independent
uniform rows of `A` lies in `V^⊥`, where `V = span_{F_q}(x̄_i)` has dimension `r`. Each row does so
with probability `q^{−r}`, so the event has probability `q^{−nr}`. Sum over `x` by linearity of
expectation. ∎

**Lemma Q2 (no modular coincidences for short tuples; proved here).** If `‖x_i‖ ≤ R` for all `i`
and `q > R^k` with `R ≥ 1`, then `rk_{F_q}(x̄_1,…,x̄_k) = rk_Q(x_1,…,x_k)`.

*Proof.* Rank over a field is the size of the largest nonzero minor. Take any `r × r` minor of the
`k × m` integer matrix with rows `x_i`, where `r ≤ k`. By Hadamard's inequality its absolute value
is at most the product of the norms of its `r` rows. Each such row is a restriction of some `x_i`,
so its norm is at most `R`. Hence the minor is at most `R^r ≤ R^k < q` in absolute value. So it
vanishes mod `q` if and only if it vanishes over `Z`. ∎

**Consequence.** Take `F` supported on `B_R^k` with `q > R^k`. Then
`E_A Σ F = Σ_x F(x)·q^{−n·rk_Q(x)}`. This is **Rogers' formula with integer sums in place of
integrals**, and it has no extra error terms. The same relation classification applies: partition
terms, geometric non-partition terms, and large coefficients. The proof is elementary linear
algebra over `F_q`. It needs no Haar measure on `SL_n(R)/SL_n(Z)`, which makes it much easier to
formalize in Lean than the Haar-lattice version (Gap G4).

**What has to change.**

1. **Parameters.** Moments of order `k = 2⌈ln n⌉ + 1` with `R ≈ GH` need
   `q^{1 − kn/m} > (C√m)^k`. That means `m ≳ 2n·ln n` and `q ≥ n^{O(log n)}`. For the fixed-order
   variant T3, it is enough that `m > kn` and `q = poly(n)`. Cryptographic shapes (`m ≈ 2n`) have
   genuine modular coincidences, and bounding them is **open**. A heuristic count gives a relative
   weight `q^{r(n+k−m)}` for rank deficit `r`, which is small when `m − n ≥ k + ω(1)`.
2. **Shell width.** `Z^m` has no integer squared norms in a shell of relative width `n^{−2}` once
   `n^2 > 4·GH²`. Use width `w' = Θ(1/n)` instead. The Gaussian weight and the lens volumes then vary
   by constant factors across a shell. Lemma A does not care, but the windows in S2 must widen to
   `[μ/C, Cμ]`, with thresholds to match.
3. **Main term (open: Lemma Q3).** The partition terms become counts of integer points of `Z^m` in
   shells, lenses and lens fibres. Gaussian-heuristic accuracy for `Z^m` balls at radius `√(cm)`
   with `c ≫ 1` is excellent [Mazo–Odlyzko 1990, mem]. Lens fibres around a **fixed integer** `d`
   are subtler: for sparse `d`, such as `d = R·e_1`, the fibre constraint quantizes one coordinate,
   and the count can differ from the volume by a factor `poly(n)` or vanish entirely.

   **Lemma Q3 (needed).** Uniformly over every `d ∈ Z^m ∩ S_{γR}` with support at least `m/2`
   (sparser `d` lie in `Λ` with probability `q^{−n}` each and can be removed by a first-moment bound),
   the lens fibre count satisfies `#{x ∈ Z^m ∩ S_R : x − d ∈ S_R} = (1 ± 1/10)·vol(S_R ∩ (S_R + d))`.
   The analogous statement must hold for the moment integrands. Plausible approach: a two-dimensional
   local limit theorem for `(Σx_i², Σx_i d_i)` under a discrete Gaussian.
4. **Initialization is easier.** `qZ^m ⊂ Λ` gives `λ_m(Λ) ≤ q` and a basis with Gram–Schmidt norms
   at most `q`. GPV sampling at `s = q·√m` works with no `2^{o(n)}` preprocessing. The ratio
   `R_0/R_m = q^{1−n/m}·poly(m)` gives a tree depth of `O(log n·log q)`.

**Assessment for T4.** This is a plausible route, and Lemmas Q1 and Q2 make the "Rogers" side
simpler than for Haar lattices. The new obstacles are Lemma Q3 (integer-point counts in lens fibres)
and, for `m = O(n)`, the modular coincidences. Even a full T4 would not cover LWE: an LWE lattice is
`q`-ary with a **planted** short vector, and sieving inside BKZ runs on projected blocks.

**Blocked, with reasons.**

4. **Lemma A, exact.** Take `U = {v ∈ Z³ : 20 ≤ ‖v‖² ≤ 26}` (222 points),
   `T = {v : 12 ≤ ‖v‖² ≤ 18}` (170 points), and a random bank of 400 points. Set
   `a_max = max a_C` and `b_min = min b_C`. In exact rational arithmetic, all 170 output
   probabilities were equal to `b_min/(a_max·|U|)` for each of three banks:

```python
import random, itertools
from fractions import Fraction as F
pts=[v for v in itertools.product(range(-6,7),repeat=3) if v!=(0,0,0)]
n2=lambda v: sum(x*x for x in v); sub=lambda a,b: tuple(x-y for x,y in zip(a,b)); add=lambda a,b: tuple(x+y for x,y in zip(a,b))
U=[v for v in pts if 20<=n2(v)<=26]; T=set(v for v in pts if 12<=n2(v)<=18); Us=set(U)
C=[random.choice(U) for _ in range(400)]
aC={u: sum(sub(u,c) in T for c in C) for u in U}; bC={d: sum(add(d,c) in Us for c in C) for d in T}
amax, bmin = max(aC.values()), min(bC.values())          # bank is "good" by construction
out={d: F(0) for d in T}
for u in U:
    for c in C:
        d=sub(u,c)
        if d in T: out[d]+=F(1,len(U))*F(1,amax)*F(bmin,bC[d])   # (1/|U|)(a/amax)(1/a)(bmin/b)
assert len(set(out.values()))==1 and next(iter(out.values()))==F(bmin, amax*len(U))
```
