This bounded exclusion concerns the experiments in Definition 12 of Kamil Kluczniak, [Witness Encryption from Garbled Circuit and Multikey FHE Techniques](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1502), printed pp. 13–14 of the supplied revision. The WE security application is Theorem 3 on pp. 23–25. Theorem 1 is not the WE hybrid argument.

**Event ledger.** First draw all rho independent uniform secret strings. For indexed specification rows, D installs actual ideal-cipher records with key tuple indexed by the row set, message the designated secret, and ciphertext the answer. A fixed target T asks whether its exact (key tuple, message, ciphertext) triple enters A's later SKE log. A ciphertext match alone does not suffice. D's installation of the record is outside that log. Real gives A the ciphertexts, evaluation key, initial AFHE encryptions, specification and target. Ideal stores initial uniform handles with their singleton labels and keys, and exposes the printed SKE and AFHE interfaces. On a cache hit Eval returns the stored handle at step 1 without step 5. On a miss step 3 resolves all input records, step 4 tests the tag union and selects a complete key tuple, step 5 calls SKE.Dec, and steps 6–7 store the union and the cache record. A mandated internal query is charged when SKE receives it. Use the same query-accounting convention in both worlds. The proof on p. 23 explicitly includes internal calls through Eval. Direct-only accounting gives different events. For arbitrary strings lacking table records, collisions with conflicting records, and the ideal evaluation-key input, the pseudocode needs conventions. The exclusion below restricts to resolved handles and conditions only on information fixed before the replacement draws.

**Conflict-miss exclusion.** Fix a target with ordered arity r>=1, key length ell, ciphertext c, and the original independent draws. Condition on the entire original challenge table, the entire prior execution and the next call, including whether this is a cache miss. For a resolved conflicting union the r replacement keys are independent fresh strings. Exactly one ordered vector out of 2^(ell*r) is the target's tuple. A designated inverse query requires this equality and the same ciphertext c; hence its conditional probability is at most 2^(-ell*r). On matching c and tuple, ideal-cipher correctness supplies the target message. This counting does not require a uniform distribution on the past: apply it separately to each finite history and average with its actual probability. It even holds when some original secret values accidentally coincide. With at most q such misses the probability of any such exact target hit is at most min(1,q*2^(-ell*r)). The hit at the first new call and at its exact cached replay are the same old log record; the replay adds no event. Distinct byte tuples computing the same semantic function can miss separately, but each still satisfies the conditional bound when conflicting. This covers shared subexpressions, arbitrarily correlated histories, and adaptively selected next calls. The fresh key calculation is classical, as are the original oracle tables. It does not establish security for coherent quantum queries.

**Persistence.** Every resolved handle obtained from a conflicting union inherits a forbidden pair by step 6. Each later descendant formed with that handle has a superset of its tags. Branching, repeated appearances, and multiple parents preserve the same pair. Cache hits recover the recorded tags under unambiguous handle resolution. Thus deriving a new expression from the tainted handle alone cannot restore the preserved-key arm of step 4. If a sequence includes separate clean branches, direct SKE calls, guesses in AFHE.Enc/Dec, or an unresolved or ambiguously resolved handle, the bound above does not cover those opportunities. In particular it bounds contributions from the conflict branch of the ideal experiment, not the total real/ideal advantage. Fresh output-handle bytes can collide; conditioning on no collisions anywhere in the *future* before applying a fresh-vector bound would be invalid. For at most n independent L-bit handle draws, charge the unconditional collision probability separately by n(n-1)/2^(L+1), if distinct handles are needed for the application.

**Matching public metadata.** Construction 1 explicitly returns the public pair (c,T) on p. 15; Lift, Add and Mul preserve or union this field on p. 16. The ideal initial handle on p. 14 is sampled uniformly from the entire bit-string domain, and its singleton tag is stored in E. With two initial encrypted inputs, let A,B be the disjoint parse sets for the two distinct singleton tags in a single deterministic public serialization. If two independently uniform strings are passed to that parser, the chance that both carry their corresponding tags is |A||B|/2^(2L) <= 1/4 by disjointness and AM–GM. This calculation holds also at fixed fan-in two for kappa=2, rho=3, row input set {1,2} and independent output index 3. Real initial encryptions have the two expected tags with probability one. This concerns the initial view. Definition 12 measures an exact SKE query event and does not measure arbitrary view predicates. A typed ideal interface publishing the correct tags alongside independently random payloads makes this particular initial-view discrepancy disappear. The conflict-miss and persistence results survive that repair verbatim if table resolution and fresh replacements keep their specified semantics. The reference to the evaluation key in IdealExp step 3 also requires a sampling/interface convention.

**Application boundary.** For the actual WE-generated table, Theorem 3 follows compatible tags to a partial consistent assignment and recursively checks the honestly generated gate rows (pp. 24–25). Arbitrary public QSpec need not encode a formula or a NO instance. Even one nonannihilating evaluation on a legitimate row can then retrieve the correct output key inside the ideal table. For polynomially many target events on a common tape space, per-target negligible upper bounds give a negligible union even when the events are dependent, provided a uniform negligible bound applies. For a hybrid comparison use a real upper bound from the two-sided marginal bound and the ideal upper bound. The printed inequality has the reverse one-sided orientation for this step. Matching nonnegligible marginal probabilities alone does not determine union probabilities. Hybrid 1 additionally discusses forward queries involving unlock; an exact single inverse-record bound alone does not cover all those queries. Neither the public metadata comparison nor the bounded exclusion relies on Construction 2's modified interface or establishes a candidate-independent WE reduction. The full typed-game comparison for compatible and direct histories, its complete oracle/logging semantics, and a falsifiable quantum-resistant instantiation remain open.

The three accompanying checked statements isolate the finite fresh-vector count, common-space union bound, and monotone forbidden-pair argument. Applying those lemmas to the printed pseudocode, and the distinctions between the interfaces, are paper arguments.
