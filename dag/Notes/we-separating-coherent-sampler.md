Fix the public correction table. Expand the final density matrix as a double finite sum over the address histories on its two sides. Each term reads only the answers at addresses appearing on either side. If their union misses a required role at each term, the static pad-and-share involution for that union preserves the public table and all the answers in that summand. Reindexing its uniform coin sum gives equality for the two messages. The involution may vary with the pair of histories. The theorem pair_losing assumes loss for every pair of allowed address tuples. Equality holds separately for every unnormalized public-record block, including records of probability zero. The oracle answer contains the vector of pads belonging to one role and source. The theorem xor_oracle realizes the usual controlled XOR on a result register with a separate workspace.

With one query the union contains at most two roles, so one_equal gives exact equality for arbitrary coherent address superpositions. For longer executions, loss along each individual history alone does not imply the hypothesis. A one-term exhaustive check found equal Fourier moments through order two for each of the eight public corrections, and opposite third-order moments. Public release of all sources is outside this model. A source authorization mechanism and its leakage still require a separate proof.

The scanner in sampler_law uses a public template of 798462 zero bits and produces one independent fair bit per position. The layout lists two coins and 12672 pad bits per term, each exactly once. The sampler costs 798493 in the Machine model. The joint_correct theorem feeds the random word, with its message prefix, to the previously checked correction encoder. It identifies the exact joint law of the retained private pads and the public 798336-bit ciphertext table. Both share coins per term are discarded from the private return port. The two machines have separately verified resource bounds.
