**Experiment.** In the ideal local bank of [PR 176](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/176), condition on the public bank B independent of the hidden lane points and blinders T. For every fixed well-formed response a, the marked-event probability over T is at most p. The marked set may have many addresses. An XOR oracle coherently returns the mark bit; malformed encodings get zero. Inter-query circuits may depend on B and an auxiliary classical leak L in an alphabet of size at most 2^ell. A leak must have the same joint law in both oracle worlds and be sampleable by any lattice reduction claiming to supply it. Set h=min(1,2^ell p). For every public subnormalized address state generated without the marked oracle, the expected squared norm on marked addresses is <=h: for fixed B guess a possible leak value, apply the single-address bound to its squared amplitudes and sum; then sum over possible guesses, dropping the correct-guess indicator in nonnegative summands. This is an average over actual L,T, not a pointwise bound after conditioning on a rare L. Quantum side information about T is not covered by counting classical leak values.

**One insertion and target-dependent verification.** Fix classical coins, purify the computation, and let Psi0 and Psi be its final states with the zero and real oracles. Replace one query at a time with a zero prefix and real suffix. The difference D=O-I satisfies D=P D P on the marked-address projector P and has norm <=2. Fixed inter-query unitaries can be absorbed into the adjoining states. Each difference term has joint (over classical randomness) L2 norm <=2 sqrt(h), so ||Psi-Psi0|| in this joint norm is <=2q sqrt(h). A final address produced by the zero computation has marked mass <=h under the same hypothesis. Projection onto a successful submitted address and the triangle inequality bound real forgery probability by min(1,(2q+1)^2 h). This test can know the secret T. A generic decision bound by state distance is min(1,2q sqrt(h)).

**Public final measurement.** Require the final two-outcome effect to use only the public information available to the circuit. This stricter hypothesis yields
`|Pr_real[accept]-Pr_zero[accept]| <= min(1,4 q^2 h)`.
For the proof take either the accept effect F or the reject effect I-F. Write delta=Psi-Psi0. The signed change for the chosen outcome equals twice the averaged real cross term <F Psi0,delta> plus the average of <delta,F delta>, which is nonnegative.

To bound the cross term, order factors with the measurement on the left. The difference product is a sum of q terms with one inserted D, zero factors outside it, and q(q-1)/2 terms with two inserted D's, **actual factors between them** and zero factors outside. One exact way to derive this is to telescope with a real suffix first, then telescope that suffix from the measurement end. Replacing the middle with zeros in general loses higher orders. The ordered_insertions theorem proves the noncommutative identity and both counts. In every resulting matrix element the forward outer vector is prepared by a zero prefix and the backward outer vector by applying F to Psi0 then reversing a zero suffix. Their norms are at most one and their squared marked norms each have expectation <=h. For a single insertion bound the magnitude by 2ab, with a,b the two marked norms. For a double insertion bound it by 4ab; the middle actual evolution has norm one. The outer variables can be correlated since 2ab<=a^2+b^2. Thus the absolute averaged cross term is bounded by 2qh+4 choose(q,2)h=2q^2 h for **both** effects.

The direct unsigned treatment gives 8q^2 h after bounding the quadratic term by 4q^2 h. Instead select the outcome whose real probability decreases. Its signed change is minus the absolute decision gap; its quadratic term can only diminish the magnitude of the decrease. Twice the cross bound now suffices. The choice is between two fixed public effects based on the sign of the overall gap, not the hidden T of an individual sample. At q=0 the gap is zero. For q=1 the constant 4 is sharp in the limit: a uniform hidden singleton among N addresses has p=1/N. Querying on the uniform state with a phase-kickback answer qubit produces overlap 1-2p with the zero-oracle state. The public orthogonal-complement projector gives gap 4p(1-p). This example does not require the evaluator to know which point was marked.

Comparing *real-bank real-oracle* against *real-bank zero-oracle* via their ideal versions costs the two endpoint advantages: one reduction with the real checker and one with the zero checker. **Public code and the modified square-framed target.** The black-box lemma alone gives no publicly executable verifier. PR 176's clear corrected-phase function contains the degree-0 and degree-1 coefficients g,gt (recoverable by full-output evaluations); they determine the original g t^8449. Thus the original target is predictable in the very auxiliary view containing that function. A computation-only public equality program requires its own distributional obfuscation theorem, with all required auxiliary fields, output payload, parameter leakage, quantitative loss and quantum model. The Wichs–Zirdelis compute-and-compare theorem specifies an unpredictability requirement on distributions of the circuit and its target. [Wichs–Zirdelis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/276.pdf)

For a distinct *full-vector* target, let x be a fixed search secret of 32 F257 elements as in [PR 188](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/188). Append ten zero coordinates and split into 14 triples. In each concrete odd-characteristic quartic field set E_i(x)=(1+aX+bX^2+cX^3)^2 for the corresponding triple; X here is the quartic basis generator. Converting F257 representatives to F769 integers is an encoding, not a field embedding. These codes are injective on the specified padded words: only one root of an honestly encoded square has constant coefficient one since roots come in opposite pairs. Canonical checks include coefficient ranges and padding. Quartic field square roots suffice and require no discrete logarithm. Choose independent uniform nonzero rho_i, publish D_i=rho_i^2 E_i(x) and set target Y_i=B_i rho_i^2 for known nonzero B_i=g_i t_i^8449. All D_i are independent uniform squares for each fixed x and base transcript, by multiplication inside the square subgroup. The simulator samples them without x. Couple the hidden rho through that same public value. Given x, Y_i=B_i D_i/E_i(x). Conversely, given the whole vector Y, B_i D_i/Y_i recovers every E_i(x); a public decoder recovers x. Handle zeros, malformed words and noncanonical roots as failures. The code and all base data for the comparison function f are allowed to be public, provided they are generated by the joint simulator and omit any unaccounted rho-dependent field. Evaluate f on a supplied address and convert its value to a candidate x by the same decoder. No target test is needed by the search reduction. D alone adds 528 canonical bits, plus other actual key material; public generation, inversion and reversible comparisons must be costed.

For example PR 188's false affine-system gate transcript is simulatable jointly with the actual fixed search instance. Its standard raw-packing gate issuer opens the 32-coordinate head on a satisfying witness. An honest wrapper can use the version sampling an unconstrained search secret x first, mask the canonical packed value with the row/terminal translation, and generate fresh D independently as above. The affine zero-target simulator is valid for each fixed secret, so the square-frame view is jointly simulatable on NO. Extra raw nonzero-block conditioning, if used, must transfer with its explicit probability. This example is a decidable circuit subfamily. Using a fixed search distribution for a reduction does not prove its hardness or install it in the WE registry.

**Computational form of the public-decision bound.** In the last experiment consider a simulator of the entire NO public view from the search challenge, including full f, public bases, masks D and modeled auxiliary fields. Suppose every oracle-free quantum recovery algorithm used below succeeds with probability at most sigma at its *larger* explicit resource budget. Then the preceding proof also gives decision gap <=min(1,4q^2 sigma) even if the additional oracle answers the exact full-vector equality test f(a)=Y. The same reasoning gives final marked-address success <=min(1,(2q+1)^2 sigma). Here sigma needs no statistical min-entropy interpretation. For a forward outer vector, simulate the zero prefix, measure its address a and decode f(a). For a backward outer vector, simulate the complete zero circuit, measure the public accept/reject flag for the chosen outcome, and on success apply the inverse zero suffix before measuring the address. Use a projective dilation of the public final measurement (or implement the positive contraction F as a block of a known unitary). Abort on its unsuccessful branch; no free postselection is allowed. The unconditional marked mass equals the squared projection norm of the subnormalized backward state. Whenever the measured address is truly marked the decoded candidate is correct. Hence each outer expectation is <=sigma, including the zero final-state address used for forgery. The native checker knowing Y is never implemented by the fixed search reduction. For an approximate full-view simulator require joint closeness with the hidden search solution and preserve the relevant ideal mark relation. For instance total variation eta changes each such recovery-event probability by at most eta, yielding sigma+eta in the mass bound on the honest view. Marginal closeness of public headers alone need not preserve this event.

Fixed-index forward recoveries use at most the q-query algorithm's zero-prefix cost, and each backward recovery uses a full forward plus one partial inverse execution, measurement/dilation, evaluation of f, all field operations and output decoding. The maximum across the two outcome-specific families (or a separately priced randomized choice) is the resource budget required for sigma. These are oracle-free *search* computations on the specified classical input. This argument is for a whole-vector equality condition: hitting just one CRT arm does not generally recover x. It does not show that issuing the equality program satisfies a compute-and-compare obfuscation theorem, or that a witness to arbitrary NAND SAT supplies x. No numerical post-quantum assumption strength follows.
