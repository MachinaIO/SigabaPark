Use one public full-tableau domain with 128 primary and 4,096 gate coordinates. At gate i, references to as-yet-unavailable coordinates evaluate to true. A passing assignment has the new output c equal to 1+ab in the squarefree Boolean ring. The final pass domain sets the final wire to one. Two public vertices s,t bound otherwise disjoint columns, one for each tableau z. Layer i connects only consecutive vertices in z's column when check i passes. A path from s to t is possible precisely for a fully consistent accepting tableau. The following offline keys implement authorized edge-difference evaluation for an explicit finite function space, without hiding a secret online evaluator.

### Exact condition for public restriction keys

Choose a public vector space V of Boolean functions including 1. Let r_i be a public linear restriction to passing domain i, encoded canonically and injectively as a function on that domain. Pick all but the last consecutive difference d_i independently uniformly in V and set the last so their sum is β·1. This is the uniform law on the affine sum fiber and equivalently samples uniform independent internal node potentials (cumulative sums). Issue just the whole tuple r_i(d_i) with a fixed public descriptor. Given a passing z one evaluates the indicated row; a satisfying full tableau sums its rows to β. No one receives the arbitrary unrestricted d_i or the sampler coins.

Put K equal to the sum of the kernels of r_i. Sending β=0 and β=1 has identical distributions on complete serialized packets iff 1 belongs to K. A decomposition of 1 into g_i with r_i(g_i)=0 translates every explanation (d_i) of each fixed transcript to (d_i+g_i). Conversely, equal supports imply that the public all-zero tuple, present for β=0, has an explanation with endpoint 1. Without such g_i, even a single shared transcript is impossible by subtracting its two explanations. The two endpoints have equal numbers of private coin tuples since the number of rows is positive. The Lean theorems exact and disjoint prove the fiber bijection criterion and support separation over arbitrary additive commutative groups. Exact equality of all classical ciphertext bytes entails equality after any quantum processing, including inspection of the evaluator code if fixed independently of the private coins.

There is an explicit public separating algorithm when the test fails. Solve for a binary linear functional ell annihilating K and taking value one at 1. For each i it factors through r_i on its image, so solve the linear systems for a_i with a_i r_i = ell (extend arbitrarily on the complement). Then sum a_i(header_i). The readout theorem gives β exactly for every choice of masks. On YES, evaluation at a true tableau itself furnishes such a functional; the matrix solver does not need a witness. Equivalently, lift every public row arbitrarily and sum in V/K. This quantity is independent of the lifts. Thus a scalable uniform construction of polynomial-size explicit matrices which always hides on false NAND inputs would decide classical SAT in polynomial time by Gaussian elimination. The bounded 128-input instance has no unconditional complexity separation. Failure on a particular NO matrix, on the other hand, gives an exact elementary distinguisher on that input.

For V given by distinct explicit squarefree monomials M no full 2^4224 table is needed. For a term not containing c copy it. Otherwise write it as mc and replace it by m and mab, canceling duplicate terms over the binary field (omit unavailable operands from the product, since they are 1). The final domain replaces c by 1. Canonical squarefree ANF on the remaining free variables is unique. For each row publish the sorted union of possible surviving monomials, with coefficient bits including zeros; the layout is independently reproducible. This is at most 2|M| candidate terms per gate before deduplication. Compression to a row-image basis is optional. On the 4,093-NOT fixture below the raw restricted layout totals 67,112,955 coefficient bits before any inter-row compression; the common basis dimension is 16,385.

### A single polynomial issuer

For each i and selected monomial m use the atom B_im=m+r_i(m), viewing the restricted normal form as a polynomial independent of the eliminated c. This vanishes on its passing domain. Choose one independent fair bit per nonzero atom and issue the canonical ANF Q=β+Σ u_im B_im. The evaluator computes its tableau from its witness, then evaluates Q there. This message-only ciphertext omits individual edge keys. Its full public laws coincide exactly when 1 is in the span of the issued atoms. If a sum of V-valued functions g_i vanishing on each respective passing domain equals 1, expand each g_i in the original basis and use linearity: Σ(m+r_i(m)) over its expansion equals g_i. The Lean theorem atoms proves this identity in subtraction notation. The converse need not hold since the atom span may extend outside V. If the space is closed under all the substitutions, the criteria coincide. Normalization and retaining only Q are essential; releasing the per-atom coin vector gives its mask polynomial immediately.

A strict separation already occurs for y=NOT x, t=NAND(x,y), o=NOT t and the check o=1. For M=(1,t,o,xyo), the respective restriction kernels are generated by xyo; nothing; 1+t+o; and 1+o. The public linear functional constant coefficient plus coefficient of o annihilates all four generators and separates 1. Yet the atom issuer can flip the independent coins for xyo, 1+xy+t, 1+t+o, 1+o and xy+xyo; these five atoms sum to 1. The extra coordinate xy cancels only after merging multiple layers. The original difference-key distributions have disjoint supports on this NO instance, whereas the single polynomial distributions coincide. The fixture is an algebraic separation between the two public presentations.

The construction can also test its atom span and find either a translation or a dual bit by Gaussian elimination on the public sparse matrix. Giving arbitrary succinct Boolean-circuit features changes the cost of canonicalization itself. For any formula F(x) define g(y,u,x)=if y then F(x) else u. The two globally independent binary features 1,g are always independent since at y=0 both values of g occur. On the one-coordinate passing cylinder y=1, a deterministic canonical representation of g compares equal to that of zero exactly when F is identically false. The Lean theorem canonicalizer formalizes this equivalence and nonconstancy. This is a coNP-hard normalization task for succinct circuit features in the usual growing family. It does not apply to the explicit monomial substitution algorithm or rule out randomized computational representations.

### Full-size positive specialization and machine costs

Both tagged fixtures have exactly the registered 128 witness positions and 4,096 NAND gates. Let u₀=x be the first input, and apply 4,093 NAND-with-self gates to yield y. The last three gates are a=NAND(x,y), b=NAND(y,x), o=NAND(a,b) in the NO fixture. In the YES fixture replace only b by NAND(a,a). The public dictionary contains every monomial on each gate's local scope and also x u_i and a x u_i for all chain wires. A NO certificate multiplies each chain violation by (1+a)x, the a gate by 1, the b gate by a, the o gate by 1 and the assertion by 1. The Boolean-ring sum is 1; all summands lie in V. Equivalently sum the chain violations to 1+x+y and expand. Translating the coins selected by those summands preserves every issued coefficient when the message bit flips. This specialization uses the chain benchmark from the earlier algebraic investigation; the public atom packet, physical layout, full-ciphertext coupling and two concrete machines are the contribution here.

The NO atom issuer takes 24,580 fair coin bits and emits 24,574 coefficient bits from 65,542 selected taps. The YES instance takes 24,575 coins, emits 24,569 bits and uses 65,525 taps. These counts include the constant coefficient and its message shift. To compile the pair, tag them by one public bit. The offline compiler groups each coin by the largest nonglobal chain-variable ID in any monomial of its atom, with x,y,a,b,o treated as global. It orders output monomials analogously. Within a row it visits sorted coin addresses starting at the nearer end. The NO issuer makes 556,947 one-cell moves between taps and the YES issuer 409,478. Both first fill and rewind the coin tape. The serial FST reads the message without advancing its input head during emission, keeps only a carry parity in control and outputs every coefficient. The decoder uses the fixture tag, the 128-bit witness (including reading the 127 unused coordinates), and the coefficient string. It evaluates all monomial bits for each of the two possible first witness bits; the static evaluation schedules are fully charged in the transition table. It XORs exactly those coefficients whose monomials evaluate to one.

The actual enc and dec in SparseAtomPair cost 1,318,248,283 combined. Enc has one work tape, 2,391,609 states, clock 671,651 and dense description 1,291,468,860 bits. Dec has zero work tapes, 197,599 states, clock 24,704 and dense description 26,083,068 bits. The registered cost theorem reduces the actual machine fields. Encoder inputs are the two raw bits (fixture tag, message); decoder inputs are the tag, raw 128-bit witness and coefficient string. No arbitrary 4,096-gate descriptor is accepted by these two machines. Their clocks are one pass over finite straight-line control, plus the tagged entry/exit; the joint cap is scoped to the public two-circuit family. Rebuilding a transition table separately on the basis supplied by an arbitrary circuit is a preprocessing cost and cannot be treated as fixed-circuit online encryption for the whole goal. The replay listing generates both machines, checks coefficient equality under the NO coin translation, and checks both possible first witness values under the YES alternative. Direct executions of the actual Lean tr functions matched every reference coefficient for both bits in both fixtures. The NO execution used all 671,651 Enc and 24,704 Dec steps; YES used 524,155 and 24,699 steps respectively. Formal functional refinement of the full controls is open.

A uniform alternative picks features by reverse substitution. Start with the output variable and the assertion atom 1+o. In reverse topological order replace each current monomial containing the gate by its local NAND normal form; record its old monomial plus its replacement as an atom. The XOR of the recorded atoms telescopes to 1+C(x), where C(x) is the fully substituted witness-only output ANF. Thus on NO the all-ones coin translation flips the message; on any accepting witness every atom evaluates to zero. The dictionary may depend on the public circuit and the substitution path but is fixed before the private coins. In the same false chain the replay found 4,098 recorded atoms, 4,099 serialized coefficient bits, and 6,145 monomials inspected by the backward loop. A different YES neighbor, changing one final gate operand, has 4,096 atoms and 4,097 coefficient bits. The replay also checks 200 randomly generated ten-gate circuits exhaustively on all eight inputs. This alternate plan is outside the two registered fixture machines. Intermediate monomials can grow exponentially, even for a compact circuit computing a product of disjoint pairwise sums; without truncation it explicitly computes the final witness ANF. An abort budget gives only a restricted class whose issuer finishes. In the accompanying reverse listing all fixture parents are prior wires; in the general rule an unavailable operand contributes multiplicative one.

### Boundaries for keyed and quantum alternatives

A succinct key-homomorphic PRF might encode differences under a common keyspace, yet each gate needs an offline key for its own passing cylinder under a shared input representation. Independent per-gate input permutations for prefix constraints change what the same PRF input means at an internal vertex. Giving the original vertex keys exposes unrestricted differences. A theorem simulating just one derived key or a secret oracle leaves the entire correlated key header to simulate. Even a redundant unrestricted coefficient or a shared seeded syntactic tag outside the canonical row can invalidate the exact coset proof. No new hardness assumption is introduced here.

For an external 528-bit target t the following conditional Fourier test applies only when the reduction has the full public preheader H for that same target and can hold it fixed across the uniform Fourier seed r. A joint sample (t,H) may be correlated; r must be independent conditional on it. Let p_b(r) be the acceptance probability with H,r,b, where all tables seen by the attacker are included or coherently and correctly computable. Set δ=E[(-1)^<r,t>(p₀(r)−p₁(r))], an acceptance difference. Start r and b uniformly in superposition, apply the distinguisher, phase-flip on acceptance and uncompute. Postselecting its clean ancilla and the minus Hadamard output for b leaves amplitude with coefficient p₁(r)−p₀(r); after the r Hadamards the amplitude at t is the negative conditional signed gap. Measuring without postselection therefore recovers t with probability at least δ² by Jensen. It costs a coherent invocation, its inverse and the seed/register processing. A guessing advantage defined relative to one half has a factor of two when converted to an acceptance difference. A syndrome depending on t is usable only if the complete correlated preheader can be supplied in the recovery experiment. The printed scalar machines do not simulate an external syndrome and garbled table or certify any fixed lattice hardness. A full all-circuit candidate still needs a suitably efficient uniform issuer secure on every false instance and a full-view reduction for any computational fallback.
