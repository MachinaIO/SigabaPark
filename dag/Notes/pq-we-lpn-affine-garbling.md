## Fixed-law encryption

Benny Applebaum's [full version, ePrint 2012/516](https://eprint.iacr.org/2012/516.pdf), Construction 3.3, Theorem 3.4, Eq. (4), Figure 1 and Lemmas 4.4–4.5, underlies this one-evaluation component. The [TCC 2013 proceedings version](https://iacr.org/archive/tcc2013/77850161/77850161.pdf) is the conference reference. The paper uses uniform binary k-by-N secret S and independent uniform t-by-k A for each basic encryption. A fixed linear code maps each message column by G. The ciphertext contains A and AS+E+GM. In the paper, columns of Bernoulli noise E exceeding the code's worst-case correction threshold are replaced by zero. This gives perfect decoding; moving back to raw LPN samples in the security argument costs a binomial tail for each column and call. For a code guaranteed through r errors this tail is Pr[Bin(t,p)>r]. The theorem allows choosing the constant p, subject to p below the correction radius. Search LPN is its stated premise; the argument after Lemma 3.5's search-to-decision step starts directly from decisional samples.

Here N=1. In the fixed core, take registry slot 0 (decision LWE), ring degree 1, rank k, modulus 2, secret uniform and error gauss(s) for positive rational Gaussian width s. Each sample has its own independent matrix row and independent error. With a=exp(-pi/s^2), the exact error bit is Bernoulli with p = (sum over odd integers j of a^(j^2))/(sum over all integers j of a^(j^2)). This follows from ModuleLWE.coeffLaw and sampleMat; no Gaussian approximation to a Bernoulli mass is required. A centered binomial with positive eta gives exactly uniform parity: conditioning on all but one fair constituent bit leaves a uniform last bit. Uniform residues do too. The bounded uniform integer law on [-B,B] gives probability B/(2B+1) for even B and (B+1)/(2B+1) for odd B. In particular its positive bounds do not realize the low rates in the examples here.

**A finite variant.** Use raw independent noise and an efficiently decoded linear code with a per-message stochastic decoding bound. This replaces the paper's worst-case code/chopping condition. The same algebra proving privacy works with raw noise; finite correctness is proved separately below. Draw noise from a dyadic p' with b=192 fair coins per position, using the strict comparison U < floor(2^b p). The whole m-bit law differs by at most m 2^-b from exact registered noise (couple the underlying uniform draws). This also supplies a fixed-clock sampler, unlike unbounded exact Bernoulli sampling. For s=9/8 the integer threshold is 898700706111295618230042562711923365687147143385080548808. It was evaluated with 100 decimal digits of interval precision by summing the terms for |j|<=12; the remaining two tails are bounded above by 4 exp(-169 pi/s^2), using the decreasing geometric ratio after 13. Both endpoints for the scaled quotient lie strictly between this integer plus .015 and this integer plus .016. No approximation is used in the definition of LWE.H.

## Finite code and one-row reduction

At k=512, the colored payload has 513 bits. Pad to 517 and split into 47 blocks of 11. Encode each as the truth table of its affine function on 10 binary variables (the [1024,11,512] first-order Reed–Muller code). Concatenate to length t=48128. Binary distances to a distinct affine function are either 512 or 1024. Decode each block by closest affine function, with deterministic ties. A Walsh–Hadamard transform of the sign vector gives all linear-part correlations; their signs determine the best constant bit. This is a direct sum code for independent channel errors. Chopping a whole 48128-bit column at the minimum-distance radius of this direct sum would give an incompatible parameter condition.

For each competitor at distance d and independent Bernoulli bit flips with probability p'<=1/6, a decoding tie or loss requires at least d/2 flips on those d positions. Every such pattern has probability at most (p'(1-p'))^(d/2), so summing at most 2^d patterns bounds its probability by (4p'(1-p'))^(d/2). For s=9/8, p is about .14317128254. In particular a=exp(-64 pi/81)<1/11, and the total odd mass divided by the normalizer is at most (2a/(1+2a))/(1-a^8)<1/6. Thus p' also meets the hypothesis. A fixed honest path uses two basic decryptions at each nonlinear gate; the intended path is independent of fresh basic encryption errors. Union-bound the first departure from the intended path over these decryptions, 47 blocks and 2047 competitors per block. For at most 4096 such gates the total is below 2^-180. The companion rm_gate_tail_budget checks the rational numerical bound, including all four factors and the weaker overlap bound 9/16. No independence between separate failure events is needed. In a larger composed construction sum over all accessible honest evaluations and add failures of other components.

A wire's inactive key equals its active key plus the same hidden 512-bit shift S. Keys of all active inputs on the path are known to the simulator. For every nonlinear gate there are four ordered cells. Each contains two *parallel* basic encryptions, one hiding a uniformly random 513-bit share and one hiding the complementary share of the colored output key. On the active cell both base keys are known. The three other cells require respectively one, one and two queries for keys of the form S+D with known offsets. The needed encrypted plaintext is M+PS, where P is either zero or embeds S as the key prefix followed by the separate color bit. The message padding and direct-sum encoder preserve linearity. The simulator samples its own shares and fresh matrices for ordinary known-key calls. This handles offsets within a garbling under **one** S; unrelated garblings with independent shifts incur a further hybrid per row. A global shift reused across invocations must be proved jointly.

For one hidden query take fresh registered rows (A0,Z0). Feed the garbling procedure the matrix A=A0+GP and the response Z=Z0+GM+AD. In the real case this encrypts M+PS under S+D over the binary field; on uniform input both outputs are jointly uniform because the transformation is bijective for fixed query. The checked affine_oracle_row gives the cancellation over general rings using subtraction. Previously issued transcript data may determine the next classical query; unused rows of the joint LWE challenge have the required fresh A and error conditional on the secret. Simulate the real and fake oracles by two such replacements (zero P and M on the fake side). With Q=4g, the one common-secret challenge needs m=Qt samples per hybrid, *not* m samples per gate per hybrid. The ideal oracle law agrees under both changes. All known-key encryptions and the nonuniform setup-independent data that either reduction can generate must be retained in their original joint law. Each invocation uses fresh A; publishing a single A across cells is a different experiment. For N>1 shared-A columns need a column hybrid with independently generated other secrets/errors; N=1 avoids it.

If the single registered decision advantage at the full reduction costs T_0,T_1 is bounded by sqrt(T_i/2^H), then the absolute privacy gap from this RK-KDM step is bounded by sqrt(T_0/2^H)+sqrt(T_1/2^H)+2m 2^-192. Each reduction uses the receiver once after preparing the entire classical view, with no rewind, coherent oracle or search-to-decision call. Therefore the same transformation works against QPT consumers of the one classical transcript **conditional on QPT hardness of the two finite decisional experiments at those resources**. The registered HardAt quantifies over classical Machine. This gives no registered quantum certificate. For equal-output comparisons through two simulated views, pay both real-to-sim errors unless an exact common hybrid removes one. For composed protocols include all other simulations, multipliers, conditional losses and the complete time of parsing the registry encoding. In the fixed core LWE.decision uses ModuleLWE.encodeSample (encoded matrix and list of naturals), not raw packed binary cells; the rectangular shape permits deterministic decoding, whose scan and repacking time count.

**Actual H evaluations.** Pinned-core evaluation of LWE.H at (k,m,q,error,secret)=(512,385024,2,gauss(9/8),uniform) gives **325**; m=788529152 also gives **325**. The first m corresponds to two nonlinear gates, the second to 4096. For the smaller batch the minimizing model is bdd_mitm_hybrid with floored log cost 325. The Gaussian-to-estimator map takes the rational width divided by sqrt(2 pi). These are values of the implemented strength function, not independently validated LPN costs. By contrast k=128, m=2044 or 8372224 and s=2/3 gives **33**, as do the tested (k,m)=(256,16760832),(512,16760832),(1024,33538048) at that width. Concretely LWE.H can be evaluated as `#eval LWE.H ⟨512,4*2*48128,2,.gauss (9/8),.uniform,by decide⟩` in a scratch module importing SigabaNeoStd.Lattices.LWE and opening SigabaNeo.Lattices. For the different sweep at k=512 and m=4*4096*26624, widths 33/32, 17/16, 35/32 and 9/8 give 130, 205, 272 and 325 respectively; code lengths and failure probabilities must be redetermined for a changed sampler. No search, ring or circular assumption is used by the single-row security calculation.

## Projected evaluator and literal costs

Use a 128-input NAND statement with front p of 126 bits and two tail bits u,v. Its public function is parity(p_1,...,p_63) AND parity(p_64,...,p_126) AND u AND v. Each XOR has a four-NAND realization and AND has two NANDs; pad the syntax with unused gates before the useful circuit to meet the 4096-gate convention. Exact projection yields the AND of the two parities; a message bit beta is ANDed into that result as prescribed by PR 148. The direct private circuit has I=127 input wires, x=124 XOR and g=2 nonlinear gates. Its functional check is independent of the projection heuristic. If front bits are split across two owners, each may precompute its two local contributions and supply only four parity bits plus beta: I=5, x=2, g=2. This assumes the preprocessing is available to those owners before encoding. With all p at one owner and the projected predicate public, precompute both parities *and their conjunction*, leaving only two inputs, no XOR and one AND with beta. In particular the satisfiability of this public fixture is easy; none of its savings is a worst-case CircuitSAT result. In a compiler handling a family of hidden predicates the latter preprocessing needs a separate ownership and leakage justification.

Each basic ciphertext contributes t(k+1) bits. The explicit raw encoding is 8gt(k+1) table bits, plus I(k+1) active input key/color bits and one output orientation bit in the single-output fixtures. This omits framing, circuit transport and any transfer of labels between owners. At the principal row:

| fixed evaluator | XOR gates | nonlinear | table bits | table plus input/output bits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| direct projected circuit | 124 | 2 | 395034624 | 395099776 |
| per-owner parity preprocessing | 2 | 2 | 395034624 | 395037190 |
| one owner computes the public conjunction | 0 | 1 | 197517312 | 197518339 |
| all nonlinear, 128 inputs | 0 | 4096 | 809030909952 | 809030975617 |

The last line prices a literal 4096-nonlinear-gate evaluation without simplification. These lengths alone exceed the exploratory 2^32-bit budget on that line. A circuit simplifier could reduce many particular lists. Neither row size nor the raw predicate gate count determines the size of a recursive AGG circuit manufacturing lower-level ciphertexts.

For the two-gate layout, explicit generation does 16 matrix-vector products with exactly 394264576 bit ANDs and 393494528 parity XORs in their dense dot products. Evaluation does four matrix-vector products with 98566144 ANDs and 98373632 parity XORs. Garbling also generates 394264576 fair matrix bits and 770048 Bernoulli samples (147849216 fair comparison bits with the literal sampler); dot products, codewords, errors and shares must be combined. Evaluating all 1024 truth-table positions in a block takes at most 10 XORs per position for the affine encoder, hence at most 7700480 more on this garbling. The decoded four columns have 188 blocks. A ten-stage Walsh transform uses 1024 signed integer adds/subtractions per stage per block, or 1925120 adds/subtractions, with at most 12 signed bits for values in [-1024,1024], followed by comparisons and sign decoding. Every XOR wire costs 513 bitwise XORs in each propagation of the active colored label (63612 for 124 wires or 1026 for two); garbling propagates zero-labels and masks too. For 4096 nonlinear gates, matrix products use 807453851648 garbler and 201863462912 evaluator ANDs alone. These are bit-operation and randomness counts for specific implementations; the comparisons, input movement, output writes and full table scans have real cost. A finite tape clock or reduction cost cannot be read off directly from a logic-operation count. Worst-case time and both Machine description lengths must still be certified. There is no claim that the full evaluator with serialization fits 2^32 or that a complete WE meets either budget.

## Terminal interface

The ideal one-evaluation simulator here chooses independent active colors only on wires not produced by XOR. At an XOR output it sets the color equal to the XOR of input colors; all four masks also obey that identity. Programming the three bit colors independently as in a naive composition with the terminal orientation method can request an impossible triple. Testing all four choices of the two input colors gives precisely their determined output color. A possible boundary uses the present public linear/arithmetic interior and independent terminal records with explicit conversions. For example, use a unary identity gate implemented as a binary projection (second dummy input fixed and selected honestly); its fresh output key/color before terminal programming costs a full four-double-cell table, 197517312 bits, plus a 513-bit dummy input label when not shared. Alternatively a dedicated two-cell unary adapter encrypts one new label/record under each semantic input key with fresh independent public matrices, giving 2t(513)=49379328 bits for a 513-bit target record. Larger terminal payloads must be coded and priced at their actual width. Its unknown-key query must be simulatable under the same S, including any terminal fields depending on pads or hidden controls. Neither a programmed nonuniform output color nor an exposed companion field follows from the standard simulator. A separate full-view coupling, including cross-role keys, adaptive private input issuance and multiple rows, remains necessary. Keeping the free-XOR component inside an ordinary arithmetic zone avoids assigning terminal colors inside that zone. The correctness and security arguments here cover one static randomized encoding; a witness-encryption issuer and false-statement hybrid are still open.

## Shorter finite instantiation

An outer Reed–Solomon evaluation code over GF(128) with 114 distinct evaluation points and 74 message symbols has minimum distance 41. Pad 513 bits to 518 and group into seven-bit symbols, identifying the usual polynomial basis of GF(128) with seven bits. Encode each resulting seven-bit RS symbol by the 64-bit truth table of an affine function on six variables. This inner RM(1,6) code has 126 competitors at distance 32 and one at distance 64. The composite encoder is binary linear and uses **7296 rows** instead of 48128. The same explicit Gaussian threshold, real/fake simulation and raw-noise interface apply.

Let T_d be the upper binomial tail at d/2 for d independent flips of probability p'. The inner block fails with probability at most 126 T_32 + T_64. It is convenient to evaluate at the larger rational crossover 7159/50000 by stochastic domination. Summing the binomial tails there gives about 0.00023569193 as an upper bound. For an exact and easily replayable certificate, bound each binomial coefficient above by its middle coefficient and its remaining factors by a geometric progression of ratio p/(1-p). Putting z=p(1-p), the resulting bound is (1-p)/(1-2p) times [126 choose(32,16) z^16 + choose(64,32) z^32], which is less than 1/4000. For any fixed message, the 114 inner error events depend on disjoint noise coordinates. Even allowing ties to be resolved deterministically, every 21-event intersection has probability at most (1/4000)^21. The outer decoder corrects through 20 erroneous symbols. The total bound over all 32768 basic encryptions of all four cells at 4096 gates is below 2^-161, with concatenated_channel_budget verifying both numerical inequalities. Honest decoding needs only 8192 basic decryptions and so gains two more bits in this bound. Using the exact inner tail gives 2^-164.87 for those honest decryptions. These correctness calculations do not degrade privacy, which uses the raw channel. A shortened outer length 108 still permits 17 errors; the corresponding conservative 8192-decryption union bound is below 2^-135, with 6912 total rows (arithmetic screen only).

Pinned evaluation at k=512, s=9/8 gives H=325 at *each* of m=29184, 58368 and 119537664, the actual single-common-secret sample counts for respectively 1, 2 and 4096 nonlinear gates at t=7296. The dyadic coupling term 2m/2^192 at the largest m is below 2^-164. This gives a real reduction budget: e.g. if each single-row wrapper has certified cost at most 2^190 times a distinguisher cost T>=1, the RK-KDM gap is at most 2 sqrt(T / 2^135) plus 2^-164, enough even after twice this single-view gap for an equal-output comparison at the 128-bit squared-advantage target. The wrapper clock and the underlying QPT conditions are separate hypotheses. A multi-row wrapper must recompute its own loss and costs.

For the five-input, two-nonlinear-gate example, the shorter table is **59885568 bits** or **59888134 bits** including its selected labels and one output bit. The unsimplified 127-input variant totals 59950720 bits, and the fully owner-preprocessed one-gate version 29943811 bits. All 4096 nonlinear gates cost 122645643264 table bits before the 65665 remaining label/output bits. The smaller two-gate garbler computes 59768832 dense bit ANDs and 59652096 XORs for its sixteen A-times-key products and uses 22413312 binary comparison coins for its error vector samples. The evaluator's four products use 14942208 ANDs and 14913024 XORs. The truth-table inner part of the four decodes uses 175104 Walsh additions/subtractions (signed 8-bit intermediates suffice at length 64). RS generation by Horner costs at most 114*73 multiply/add pairs per encryption. Nearest-affine inner encoding costs at most six XORs per position after initializing the constant. These counts are independent of any multiplication or XOR costs for PRF-based alternatives.

One explicit deterministic outer decoder is Berlekamp–Welch. Solve for monic error locator E of degree 20 and polynomial Q of degree at most 93, imposing Q(a)=y_a E(a) at all 114 points (114 unknown field coefficients). If the system is inconsistent, or E does not divide Q with degree-at-most-73 quotient, return a fixed default. Otherwise return the quotient; any solution is correct through 20 errors since Q-fE vanishes at 94 or more points and has degree at most 93. For that many or fewer errors a monic degree-20 locator exists by multiplying a minimal locator up to degree 20. A straightforward upper for Gauss–Jordan elimination is 114*114*115 field multiply/add pairs for row elimination and 114 inversions plus 114*115 multiplications for pivot normalization per decode. Matrix setup, deterministic pivot search/swap, final division and binary transport also count. This is a concrete elementary-operation schedule instead of a free invocation of a decoder. It still supplies no end-to-end registered tape clock or WE submission. The shorter code also reduces the 513-bit unary adapter payload to 2*7296*513=7485696 bits per independent adapter, before proving its auxiliary-field law.
