# Audit of T4 and T4b (21 November 2026)

**Verdict.** The static conclusions S1, S2, S3′ and S4 hold at the stated sieve radii, using the classical transverse Gram density and the Euclidean diagram estimates of the G1 audit. The exhaustive real-RAM SVP corollary follows with the cited basis reduction and sampler. Two stronger readings fail. T4 cannot transfer Proposition 5.1 uniformly over all positive radii, and Hypothesis P in §3.9 of the companion note has incompatible requirements. The faster time under an implementable, narrowed search hypothesis remains undecided here.

## T4 integration

Write spatial coordinates as rows and let the columns be the nonzero vector slots. A supported column has norm at most $s\sqrt m B$. The logarithm of its Hadamard bound to order $k$, divided by $\log q$, is at most $kn/m+o(1)=o(1)$. Thus the exact weight is $s^{-rm}$ on rational rank $r$. Maximal-minor pivots give reconstruction coefficients of magnitude at most one by Cramer's rule. The projection lattice of index $D$ contains $D\mathbb Z^r$, so the common denominator divides $D$. Counting numerators, pivot choices and indices gives $2^k(2d)(4d+1)^{k^2}$ possibilities per dyadic bin. Changing pivots multiplies the projected covolume by the absolute Jacobian and the integral by its $m$th power. This gives the ordered Rogers coefficient even for nonprimitive pivot projections.

For the discrete tail, keep the spanning condition until after counting. Independent projected rows in the box generate a lattice of covolume $V$. Each of the $V/D$ cosets contributes at most $[2(r+1)sB]^r/V$ points by disjoint translated cells. After taking the $m$th power and applying the probability weight, the cost per space is at most $E[2(r+1)B]^{rm}D^{-m}$, also sufficient for the integral. The dyadic ratio is at most $2^{-m+k^2+1}$. At $H=\lceil e^{m^2}\rceil$ the total is $e^{-m^3+o(m^3)}$.

For smaller indices, failure to span forces the first $r$ spatial rows to have zero determinant. This nonzero polynomial has at most $rN^{rm-1}$ zeros on an $N$-point Cartesian grid per coordinate. The resulting loss per space is $s^{-1}Er(2B+1)^{rm-1}$. Each of the $D^{(r-1)m}$ residue classes has cell volume $(D/s)^{rm}$, giving overall coefficient $D^{-m}$ on the averaged quadrature. A crossing moves a squared distance by $O(mkr(B+1)H/s)$. For a nonconstant test fix all coordinates except one with slope of magnitude at least $1/D$. A quadratic window of width $2\eta$ cuts length at most $4D\sqrt{2\eta}$, including tangencies. Identically zero differences give constant tests. Multiply by the remaining box volume, the number of tests, and twice the workload bound. Off crossings use the within-piece Lipschitz bound on each cube. Including all small indices costs $s^{-1/2}\exp(O(m^2\log^2 m+m\log^4 m))$, below $e^{-2m^2}$. The binomial expansion costs at most $2^{2h}$, leaving the claimed $e^{-m^2}$.

For compact nondegenerate central triangles the two-distance density is sphere area times $ab h_\perp^{m-3}/|d|$. The middle half of both windows has area comparable to $(R/m^2)^2$ and transverse radius at least $cR$, giving $K_R(d)\ge R^m e^{-O(m\log m)}$. Translation by distance $t$ changes a ball by symmetric-difference volume at most twice its projection volume times $t$. Apply this to both boundaries to control the convolution and then divide by the infimum of (K) squared for the weight. Telescoping products fits the stated $E$ on the sieve radii. In integer coordinates the half-width is $sR/m^2$, much larger than the cube radius $\sqrt m$ in both ensembles. Cube rounding alters the window area relatively by $O(m^{5/2}/(sR))$ and the log density by $O(m^{3/2}/(sR))$. These use only the distance between centers, so sparse centers satisfy the same bounds. In particular the native squared-norm interval has length $4s^2R^2/m^2\gg1$. The cube sandwich supplies the actual count guarantee that this spacing observation alone cannot give.

**Radius counterexample.** If “on their respective domains” includes every $R>0$ in Proposition 5.1, fix a sufficiently large dimension and its prime and send $R\to\infty$ at a fixed interior target ratio, with $h=1$. The event $A=0$ has fixed positive probability $q^{-nm}$. There the lattice is the full mesh. Uniform cube rounding gives target population asymptotic to $s^m Q$ and weighted degree asymptotic to $s^m m_*$, uniformly in the mark. Both $Q$ and $m_*$ scale as $R^m$. The event contributes order $R^{3m}$ to the centered second moment, whereas the proposition bounds it by order $R^{2m}$ plus a constant. Restrict the transfer to the radius and workload range actually proved. [Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13635v1#S5.SS1)

## T4b modular ranks and centered moments

For rational rank $t$ enumerate a spanning list of row *values*, costing $(2sB+1)^{kt}$. No row-position factor is needed to specify the space. On any injective set of $t$ coordinate pivots, the projected residue span has dimension at most $u$, hence at most $q^{tu}$ generator lists suffice including zero padding. Its full inverse image has index at least $q^{t-u}$. Apply the preceding coset argument to the projected rows and raise to power $m$. Multiplication by $q^{-nu}$ leaves $s^{tm}q^{-m(t-u)-nu}=q^{-(t-u)(m-n)}$. This allows pivots singular modulo $q$. For $u=0$ the generator list is empty and the index is $q^t$; in fact $sB<q$ eventually rules out a nonzero row in this case. The log of all positive factors is $O(\ell^8+m\ell^4)$, including rank choices and the signed expansion. A single rank lost costs at least $\delta m\ell^6$. This proves the replacement even at ratio two.

Use the exact normalized full-grid fibre $M(v)$ as a deterministic center. It need not equal a conditional expectation given lattice membership. Freeing the mark and occurrence pivots in partition sums introduces rank $d<t$ arrays at cost at most $E(2sB+1)^{td+dm}s^{-tm}$ per pattern. Its leading mesh factor is $s^{-m(t-d)}$, enough after summing all patterns. For each set of nonsingleton blocks, a remaining label is either a new singleton of mass $M(v)$ or a center of mass $-M(v)$. Their sum vanishes at each grid mark. A target reflection $x\mapsto v-x$ preserves the grid and both tests. Each later slot has two orientations for a target block and one for an endpoint block. Thus at most $h$ blocks remain and their sum is bounded by $Qe^{O(h\log(2h))}M^h$ for $M\ge1$. Replacing the center by a continuum approximation before cancellation would lose this identity.

For nonpartitions, $D>e^{\ell^5}$ costs $e^{-\Omega(m\ell^5)}$ by spanning and the same dyadic ratio. Below that cutoff the cube extension $2k\sqrt m H/s$ is smaller than any inverse power of $m$. Bound the unsigned terms using positive enlarged indicators, then change pivots. Free fibre masses change relatively by $o(1/m)$. The longitudinal error stays $O(k/m^2)$. For target height-one signs the integer condition $2A+\sum B_i=1$ excludes two parents. For endpoints $2A+\gamma^2(\sum B_i-1)$ is a multiple of $1/\ell^2$; equality and coprimality force $\sum B_i=1,A=0$ at logarithmic order. In particular no extra low-parent relation enters after enlargement.

With the classical transverse Gram density, the offending column of relative coefficient norm at least two costs determinant base at most $e/4+o(1)$ by Hadamard and AM–GM. Three or more unit signs give at most $16/27+o(1)$; ordered denominator at least two costs $2^{-m}$. In the height bins the exponent is initially $(m-O(k))/2$, and the count grows only as height to $O(k^2)$, so the dyadic sums converge before replacing the exponent by $m/2$ in the first bin. At most $j-1$ free occurrences among $j$ retained give the lost power of (M). Enlarging windows preserves all these estimates, including their $\exp(O(k^2\log m))$ overhead. This uses the raw diagram estimates, since an upper bound on an already centered signed sum would not justify termwise enlargement. [Continuum derivation](g1_audit.md)

## Static events and exact SVP

On each mark band $M(v)/M=1+O(1/m)$ by differentiation in separation. Double counting gives the endpoint average from the target average times $V_{\gamma R}/V_R=\gamma^m$. Hence each S2 violation supplies cost at least $(cM)^{2h}$ for some fixed (c>0). The endpoint at the terminal radius gives the conservative bound $\log M\ge m/(3\ell)+O(m/\ell^2+\log m)$ by expanding $\log\gamma+1/\ell+\frac12\log[(4/3)(1-\gamma^2/4)]$. The mark-volume exponent there is $\frac12\log(4/3)+o(1)$ per dimension. With $h=\ell$ the partition exponent is at most $\frac12\log(4/3)-1/3+o(1)$. Scaling radius raises population and degree by the same power. The three correction exponents after division are at most $\log(8/9),\frac12\log(e/3),-\frac12\log3$, plus $o(1)$. The target has at least as much slack. Markov already sums over marks, leaving only two mark types and $O(\log^3m)$ levels.

For S3′, the nonzero population inside $R^*/2$ has expectation $(1+o(1))2^{-m}$ by order one. From there to $r_0$, use $O(m^2)$ full bands of relative half-width $m^{-2}$. Each has $Q\le e^{O(m/\ell)}$, whereas the lens is at least $(13/12)^{m/2}e^{-O(m/\ell)}$ from the transverse radius at the outermost separation. At order two each missing mark contributes its center squared, leaving failure $O(Q/M)$ up to polynomial factors and negligible mesh errors. S1 and S4 use the ordinary second moment. T4's primitive-multiple bound costs $O(V)$ in variance, T4b's rational-line overcount costs $O((2sB+1)\mathbb E N)$. Here $\log(sB)=O(\ell^6)=o(m/\ell)$ in T4b. The shell volume is exponentially large in $m$, and the S4 ball volume is $(1+1/\ell)^m$. This gives the claimed respective failure exponents.

Full rank fails with probability at most $q^{n-m}$. Conditional on full rank, each nonzero dual residue is included with probability $(q^n-1)/(q^m-1)$. In physical coordinates the dual test ball has radius $(q/s)R^*/2<q$, excluding nonzero zero-residue lifts. Cube rounding inflates its volume by $1+o(1)$ since $q/s$ dominates every polynomial. Its expected nonzero occupancy is at most $(1+o(1))2^{-m}$. Transference now bounds the last successive minimum by $2m/R^*$. The cited reduction in S5 and GPV initialization apply on this event; the input kernel basis can be obtained by modular elimination and integer lifts in polynomial input length. The shrinking-tree independence argument uses fresh movers and the bank-independent accepted law. Terminal lists cover the shell with high probability, S3′ supplies a shortest difference, and exhaustive comparisons cost $(4/3)^{m+o(m)}$. This invokes the cited reduction and sampler as black boxes in the companion computational model. [Algorithm, §§3.4–3.8](provable_sieving.md)

**Counterexample to Hypothesis P as written.** For a fixed cosine $c=0.45$ in its open window, a direction passing both half-threshold caps must have squared projection onto the axes' plane at least $1/[2(1+c)]$, by projecting on the normalized sum. A uniform direction's squared plane projection has tail $(1-t)^{(m-2)/2}$. Thus $W(c)\le(1-1/[2(1+c)])^{(m-2)/2}$, whose base is strictly below $2/3$. With $N_f=(3/2)^{m/2+o(m)}$, P2 bounds candidacy by $e^{-\Omega(m)}$, while P1 requires reporting with probability approaching one. Reporting requires candidacy in this search. This contradiction is independent of lattice statistics. A conditional implication with that P is vacuous.

A repair would require P1 only for actual query cosines, whose reverse minimum is $1/2-O(1/\ell)$. For the terminal shortest pair, order one also excludes lengths below $(1-1/\ell)R^*$ with failure $e^{-\Omega(m/\ell)}$; together with S4 its cosine is $5/8+o(1)$. A subexponential filter overhead can absorb the reverse-angle displacement. The q-ary annealed work estimate is compatible with this repair: condition on the filter direction for independent slots to obtain the product of cap masses (in T4b exterior cubes move the cap threshold by $O(\sqrt m/(sR))$). Proportional pairs in T4b add at most $(2sB+1)V_R C_m$, a subexponential multiplier at the relevant densities. In T4 distinct absolute multiples require largest multiplier $j\gg\ell$, giving the summable tail $V_R C_m\sum 8j^{1-m}$. For the correctness union bound one can expose the ideal exhaustive transcript independently of the search randomness and union bound all compared pairs, at most $(4/3)^{m+o(m)}$, choosing the constant in P1 accordingly. Existence and cost of a product-code structure satisfying the repaired guarantees remain unverified. [Search interface and work argument, §3.9](provable_sieving.md)

## Prior work and formal scope

Kim, Kwon, Lee and Lee already give provable exact SVP for random q-ary kernels, posted 31 October 2026. Their density is $n\log_2(q)/m$ in this audit's notation. Their stated fast regimes extend through density $3/2$, while both T4 densities diverge. Their coordinate lists, modular matching and dynamic programming address a different algorithmic question from shrinking-shell incidence. A blanket priority claim for random q-ary exact SVP is false. The search located no earlier proof of these shrinking-shell statistics at these large moduli; priority for this narrower claim remains unverified. [Paper and algorithms](https://eprint.iacr.asia/2026/2711.pdf)

Pouly–Shen analyze random real lattices via smoothing and discrete Gaussian sampling. Doulgerakis–Laarhoven use the Haar ensemble for their shrinking-shell sieve. Hhan's q-ary coset tree is internal to an algorithm with a general-input SVP bound. Gao–Feng–Hu use random auxiliary superlattices and a BDGL decoding layer for general inputs. Worst-case guarantees already apply to q-ary inputs. None of these observations supplies a discrete marked-moment transfer. [Pouly–Shen](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1805) · [Doulgerakis–Laarhoven](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37482) · [Hhan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02764) · [Gao–Feng–Hu](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1844)

The local Lean statements give finite nonnegative workload averages, short-rank preservation and short-shell moments. The marked-tail theorem accepts a supplied witness. The defect inequality charges a supplied exceptional set at full modular weight. Neither statement estimates its geometric or asymptotic inputs. This review inspected their source signatures and proofs; the integration, defect count, grid cancellation, Gram estimates and algorithmic corollaries are paper arguments. I did not rebuild Lean or independently formalize the classical Gram, basis-reduction or sampling inputs.
