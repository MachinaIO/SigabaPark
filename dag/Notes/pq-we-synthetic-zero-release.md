Consider a candidate public program with actual trace-prefix responses P(H_A(r)), followed by a publicly computable decoder mixing that response with static ciphertext fields. A candidate intended to release a masked secret at zero may enforce by its syntax, on every statement including NO, that this downstream decoder at the synthetic response P(0) recovers its message (or a reusable pad). The interface may restrict callers to graph-shaped bit traces with final output one, and need not expose coefficients. If each returned response component has mixed additive difference zero in d+1 directions, then this interface alone still permits recovery on a concrete false circuit. Polynomial maps of total degree at most d over a commutative ring, including extension or product rings, have this difference property. Every monomial on substituting a sum of directions is insensitive in at least one of d+1 factors in its alternating Boolean expansion. The statement applies to the response coordinates before an arbitrary public final decoding operation, not to arbitrary randomized encodings that merely release a bit on genuine YES witnesses.

Use constant wire zero and 1366 separate true gates NAND(zero,zero). Conjoin their outputs, then complement the conjunction. A constant-zero NAND(true,true), the true gates, 1364 two-gate intermediate ANDs and a final NAND give exactly 4096 NANDs. The honest output is always zero. Flip any nonempty subset of the true gates, recompute the entire suffix honestly, and the resulting bit trace has final output one. Its signed residual is -1 exactly at the selected true gates and zero elsewhere. Hold the 128 witness inputs fixed throughout. Choose d+1 of these gates. All nonempty subset sums of the corresponding signed hash directions now have legal syntactic traces for the response program. The alternating cube identity reconstructs the one missing response from those 2^(d+1)-1 observable responses, with integer signs in the additive output group. In characteristic two this is XOR. The completion lemma is formalized over any additive commutative group. A collision of two hash addresses is unnecessary; if a queried trace already hashes to zero, its answer of course suffices. The proof is for each fixed header and ciphertext randomness. For probabilistic evaluators the same coins must define one consistent polynomial, or one must budget the aggregate response errors. If any static auxiliary field is also evaluated as a function of the trace, it must be recoverable at zero by the same argument or supplied separately.

For example take a hidden key K and an affine ring-valued response K+L(H_A(r)) followed by bit decryption using K. Two choices of a single gate and the trace with both flips give K exactly by adding the first two answers and subtracting the third. This falsifies a specific release even if the code hides its affine coefficients and queries must come from actual traces. A filter that accepts only zero hash and suppresses all other responses removes this interface; efficient implementation and full description privacy of such a filter remain open. A standalone collision finder or a coherent equality oracle is not equivalent to seeing the complete public program.

**When only rounded bits are exposed.** On the fault cube let a public response bit be bit j of a scalar polynomial of degree d over Z/(2^s), with j<s. Its Boolean algebraic degree is at most d*2^j, including carries. Indeed reduce to square-free monomials on Boolean inputs and give each coefficient a nonnegative representative a_S. The coefficient of T^{2^j} in (1+T)^P over F2 equals bit j by the binary expansion of P. On Boolean x the product over monomials has factors 1+x_S*((1+T)^{a_S}-1). Every chosen nonconstant T factor consumes at least one exponent, so at most 2^j factors and at most d*2^j distinct variables contribute to the desired coefficient. Coefficients and fixed dither may depend on the header or ciphertext, independent of the choice of query vertex. Boolean XOR preserves the maximum degree. Thus a q=128 affine scalar readout even when thresholded to its upper bit needs at most 2^65-1 traces for this exact extrapolation; at q=64 it is 2^33-1. A direct full-ring affine readout takes only three. Arbitrary branching, keyed nonlinear postprocessing before exposure, or statement-dependent canonicalization of infeasible zero values can invalidate the degree and synthetic-zero hypotheses. This is an operation count; every actual trace generation and evaluation must be charged before comparing with a specific registered advantage/cost budget.

These results leave open a fully specified efficient public description that releases a bit on a genuine witness while simulatable on general NO statements. Perfect completeness at true witnesses alone does not force correctness at the algebraically extended zero on false statements.
