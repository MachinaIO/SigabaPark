For binary Hx=y, |x|<=w, preprocessing can depend on y while keeping exact NO hiding. First solve the linear equations ignoring weight. If infeasible, output a common dummy distribution. Otherwise write every solution as x=x0+N t, with columns of N a kernel basis. A zero row of N fixes a coordinate. Group all other coordinates by identical rows of N. Within each group the coordinates equal a single binary variable up to known flips from x0. If the group contains a ones and b zeros in x0, orient its variable so its cheaper setting has cost min(a,b). Its more expensive setting costs an extra |a-b|. This orientation is valid also at a tie.

Add the fixed ones and all group minima to c. Write x=v+J z for the oriented group variables: v is the baseline and each column of J toggles its group. This parametrization admits extra assignments if the group symbols have further dependencies, so retain the full equations HJ z=y+Hv. Their solutions correspond exactly to original linear solutions. The residual constraint is sum_i d_i z_i<=W=w-c with d_i=|a_i-b_i|. If W<0 the instance is false. Fix d_i>W to zero; eliminate zero-cost symbols by projecting the target and positive-cost columns to the quotient by the span of the zero-cost columns. Denote the resulting equations by H' u=y'. Any original witness gives u in polynomial time. Conversely a bounded-cost u lifts by solving an ordinary linear system for the free symbols and expanding the groups. This is an equivalence of witness relations, with no hardness assumption or need to enumerate short witnesses.

**Mask for arbitrary positive residual costs.** For a budget j let S_j be the support vectors of cost at most j in the residual m coordinates, V_j its size, and A_j(e)=sum_(v in S_j) (-1)^(v.e). Choose a public splitting budget a between 0 and W and put b=W-a. Draw e and a private tag bit h with probability

    (A_a(e)+(-1)^h A_b(e))² / (2^(m+1)(V_a+V_b)).

Send (H'^T s+e, s.y'+h+mu) for independent uniform s. The tag distinguishes the two amplitudes inside the sampler and does not cost an extra wire bit. Orthogonality gives E[(-1)^(h+u.e)] = 2 |S_a intersect (u+S_b)|/(V_a+V_b). This vanishes whenever cost(u)>W by subadditivity. Hence all message-dependent ciphertext Fourier coefficients vanish on a false instance, as in the syndrome-mask proof. On a true instance a witness gets that bias and can amplify by fresh repetitions. Unlike the two equal balls, an unbalanced split can include an indivisible heavy coordinate. The fallback a=0 gives positive bias 2/(1+V_W) for every allowed witness. A public mixture of budgets has the corresponding mixture bias for an unweighted vote. For unit costs and even W, the balanced choice recovers the earlier ball formula.

**Classical polynomial sampling per repetition.** W<=n for an original n-bit witness and w<=n. For any tag h and prefix of e of length l, let C_j(k) be the signed sum over prefix support vectors of cost <= j-k, with signs given by the fixed e prefix; a negative remaining budget contributes zero. Let M(k) count suffix support vectors of cost exactly k. Summing Fourier squares over the unfixed e suffix gives the unnormalized prefix weight

    2^(m-l) sum_k M(k) (C_a(k)+(-1)^h C_b(k))².

This follows by orthogonality on the suffix. Signed and unsigned knapsack counts are computed by ordinary include/exclude dynamic programming with integer counts of polynomial bit length; only k<=max(a,b) is needed. Sample h and e down this prefix tree using integer weights, for example by unranking a uniform integer below the root total. Exact rejection sampling takes expected polynomial time; a bounded rejection cap costs its abort probability. Decryption of a single sample uses just the parity of u against its header.

**Concrete structured family.** In 128 coordinates impose 48 independent opposite-bit pair equations x_(2i-1)+x_(2i)=1, leaving 32 other coordinates. Add arbitrary further linear equations, and set w=50. One may apply just the known 48 pair groups, preserving their zero-cost switches through quotient elimination. Every solution spends exactly 48 on these pairs. The residual instance has 32 unit-cost coordinates and threshold two. For every valid witness, the balanced mask has bias at least 2/33, payload 33 bits per repetition, and squared inverse bias at most 1089/4. Further equations can be arbitrary, including couplings to the pairs. Inconsistent linear systems can be handled as above. These numbers are for the single-bit payload before amplification and exclude the public statement. At general size this removes a large nominal witness weight when it is mostly forced. Unstructured instances can retain the full residual budget; no general polynomial bound on repetition follows.
