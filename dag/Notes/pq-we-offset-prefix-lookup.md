### A common tag between the two exact prefix domains

Use the 12417-bit raw address of 4225 wire primaries and 8192 separate gate pins. The first chart has a single 4096-zero prefix enforcing NAND correctness by adding 1 plus the product of its two pins to each output bit. In the second chart a single 8194-zero prefix enforces the constant and final-one pins and the 8192 wiring differences. A self or forward reference uses the true constant. Both charts are bijective, and a valid witness gives an address in both domains. In common causal order a transformation of the form x_i XOR h_i(x_<i) maps each prefix to one prefix. Moving all the various constrained coordinates to the front is not generally an automorphism of that common prefix tree.

Choose a public tag L on raw addresses; both chart evaluators first check their own prefix, invert their chart and compute L. Call the respective reachable tag sets S,T. With two public outer covers U⊇S and V⊇T, publish in key A a random Boolean table restricted to U, and in key B its translate by the message on V. These are two restricted evaluation descriptions. At every matched raw address their outputs XOR to the message. If U∩V is empty, flipping the table on V couples both entire serialized keys simultaneously, preserving all A entries. JointLookup checks the involution and both outputs. A tag with two different preimages in the two covers is enough to read the bit if both lookup records can be evaluated at it. The proofs below concern disjoint covers on the NO members of the stated subclasses; they do not require every false circuit to be separable.

For an additive tag in a finite group, express L as a sum of contributions from individual raw coordinates. On the first chart each NAND row supplies four possible combined contributions; each of 129 independent source bits supplies two. On the second chart collect the contributions of all copies of each unfixed primary and pin the two fixed primaries. Each of 4223 binary groups supplies two values. Starting from the constant offset, repeated addition of each such set computes the exact reachable tags independently in both charts. Over 8192 tags a dense bitset recurrence needs at most (4096·4+129·2+4223·2)·8192=205520896 source-membership checks across both tables, apart from indexing and initialization. This is not a Turing-machine time estimate. The key generator can also use analytically certified outer ranges. Since public cover intersection is decidable directly, efficiently constructing small separating covers on every NO would itself decide the restricted SAT problem.

### A registered family in which 4096 independent contributions matter

Use one initial dead gate whose right input is the constant. Append 4095 gates z_i=NAND(z_(i−1),1), with z_0 equal to the true constant for the false member, or to witness input x for the true member. Last output is pinned to one. On odd gates contribute 2−d_left−d_right−z_i; on even gates contribute z_i+d_left−1; add 1−d_right from the dead row. This is a single affine integer score on the *common raw* variables. RowSlacks proves that each summand on a valid local NAND row is zero or one. Each has both values and the choices in the first chart are independent. Thus its exact image is the entire interval [0,4096]. In the wiring chart the dead term is zero, and every adjacent odd-even pair cancels its shared primary. AlternatingWires gives the odd terminal sum 1−z_0−z_4095=−z_0. Thus the constant-start circuit has second image {-1}, while the witness-start circuit has image {0,-1}. The latter is satisfied precisely at x=0. These are full-size instances with 128-bit witnesses and registered pins. Individual intermediate gate primaries on the second side may be arbitrary: the cancellation is an identity on the whole authorized wiring chart.

Use modulus 16384 and the 14-bit canonical representation of the score. On A every tag has leading bit zero. On the false member every B tag has leading bit one. The two distinct length-one tag prefixes cover both domains. On the true member B may also issue the leaf at tag zero. This choice is determined by the public start wire. Furthermore a direct two-bin version chooses independent fair bits for the two half intervals. The complete NO public view consists of one pad and the other pad XOR the message and is exactly uniform. On the true member the second key includes the shifted pad for zero as well; the matched address recovers the message. Parsing, chart maps and the circuit itself are public. This elementary version has no assumption and a constant lookup within each bin.

An integer lookup can also separate an instance whose two real convex hulls meet. Take g=NOT(x), h=NAND(x,g), out=NOT(h). For the six differences between a gate pin and its referenced primary and the seventh difference out−1, assign coefficients 1,2,...,64 (successive powers of two). A highest nonzero signed digit exceeds the sum of lower absolute weights. Thus the tag modulo 128 vanishes precisely when all seven equalities hold. B has only zero and A has no zero, since x and its complement cannot make the circuit accept. Enumeration of all 1024 raw assignments gives 91 A tags. Yet the fractional point x=g=h=1/2, out=1 and matching pin marginals lies in both hulls: in the first two NAND rows mix (0,0,1) with (1,1,0) equally, and in the final row mix (1,0,1) with (0,1,1) equally. Independent row mixtures realize the first hull, while the affine wiring distribution with these primary means realizes the second. Padding by unused gates and ignoring their tags preserves the result. This separates the finite-image method from real hyperplane separation.

### A shared nonconstant field lookup from the registered fixed game

Here the lookup value can vary at every 14-bit tag, even within a covered half. Use the existing RingLWE.decision at N=512, m=8, q=128, uniform ring secret and independent CBD(1) errors. The matrix consists of eight uniform ring entries and is public once for the whole tree. This same choice has registry estimator H=177. H measures the registry's classical game; it is not a certification of a quantum assumption, a HardAt proof or a concrete reduction clock. There is no circuit or chart in this challenge distribution.

Given the matrix, expand one uniform 11776-bit seed. The first 3584 bits encode the 512 uniform 7-bit secret coefficients; use 8192 further bits, in pairs, to subtract two fresh independent bits for each of the 8·512 error coefficients. All coins have fixed length. Compute the eight bodies A_j s+e_j in the negacyclic ring and encode all coefficients in seven bits in a fixed order. Take the first 23554 of their 28672 bits and split as (s_left,r_left,s_right,r_right), each s of length 11776 and each r one bit. In the real registered experiment these are exactly one expansion from a fresh uniform seed. In the ideal experiment they are independent uniform chunks, conditional on the matrix. At ring degree 512 the registered game and seven-bit body encoding have a deterministic public conversion by reading the specified coefficients; its machine cost must be charged in a quantitative submission.

Each node carries (s,p). Its children carry (s_left,p XOR r_left) and (s_right,p XOR r_right). Start with fresh independent root seed and root pad. A subtree key discloses the node seed and pad. For each B subtree flip its node pad by the message *before* serialization. The evaluator applies the unmodified expansion repeatedly and returns the final pad. OffsetTransport proves the Boolean invariant; by induction it holds down to every leaf. No separate clear message or root key is given. Unlike the prior equality test between independently rooted strings, the two scalar lookup outputs XOR to the bit with zero correctness error, even if unshifted leaves collide.

For the full-size false member, the two issued keys correspond to the root children. Give a simulator a single Ring-LWE challenge, copy the public matrix, split its supplied bodies as above, sample the independent root pad, apply the requested message shift, and publish the two child records in the public order. This simulates the complete real keys exactly in the real experiment without knowing the challenge secret. In the uniform experiment the node seeds and transmitted pads are all independent uniform in either message world, even after exposing the shared matrix. Thus message advantage is at most twice the single-challenge distinguishing bound with the encoding and adversary overhead. Equivalently randomize the simulated world and conditionally complement the test output to obtain half of the original signed gap. All inputs to the test are classical strings and the argument also permits quantum postprocessing provided the fixed distribution is secure against that class. No quantum claim is inferred from the classical registry.

More generally, when U and V are disjoint unions of whole tag prefixes, their minimized covers form one antichain. Replace the expansions at each strict ancestor of an issued node in top-down order. The seed for the next challenge is uniform, unpublished and unused elsewhere since earlier ancestors are already ideal. Use the same challenge matrix for every honest sibling expansion simulated locally. Independent edge pad bits at ideal parents make the issued pads independent as well. With J expanded ancestors, the joint message gap is at most 2J times a one-challenge bound, with the cost of the other expansions. If a prefix belongs to both covers on a false circuit, the underlying correlated descendants expose the message and this proof no longer applies. The keys are single *raw chart prefix* evaluators; the number of subordinate tag-prefix records is charged separately.

In the two-child NO encoding the matrix and two seed-plus-pad records occupy 28672+2·11777=52226 raw bits if their order and public routing are fixed. Allowing the additional B leaf in the true family member costs another 11777 bits when a full seed-plus-pad record is sent, or a single shifted pad if the known depth-14 leaf is specially encoded. A tag evaluation below a root child takes thirteen expansions. Two such evaluations use 54,525,952 individual coefficient multiply-add iterations with schoolbook negacyclic products; their 7-bit arithmetic, all tape access, chart inversion, witness-to-trace evaluation, matrix setup, framing and both machine descriptions remain to be priced. FixedExpansionCost checks the expansion lengths and the schoolbook count. The small two-bin perfect scheme does not require a cryptographic evaluation. No full WE submission with the general 2^32 objective follows.
