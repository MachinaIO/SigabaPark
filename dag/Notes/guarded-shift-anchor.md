## Guarded bit and its public residue

Source: Abram, Malavolta and Roy, *Tree Encodings I: How to Authenticate a Truth Table*, [ePrint 2026/741](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/741), Theorem 5, Sections 4.1–4.4, especially (27), (28), and Lemmas 12–14. Use the printed floor convention. Let q=8s and p=4. For a witness w let y be the scalar master phase including its public mask and b the message bit times C(w). Choose f = 2b − floor(y/(2s)) mod 4. The raw shifted phase is R = y+2s f+e mod 8s, subject to the raw-phase invariant in the proof of Lemma 13. Accept bit 1 if (R+s) mod 8s ≥ 4s. Division with remainder gives 0 ≤ (y mod 2s)+e+s < 4s for every integer |e| ≤ s. This includes both endpoints and negative master phases. For errors up to s+1, the pairs (remainder 2s−1, error s+1, bit 0) and (remainder 0, error −s, bit 1) produce the same raw value. Every evaluation exposes R mod 2s = (y+e) mod 2s. Arbitrarily many evaluations obtained from the same issued key can be correlated.

The Lean node verifies these scalar equalities and inequalities. A noise-tail theorem for the actual key-dependent invocation is still needed for the registered per-witness error. The shift includes information sufficient to compute its own phase. Lemma 14 quantifies over constraints fixed before setup. In Lemma 12 the outer CRS is programmed as A_i = A_i* + c_i Gbar, with c_i taken from the inner GSW ciphertext. Encoding a master derived from those same CRS matrices creates a second dependency. Merely hiding GSW encryptions of the secret does not implement that joint programming.

## What the envelope charges

Write l=log2 q, n=k l, t=2mn as in Section 2.4 and Section 3. Assume positive integer m,k, depth δ ≥ 1 and σ√λ ≥ 1. The numerical noise *envelope* B displayed in the proof of Lemma 13 dominates (tm n^4)^(dδ) ≥ (2(k l)^5)^d. Certifying B ≤ q/8 by this particular expression therefore requires 2^l ≥ 8(2(k l)^5)^d. This is a bound on a sufficient proof criterion. There is no lower bound here on the sampled error. At d=128, k=1, iterating L ← ceil(log2(8(2L^5)^128)) from L=1 gives 131, 4633, 7925, 8421, 8477, 8483, 8484, then equality. Monotonicity makes each iterate necessary. The outer bodies of Section 4.1 have η((k+1)l)^2 rows of (k+1)l residues, hence charge η(k+1)^3 l^4 bits with literal fixed widths. At η=k=1 the single body uses at least 41,446,954,694,412,288 bits. This crosses a 2^32 design target. The registered primitive cap is 2^100.

The scalar self-dependent input costs substantially more if it transmits the master material explicitly. Theorem 5 gives M of shape n by r m n^4 and Section 4.2 uses r=nµ. Even at µ=m=k=1, putting the full fixed-width M into the shift input costs η≥n^6 l; the outer bodies alone therefore exceed 2^146 bits. Precontracting sᵀGM to its single row of r m n^4 residues still gives η≥n^5 l and more than 2^133 body bits. Both comparisons to the registered cap follow already with l≥8484; the scalar bounds and integer implications are kernel checked. Neither count applies if the input carries a short algorithm/seed reconstructing these data without literal transmission. The size of any such algorithm, its evaluation depth, and its effect on the self-dependent security proof must be charged. Deterministically computable from the CRS does not mean independent of the CRS.

For fresh independent key bodies for positive chunks summing to 128, the reproducible integer recurrence is L(d,k) obtained by the iteration above with input d and multiplier k kept fixed. Define B(d,k,η)=η(k+1)^3 L(d,k)^4; D(0)=0, D(j)=min over 1≤d≤j of D(j−d)+B(d,k,η(d)). Induction on the last chunk proves exact minimization of the stated lower bounds only. At k=1, η(d)=kL(d,k), the minimum is 21,003,416,576 bits, or 724,255,744 when η=1. At k=2, η=1 gives 5,186,160,000. For literal M set η=n^6 l and for its contracted row use η=n^5 l, taking µ=m=1. The same recurrence exceeds 2^106 for the former at k=16, and 2^105 for the latter at k=32. In all these reported cases singleton chunks minimize this arithmetic objective. Separate chunks still require an actual composition to evaluate C across them.

One possible alternative noise certificate is per path. In (28) write the tree contribution as ⟨e,v⟩ with v the centered integral lift of H V′ modulo q. For a fixed w, v is determined by pk,crs and the fixed universal evaluation circuit, independently of the fresh tree error e. If entries of e satisfy E exp(t e_i) ≤ exp(t²a²/2) independently, then except with probability 2 exp(−u²/(2a²‖v‖₂²)) its magnitude is ≤u, with zero contribution for v=0. This follows by multiplying the moment bounds, applying Markov and optimizing t. One can allocate the remaining s−u to the LFE error and union-bound those two failures, without assuming mutual independence. If v or the chosen lifted coefficients depend on e the argument must be re-established. No useful uniform finite norm bound or concrete self-dependent LFE error law is established here.

## Shared correction vector with a joint NO reduction

Use the additive trapdoor hash (TDH) syntax in Section 4.3, Definitions 2–4, independently of the guarded tree construction. Instantiate the output group as F₂ and use a fixed family f(w,(C,b)) = b C(w); fix C for each challenge and allow both bits. Let U(w)=Enc(hk,ek,w) be the public share and V(w)=Dec(hk,td,Hash(hk,w)) the private share. On a correctness event of failure probability ε_b, U(w)+V(w)=b C(w) for all legal w. The published-key hiding experiment uses fixed (C,0),(C,1). The paper's Theorem 7 states a construction from LWE; the quantitative registry reduction and postquantum compilation for these parameters are not supplied by this note.

Suppose public coins, hk and C select anchors a_1,...,a_r, along with a public evaluator Rec(w,t). At generation issue the single TDH encoding key and the r bits T_i=V(a_i). On the reconstruction event Rec(w,T)=V(w) for every needed w. Decryption outputs U(w)+Rec(w,T). For instance, if V(w)=⟨v(h(w)),θ⟩ for **every** issued trapdoor and each public feature row v(h(w)) is a known linear combination of anchor rows, the same linear combination recovers V(w). A digest table with one representative per reachable digest is a special case. Indexing unattained digests and supplying their unchecked trapdoor decodings is unsound. No general feature representation or short spanning set is asserted for the paper's TDH.

For a false C substitute S_i=−U(a_i) on both sides. By TDH correctness all coordinates equal T_i simultaneously except with probability ε_b. A single fixed-input TDH hiding challenge supplies (hk,ek); the reduction samples the public coins, selects the anchors, computes the entire S, appends it to the unmodified public data, and invokes the final distinguisher. In acceptance-probability difference convention, Adv_WE ≤ ε_0 + Adv_TDH + ε_1. The reduction adds the coin/anchor generation, r public evaluations and r-bit formatting. An honest issuer adds r private digest evaluations and transmits r corrections, plus hk, ek and any public selection coins. Decryption pays one public evaluation and Rec; a full dense reconstruction costs up to r field products and additions after computing coefficients. Give the distinguisher exactly the honest public view. This endpoint argument handles arbitrary joint processing of the public ciphertext, including quantum processing of classical samples if TDH hiding holds against that class. The arithmetic is formalized in recover and simAll. The distributional argument is on paper.

There is a sharp limitation even for succinct reconstruction. Let G be the event that all anchors reject on a satisfiable C and fix a valid witness w in the *hiding distinguisher*, independently of setup. On b=0, fake corrections S lead to output 0 except with probability at most e_0; on b=1, if G holds and both correctness and reconstruction hold, fake corrections at w give 1. Write e_b for an upper bound on the joint failure of row correctness and reconstruction in world b. Testing the fake output at w gives Pr[G] ≤ Adv_TDH + e_0 + e_1 for selection depending only on C,hk,independent coins. The sampler followed by ordinary verification of the r anchors is therefore a public *search* algorithm on every YES on which the hypotheses and bounds apply. No uniform solver for arbitrary NAND is claimed. In particular for a point predicate and a statement-oblivious compressing hash with a public canonical section, at most 2^k of 2^d inputs are representatives per hk. Averaging gives a fixed point outside the section with probability at least 1−2^(k−d). Since its representative is then rejecting, the holder of that known point can distinguish by U(w)−U(rep(hash(w))). This direct experiment evaluates one section, even when a table over 2^k digests is infeasible.

A more general public *decision* test does not require actual representatives. Suppose S(hk,ek,C,publicCoins) deterministically reconstructs the exact auxiliary bits T of that same issued ciphertext with failure ≤δ_0 for all C when b=0, and ≤δ_N for NO when b=1. The issuer can generate its own matching (ek,td) and compare T to S with b=1. On NO the false-positive chance is ≤δ_N. On YES suppose decryption with a fixed valid witness has error ≤c_b for genuine T. For b=0 the fake vector decrypts to 1 with probability ≤δ_0+c_0. By TDH key hiding this rises by at most Adv_TDH for b=1. Since T=S together with honest correct decryption would yield 1 there, the public equality test has false-negative chance ≤δ_0+c_0+Adv_TDH+c_1. This decision test itself uses no witness. The hiding bound charges the separate distinguisher that has w as advice; in the finite setting its description and work must be included. This argument requires same-key deterministic reconstruction and the zero-message condition for YES; it does not apply to ordinary computational joint simulation with freshly sampled keys. Randomized couplings with hidden coins and unrelated complete-view simulators remain genuine directions. No new assumption or general witness-encryption submission is introduced.
