## Size of an individual fixed-schedule chart

Every 2-CNF solution set is closed under coordinatewise majority: among three satisfying pairs of a clause, at least one literal is true under their majority. Literal complementation commutes with majority. The six-bit truth-table statement is formalized by binaryMedian. Projection after existential auxiliary bits preserves this closure by lifting three witnesses and then projecting their majority. Translating all primary assignments by a fixed binary word is a coordinate complementation and also preserves the property.

Let C be a binary linear code on n coordinates, and let nu(C) be the largest number of nonzero members with pairwise disjoint supports. Any nonempty majority-closed Q contained in C has size at most 2^nu(C). Translate by an element of Q so that zero belongs, still within C. Regard solutions as sets of coordinates. Their family F is intersection-closed because majority(A,B,empty)=A intersect B. Inside each interval below a member T it is union-closed since majority(A,B,T)=A union B for A,B contained in T.

For every used coordinate i let M_i be the intersection of all supports containing i. It contains i and belongs to F. Partition the used indices by equality of their M_i. If j belongs to M_i then M_j is a subset of M_i. The union U of all strictly smaller M_j for j in M_i belongs to F: this is an interval union under M_i, with empty union allowed. The difference M_i minus U is exactly the block of i. The two inclusions are formalized by forcedBlock. Since both M_i and U are codewords, their difference is a nonzero codeword by linearity and inclusion. Distinct blocks are disjoint, and every support in F is a union of whole blocks. This proves the bound. Conversely choose nu(C) disjoint nonzero words. Their span has 2^nu(C) points and is described by copying a representative bit throughout each block and fixing unused positions to zero, all with unit and binary clauses. Each coset inside C is another 2-CNF by literal flips. Consequently the *minimum* number of arbitrary original-coordinate 2-CNFs whose exact union is C equals 2^(dim C−nu(C)). A routing schedule need not realize every such formula; the expression gives a universal lower bound for a fixed-schedule cover. Exhaustive finite enumeration verified the largest majority-closed subrelation for every binary subspace in dimensions one through four (2,5,16,67 codes).

## A fixed circuit that needs at least 2^92 components

Index 128 input coordinates by t in GF(128). Accept iff the three field/parity checks sum x_t=0, sum t x_t=0, sum t^3 x_t=0 hold (the first is a binary sum). This is a linear code of dimension at least 113. Computation using X^7+X+1 gives check rank exactly 15; the bound only needs the lower dimension. Its minimum distance is at least six: parity rules out odd weight, distinct coordinates prevent weight two by the second check, and four distinct elements a,b,c,d of zero sum have cubic sum (a+b)(a+c)(b+c) nonzero. Directly enumerating all binom(128,2)=8128 two-column sums found them distinct. Thus disjoint nonzero codewords use at least six coordinates each, giving nu≤21 and the stated 2^92 lower bound.

The circuit fits the actual NAND format. The all-input parity has weight 128 and the other 14 binary parity rows have weight 64 since t↦t^3 permutes GF(128). Binary XOR takes four NANDs. The 15 rows use 4(127+14*63)=4036 NANDs, with 15 inversions and 28 NANDs for the conjunction of the negated checks, totaling 4079. Repeat the last NAND with its same parents and then use eight pairs of inversions to obtain exactly 4096 gates. A registered-index replay on 84 input words compared the full gate list with the syndromes. This is an easily publicly recognized linear target, and Gaussian elimination can solve it directly. The lower bound concerns publishing or trying individual sound literal schedules as the sole way to cover a witness.

In a random menu of K sound schedules each draw covers at most K·2^21 codewords. Averaging a uniform codeword proves that some fixed witness has coverage at most E[K]/2^92. A method requiring coverage with probability at least 1−epsilon thus needs E[K]≥(1−epsilon)2^92. If it guesses the payload on uncovered inputs and is otherwise correct, the weaker necessary factor is 1−2epsilon. These are component/membership counts; a single implicit generator for many components, or public preprocessing that finds a satisfying assignment once, does not pay one explicit row per component. Nor is 2^92 by itself above the registered cost cap 2^100. Independently issuing 129-bit keys at that width would exceed 2^99 bits, apart from framing, preparation and evaluation. A bit count is not a tape-clock proof.

## Even changing the affine primary chart can have a cost

The linear-code example itself becomes simple in a suited parity basis. Here is a different bound that permits a separate invertible affine primary chart for every component. For arbitrary S containing no affine subspace of dimension greater than r, any such 2-CNF Q inside S has size at most sum_{j≤r} binom(d,j), where d is the affine-span dimension of S. To see this, work in the component's coordinate basis and translate one member to zero. The minimal-support blocks defined above are disjoint; the difference of the two members M_i and U is now a vector in the linear span of translated S, so there are at most d distinct blocks. Each A in F is uniquely the union of its maximal distinct supports M_i contained in A. If there are k maxima, any subset of their top blocks may be removed while staying in F: retain the strict sub-supports of each removed maximum and all kept maxima, then take their union under A. These 2^k choices constitute an affine k-flat with independent block directions. Thus k≤r. Recording the maximal blocks injects F into sets of size at most r out of at most d labels, proving the bound. Affine changes preserve numeric flat and span dimensions.

A concrete target uses five independent pairs (x_j,y_j) of 12-bit field words constrained by y_j=x_j^3 in GF(4096), with the other eight bits pinned to zero. The graph of a single cube has no affine plane: projecting a hypothetical plane to its x coordinate is injective, and distinct nonzero directions a,b have nonzero mixed difference a b(a+b). A flat of the product projects to a flat of dimension at most one per block, so its dimension is at most five. The target has 2^60 members and span dimension at most 120. Every independently affine-reparameterized 2-CNF therefore covers at most sum_{j≤5} binom(120,j)=199080695 members. Covering the whole relation needs at least 5791227043 components. A body with arbitrary nonlinear auxiliaries or a nonlinear coordinate change is outside the claim.

Here is a reproducible circuit-size calculation. In the degree-twelve all-ones cyclotomic field modulus, 2 has order twelve modulo 13, so the thirteenth roots give this irreducible factor; also X^13=1 in the field. Use coefficients 0..11 as the canonical field encoding. For x cubed, put each diagonal bit i into bucket 3i mod 13. Compute one NAND for each unordered pair i<j and put its output into both buckets i+2j and j+2i modulo 13, tracking the two known complement constants. XOR each bucket's terms. To reduce the degree-12 coefficient, XOR bucket 12 into each of buckets 0..11; check the twelve equalities against y using XOR, applying any known complement. Across five blocks, fix all eight excess input bits and conjoin the 68 single-bit checks. Counting this literal gate generator gives 3632 NANDs, extended by 232 double inversions to 4096. Eighty full-netlist evaluations checked randomly generated valid words and one-bit mutations against the field formula. Both explicit examples are structural tests of schedule compression. Neither is a computational hardness assumption.
