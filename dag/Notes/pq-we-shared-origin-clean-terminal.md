### Shorter root actions

When only the image of a designated point is tested, the root of the 16-ary width-20 construction needs each of its 16 children once. Start at marker 0. Child i targets (i i+1 h) if true, with h distinct from 0,...,16. If child i first fails, the marker stays at i and no later cycle moves it. Otherwise it reaches 16. The two internal levels still return clean 3-cycles. This gives 6·60²·16=345600 factors, 46656000 bits for two 61-bit rows and a 13-bit selector at each position, and 93312001 bits with one length frame. This root promises only the action on one point.

For a clean 3-cycle leaf target the six-factor NAND gadget is optimal in the two-row group instruction model. To see this, normalize zero rows to identity by conjugating each one-row difference by baseline prefixes and dropping the identity all-zero product. Each of x and y needs two reads: with the other bit zero its toggle has no effect whereas with the other bit one it changes the output; group cancellation forbids a single such read. At length five there is exactly one z read. Write the word at z=0 as CD=t^(xy) and at z=1 as CtD=t^(1−xy), with C,D depending only on x,y. On each singleton (10,01) both C,D commute with t. At least one side of z has at most two instructions. On (11) that side still commutes with t: with one variable it has its singleton value, and with two different variables it is the ordered product of their singleton values. Since CD=t, both sides then commute with t, forcing t²=1 at (11,1). The lower bound applies to all group targets of order above two, including 3- and 5-cycles. It concerns clean exact output on every row; an action with ancillary scratch initialized to a particular state is a different interface.

### A message-controlled clean product without lamps

For each m in (13,17,19) give an m-cycle t_m an orbit containing a common point O and m−1 private points. Different orbits intersect just at O. A reflection s_m reverses t_m, and [t_m^((m+1)/2),s_m]=t_m. On one row use LocalNandSix with z replaced by 1−z. The product is t_m exactly for an incorrect NAND gate, identity otherwise. Concatenate 4096 such words. For a consistent assignment with the final output pinned true this gives K_m=t_m^v where v counts wrong rows. Let K=K_19 K_17 K_13. The first nonzero residue moves O to a private point on its orbit, which neither later rotation moves. Therefore K(O)=O iff v=0. SharedOrigin formalizes the shared-point argument, and CrtCleanCost verifies the finite residue criterion for 0≤v≤4096.

Add four fresh points c,d,e,f. Write D=(O c d), A=(O e f) and define the complete word Wβ=[A^β,K D K⁻¹]. If K(O) differs from O, the two cycles have disjoint supports and Wβ is identity. If K(O)=O, Wβ equals [A,D]^β, a fixed nonidentity 3-cycle for β=1. This is equality of the entire group element. The width is 13+17+19−2+4=51; use S51, which includes all the reflections. The residue scans cost 3·4096·6 positions one-way. K and K⁻¹ each occur twice in the commutator. The total is 294916 positions, including the two D and two message factors. With two 220-bit S51 indices and a 13-bit control per instruction the one-payload frame costs 267193897 bits. Concrete permutation arithmetic checked all 24 one-row residue/input cases and both terminal outputs for every one of the 4097 possible violation counts (8194 full permutation comparisons). These sizes price the indicated encoding of the clear or independently framed group instructions only. They omit machine operations and descriptions. In particular direct publication of the clean word reveals the encoded message, and independent row masks admit mixed choices as in the companion note.

The earlier trace-lamp construction used three residues with a separate lamp commutator for each. Here the shared-origin sequential action implements the conjunction of the residue tests, leaving one outer commutator and no lamp group.

### Public release on a materialized consistency graph

Use the prior canonical-suffix gauge on the graph whose vertices store the values of all variables live across the cut. A forced access uses its stored value. For each first access choose 0 for the canonical continuation, and follow stored choices thereafter. For each vertex u let a_u,b_u be the two canonical suffix products for the zero and one words. Write h_u=b_u a_u⁻¹, so the terminal potential is identity. Publish h_source and the relative corrections
g₀(e)⁻¹ h_u⁻¹ g₁(e) h_v
for message one. For message zero send identity throughout, with identical public indexing. During decryption use g₀(e) times the correction; the header and path product telescope to the corresponding Wβ. On NO all complete consistent paths have equal products. For any prefix to u, complete it by the canonical suffix at u or across e by the canonical suffix at v. Equating the two worlds along each path proves the correction and header both identity. This is exact equality of the complete encoded arrays, including malformed-read-independent, off-path rows. No cryptographic assumption is used in this explicit construction.

Two economies depend on the actual encoding. First, Wβ and W₀ differ in just two occurrences, each a 3-cycle or inverse. The suffix ratio is a product of at most the number of such occurrences in that suffix, each conjugated and hence still a 3-cycle. The relative edge correction is a product of at most four 3-cycles: when the suffix after e contains j message occurrences and e contains δ, its source suffix contains j+δ, giving 2(j+δ)≤4 factors. An identity or one 3-cycle on 51 points has 41651 choices, hence fits 16 bits. Use four slots, padding with the identity; if their product is identity write four zero slots regardless of how the expression was derived. This ensures identity has the same encoding in both worlds. The header fits the same format.

Second, forced edges and the 0 edge of each first read have suffix(u)=e·suffix(v), so their normalized correction is identically one on both true and false circuits. Only the 1 edge at a first read needs a slot. For p_i previously live bits there are at most 2^p_i such slots at that layer. Thus the dense correction table costs at most 64·(1+Σ_first 2^p_i) bits plus its framing, where there are at most 128+4095 free first-read choices. The verifier's final output is pinned and forward references use the constant true.

A sparse sequential format can instead list the nonidentity records with layer and live assignment addresses; include a canonical empty array and the header. At a given first-read layer there are at most as many nonidentity records as accepting witnesses. For a nonidentity correction at u choose any consistent prefix reaching u. Complete it once along the canonical edge and once along the 1 edge followed by its canonical suffix. If both reject, their two world products agree along this prefix, and the two equalities would imply an identity correction. Therefore one completion is an accepting assignment. Different vertices at the same layer cannot receive the same assignment. Every accepting assignment to the read variables is determined by a satisfying witness, since every gate output is tested and determined by its inputs; missing unused witness coordinates can be filled canonically. This proves the bound. Computing this sparse support within budget is open: enumerating 2^128 witnesses or 2^p_i cut assignments does not supply an adequate uniform issuer. Computing both possible deterministic ciphertexts and comparing their bytes also decides SAT, by exact NO equality and YES correctness. The constructive release is fully specified for enumerable frontiers; no general 2^32 clock, compact general WE or computational hardness improvement follows.

An independent replay built the full framed correction algebra on two small circuits. For two double-negation gates it checked all four consistent assignments to the witness and internal trace, with 148 positions, three first-read-1 slots and one nonidentity slot. For NOT(x), NAND(x,NOT(x)), and the final negation it checked all eight assignments, with 220 positions, seven candidate slots, identity header and no nonidentity slot. Both decrypted path products matched their original complete permutations at every assignment.
