The explicit guarded-frontier graph in the companion note can be large even for an efficiently checkable core. Here is a conditional compiler that genuinely shares rows across those states, with an exact small-secret rather than a modulus chain. At layer i a known integer vector h represents the state. Each read-once core bit b chooses a public integer matrix M_(i,b), giving h'=M_(i,b)h; constant coordinates handle affine updates. On every actual path bound ||h||_1 by H. For each NO, Lh=0 at the terminal for every path, and on valid witness paths Lh=1. More generally exact recognition uses the two values zero and one on all paths. These are representation hypotheses to prove for the source verifier. A closed coefficient graph of a polynomial expression need not obey these hypotheses.

For a direct reduction assume the issuer uses layer coordinates in which all reachable features lie in a coordinate space E_i, each M_(i,b) carries E_i into E_(i+1), and L vanishes on E_last on NO. It suffices that E_i is the rational span of actually reachable vectors expressed as a coordinate space, including when non-mentioned features are retained. One uniform choice computes those rational spans by forward matrix products and linear algebra. The saturation of the reachable integer lattice is primitive and extends to a unimodular basis of the integer module (Smith normal form). Use such adapted coordinates, recomputing the bound H *after* the change and charging the basis construction and the transformed integer coefficients. If some columns vanish in every future calculation they may be removed. A smaller proved bound may hold for a known compatible feature basis. Small independent secrets are sampled in the actual issuance basis: arbitrary changes of basis in a security proof would change the small-secret/error law and are not permitted.

Choose a fixed rank-one Module-LWE game at power-of-two ring degree N, q a power of two, two ring rows, all secret and error coefficients independently CBD(1). Use the same pair of uniform ring elements A_0,A_1 at all layers. For every basis vector j draw its own short secret polynomial s_ij. Define a formal integer token k_i(h)=sum_j h_j s_ij coefficientwise, distinct from the ciphertext ring reduction. For each basis j and branch b issue the *one* ring element

    c_ijb = A_b s_ij + e_ijb + Delta k_(i+1)(M_(i,b)e_j)   mod q.

For the terminal use one row c_j=A_0 s_last,j+e_j+Delta*(message)*L(e_j) in coefficient zero (the first two terms are ordinary ring elements). Publish the initial token, not all keys. Its value depends only on keys in E_initial. Reading a word, combine the issued rows with the actual signed integer h_j, then subtract A_b times the current token. All child terms combine to Delta k_(i+1)(h'); coefficient error is at most H. Every coefficient of the next token is at most H in absolute value because the secrets are short. If Delta>2H and q>2(Delta+1)H, the centered lift and nearest-integer division by Delta recover that token exactly. At the terminal only output recovery on valid paths with Lh=1 is required. Use H≥1; the same bound then covers the terminal shift. New errors are local and do not accumulate. Very large matrix entries in individual columns may be reduced modulo q before transmission; their generation, state updates and their bit lengths are still charged.

On NO replace both output rows for each basis coordinate outside E_i by independent uniform responses, layer by layer. No untouched response at an earlier basis coordinate in E can contain that secret: the transition maps the entire coordinate span into the next E. Every earlier outside row has been erased. Future keys are sampled independently, so the public target row is exactly the two-row registered challenge plus known child offsets, with the challenge's header A common to all other honestly generated rows. The initial token is simulable. Once all outside rows are uniform, terminal rows in E have zero message shift and outside terminal rows are also independent of the bit. This accounts for the full classical packet, including unrestricted public linear recombination. Union bounds on distinguishing gaps, simulator/attacker time overhead, and the distinction between the classical registry and the analogous quantum assumption are as in the companion note. If only a basis of *reachable* spans is emitted, every NO terminal shift vanishes outright: the entire distribution is identical on both bits. The ability to compute such spans also gives a clear decision algorithm. The encrypted presentation does not turn efficient public linear recognition into a harder decision task.

For an explicit state-count separation let a core give 64 data bits followed by six address bits selecting x_j. Any deterministic recognizer in this order has at least 2^64 states at the middle: differing words are distinguished by one of the future addresses. A NAND multiplexer tree for this predicate uses 63 three-NAND muxes plus six shared complemented address wires. Integer features (1,x_1,...,x_t) implement each prefix read; on the last data read discard the constant. Each address read selects one half of the remaining feature vector by a projection. Across the 64 data layers there are 1+...+64=2080 parent basis rows, with 64+32+16+8+4+2=126 more for the address. Every state has l1 norm at most 65. This separation is about realizing the exact supplied-core predicate; the existence of some accepting word for this example is easy.

Choose Delta=256 and q=65536. For N=1024, 2206 parent basis rows require 72,286,208 ciphertext bits (two 16-bit coefficient ring words per basis coordinate) plus the common pair, the initial token, terminal response and descriptions. There are 4412 issuer ring products before the terminal, along with matrix-weighted secret combinations and rounding. For the known sparse index matrices those combinations cost at most one addition per child-key column; a generic dense M has a different cost. The actual registry evaluator returned H=184 at this N,q, 94 for N=512 at the same q, and 162 at N=512,q=256, all for two rows with CBD(1) secrets and errors. The q=256 choice does not meet this H=65 feature-norm bound (feature norm and registry strength are distinct). No measured Machine clock or all-circuit feature bound follows. An exact integer negacyclic computation at N=1024 generated all 4412 ciphertext bodies, followed one 70-bit index path, verified each reconstructed token against the integer definition, and recovered the expected endpoint for both payloads.

For comparison, the companion explicit-state uniform-secret parameters N=512,q=128,m=14 had registry strength 177. Its 20540-parent upper bound costs 1,030,672,384 raw bits plus topology; for U≤20541 the 2U hybrid factor leaves at least 146.34 bits before charging the simulator/attacker cost ratio. An exact test built the guarded residual for a two-NAND satisfiable instance requiring both input bits true and a two-NAND always-false variant with a constant final gate. It recovered all 64 routed child keys with fresh CBD(1) errors, identified 48 rejecting paths, and recovered the correct payload on all 16 accepting paths. These small-instance and finite parameter checks do not certify a complete registered submission.

A final linear readout entails a rank restriction: at any input-prefix cut the matrix of terminal values on all prefixes and suffixes factors through the current feature dimension over the rationals. For instance the equality predicate on two k-bit strings in grouped order has the identity truth matrix, so exact output needs at least 2^k features there, despite admitting a width-two comparator when the order interleaves the two strings. When correctness on a YES statement imposes no output on invalid witnesses, an exact-recognizer lower bound does not directly lower-bound witness encryption. General guarded completion still needs a compact small-norm feature representation, or a different way to issue non-linear transition states under the registered assumptions.

### Consistent rereads on aligned passes

The construction has an exact positive case for repeated guard bits. Suppose a deterministic width-d program reads the same ordered list of n bits in p successive passes, possibly using different transitions at each place. In parallel consider every tuple gamma of p-1 guessed seam states. A gamma-block stores a one-hot tuple of p current states initially (initial,gamma_1,...,gamma_(p-1)). On the *single* read of bit i update all p components by the transition at place i of their respective pass. This is a nonnegative integer linear map on tuple indicators. At the terminal sum coordinates whose first p-1 components equal gamma and whose last state accepts. For any input exactly one seam tuple matches all boundaries, by deterministic execution from the initial state. The summed readout is exactly the original zero/one answer. The feature dimension is at most d^(2p-1) with l1 norm H=d^(p-1), one active coordinate per seam guess. Thus the two-row compiler enforces equality of all p readings by construction, with no per-input enumeration.

In this nonnegative basis, use as E_i the coordinate span of the union of supports of reachable features. Each letter maps this coordinate span into the next: a positive contribution from a reachable coordinate cannot cancel in its originating path. On NO every reachable support coordinate has zero terminal weight since the weights are nonnegative. The fixed standard issuance basis therefore suffices without invoking a change of the small-secret law, even though the span of reachable *vectors* may be smaller. A clear support-reachability algorithm is again available. For passes in the same or reverse order, a more conservative construction maintains the full cumulative d-by-d transition matrix of each pass, multiplying on the left or right as its ordering requires. The tensor of their vectorizations has dimension d^(2p) and l1 norm d^p because each deterministic transition function has d ones. The final contraction composes the matrices in pass order, giving the same nonnegative support property. Neither construction handles arbitrary interleavings at uniformly bounded width. An exact test compared the seam construction against direct sequential evaluation on 3072 words of random two- and three-pass width-three programs.
