### Issuer, homogeneous test, and exact privacy

Take a nonzero binary secret functional pi on a linear code E in F2^n. Assume a binary linear L on F2^n satisfies L(uv)=pi(u)pi(v) for coordinatewise multiplication of every two codewords. Use independent representatives in E for the four ports A,X,Y,Z of each raw NAND gate, gluing only pi values at wire uses, common anchors, constant one, and final output (both equal to the common anchor). Call the resulting binary address subspace W. Encrypt b by adding three binary vectors of length 4nG: independent slot masks, a uniform member of W-perp, and b times an ambient representative of the common anchor functional. If a Walsh coefficient on an address is nonzero, the address belongs to W and every local quadruple satisfies XY=A(A+Z), provided the local mask transform vanishes at every violation. Applying L gives the identical relation on pi values (projectGate). Thus an odd common anchor implies an actual accepting circuit trace. On a false statement Fourier inversion gives equality of both entire ciphertext laws, including any independent public representation of the code or wiring. No hardness premise is needed. This projection allows different codeword representatives at occurrences of the same wire.

### All local maximizers

Order the four legal odd row evaluations into complementary pairs (A,B)=(00,11) and (C,D)=(01,10); these letters in this section denote signs, not gate ports. The four rows are a basis. The six forbidden characters are AB, CD, ABC, ABD, ACD, BCD, by listing all sixteen addresses. Correct each marginal by a fixed sign so its expectation is nonnegative; this is a fixed translation, preserving zeros. For each pair, the sum of its biases is at most 1, with equality iff the double-negative event has zero mass (pairBias). Hence a law maximizing the minimum absolute odd-row bias at 1/2 has all four negative marginals 1/4 and cannot place negatives twice in one pair.

Let i in the first pair and j in the second. From E[CD]=E[iCD]=0, occurrence of a negative at i is independent of a negative pair product CD; the converse holds with pairs exchanged. A pair product is negative exactly when it has one negative. The four probabilities q_ij of exactly those two negatives therefore have row and column sums 1/8. The matrix is [[theta,1/8-theta],[1/8-theta,theta]] for 0 ≤ theta ≤ 1/8 (symmetricFlags). Each exact singleton has mass 1/8; the all-positive atom has mass 1/4. Conversely sample one erased index per pair with joint law 4q, then fill each erased coordinate with an independent fair bit and retain the other two at zero. This gives precisely those nine masses and all six required zeros. Each such law, including zero entries, is invariant at the flag level under the simultaneous exchange of both pair members. Independent erased indices within a slot are unnecessary; known sign translations can always be undone.

### Complete ciphertext bound for arbitrary local maximizers

Reveal both selected erasure flags for every slot. Conditional noise is uniform over an affine subspace. Its perpendicular restricts each local address to the span of the two retained odd rows, and projection through W-perp also requires membership in W. All retaining planes consist of homogeneous legal vectors: the two rows have a shared input bit and their sum is legal. The conditional payload laws are equal cosets or disjoint cosets, with separation exactly when some admissible address has odd anchor. The projected space of four gate secrets is a linear space contained in the legal quadric by projectGate; it cannot contain two members of one complementary odd pair, since their difference is forbidden. The simultaneous swap of the two inputs' *values* at odd anchor is the linear substitution (a,x,y,z) ↦ (a,x+a,y+a,z+x+y+a). It preserves legality (swapLegal), E membership, and secret projection. Its induced swap on the flags preserves the law of every maximizing slot, even when distributions differ by position. Independent slots within a gate, or more generally any joint flag law invariant under this substitution for the entire gate, therefore assign any specified legal odd row probability at most 1/2 of belonging to the projected space.

Taking independent gate blocks, an odd global address requires every row of one actual accepting trace to be available, so with a union bound the TV distance including all flags is at most min(1,#SAT times 2^(-G)). Marginalization and arbitrary witness-dependent postprocessing cannot improve it. At 4096 unsimplified gates and 128 inputs this is at most 2^-3968. Multiple independent packets add at most their summed TV. The explicit n=7 code takes punctured evaluations i↦s+a·i on the seven nonzero three-bit points, secret s, and L=parity. All 256 pairs meet the product condition; the 128 subsets give 64 recoverable sets. With the independent uniform pair flags, eliminating all twelve slopes for each of 4^7 plane patterns yielded exactly 8192 containing each specific odd secret row. These exact counts refer to that flag distribution.

For arbitrary position-dependent admissible *nonoptimal* masks the projected channel for row r is a BSC dominated by erasure retention |E r|. Complementary projected rows have retention probabilities summing to at most one coordinatewise. Couple hypothetical retained subsets disjointly by using opposite ends of a common uniform interval. Share recovery requires support of some e in E with pi(e)=1 inside that subset. Such supports intersect pairwise by multiplication reconstruction. Thus one of the two rows has erasure recovery at most 1/2. Take 64 disjoint primary pair NANDs on all 128 inputs, make each output feed a NAND with derived zero, conjoin the resulting ones, and pad with pairs of inversions or constant-preserving gates to exactly 4096. This tautology has witnesses choosing the weaker rows at all 64 primary gates. For that witness the projected channel for the XOR share has TV at most 2^-64. The bound here uses witness projections. An arbitrary full-view statement for nonoptimal masks requires additional arguments.
