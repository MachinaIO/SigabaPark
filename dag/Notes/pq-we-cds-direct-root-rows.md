## A noninteractive compiler

Use the full version of Döttling, Garg, Goyal and Malavolta, [Laconic Conditional Disclosure of Secrets and Applications](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1353), Definitions 32–35, Figures 6, 7, 10–13 and Section 7.4. An honest Figure 13 receiver commits its PCP database to a LOT root d, then sends one ordinary first-round string-OT message per bit of d. Its private state includes both the OT states and the processed PCP database. Definition 35 simulates the public view. It supplies no algorithm for generating either private state for a chosen witness. The construction below dispenses with the outer OT layer entirely.

Given an honest Figure 13 CRS, a public classical algorithm chooses auxiliary string z and an ordered list of K candidate LOT roots using the circuit C and fresh coins independent of the payload. The coins needed for deterministic witness processing are included in z. For each root d, independently sample the PCP verifier tapes, derive their query locations, and garble the amplified PCP circuit with the message bit hardwired. For each queried location L, run the *ordinary string-LOT sender* at d and L on the pair of garbled input labels for that occurrence. Serialize the garbled PCP circuit, all LOT ciphertexts and locations. The ciphertext consists of the CRS, public data and these framed rows. Independent row randomness includes fresh garblings. No garbling of the LOT sender circuit or outer OT messages appear in this compiler.

On a true circuit, require a deterministic Open(C,w,CRS,z,list) returning a row and processed proof. It must give the LOT receiver the honest answers of a single accepting PCP database for this witness at the chosen root. Decode all labels by LOT.Receive and evaluate that row's garbled PCP. Account separately for failure of Open, verifier completeness, LOT decoding at all queried positions, and garbled evaluation. With failure bounds under this *selected* joint law, their sum bounds per-witness error. If Open computes PCPProve and Hash using independent honest coins published in z, and the list covers every possible result under those coins, the selected row's fresh sender tapes retain the honest law; worst-case correctness does not require all K rows to succeed. A guess-and-condition opener requires a new law comparison. The registered tolerance is 2^-128 for each true witness and each payload. Theorem 4 for the base LWE LOT provides weak correctness; the final quantitative error is a separate obligation.

**Single-row false-statement reduction.** Suppose z and the roots are sampleable from the honest challenge CRS without hidden setup information. In Definition 34 for false C, the first-stage adversary chooses the challenged digest d and runs honest ordinary OTReceive for its bits, retaining all the OT states. Any bit string can be used this way; the malicious receiver in Definition 34 need not run PCPProve. The second stage decodes the chosen labels from the challenge and evaluates its garbled LOT circuit. On successful outer decoding and garbled evaluation it obtains exactly the inner LOT ciphertexts the challenge sender generated for d, with its own query indices and garbled PCP. Discard the outer garbled circuit, keys, randomness and OT messages. The law of the retained row is the direct compiler's row when the coins used in Figure 12 have the direct sender law. This gives a coupling: for challenge bit b the distributional error is at most the failure probability eta_b for this wrapper. When these operations are perfect the transformation is exact. No PCP database is needed to simulate a false row. For an adaptive d, wrapper correctness must hold in the induced CRS-dependent law, or be charged there.

For fixed list size K, choose a random index independently of the list, generate earlier direct rows on bit 1 and later direct rows on bit 0, then supply the entire CRS, z, list and all framed rows to the WE adversary. Other rows share the actual CRS and use independent coins. The signed adjacent differences telescope. With one-row CDS advantage at the reduction's resources at most epsilon and wrapper errors bounded by eta_0,eta_1, the full gap is at most K(epsilon+eta_0+eta_1); identical public padding handles variable lengths bounded by K. Generating and presenting other rows costs at most K-1 direct sends plus framing, one set of digest-bit OT requests and decodes, one LOT-circuit evaluation, construction of the public header and one call to the distinguisher. If root generation needs a trapdoor, this argument applies only after a separately quantified switch of the *whole* public header law under which the simulator can generate the rest. Include the switch errors for both messages. Publicly simulatable marginals do not suffice. The classical preprocessing and postprocessing also give a straight-line reduction *if* the single-challenge CDS primitive already supplies that quantum-adversary guarantee, including its state and time. Definition 34 and the paper's classical hybrid/context-extraction analysis do not establish that premise for the registered fixed machines. The WE metric uses squared advantage; losses must be carried into both concrete time and strength.

**Two enumeration controls.** For arbitrary CDS, generate one message-independent public honest receiver tape independently of the CRS, enumerate an efficiently indexed support containing all first messages obtainable from true witnesses and that tape, and send fresh CDS replies per row. Dec recomputes CDSReceive on its witness and the published tape, selects the corresponding response, and runs CDSDecode. Correctness has the ordinary receiver/sender law. A single Definition 34 challenge embedded at the random index gives a K-fold full-view bound with no LOT wrapper. Receiver privacy is unused. Figure 13 permits replacing the full first-message support by the LOT-root support in the direct compiler. For either method, maximal support size is different from the number of encoding bits. If K independent samples and P independent probes are each uniform on a domain of size U and the two collections are independent, the chance of any match is at most KP/U by the union bound. Conditional on a sampled list with k distinct entries it equals 1-(1-k/U)^P. This is a fixed-witness, independent-uniform calculation only. A correlated opener, biased root, or efficiently known canonical proof is outside the calculation. The tests below address correlated openings through usable states.

## Testing one root and one outer header

In Figure 6 stack the lambda independent uniform n-by-m matrices to obtain A; its exact base digest is AD modulo q for binary D, with processed state (D,d). For fixed distinct databases D,D' independent of A the digest collision probability is q^(-n lambda): one coefficient of D-D' is +1 or -1, a unit even for composite q, so condition on every other column and count exactly one admissible column vector. There is no receiver coin in this hash. Figure 7 retains it. Figure 10 recursively applies the base hash to encoded children. Reused setup across levels precludes blindly multiplying independent collision probabilities.

A concrete padding experiment replaces the base matrix by [Adata | G], where G has binary-power gadget columns for each output coordinate. Fix any root d. Given data P, compute binary pad digits for d-Adata P modulo q. Then [P;pad] is a binary database with exactly the chosen digest. Let the PCP ignore the appended pad. The construction really gives the requested algebraic opening at a single hash node. An explicit gadget block fails the honest-uniform matrix law; a permuted or hidden trapdoor would need its own setup law. More fundamentally, this public opener works for arbitrary P. Two different P with usable receiver states at the same digest give opposite choices at a differing data position in the *same* fresh LOT transfer. Any assertion that this retains ordinary LOT sender security on that setup must account for those states. The algebraic identity neither extends correctness through the recursive tree by itself nor implies security of the modified CDS on false instances.

The same test applies to a hypothetical opener for the literal Figure 13. Fix one setup and public data, and a family of successful openings with distinct processed proof databases. If there are fewer headers than databases, two share a row. Different roots for that row require opposite ordinary OT choices at one digest bit. Identical roots with different databases require opposite LOT choices at a database position for that root. The finite choice dichotomy is the checked theorem two_layer_choice_separation. It needs distinct *databases*, not merely different satisfying witnesses. A true fixture admitting all inputs can enforce distinct databases for a chosen PCP variant by appending the witness as ignored data to the prover output. Perfect completeness and original soundness are preserved by ignoring this trailer, as is any extractor's original restricted-oracle access. The test then applies to genuine openings of this variant's entire database. A different encoding can canonicalize irrelevant data; authentic full states are a stronger requirement than correctness solely on the queried positions of a particular CDS execution.

For the cryptographic reading, require a sampler of the joint setup, headers and opening auxiliaries from the corresponding honest challenge CRS, and efficient generation of tested witnesses and usable states. Choose the shared row and the first differing index **before** receiving any fresh challenge transfer. Compute them by sorting K+1 marked records by their selected row and comparing the colliding roots/databases. Thus the selected coordinate need not be guessed in a single transfer game that permits its adaptive choice before Send; a challenger fixing the coordinate in advance requires an index loss or another argument. On any selected instance where both opposite openings decode fresh independent uniform ell-bit messages jointly with probability p, the pair-recovery success is p. An ideal single-choice receiver has pair-recovery success at most 2^-ell. For LOT's Definition 27 an equivalent direct test on a reliable opening pair compares both decoded bits with the prescribed pair: replacing either one by a fair bit changes acceptance by at least 1/2 minus the sum of the real and replaced decoding failure probabilities. Setups or openings with no such reliability carry no such conclusion. A claim of very high per-witness *ordinary full-state* success for fixed K+1 witnesses gives joint success at least 1-(K+1)delta by union bound; the probability mass of that event splits between the two cases. Final WE correctness alone gives no ordinary inner/outer transfer guarantee. Distinguish true-circuit functionality from the false-circuit CDS security reduction above.

## Concrete output filter and unresolved costs

For the literal LWE bit sender in Figures 6 and 7, write b=ceil(log2 q). Count only the lambda^2 vectors of m-1 residues in the fixed-width transmitted b-vectors of Figure 7. Even a single bit transfer uses at least lambda^2(m-1)b bits in this serialization. Section 6.2.4 imposes m >= 2 b n lambda, c=4 and q >= lambda^3(2mBc+c). At lambda=128, n>=1, B>=1 these imply 256b<=m and 128^3(8m+4)<=2^b. Then b>=33, m>=8448, and the counted fields alone need at least 4,567,056,384 bits, above 4,294,967,296. The checked literal_response_floor proves the strict comparison from the two arithmetic inequalities. The direct-root row at this base-transfer interface therefore misses the 2^32 aggregate target even before string conversion, PCP repetitions, garbling, parsing, auxiliary fields or setup. In the recursive LOT the first base transfer appears in the root sender. The bound pertains to the explicit serializer and that base-transfer implementation; it does not lower-bound every garbled implementation, alternative packing, or the formal 2^100 cap.

More generally, if an implementation serializes K direct rows of lengths L_j after a prefix of length F (including CRS, z, roots, lengths and framing), Enc's worst-case clock must pay at least F+sum L_j on that output. A serial implementation must also charge setup, root generation and tape copying, each row's verifier/garbling/LOT work plus writes, and Dec's deterministic Open, read/seek, LOT evaluation and garbled PCP evaluation. Add both machine description lengths. For equal row length L and a nonempty prefix the 2^32 filter requires K L < 2^32 even allowing zero other costs. Neither a fixed-strength parameter selection for the outer building blocks nor a complete tape-machine upper bound is presently supplied. PR 83 enumerates partial-witness policy keys and complementary encryptions; PRs 129 and 130 replace/price policy-key issuance through a locked program. The table here issues independent conditional-disclosure responses at *roots*, with a different single-row reduction and a different opening obligation.

The registered registry contains decision LWE, Ring-LWE, Module-LWE and circular Ring-LWE. No reduction here instantiates the paper's complete CDS stack at its concrete strength against quantum adversaries under that registry. A potentially viable successor must supply a root/processed-state mechanism with an honestly simulatable public header and a substantially smaller sender representation; security of its false-case view can then use the direct-row reduction. A setup mode that intentionally gives up ordinary LOT security needs a new CDS proof in that mode.

## A two-round CDS without the LOT size floor

At this instance size the witness itself is 128 bits. Forgo laconicity and use 128 ordinary receiver-first string OTs and one garbling. For each bit w_i the honest receiver sends OT.Receive(crs,w_i) and retains its private state. For bit b the sender garbles the fixed topology for F(C,b)(v), which outputs a fixed failure symbol if C(v)=0 and the encoded payload on acceptance. Use an encoding with a separate validity bit and payload masked by the circuit so that the failure output is identical for both payloads. Each OT sender transfers one pair of input labels. Its response, the garbled circuit and ordinary public output decoding data form the second message. Deterministic Dec recovers the 128 selected labels and evaluates. Perfect or quantified OT and garbling correctness on the actual joint law suffice for honest accepting inputs.

A full-view false-row security reduction applies under malicious-receiver UC security of the 128 classical-message OTs with joint honest CRS and auxiliary state, and simulation privacy of the garbler for a single chosen encoded input. In the ideal OT hybrid a corrupt receiver selects at most one label per wire before seeing the sender payload; pad missing choices and discard unwanted labels. Conditional on this vector the output is the same failure value on *every* false C. Keep a common public topology, both programs' size and any leakage identical. A garbling simulator on that topology, vector and failure value can then replace the garbling plus its encoded input (including any public output decoder) in either payload case. The garbling must provide this privacy for the two hardwired functions, or use the same universal circuit with private sender inputs for b; revealing hardwired b as public circuit metadata would invalidate the step. The intermediate simulation is for one input under one set of labels. The full common-CRS reduction must also simulate any OT-programming auxiliary data disclosed to the receiver. With quantum lifting supplied separately and each payload's OT and garbling replacement errors bounded by epsilon_OT and epsilon_G, the direct single-row distinguishing gap is at most 2(epsilon_OT+epsilon_G); K rows cost K single-row hybrids. The factor 128 is already in the multi-session OT bound, or must be paid when composing single-instance bounds. A single garbling's input labels must never be publicly issued for both choices on any wire by this argument.

To form a noninteractive candidate, choose K receiver-header vectors before knowing w, publish independent copies of these sender responses and use Open to recover all 128 OT states for the selected row. This avoids the entire Figure 7 output floor. With header length H, 128 sender responses of at most S bits, garbling length G including output metadata, row framing J and prefix F, one literal K-row upper serialization screen is F+K(H+128 S+G+J) for uniform allocations; actual fields give exact lengths. Charge times for setup, header construction, K garblings, 128K OT sends, concatenation, Dec's selected-row access, 128 decodes, evaluation, and both machine descriptions. For orientation only, a hypothetical two-lambda-bit-per-NAND garbling at lambda=128 would use 1,048,576 gate-table bits for the original 4096 NANDs, before payload logic, OTs, metadata, framing and sequential-tape work. This byte-count model has no asserted post-quantum or fixed-registry garbling theorem. No realized OT sizes or aggregate upper clock follow from it.

On a circuit accepting all 2^128 witnesses (the final gate can read a known false wire twice), suppose each published row has a unique 128-bit choice vector to which honest usable states can be produced, and the decoder must open exactly the raw witness. Pointwise each row covers at most one raw witness, so K rows cover at most K. Averaging over uniformly chosen w and setup gives success probability at most K/2^128; in particular some fixed witness has success at most this. If per-witness opening succeeds with probability at least 1-delta, K >= (1-delta)2^128. A setup-exception probability gamma adds at most gamma to the average bound. This counts witness coverage of coordinate-wise *authentic receiver states under binding rows*, not WE decryption events. Ordinary computational OT security alone is not a pointwise information-theoretic uniqueness claim. If the opener canonicalizes witnesses, or only produces a successful nonlocal decoding result, the counting hypothesis requires a new argument. A modified setup with intentional dual openings must separately prove false-case secrecy on its joint public view. The construction removes the previously priced sender bottleneck but leaves an explicit authentic-opening requirement.

For comparison an unconditional authentic-root count is possible for a concrete flat specialization. In the full-verifier variant use the honest Figure 6 root for each raw 128-bit witness padded with zeros to m, then use one LOT per input and a single garbled verifier. Take the circuit accepting all witnesses. For any fixed pair of padded distinct words the earlier exact collision count applies. By a union bound over all pairs, with probability at least 1 - choose(2^128,2) q^(-128 n) *all* raw-witness roots are distinct simultaneously. Even an adaptively chosen K-root list covers at most K raw witnesses on this event. Hence its average probability of covering a uniform witness is at most K/2^128 + choose(2^128,2) q^(-128 n), without requiring independent probes, receiver binding or computational assumptions. This continues to hold for any fixed injective family of single-node binary databases chosen independently of the setup. It does not apply through recursive reused matrices or to CRS-dependent padding. The latter is precisely what the gadget-padding experiment changes.
