Fix a public statement. Suppose its ciphertext exposes the measured vectors A_g,l, B_g,l and final C_g of each bilinear group in a consistent coordinate order, over the binary field. The bit shares b_g XOR to the encrypted bit. If the row space of group g contains a unit with coefficient vector u_g fixed for this statement, then on every correctly issued ciphertext

    b_g = u_g · C_g + sum_l (u_g · A_g,l)(u_g · B_g,l).

Take the public features of each group to be its r coordinates of C, and for every p<=q a quadratic feature summed over all lanes. For p=q use A_p B_p, and for p<q use A_p B_q + A_q B_p. The coefficient for the latter features is u_p u_q including the diagonal case. The total dimension D is the sum over groups of r+r(r+1)/2. Concatenate the coefficients from any fixed genuine accepting witness. The full plaintext equals one fixed binary linear functional of the features on every encryption in the YES world. The distributive identity is Lean-verified. This derivation allows arbitrarily correlated masks within and between groups, share splitting, and nonuniform ciphertexts of variable content provided the public program can parse and align the features.

Generate m independent encryptions of independently uniform bits. Compute their m-by-D feature matrix F and test whether F theta=b is consistent. The decision test needs neither the private coefficients nor the witness, hidden columns, polynomial oracle, or controllable private randomness. Gaussian elimination suffices. A fixed predictor correct with error eta on each fresh YES encryption yields YES acceptance at least 1-m eta by the union bound. In the ideal experiment replacing labels by independent fair bits while preserving the ciphertext marginal, conditioning on F gives probability exactly 2^(rank(F)-m). In particular m>=D+kappa gives at most 2^(-kappa). The counting upper bound for general D-bit-indexed predictors is Lean-verified.

There are two distinct ways of transferring the NO bound.

* If one-challenge distinguishing gaps of the hybrid testers are at most epsilon, each replacement of a joint bit/ciphertext pair by independent marginals costs at most epsilon/2. Indeed the signed change is one quarter of the difference of two signed single-challenge distinguishing gaps, according to the supplied bit. Simulate all other pairs with the public encryptor. Thus NO acceptance is at most 2^(D-m)+m epsilon/2. These hybrid testers pay for up to m-1 extra public encryptions plus the feature computations and elimination.

* The registry's pointwise false-statement security also permits one fixed predictor per theta. Let C_pred bound the full registered Machine cost of any one-ciphertext predictor, including its theta description, parsing and evaluation. The squared gain definition implies absolute gap at most epsilon_pred=min(1,sqrt(C_pred/2^128)). Thus success of a particular predictor on a random bit is at most (1+epsilon_pred)/2. Independence across experiments and a union bound give NO acceptance at most min(1,2^D*((1+epsilon_pred)/2)^m). No enumeration occurs in the decision algorithm, and no training encryptions enter C_pred. This argument relies on the universal fixed-statement security quantification. Its numerical use depends on a concrete Machine implementation and cost bound.

For r=129 in one group, D=8514 and D+128=8642. Honest sampling for the decision test costs m public encryptions. Per encryption, constructing dense features costs O(k*r^2) binary operations, and straightforward streamed elimination uses O(m*D^2) bit operations and O(D^2) bits of state across the test. Serialization, machine descriptions, randomness and tape access require additional charges. The cap 2^100 in the registered task concerns the combined Enc/Dec cost. It does not certify m issuances plus linear algebra or supply a numerical predictor-machine cost.

The condition is the existence of one fixed linear law on all YES ciphertext features. The public row matrices themselves may remain hidden. Publicly invertible changes of basis can be undone before extraction. Fresh coordinate changes unknown to the tester can make the required witness coefficients vary from sample to sample. A decoder requiring a presented witness to compute genuinely varying coefficients is a possible weaker interface; neither its construction nor its security for general NAND statements has been established. Giving the issuer only local column evaluation (at a supplied witness) also avoids a polynomial-evaluation oracle, but no general short way to generate the desired projected moments is known. This test gives a bounded public SAT decision consequence for the specified interface and budgets, not a complexity separation or a general impossibility theorem for WE.
