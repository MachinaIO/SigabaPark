### What the fixed lattice provides

Feige and Micciancio, [*The inapproximability of lattice and coding problems with preprocessing*](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~daniele/papers/GapCVPP.pdf), JCSS 2004, equations (6)–(13), Lemmas 1–3 and Theorems 4–7, have a code determined by the size parameter. Only the target encodes the individual equation system. For a system of n equations, field size Q, universe M and variable universe X, their intermediate code has dimension 3Q|M| and length 3Q|M|+2|M|+B(3|M|+Q|X|), B=5n. Equation (13) constructs a codeword from **any** consistent variable assignment. Its distance is (3+2v)n when v is the violated fraction, and every codeword has distance at least 3n. In particular a public all-zero assignment always gives distance at most 5n. Theorem 4's inner Hadamard code over GF(2) multiplies every nonzero outer-symbol distance by the same factor H, e.g. H=2^(d-1) for GF(2^d). Theorem 6's binary lattice is the integer lift of the code together with 2Z^N. Binary target and codeword lifts attain distance squared equal to Hamming distance; adding an even integer coordinatewise cannot improve it (binaryLift). Their worst-case Euclidean hardness factor can approach sqrt(5/3). The complete finite-size original-NAND-to-gap-system compiler and its honest costs are not supplied by the hardness theorem.

### Affine frames and the actual ball interface

Let B generate the lattice, t be the public target. Publish a translation h and all increments RB so that a witness coefficient vector z evaluates to h+RBz. If Bt' = t is solvable over the ambient computation ring, the center h+Rt is public. Rational t' works for exact rational full-rank increments; the ambient evaluator accepts the center when it is in its promised domain. For a modular lifting lattice containing lI, the increments determine lRt. For prime modulus q not dividing l they uniquely recover Rt, using the public inverse of l. In particular a binary preprocessing lattice contains 2I and the odd prime of the ball construction permits exact recovery. A nontrivial quotient does not by itself solve this: if kt is in the column module and the integral center coordinates are known within an interval of width strictly below q/k, k times the center suffices to recover each coordinate (shortLift). For arbitrary unrestricted, independent uniform frame rows modulo q, conditioning on the public increments leaves the image of the annihilator of the columns under evaluation at t. In the module over Z/q this is a coset of size the additive order of t mod the columns, by the finite character dual of the quotient. Short frame rows can further reduce this support and need their own norm analysis. Restricting an honest decoder to integer lattice coefficients does not impose that restriction on the raw evaluator.

[On the Hardness of LWE with Trapdoor Hints and Applications to Fuzzy Cryptography](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2927), Definition 5.1, Figure 2 and Theorem 5.1, use a center x sampled from D_(Z^m,sigma) conditioned on the Euclidean radius-B domain X. Correctness holds simultaneously for **every** bounded ambient x' in X. The public object is (A,T,A^T s+x mod q); Eval runs TdInvert on the syndrome minus x'. Its simulator uses independent uniform syndrome with the usual trapdoor pair. Its distributional guarantee includes any one predicate of x. It does not grant arbitrary joint simulation with a helper whose value reconstructs x. Theorem 5.1 assumes a polynomially bounded prime q, m at least 4n log q+1, tau>2, r=m tau omega(sqrt(log m)), t_ball much smaller than q/(4rm), sigma at least tau^(ceil(log_tau q)-1) omega(sqrt(log lambda)) and B<(q-t_ball)/2 together with the stated Gaussian-tail condition. The computational premise and concrete sampling/strength still have to match the registry. The lock releases a Boolean ball evaluation; it does not itself accept a payload.

### A tested center-transfer replacement

For a fixed binary generator G, sample independent Bernoulli(theta) coordinates of r and send (Gr, b xor <r,t>). A witness for c=uG obtains a corrected vote with bias rho^wt(t+c), rho=1-2theta. On NO the one-row signed masses f(y)=E[1_{Gr=y}(-1)^<t,r>] give the exact message distance sum_y |f(y)|, bounded by the square root of the sum over c in C of rho^(2wt(t+c)) for a full-rank generator (Walsh/Parseval). This is a condition on the **joint public header**, and a lower bound on distance alone may be inadequate.

Here is an exact nontrivial specialization with arbitrary rank and coordinate-dependent noise. Let the generator supports be disjoint nonempty blocks covering every coordinate, one repetition generator per block. Within a block split positions according to the target. Let a and b be the products of their respective coordinate biases. The two parities are independent; their XOR is the header bit. The signed masses for the target parity at header 0 and 1 are (a+b)/2 and (a-b)/2. Their absolute sum is max(a,b). Independent blocks multiply the signed masses under XOR, hence the full one-row distance is exactly the product of these maxima, including when some noise is zero. Thus the natural best corrected parity actually attains optimal distinguishing advantage against the **whole** row, even with many independent blocks. A generic bound with a 2^(rank/2) factor can be vacuous here. The replay compares the exact joint law on 280 small unequal-bias cases. Binary indicators outside the blocks can be treated separately as independent parity noise.

This channel can supply the **paper's actual Gaussian center and actual YES query**. Sample x using the required law and encode its integer coordinates in M fixed bits. For each of those bits issue fresh h rows as above, with the bit as b. Use real Figure 2 for payload one and its independent uniform-syndrome simulator for zero. On YES the holder uses one supplied witness to recover each bit by majority, then applies Eval to the recovered point (reject if outside X). This is a direct construction of a point in X without any trapdoor secret. Conditional on successful reconstruction the real lock accepts except for its setup error. For the uniform syndrome branch the candidate point was computed independently of that syndrome, and the acceptance probability is at most q^(n-m)(2 floor(t_ball)+1)^m when inversion verifies an error of norm at most t_ball; include setup failures. This bound counts at most q^n secrets times the number of small errors. For NO, if the one-bit channel has joint message distance delta on every applicable target, replace each row's masked bit by a fair independent bit retaining its header. Distance per replacement is delta/2 even conditional on the sampled x and on the lock. Thus both legs cost in total at most Mh delta. The remaining comparison is exactly a fixed-parameter Gaussian Figure 2 challenge with an independently simulatable header, which the reduction can sample from G and public t. Auxiliary correlations do not survive this replacement. For a computational channel substitute an ordinary two-message indistinguishability hybrid for the statistical step; each row reduction samples x and the lock itself from the outset.

For example take 256 disjoint 2-bit blocks and theta=1/4. On targets with some witness at distance at most 1, each vote has bias at least 1/2. For targets with one discrepancy per block the one-row distance is exactly 2^-256. At h=1025 and M<=2^32, the majority union bound is M exp(-1025/8) < 2^-152 and the full NO statistical replacement costs at most M*1025*2^-256 < 2^-213. The target weights defining this example are efficiently decidable. Each bit costs 263425 raw output bits (1025 rows of 257 bits), 524800 Bernoulli samples and the actual arithmetic before the ball lock and both registered machines. No concrete legal Gaussian parameter set, quantum reduction or pair of capped machines is claimed here. For a direct one-bit application the first channel by itself suffices. The example exhibits a computable NO simulator for an actual conditional center transfer.

### Why the published preprocessing gap does not give a secure parity release

Take any h independent corrected votes with per-row coordinate-homogeneous public biases rho_i and a common nearest-word decoding rule. The honest bias on row i is at most b_i=rho_i^(3nH) for the Feige–Micciancio code. For two opposite messages, the Bhattacharyya coefficient of the product of corrected votes is the product of sqrt(1-bias_i^2). Cauchy–Schwarz on square roots of probability masses bounds total variation by the square root of one minus the squared coefficient. Hence any honest test on these votes with distinguishing gap eta requires max_i b_i >= eta/sqrt(h), without assuming majority or identical row parameters. On *every* false system the public zero assignment yields a word of distance at most 5nH. Correcting the maximum-bias row using this word distinguishes its masked payload with gap at least (eta/sqrt(h))^(5/3). This relative-distance inference is also the gapWitness integer-power identity. For eta>=1/2 and h<=2^100 this is at least 2^-85, and for h<=2^32 it exceeds 2^-29. These are lower bounds for the standalone masked-bit transcript. They quantify a leakage obstruction to a hybrid requiring 128-bit closeness for the transferred bits. Distinguishing a complete lock from that weak leakage alone would require a further argument. The computation of the correcting word uses the public equation instance. The lower bound on b_i uses honest performance on a YES target at the same shape and rates; arbitrary target-specific choices need their own analysis, or an independently imposed uniform promise on the baseline rates. A receiver exploiting additional authenticated information is outside this argument.

A separate concrete fixed code routes gate ports by producer identity through a 16384-port Beneš network. The 4 linear bits z,z+a,z+b,z+a+b with target (1,1,1,0) cost one mismatch for a correct NAND and three otherwise. Check a switched equality L=0 with two copies: targets (0,0) activate it and (0,1) give one constant mismatch. Use two active pairs per switch, group equal adjacent sorted identities, pin true and output, and include a dummy group. For the 4096-gate family this gives length 1818626, dimension at least 442368 (a triangular incidence submatrix on stage outputs), baseline D=450689 and exact distance D+2 for every NO by flipping the terminal outcome. Each row needs at least 442368 syndrome bits in this direct explicit format; h<=9709 under a 2^32-bit subbudget. The positive reduction builds all internal bits from the witness. Prior signed short-row encodings similarly have a tight squared-distance gap 4225 vs 4227. This new code keeps G fixed across wiring.

Even independent row- and gate-specific public rates cannot fix the parity construction merely by downweighting inactive wiring. Suppose each NAND's four coordinates share one rate within that row, and each inactive equality's two copies also share a rate. Place a dead gate first and then 4095 successive repeated-parent NOTs starting from true; the terminal equals false. For row i set w_j=-log rho_(i,j) on the live NAND blocks; the baseline log bias D_i includes at least the sum of all live w_j. Choose a minimum-weight live gate, flip its output, and propagate that value down the remaining chain. This leaves exact pin and routing consistency, and increases the score by only twice that minimum. The receiver may use a **different codeword for each row**. Its bit bias is between b_i^(1+2/4095) and b_i, where b_i=exp(-D_i) is the valid-trace baseline. For positive finite rates this follows by the minimum/average inequality (minimumFault); zero-bias boundaries follow by continuity or direct inspection. The KL divergence from this row's corrected vote to the baseline vote is bounded by the chi-squared expression (b_i-b'_i)^2/(1-b_i^2), at most (2/4095)^2/2 since 1-exp(-u x)<=u x and exp(2x)-1>=2x^2 for x>=0. Tensor additivity and Pinsker bound the full product distance by sqrt(h)/4095 < 0.0241 for h<=9709. This compares virtual baseline and explicit NO corrected votes with these rates. It applies when correctness requires such a baseline to carry the bit; a public algorithm deciding in advance that this special chain is false can instead issue fixed zero. Fully anisotropic rates inside a gate, global correlations, compressed headers or a computational trapdoor channel need different arguments.

For odd h the exact majority response at single-bit bias x is x times the sum of binom(2j,j)(1-x^2)^j/4^j for 0<=j<=(h-1)/2. This follows by subtracting majority probabilities at consecutive odd lengths, leaving a tie at the middle boundary. The scaledMajority lemma proves the attenuation inequality F(sx)>=s F(x) on the resulting polynomial. The replay checks 204 rational identities and 128 full-chain weighted certificates. The general research obligation is a center transport using a fixed hard distribution whose NO transcript can be simulated despite the accessible approximate codewords, together with full concrete costs. Statistical closeness for every false formula in a uniform asymptotic classical public WE would put SAT in SZK via the two public sampler circuits for opposite payloads; this does not give a finite-size lower bound.
