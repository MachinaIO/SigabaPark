In the syndrome ciphertext (H^T s+e, s.y+mu), use a single noise law for size n and threshold w, independent of H,y. Write b_x=E(-1)^(x.e). Exact hiding for all NO statements implies b_x=0 for every |x|>w by taking H=I,y=x (for a class including that matrix shape); conversely that cutoff suffices for every matrix shape. The following optimization applies wherever this common cutoff is required. Fix the ordinary parity decoder with the positive orientation for all witnesses.

**Exact linear program.** Randomly permuting the coordinates of the noise preserves the cutoff and cannot decrease the minimum signed bias among allowed witnesses. Thus optimize radial laws. For d=0,...,w define K_d(j)=sum_i (-1)^i binom(j,i) binom(n-j,d-i), with out-of-range binomials zero. Let b_0=1. Fourier inversion gives the density relative to uniform at noise weight j as f_j=1+sum_(d=1)^w K_d(j)b_d. Maximize t subject to f_j>=0 (0<=j<=n) and b_d>=t (1<=d<=w). Every feasible point gives a normalized probability law by Fourier orthogonality. Draw j with probability 2^(-n) binom(n,j) f_j, then draw a uniform j-subset. Thus a rational optimizer specifies an actual sampler. The LP has polynomial dimension and coefficient bit length, admitting polynomial bit complexity and an optimal vertex of polynomial bit length. Rational sampling may use rejection with an explicit abort budget.

At n=12,w=4 the optimal biases in increasing weight order are (3,3,1,1)/11. Substitution verifies feasibility. To certify optimality, write the density inequalities as -sum K_d(j)b_d<=1 and the bias inequalities as t-b_d<=0. Take the nonnegative combination of density rows j=4,5,8 with weights 1/44,1/33,5/132, and bias rows d=3,4 with weights 4/33,29/33. The left side is t and the right side 1/11. The previous ball achieves 6/79 at these dimensions. The optimized squared inverse bias is 121 versus (79/6)^2, about 30% smaller before rounding the repetition count. This is an optimization of the parity decoder, not a benchmark for arbitrary WE.

**A universal limitation.** For any common cutoff noise, including a nonsymmetric one, its density f=2^n Pr[e=.] has Boolean Fourier degree at most w and ||f||_1=1 under uniform measure. The low-degree norm inequality gives ||f||_2 <= (p-1)^(-w/2) ||f||_p for 1<p<2 by Boolean hypercontractivity. Interpolate ||f||_p <= ||f||_1^theta ||f||_2^(1-theta), theta=(2-p)/p, and take p up to 2 to obtain ||f||_2 <= exp(w). See [O'Donnell, Analysis of Boolean Functions, Chapter 9](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/papers/Analysis-of-Boolean-Functions-by-Ryan-ODonnell.pdf). Parseval now implies

    min_(|x|=w) |b_x| <= sqrt((exp(2w)-1)/binom(n,w)).

Even a decoder allowed to correct the sign of its witness parity encounters this worst case. For n>=16w, binom(n,w)>=(n/w)^w yields a bound of (exp(1)/4)^w. If w grows faster than log n here, no common exact-cutoff noise achieves inverse-polynomial boundary bias. For a decoder receiving only T independent witness parities, constant distinguishing advantage needs Omega(1/b_x²) samples at small bias: relative entropy against T fair coins is at most T b_x² by the single-bit chi-square bound, followed by Pinsker and triangle inequality. This argument does not bound a decoder using other information in the ciphertext.

The estimate also quantifies a possible escape. If the Fourier coefficients beyond w have Euclidean norm eta, the degree-w truncation g has ||g||_1<=1+eta by Parseval and Cauchy-Schwarz. The same argument gives min_(|x|=w)|b_x| <= exp(w)(1+eta)/sqrt(binom(n,w)). Thus an inverse-polynomial uniform witness bias in the above sparse regime needs substantial aggregate high-degree mass. That mass can be distributed over many individually tiny coefficients. Its presence alone neither gives an efficient NO distinguisher nor proves a reduction to LPN. A computational construction would need to explain why the publicly specified syndrome coset cannot use that tail, or use statement-dependent noise or a different decoding operation.
