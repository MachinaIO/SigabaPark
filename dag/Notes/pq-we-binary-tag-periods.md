### Why many independent gate contributions behave differently in characteristic two

Suppose a tag is a k-bit binary XOR of independent gate or input contributions E_i, each a nonempty subset of the vector space over F₂. Translate each contribution set to contain zero, retaining the overall public shift, and let S be their sumset. Let H={h:S+h=S}. Then H is a subspace, S/H is aperiodic, and omission of any tag implies d=dim(F₂^k/H)>0. In that quotient at most d−1 contribution groups can remain nonconstant. This bounds the number of nonconstant groups *after quotienting by the actual final period*. It does not bound the number of syntactically referenced wires. A group of two pin bits on one valid NAND row counts once.

Here is a proof. If a nonempty family J of nonconstant quotient groups spans a space of dimension at most |J|, take a minimal such family. The span is generated by the group's possible values (zero already included). A singleton minimal family has rank one. Otherwise removing any one group leaves rank at least |J|, so the whole family has rank exactly |J|. Every subfamily has rank at least its cardinality. Therefore one can pick independent vectors, one from each group, spanning that whole space. For completeness, prove the independent-representative criterion by induction: if a nonempty proper subfamily has equality, first pick its representatives and pass to the quotient by their span; the rank condition on the union with any remaining subfamily proves it survives in this quotient. If every nonempty proper subfamily has strict inequality, take any nonzero representative from one group, quotient by its line (each remaining rank drops by at most one), and apply induction to the remaining groups. For the one-group case take any nonzero vector. Representatives in a quotient can be lifted to the group that supplied them.

Taking zero or the chosen representative independently in each member of J supplies every vector in their span, since scalars are binary. Their whole sumset equals that nonzero subspace. All its elements are then periods of the *full* sumset; SumsetPeriod formalizes propagation of a period through addition. This contradicts aperiodicity, so every nonempty family has rank strictly greater than its size, in particular there are at most d−1 nonconstant groups. The quotient is indeed aperiodic: a quotient period lifted to the original group preserves a union of entire H-cosets and therefore belongs to H. Exhaustive verification checked all 349504 unordered size-three multisets of nonconstant subsets containing zero in dimension three, including repetitions, as well as all size-two and size-one families in their respective dimensions.

If both chart tags are binary linear on the raw coordinates, the affine wiring image is a coset. One can first quotient by its variation space to reduce matching to the zero target, then apply this period result to the local NAND image. A 16-bit checksum whose zero is absent leaves at most 15 nonconstant independent groups in its final aperiodic quotient. The cyclic-integer score in the companion note admits all 4096 groups with independent alternatives 0 and 1, has attainable set [0,4096] modulo 16384, and omits the wiring target −1. Binary-vector addition and addition with carries have genuinely different coverage here. Neither statement limits nonlinear tag maps.

### When the underlying lookup may depend on the public covers

The two chart keys need not derive their hidden nonconstant lookup from a common global root if its law may depend on the public requested prefix covers. In their union discard a node if it has another requested node as a strict ancestor (do not merge sibling trees). Sample independent seed/pad anchors at these minimal-depth nodes. Derive all other requested nodes below them, using any deterministic child expansion with the Boolean pad transport of the companion note. Shift the transmitted B pads by the message. A tag covered by both sides uses a common anchor, even if the covers request different depths. Hence output XOR is exact. In the NO case of disjoint reduced covers no anchor has descendants requested by the opposite side. Translating the pads of all B anchors by one couples all their output pads simultaneously and leaves all seeds and all A keys unchanged. The serialized view is perfectly independent of the message without pseudorandomness. Independent anchor seeds may still specify a nonconstant field lookup, for instance an affine Boolean form on the remaining tag bits. This is a cover-dependent distribution of functions. The fixed-root construction separately preserves one lookup sampling law independent of both circuit and cover.

A common public branch selector can also choose different local score formulas. If every A-branch tag falls in the low half and every B-branch tag in the high half on a NO member, route by the half bit first and put the public branch identifier afterwards. One root child per side then suffices regardless of the number of branches. The score rules, how the issuer establishes the covers, branch clocks and descriptions still need to be charged. Cheap evaluation of a candidate score on a supplied input alone does not certify that every possible B input avoids the low half.

### Shorter exact fixed-root expansion

Another existing RingLWE.decision tuple takes N=512, m=1, q=512 and both secret and error CBD(1). A fresh uniform 2048-bit seed splits into 1024 secret bits and 1024 error bits. Each coefficient is the difference of two independent bits. One 512-coefficient 9-bit body gives 4608 output bits, of which the first 4098 are two 2048-bit seeds and two one-bit edges. The challenge supplies exactly one 4608-bit uniform public ring element, and its body supplies the two routed subkeys as in the companion note. On the disjoint-half family the complete two keys plus public ring element occupy 8706 bits with fixed order; an explicit framing or chart/program description increases this. Each party may make thirteen expansions below its root child; two such calls to the full lookup cost 26·512²=6815744 schoolbook coefficient multiply-add iterations. None of these counts certifies a Machine clock.

The registry function RingLWE.H evaluated in the core to 148 for this tuple. It evaluated to 177 for N=512,m=2,q=128 with both CBD(1), and to 196 for N=512,m=3,q=64 with both CBD(1); these also have enough output bits to expand two seeds and pads. For a one-hop test with raw signed gap at least half the target absolute gap, the decision gain (squared gap) loses a factor at most four. Under a source HardAt hypothesis at 148, a sufficient overhead for a 128-bit transfer would be cost(B)≤2^18·cost(A). This is arithmetic conditional on an actual costed simulator. No such concrete machine-cost proof or quantum registry strength is submitted here. All of these game parameters are independent of the circuit.
