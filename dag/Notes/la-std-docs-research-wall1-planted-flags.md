# A missing case in the planted flag criterion

A primitive vector can have a nonprimitive image modulo a primitive prefix. Consequently a count of competing flags excluding the planted line can vanish even when the planted projection is strictly longer than the terminal minimum. This occurs for a fully reduced basis in the plain planted ensemble.

## Exact criterion for a fixed prefix

Let $W$ be a primitive subgroup of a Euclidean lattice $L$, let $Q$ be the projection of $L$ orthogonal to the span of $W$, and write $v$ for the projection of $u$. Assume $v\ne0$. Write $v=a v_0$ with $a\ge1$ and $v_0$ primitive in $Q$. The projection is an isomorphism from $L/W$ onto $Q$, since primitivity gives $L\cap\operatorname{span}(W)=W$. In particular $a$ is the gcd of the absolute suffix coefficients of $u$ in any basis extending a basis of $W$.

The only shortest vectors of $Q$ are $\pm v$ if and only if both of the following hold.

1. $a=1$.
2. Every primitive extension $U\supset W$ of rank $\operatorname{rank}(W)+1$ with $u\notin U$ satisfies $\det U>\det W\,\|v\|$.

Indeed the primitive extensions are precisely the inverse images of primitive lines in $Q$, since the respective quotients are isomorphic. Each extension has determinant equal to $\det W$ times the length of a generator of its line, by orthogonal base times height. On the line of $v$ this generator has length $\|v\|/a$. On every other line condition 2 puts its generator strictly beyond $\|v\|$. These comparisons exhaust the nonzero vectors because every such vector is an integral multiple of a primitive one. For membership among the shortest vectors allowing ties, replace the strict inequality in condition 2 by a weak one. When $v=0$, the prefix already contains $u$ and the terminal shortest-vector test does not apply.

## Counterexample within the plain embedding

Take one error coordinate, $n\ge1$ secret coordinates, an odd prime $q\ge3$, the zero matrix, zero error, and secret $2e_1$. The public right-hand side vanishes, so $L=q\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb Z$. Write $f_x,f_1,\ldots,f_n,f_t$ for the ambient unit vectors. The primitive planting is $u=2f_1+f_t$. Order a basis as
$$
 f_t,f_2,\ldots,f_n, f_1,q f_x .
$$
Here the middle list is empty for $n=1$. The basis is orthogonal with nondecreasing lengths. In every projected block, a nonzero integral combination has squared length at least the square of the first length in the block. Hence this basis satisfies even full-dimensional exact block reducedness and is size reduced.

Take the terminal block of size two. Its prefix has determinant one, $Q=\mathbb Z f_1+q\mathbb Z f_x$, and $v=2 f_1$. The minimum is one. Every line other than $\mathbb R f_1$ has all its nonzero lattice vectors of length at least $q>2$, by the $x$ coordinate. Thus at cutoff $X=1$ this prefix fails strictly and has zero witnesses of the proposed kind. Taking $n=1,q=3$ gives a three-dimensional example. At the requested plain shape $n=512,q=3329$ the same construction has $m=1,d=514,k=512$, and the least competing extension determinant is exactly 3329. Conditional on this planting the zero matrix has probability $3329^{-512}$ under independent uniform entries. This is an exact single-instance obstruction to the equivalence. It gives no typical-case estimate for a selected basis.

## Reducedness and the projection split

Even a unique ambient shortest vector can retain its entire length in a reduced terminal block. For an explicit example in dimension three take $b_1=(1,0,0)$, $b_2=(1/2,\sqrt{3}/2,0)$, $b_3=(0,0,9/10)$ and plant $u=b_3$. The planar squared norm of an integral combination is $i^2+ij+j^2$, a positive integer for a nonzero pair. Thus the unique shortest line is generated by $b_3$. The first two-vector block has minimum one and the second projected block has orthogonal lengths $\sqrt{3}/2$ and $9/10$, in increasing order. The basis is size reduced and exactly block reduced at size two. Projection off $b_1$ preserves the full planted length, yet the terminal minimum is strictly shorter. In particular reducedness alone supplies no uniform contraction factor below one, even with a unique ambient shortest line.

The fixed-prefix criterion holds simultaneously by deterministic quantification. A probabilistic use still requires a bound on prefixes for which $a>1$ in addition to a count of competing lines. Neither the examples nor the deterministic criterion evaluates that probability.

## Independent audit (Nov 2026)

Verdict: **correct** (exact Fraction arithmetic for both counterexamples; criterion checked in both directions).
Scope caveats: in counterexample 1 the planted vector (`‖u‖ = √5`) is not the ambient shortest vector (`f_t` has
norm 1), so it lies outside the uSVP regime; counterexample 2 is a generic lattice, not a planted one. Nothing here
bounds the probability of a reduced prefix with `a > 1` for random planted instances.
