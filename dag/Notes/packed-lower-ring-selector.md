**Native bank and challenge.** Let n be a power of two and q=2p=2^w. The fixed assumption is the registered ordinary RingLWE.decision at (N=n,m=all native columns,q=p,secret=uniform,error=gauss 9), using one shared uniform upper secret t. Sample a uniform binary polynomial a; s=a+2t is precisely uniform modulo q. This follows coefficientwise from the low/high finite equivalence in the definition. Independently lift each uniform challenge header H to a uniform A mod q. From its challenge body L return the column (A,2L+aA+U), with independent D24 coefficient dithers U. In the real branch the phase modulo q is 2e+U. Representative changes add multiples of p under a factor two. The lowLift theorem checks the identity in any commutative ring in which 2p=0, including the negacyclic ring over Z/q. The core ModuleLWE.ofCoeffs_comp_sample identifies its flattened ring challenge with this product. Apply the desired GSW column shift to the public header or body after this transformation. It can be any function of a and public independent coins since the simulator actually sampled a. For a scalar selector j the message is the constant polynomial a_j; for the single matrix below it is the whole polynomial a. Challenging each matrix under a fresh independent high key would change the native bank.

**Statistical part.** Use the core convention rho_s(x)=exp(-pi*x²/s²). The mass at z of 2D9+D24 is proportional to rho_30(z) times sum_x rho_7.2(x-c_z), by completing the square (c_z=0.18z). Poisson summation gives this last sum as 7.2 times a number within theta(7.2) of one, uniformly in c_z. Here theta(v)=2 exp(-pi*v²)/(1-exp(-pi*v²)) bounds the two nonzero Fourier tails. Normalization bounds the per-coordinate TV by theta/(1-theta). In the ideal branch L is independent uniform mod p. Conditional on A,a, only the parity of U can keep 2L+U from uniform mod q; conditional on each parity its high part is exactly uniform. Compare the two Gaussian coset masses for D24 using v=12 and the same bound. After shifts the joint ideal bank is still independent uniform even conditional on a, up to m*n times this error. In the real branch the comparison with independent D30 costs at most m*n times the first bound. For n=1024,m≤53248 these contributions are respectively below 2^-207 and 2^-620 (real, ideal). The latter estimate is deliberately loose. All estimates compare full views before postprocessing; a supplied artifact depending on unknown t, native errors or secret openings still needs its own joint simulation. These Gaussian probability inequalities are analytic derivations, not claims of a Lean end-to-end security reduction.

**Finite sampler.** Truncate the honest D30 and simulated D24 at [-200,200]. For any of the three widths up to 30, the tail is bounded by 2 exp(-pi*201²/900)/(1-exp(-pi*403/900)); multiplying by 54,525,952 is below 2^-174. A fixed 220-bit table sampler rounds each of 400 cumulative probabilities using lower/upper enclosures of width <2^-224 and rounds down to a dyadic. Individual CDF error is less than 2/2^220; the total variation for a sample is at most 800/2^220 by summing neighboring CDF errors. The attached code derives outward enclosures using the standard library correctly rounded exponential and verifies its rational bracket for pi by the Machin identity and alternating arctangent series. Each call consumes exactly 220 fresh coins. Headers, the secret, and lifting each require their own uniform bits. The registered D9 challenge itself is supplied to the reduction. It is not a machine for the sampler to call.

**Algebra and correctness.** Interpret each right ring ciphertext (A,B) with phase B-sA. Write its components as sums of digit polynomials times gadget weights. The columnwise sum after multiplication has phase mu*(B-sA)+sum_i e_i z_i, checked in productPhase (for any commutative ring and decomposition). Binary digits have coefficients zero or one, so when all errors are bounded by T, every coefficient of the added error has magnitude at most 2wnT, for all operands, even adaptively chosen. The noise theorem applies the pinned core's actual negacyclic convolution. Coefficient j of sA is the scalar dot product with the row of rotMatrix A: entry k is A[j-k] for k≤j, otherwise -A[n+j-k]. For an existing scalar ciphertext choose the reversed signed header and constant body fitting the target coefficient; the other phases are generally unrelated. Scalar selectors preserve separation of the coefficients. They do not convert an evaluated row to a GSW selector. D30 block errors are fresh only at bank generation. Conditional on a right operand independent of its error block and an index independent of those errors, the additional noise per coefficient has tail ≤2 exp(-pi*u²/(900*2wn)); unconditional reuse does not justify this stronger estimate.

**One polynomial matrix and a terminal family.** To multiply one fresh message v by every a_j, issue a *joint additional row* encrypting Delta*v only in degree zero, with a small error in every coefficient. Delta=q/2. Multiply this row by one polynomial-a GSW matrix, then extract any coefficient j. Its phase is Delta*v*a_j+(a*e)_j+sum_i(e_i z_i)_j. The singleCoeff theorem checks isolation of the noiseless monomial. For w=26,n=1024,T=200 the total added error is at most 10,854,400<16,777,216, even for bank-dependent use of this particular correctly formed right ciphertext. The bank and right row together have 53 columns, 2,822,144 raw bits and 5,644,289 framed output bits. The same ordinary challenge simulates the fresh row by adding the known body shift Delta*v. This construction does not isolate a_j times an existing arbitrary scalar LWE row, since filling its unused phases with correlated small errors is a different operation. Counterexample at n=2: a=X, a right phase X and a read of the constant coefficient return -1, although both constant coefficients vanish.

The matrix bank for k binary scalar selectors has B=4knw² bits. An ordinary output encodeTuple [ct] has length 2B+1. At w=26,k=n=1024 this alone takes at least 5,670,699,009 ticks. At w=20 the issuer writes at least 3,355,443,201 ticks; a sequential decoder required to visit even the start of the last contiguous matrix must additionally traverse at least 2*4*(k-1)*n*w² input positions. Both latter costs together exceed the 2^32 engineering target; this says nothing about representations allowing another access pattern. The registered goal's actual numerical cap is 2^100. The direct 220-coins-per-error sampler would by itself use over nine billion bits for the replicated binary bank.

For the 53-row single-polynomial *local issuer*, a loose finite-state construction fits the larger cap. A straight-line Boolean circuit may count an arbitrary two-input Boolean function as one gate; fixed inputs and the secret/header/finite Gaussian coins count as cells. Store each wire on one work-tape cell. For a cell at the current write frontier, visit its two hard-wired source positions and return with at most two retained bits, then write the new value. Output can be streamed in the prescribed framing by visiting its wire positions. For S bounding input births, internal wire births and output positions, there are at most 4S²+O(S) head steps and at most 16S² states (copy-specific paths use at most one retained argument on the second-source leg and one computed output on the return leg). No ROM or word arithmetic is free. In a safe loose bound use 8S² ticks. The same tape initially births a fresh coin cell in one tick. Because time and description are counted separately, the resulting table has at most 18*states*(8+Nat.size states) bits. This is a construction and priced layout; no compiled 53-row issuer or general-WE machine accompanies it.

At n=1024,w=26,m=53, fresh honest coins cost mn*220+(m+1)nw. For each independent error, compare its 220 bits with all 400 fixed thresholds using two gates per input bit, take neighboring indicators and OR the signed w-bit constants for its interval (≤400*(2*220+2)+401*(w+2) cells). For a negacyclic schoolbook product the n² coefficient pairs use at most 8(w+1)² gates each: partial products by AND, shifted additions truncated to w bits with five gates per full adder, optional negation and accumulation into the w-bit coefficient. Extra 1000mn+1000wn+1000 cells suffice for error and gadget additions, body shifts and framed output positions. The scalarPrice theorem checks S<2^39 and the resulting abstract clock-plus-table envelope <2^93. The comparison is for this isolated bank; possible evaluation, simulator, relation, public terminal and both full primitive roles are separate. ModuleLWE.encodeSample explicitly encodes the expanded module matrix ahead of the challenge responses; the reduction cannot assume immediate access to a packed polynomial header. It must pay to reach its responses and produce its structured transcript. Thus even the estimated registry H cannot be copied into a full security-per-cost claim.

**A structure-preserving partial NO hop.** For a public binary matrix M with n rows and target b, let the low polynomial's coefficient vector be a. Issue, alongside the GSW bank, M^T a and beta+<a,b> over F2. Given x with Mx=b, subtract <M^T a,x> to recover beta. Given b outside the image, choose h with M^T h=0 and <b,h>=1. In the ideal bank the independent uniform a and a+h give identical shares and opposite masks. The idealShares theorem checks equality of the entire finite output laws under such a permutation; the retained public bank can itself be fixed as side information and averaged over afterwards. Both the native shares and the terminal are efficiently simulatable from the sampled low digit a in the real/ideal challenge transformation. For two hybrids the gap is bounded by the two challenge distinguishing gaps plus the statistical deviations on each side. M,b can depend on the public bank if the YES witness equation and NO separation hold for each realized bank. This establishes a complete joint NO argument for the stated span family; the simplest instance can omit the bank entirely. A general NAND embedding would have to produce the witness x and a separating h on every false statement with bounded description. Simply replacing each product of distinct wire variables by an unrestricted scalar fails: take y=NAND(x,x), g=NAND(x,y), o=NAND(g,g). The circuit never accepts. Even reducing both squares by Booleanity leaves a fresh scalar u=xy, with o=u, so the relaxed linear system accepts u=1. The example pads to the registered size with dead gates before the terminal. Publicly decrypting arbitrary selected encrypted outputs supplies additional low-digit information and need not preserve idealShares. A witness-dependent terminal for arbitrary circuits, the complete two-role cost and a costed registered Machine reduction remain open.

**Signed radix and reuse.** With radix 16 choose balanced digits in [-8,7], carrying between positions; center the last digit modulo its smaller final power of two if w is not divisible by four. Put ell=ceil(w/4), k preselected binary secret positions. There are 2ell*k columns. On each block of L=2ell*n independent D30 coefficients, a union over the 2^L sign vectors and the discrete Gaussian subgaussian bound give Pr[sum |e|≥15L] ≤ 2^L exp(-pi*225L/900). Union-bound k blocks at issue time. On this event's complement the core convolution bound in radixNoise gives error at most 8*15*L simultaneously for every right operand. For CMUX with public left and right ciphertexts, return left+external(selector,right-left); its error is the selected child's error plus this bound. By induction one may reuse these same selectors throughout a depth-D mux program with D times the bound, without any independent-evaluation premise. At (2048,38,128) the framed bank costs at least 796,917,761 output ticks and 4096 levels add at most 20,132,659,200, below q/4=68,719,476,736. At (1024,26,1024) the framed floor is 1,526,726,657 ticks and the zero-initial-error depth is at least nine. A computed encrypted wire is not automatically an available selector; branching gates must use the preissued indices, and the number of evaluated nodes enters cost.
