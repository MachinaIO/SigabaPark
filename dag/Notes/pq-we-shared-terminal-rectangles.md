For N≥2, a supplier opens independently generated source secrets indexed by three *fixed* subsets A,J,C of [0,N). A route requires i∈A, j∈J, k∈C with i≠j and k≠j; i=k is allowed. These masks, including the positions of zeros in fixed-length slots, can be public. They are fixed before sampling the secrets. No other tester or correlated auxiliary string is issued. Goal e30dec5c requires a context-bound supplier implementing the full registered relation, which is open.

### Issuance and correctness

Each A or C source has two independent uniform 256-bit outward pads; J has one uniform 256-bit word. Emit each source flag followed by the actual corresponding word or pair when open, zeros otherwise. A range at j is strict: L represents available indices less than j, R represents indices greater than j. There are N−1 of each per role. In the increasing L sweep, for j=1,...,N−1 choose a fresh seed X_j. Publish X_j xor the L pad of source j−1. If j>1 publish X_j xor the propagation half of G(X_{j−1}). Use the decreasing mirror for R. Instantiate independent range seeds for the four sweeps. Each G produces separate 256-bit propagation and terminal halves. Induction recovers the proper range seed and its terminal pad from each eligible source, even when that source is the sole available ancestor.

Choose uniform T of width 256 and output beta xor bit₀(T). At j store T xor J_j xor Aterm_{j,a} xor Cterm_{j,c} for side pairs (a,c). At an interior index omit RR, whose entire 256-bit word is LL xor LR xor RL. At an endpoint store the single existing pair, including RR when j=0. Applying the recovered side pads and opened J_j yields T and then beta. The theorem terminal proves the coordinate reconstruction and simultaneous invariance for every pair; pointwise application lifts to words.

### Static losing unions and the single-block hybrid

The theorem losing proves the exact equivalence: for every opened j, either A⊆{j} or C⊆{j}. If any role is empty, extend the other two to full. Otherwise if A has at least two elements, every j∈J forces C={j}, hence J=C={c}; extend A to full. The symmetric argument applies for |C|≥2. For singleton A={a},C={c}, J⊆{a,c}. If a=c extend the other source role to full; if a≠c extend J to {a,c}. All displayed supersets are losing and maximal: adding one element to an empty full-neighbor role permits choosing two off-index sources, adding an element to a singleton case permits the opened join or the new join, and in the distinct pair case a new J works immediately while a new source works at its own role's previous singleton index. The full-neighbor empty-role case uses N≥2. A reduction for a larger opening mask simulates any smaller one by deterministic flag replacement and zeroing corresponding fixed-width slots. Include its scan in simulator cost.

Select dead ranges whose terminal pads are to be switched and include their strict ancestors in that role/side. For no-A or no-C switch both chains of that role, D=2N−2. For no-J switch none. For A=J={a}, only the left chain ending at a (length a) and right chain ending at a (N−1−a) are needed. All strict source ancestors differ from a. Select unopened J_j for j≠a. Mirror for C. For the distinct pair use A at j=a and C at j=c, and J at every other j, giving D=2N−2. Ancestor closure is forward in the emission order of the particular sweep; the selected chains never consume an open outward pad to enter a selected node. A source's *other* outward pad may have another use but is independently sampled.

For each message world switch the D selected expansions to independent uniform pairs in parent-before-child order. For a challenge word Y of 512 bits for the current node v, take its two halves for that node's outgoing data. Every incoming ciphertext is the xor of the seed X_v with a different independent uniform OTP: a dedicated unopened source pad, or the already switched parent's propagation half. These incoming pads have no other occurrence, including in auxiliary outputs; the parent's terminal half is independent. Marginalize the entering pads and generate all entering ciphertexts independently uniform, without knowing X_v. The theorem incoming proves for every fixed seed and every tuple of incoming rows that exactly one tuple of separate pads produces it, for any number of rows and any word width. Thus conditioned on the seed, the encrypted incoming tuple is uniform; this stays true jointly with its generator output. This includes two entering rows sharing X_v. Earlier and later stages, including all cuts, side reuse and J words, can now be sampled using Y and known fresh values. In the real challenge Y=G(U256) the hidden seed may be coupled to U256; when Y is uniform the same insertion implements the next hybrid. This reduction handles a challenge to the *output* of a hidden generator, without ever flipping a seed or requiring its disclosure. The first/last nodes of every selected segment have the same argument; terminated propagation halves need no use.

In the ideal endpoints flip bit₀(T) and beta together. At each j flip bit₀ of J_j when closed; otherwise flip that bit of *every* available terminal-side pad of the selected whole dead role at j. These roles are disjoint private independent coordinates across indices, separate from propagation halves. The terminal lemma preserves all pairs simultaneously. The flip is its own inverse on the uniform coordinates and preserves all visible bits, so the ideal laws for the two message bits coincide. This part is statistical equality and survives arbitrary processing of the static classical transcript.

For a classical Machine A testing the two real laws, telescope both sides. For D>0 one of ≤2D single-block distinguishers B has absolute acceptance gap at least the absolute router gap divided by 2D. Nonuniformly selecting the best of finitely many machines is allowed by HardAt; its description, redaction, sampling, composition with A and tape moves are charged in B.cost. For D=0 the real laws are equal directly. For block laws g₀=law(G(U256)), g₁=U512, the Lean theorem hardAt takes a real SigabaNeo.Problem.decision.HardAt bound and the per-edge advantage and cost simulator hypotheses. Its proof telescopes the chain and selects a maximum-gap edge, including the zero-length case. It proves the typed HardAt conclusion; it does not exhibit the compiled simulator or certify its cost. With (2^k)(2D)^2 B.cost ≤ (2^h) A.cost the bound is k. At N=4224, D≤8446; 2^156 < 2^128·16892² ≤ 2^157. Thus at cost ratio one this proof requests h=157 for k=128; include the actual ratio for any quantitative use. The proposed single-block H=128 in task dd4d6ebd-25b8-435a-969d-112fc50994c6 cannot pay that worst-case loss. This is a limitation of the proof. ### Packet, expansion and machine budgets

There are 4N−4 range expansions, 8N−12 propagation rows, 3N−4 terminal rows, and one masked bit. For N=4224 this is 16,892 calls, 33,780+12,668=46,448 rows, 11,890,689 router bits and 5,419,392 actual flagged source bits, total 17,310,081. The optional compact source-seed interface substitutes a single 256-bit seed per role/index and uses 25,340 expansions. It needs extra source switches and a fresh security ledger; the D bound above was proved for independent source pads. If only beta is needed, replace the word terminal rows by coordinate zero, use independent one-bit J, and put beta directly into every terminal row instead of emitting T. The same losing involution and D hold, now flipping beta and the designated missing role without a T flip. Costs become 8,660,348 router bits and 4,342,272 flagged source bits (13,002,620 total). This alternative recovers one bit and does not offer the word T to callers.

For the word variant a 17-field/index layout (five source fields followed by four triples seed/propagation/terminal) requires 288,616,192 field seek ticks and 27,024,384 field combine ticks under the literal address replay. With the 52,421-tick/3,254-state three-return-bit expansion replay and the predecessor's allowances, the partial bounds are 1,229,305,324 issuer ticks, 615,813,590 decoder ticks, and 1,138,639,680 bits for two standalone six-tape kernels. The remainder to 2^32−1 is 1,311,208,701. Merging a wrapper changes the state-width charge for *all* transitions; these estimates do not establish that tighter bound. Figures for the three-bit return version are replay estimates, distinct from the five-tape 3,206-state/51,941-tick kernel.

A deliberately loose full local implementation envelope for the word variant is also available. It uses straight-line schedules and large dense tables. Put the fixed flagged source slots before the four complete, topologically ordered chain edge sweeps, then the terminal rows and the message bit. The decoder reads a framed 39-bit triple before one framed packet field. Reject invalid coordinates, equal forbidden coordinates, or a closed selected flag. Tries of depth 39 handle triples with fewer than 2^40 binary nodes including the last level, with a constant factor for framing. For each valid triple there need be one linear control schedule only. At most N³<2^37 valid triples. Each schedule streams *once* in packet order, retaining the selected A and C source pads and J, then follows only the two indicated chains. The first edge uses the source pad, subsequent edges consume a recovered propagation half, and the final G for each side keeps its terminal half. For RR xor the three stored words. Read β xor bit₀(T) at the end. No secret-dependent positioning. Fixed-length packet validation including terminators takes <2^26 input ticks per route; any malformed input can halt at the first syntax error.

Use five isolated work tapes for the five-tape expansion, one for the virtual input and one collecting its emitted bits, plus A,C and J/terminal accumulator tapes (ten total). G has fixed head travel and fixed emission positions. Implement one call by copying its known 3,206 control states, routing emitted bits to the collection tape; on halt jump to a local continuation. Account for resetting workspace without assuming blank tapes: the five kernel heads, virtual input and collection head each travel at most 51,941 from origin, with fixed final positions for a valid seed frame. Return each to the left edge of its used window, sweep the union of used cells blank in parallel, and return to origin; use ≤8·52,000 ticks/states for reset and ≤8·1,024 for copying/framing and XOR. The seed and result live on the A/C work tape before calling and after extraction, respectively. Together a call uses <2^19 ticks and <2^19 states including scratch cleanup. The raw virtual input must match the five-tape block's input convention; if a private seed has to be copied twice, both passes fit the copy allowance. There are ≤8446 such calls; ≤8·256 extra ticks per visited range for pad combines/buffer rewinds and <2^26 for all other non-kernel streaming operations in addition to input scanning. In particular each per-triple clock and state schedule costs <2^33, allowing duplicated XOR-carry bits at their local instructions. Short-circuit invalid openings without retaining three flags in every kernel state. Over all valid routes and the input trie the decoder uses <2^71 states. Dispatching upon each kernel halt is a graph edge, not a second stored input triple. Its dense table with ten tapes has fewer than 6·2^71·3^10·(4+40+72)<2^97 bits.

For Enc, one extra tape after the seven block work/input/output tapes stores the 17-field layout, a ninth holds the terminal word and a tenth can buffer row XOR. Since the authenticated-opening flags are fixed input supplied to Enc, a separate traversal is allowed: write all source slots first, sampling fresh words into the layout and outputting zeros or the word as indicated by that flag; use local print/discard modes for one slot. Fill the range triples in address order, then emit in sweep order. For a crude bound allow *every* scheduled field fetch to traverse the whole array (17N·256<2^25 cells), charge one round trip per field, and allow 16 fetches per row or range plus 16 per source index. Fewer than 2^21 round trips of <2^26 ticks, with <2^35 for all G calls and a further 2^35 ticks for copying words, public framing, sampling and XOR. Counters may be unrolled for these fixed addresses. A two-mode bit buffer suffices for pairwise xor by reading successive tapes or accumulating a word. Thus separately for Enc time <2^49 and states <2^50 including two-way branching at fixed print/discard and local XOR instructions, giving a dense ten-tape description <2^76. Together both algorithms are comfortably below 2^100 in this static cost model. This is a constructive paper schedule with a deliberately loose budget. ### Independent wider-source endpoint

If each open A or C may carry N−1 fresh bits, a direct bit router has *perfect* static privacy with independent delivered secrets. Sample a_j,c_j,J_j independently for each j. For each R∈{A,C} and i≠j sample independent kᴿ_{ij}; emit kᴿ_{ij} xor r_j, where r=a or c. Open exactly the vector of k's for the authorized source (R,i). Emit beta xor a_j xor c_j xor J_j per j. A losing view is paired bit-for-bit: at each closed j flip J_j; at each other j choose an absent off-index role, flip r_j and *all* kᴿ_{ij} for i≠j in that column. No flipped k is opened; both every correction and every terminal are preserved. This involution on independent coins proves exact equality of both laws without a block assumption. An honest triple uses exactly two correction/opening pairs and its actual J bit. This complement costs an independent N−1-bit static opening at each A,C source, and applies to a single message bit with no extra tester.

A sequential fixed-layout encoding permits a small local budget. First output for each j the J flag, opened J or zero, terminal bit. Then for each i output its A,C flags and N four-bit slots (A correction, A opening-or-zero, C correction, C opening-or-zero); write four zeros at i=j. Total 4N²+5N=71,389,824 bits at N=4224. Enc reads beta, J flags and A,C flags in this order. During J issuance prepare two N-bit tapes of a,c and a third with one moving hole at outer i; initialize the bank at tape cell 1, with blank sentinels. During each matrix row scan the three banks jointly; at ordinary j sample two fresh bits and hold each for one tick to output its correction then the opening slot. At the hole output four zero bits. Rewind all three, shift the marker right one on the return at its old site, and halt after the last row. No random access or duplicate use of a source bit. Coding tuple fields uses prefix/data pairs in input; output above is raw, and the decoder reads the frame. A fixed unrolled init loop of <8N states accommodates beta and the three sampled bits via successive steps (write a, write c, emit the J and terminal bits; alternatively park beta and use a constant-state loop with the sentinel preinitialized in N states). Preinitialize N false cells of the marker using N states to implement this latter option; retain beta in the two copies of the constant-state J loop, use the right marker-bank blank to end the loop. Then mark its first cell and run fixed loops carrying two eligibility flags. With this implementation both generic control and local variables fit 1,000 states beyond the N initialization states: for example the matrix phases carrying the two open flags and at most one sampled bit require <128 states, including the backtrack on moving the hole. Allow 20,000 states and 3 work tapes. Even at 16 ticks per coordinate plus 64 per row the issuer uses <2^29 ticks.

A binary-triplet decoder can initialize three separate one-hot tapes of N cells in N states. For each 13-bit coordinate read the framed bits by a binary trie (<2·8192 states per coordinate plus terminator checks), seek right by the decoded index via a countdown chain (<N+1 states per coordinate), set the one-hot flag, rewind to the left blank sentinel. Then the first and third selectors advance one per outer row (hold row selection in finite control); the middle selector scans once over the J section, rewinds, then scans and rewinds inside each matrix row. Read the full fixed-width framed packet sequentially. Accumulate the two selected correction/opening XORs, the selected J, and terminal in a single parity bit. Abort on a closed required flag and also on diagonal A or C coinciding with the middle marker (a one-bit diagonal-marker check can instead be performed while initially scanning the three one-hot tapes jointly and resetting them). Invalid strings halt or time out. Both orientation choices and A=C work. With 70,000 states (4,224 + 3·(16,384+4,225) + <3,000 miscellaneous) and three tapes its dense table is ≤374,220,000 bits. The issuer table is ≤100,440,000 bits. At 32 ticks per coordinate plus 128 per row and <2^20 preprocessing, decoder time <2^30. Sum of both budgets is <2^32 by these paper schedules. This accounting concerns independent wide static openings and intentionally includes never-used diagonal slots.

### Verification and boundary

Exhaustive mask tests at N=2,3,4,5 found 49,214,849,3302 losing triples of masks and 9,15,23,33 maximal ones, respectively. Seeded ideal-pad fixtures verified all authorized paths, every missing RR reconstruction and the simultaneous flips at those sizes. A separate wide-source packet encoder, decoder and full-transcript involution check covered every mask and 16, 768, 18,432 valid routes at N=2,3,4, respectively. These checks do not estimate real generator advantage. The formal modules prove Boolean identities, the exact losing criterion and the quantitative implication with cost hypotheses. The all-circuit research goal still needs witness-dependent openings which cannot be pooled across incompatible evaluations, their full joint simulation for every unsatisfiable statement, a permitted quantitative premise when using G, and registered end-to-end encryption, decryption and cost proofs. A public circuit and arbitrarily chosen static sets alone do not supply these obligations.
