## From the registered statement to one zero test

For a registered 128-bit witness put the true constant and the witness in history order. At gate i take x,y from the referenced positions of the previous history, interpreting a missing index, including a self or forward reference, as true. Take z from the i-th claimed bit, then append z. With exactly 4096 gates and claimed bits, our definition `samples` and theorem `samples_fit` identify absence of NAND mismatches with the existing `GKW.TraceFits`. Require the last claimed bit to be true. This handles repeated operands and dead gates too. Correct forward evaluation produces the unique consistent trace by gate induction. The registered decryptor can use the prior [trace-emitter component](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/138), with 484,513,167 ticks and 88,452 description bits, before evaluating the group program. Inlining and subsequent parsing require additional clocks.

Set t=1-z. With law (a,b,c)(d,e,f)=(a+d,b+e,c+f+ae), the six factors are (x,0,0), (0,y,0), (2t,-2t,0), (-x,0,0), (0,-y,0), (-2t,2t,-4t²+3t). Their product has only the central coordinate xy-2xt-2yt+3t. The gate theorem checks the identity over integers and its range 0 through 3, with zero precisely at a correct bit. Central elements add. An additional central shift 1-out gives E in [0,12289], so reduction modulo 16384 preserves the exact zero predicate on every trace. This takes 24577 single-bit reversible instructions. The constant 3t is folded into the sixth factor.

For a shorter count use a strip (u,s) in Z/m × Z/3. The five instructions add x to s, add y to s, increment u if z differs from the bit [s≠2], subtract y from s, and subtract x from s. Each fixed branch is a permutation, including on scratch states other than zero. The central test in the third instruction is selected by one external bit z; the two fixed permutations may inspect their internal scratch states. Starting at s=0, one block increments exactly for a wrong claimed bit. The Lean `nand_trace_word` quantifies over the entire gate list and accumulator group and proves that the scratch returns to zero and the accumulator increases by the mismatch count for every evaluation. Add 1-out to obtain C in [0,4097]. Thus each residue program K_j costs K=20481 instructions. A compiler needs only gate positions and public wire selectors, with the modulo update encoded by fixed permutations. The uniform algorithm and its logical count here are separate from a certified finite-tape implementation.

## A bit-programmed exact terminal

Use moduli 13,17,19 with product 4199. The 3 strips have 39,51,57 points. Identify their origins, leaving 145 positions. Each individual permutation is extended by fixing points of all other strips outside the shared origin; it acts within its own strip. Use the resulting action in a group of lamps indexed by these positions, with the permutations transporting lamps. Use the dihedral group with 16 rotations at each point. Let A=r be its generating rotation and B be a reflection, both supported at the common origin. Form W₀=A^β and W_j=[W_{j−1},K_j B K_j⁻¹] with [X,Y]=XYX⁻¹Y⁻¹. Use the action convention in which K_j transports the origin to (C mod m_j,0); the printed multiplication word for each composed permutation follows that convention, reversing instruction order when necessary. At each step the second lamp is either at the origin or disjoint from it. In the latter case the commutator is identity and stays identity. If all residues vanish, the successive rotation exponents are 2β,4β,8β. The final function is the identity on all positions for every rejecting trace and both bits, and is the nonidentity origin lamp r⁸ for exactly β=1 and an accepting trace. The argument concerns the entire evaluated group element.

The fully expanded word length is L₀=1 and L_j=2L_{j−1}+4K+2, giving 573490 instructions. In a naive two-branch raw table, two three-bit S₃ lamp arrays per position would use 498,936,300 bits with these moduli. The S₃ variant takes distinct transpositions at the first commutator and a transposition at each later commutator. Three-bit serialization is not exact uniform fair-coin sampling. Dihedral elements take five bits, or 831,560,500 bits for two full arrays per step. These figures only charge the specified arrays and assume two records even at constant instructions. They do not establish security or a machine clock. Computing all 8196 pairs of counts and message bits gave the stated terminal condition. Each of the 24 modulus/triple cases also gave a permutation on the full corresponding strip.

## Consistency of every published edge

A layer mask per instruction is insufficient. Even if both consistent readings of one repeated bit give identity, consider two instructions whose branches in the message-zero world are all identity and whose message-one branches are (1,r) and (1,r⁻¹). A mismatched path evaluates to r. Insert inverse masks on adjacent layers and the mismatched path still evaluates to r. The example is a group-program counterexample to preservation of consistent-input functional equivalence by that public table interface.

For an arbitrary read schedule form a layered DAG. At cut i remember the values of precisely the variables occurring both among the first i reads and after that cut. The next edge reads its literal, checks its value when already remembered and carries forward precisely the new live assignment. A constant uses one edge. Both endpoints have empty memory. All vertices are reachable and extendible. Every path corresponds to a consistent assignment of the bits that occur, and each such assignment gives its path. The schedule and graph may be published. In particular the gate claims are independent inputs to this graph. A consistent trace assignment here means consistency of repeated *reads*, and the group evaluator additionally checks NAND validity.

Let g₀(e),g₁(e) be the two group programs. On a false circuit every complete path has equal products in the two worlds. For each vertex v choose one compatible future path, e.g. set all new variables to zero. Write its two products a_v,b_v in path order and put h_v=b_v a_v⁻¹. Every pair of prefix products i,j reaching v obeys i a_v=j b_v, hence i=j h_v. Applying the same identity after appending an edge gives g₀(e)=h_u⁻¹ g₁(e) h_v. This is the Lean theorem `frontier_gauge`, using only reachability, edge closure of prefixes, and agreement after the chosen suffix. At the terminal h=1. At the source h=1 on false statements since its canonical full path also agrees.

One randomized realization publishes R_u⁻¹ gβ(e) R_v for all edges, with independent uniform internal masks and identity boundary masks. Correct path products telescope. On false statements R'_v=h_v R_v couples the complete edge arrays bit for bit, with the same mask law. Each h_v lies in the normal subgroup of rotation-valued lamps: changing β inserts only conjugates of rotations and the projections to position permutations coincide. Thus it suffices to sample four independent fair bits at each position of each internal vertex to specify one mask, with no cutoff or sampling error. The full-frame equality holds even for quantum postprocessing of this classical data. In the two-read example, enumerating both 1024-tape distributions with two independent dihedral masks gave exact equality for all 1024 frames.

A deterministic realization needs even fewer public bits on materialized graphs. On β=0 publish the world-zero frame. On β=1 publish h_source as header and the normalized edges h_u⁻¹ g₁(e) h_v, where all h are computed by canonical completions even on YES. Include the identity header in world zero. Evaluation along a path with the header returns its original group product, since h_terminal=1. On NO the complete deterministically serialized arrays coincide. No decision of satisfiability and no cryptographic oracle is required to form a particular addressed edge. Use identical canonical encodings in both worlds, including all unused fields.

The public statement determines g₀ at every addressed edge. Only the relative lamp correction g₀(e)⁻¹ h_u⁻¹ g₁(e) h_v needs to be stored. The suffix at a vertex contains at most eight occurrences of the original β lamp or its inverse. Conjugating and multiplying those occurrences changes the suffix by at most eight single-position rotations. For a given edge the suffix count at its source equals the count at the destination plus the occurrence indicator, so the two suffix corrections and the direct change at the edge together have at most sixteen support positions. Each position needs eight bits and a rotation four. A fixed five-bit count and sixteen sorted, zero-padded pairs cost 197 bits per edge; the header has at most eight positions and can use the same layout. Canonicalize cancellations first. The correction has the same permutation projection as the identity. All corrections vanish byte for byte on false statements.

## Range of the positive result

For live width f each layer has at most 2^f vertices and at most 2^(f+1) edges. Thus 197(1+L·2^(f+1)) bits suffice for this particular deterministic correction table plus header, if the layer schedule and sorted addresses are reconstructed from the public statement. Computing each canonical suffix independently takes up to L group instructions per vertex, plus reads, group operations, indexing and output. Dynamic programming backwards can reuse chosen suffix products using one chosen edge and the stored successor product per vertex. A dense permutation and lamp representation during construction still pays for group operations, memory access and the raw statement. No Turing-machine clock for this implementation is claimed. For small graph size there is also a direct SAT test: compute canonical prefix products along a spanning arborescence, check all edge gauges and the header, and compare the two candidate deterministic frames. More simply, the two normalized frames differ iff some complete path has different products, since every vertex has a common suffix and witnesses telescope. Thus constructing and comparing the two public tables decides the same subclass directly.

For the unrolled three-residue word the first full forward/inverse traverse puts every variable of K on both sides of some cut. In particular all 4096 trace bits are read by the block itself and recur. A cut then has at least 2^4096 live assignments before any additional witness bits. A procedure publishing code for an addressed edge with β as clear code/data leaks it; the edgewise normalization theorem does not prove hiding for such succinct programs. A useful unresolved obligation is a simulation for an implicit array that enforces consistency and hides the secret-dependent evaluator itself, from a permitted statement-independent registry assumption, together with all finite machine costs. Efficient evaluation of the raw reversible word alone supplies neither.

The affine-certificate approach reduces witness dimension using linear dependencies among selected operands. Here every raw gate is checked via direct modular accumulation and no such dependency is presumed. A candidate exact-cover checker likewise needs to protect reuse of variables and its full zero-test representation. The GGH15 chain note documents an open reduction for a different multi-level encoding; our explicit frontier coupling uses no graded encoding and gives no compact substitute for one.
