The near-boundary syndrome issue can be removed at the frontend for general CircuitSAT. Use the *intermediate binary linear system* of Fenzi and Weissenberg, not their final nearest-codeword approximation problem. [Nearly Optimal Hardness for CVP and NCP with Preprocessing](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2634), Construction 4.5, Theorems 4.6 and 4.8, Section 4.3 and Appendix A.3.

For each padded input length fix the verifier for circuit satisfaction, computing its accepting trace from a satisfying assignment. Equivalently use the paper's length-specific 3SAT verifier after the usual gate-variable reduction, filling gate variables from the supplied assignment and padding to a common length. Let N be the number of assignment/trace coordinates of the fixed verifier. Choose characteristic two, fixed tradeoff integer a>=1 and an integer gap Gamma. The system has a common binary coefficient matrix H, D blocks and m=DQ columns. Only the syndrome y depends on the instance. A valid trace gives a solution of weight <=D. On every false statement every solution has weight >Gamma D, including the possibility of no solution. Concretely the witness map computes the prefix encodings of the trace, forms their evaluation tables, and includes a table or zero according to trace bits and their pair products. Appendix A's table construction computes those entries in polynomial time. Theorem 4.8 bounds m by (N+1)^C times Gamma^(1+1/a) for a constant C depending only on a at characteristic two. Matrix and target generation are deterministic polynomial time for polynomial Gamma. This use of the paper does not assume any cryptographic hardness theorem.

**End-to-end candidate algorithms.** Set Gamma=8(lambda+1) for security parameter lambda>=1. For D>=1 let B be the least power of two at least 4D, and sample each noise bit independently with probability p=1/B. Each repetition sends H^T s+e and s.y+mu for a fresh uniform s of the row dimension. If using a full-row-rank search game, keep a fixed independent row basis and transform the target accordingly, after testing consistency of dependent equations. Inconsistent targets can use a common dummy law. Keep output length constant at each size. An honest decoder derives the above witness once and votes the recovered parities. With rho=1-2p, Bernoulli's inequality gives rho^D>=1-2D/B>=1/2. T=8(lambda+1) independent samples give majority error <=exp(-T/8)<2^(-lambda). Ciphertext length is T(m+1) bits for one message bit. Encryption, witness conversion and decryption are polynomial in the original circuit encoding and lambda. These are candidate correctness and upper bounds, not a proof of WE security.

**Provable scope of hiding.** For NO instances and any parity of the entire T-fold transcript its expectation either does not depend on mu, vanishes, or has magnitude at most rho^(Gamma D)<exp(-Gamma/4). Indeed the Fourier coefficient factors by repetitions. A message-sensitive component with a tag bit in its character requires Hx=y to survive averaging over its secret; every such x has weight >Gamma D and B<8D. An odd number of tag bits is necessary for message dependence. This also handles parities mixing many repetitions.

Any classical test reading at most l adaptively chosen affine parities of the entire ciphertext consequently has acceptance gap at most 2^(l+1) exp(-Gamma/4). Fix its coins. Each accepting leaf indicator expands as a product of affine parity tests, with Fourier coefficient absolute sum at most one. Its expectation difference is at most twice the preceding bound. Sum over at most 2^l leaves, then average over coins. In particular l<=lambda gives negligible gap. This bounded-observation theorem allows parities chosen with knowledge of the statement, but an unrestricted polynomial-time quantum distinguisher receives the full classical ciphertext.

**Why distance is insufficient as a security argument.** For a separate family take k>Gamma D and a matrix consisting of many copies of each unit column in dimension k. The all-ones syndrome has minimum weight k, yet the header gives independent noisy copies of each secret coordinate. With p<=1/4, O(log(k/epsilon)) copies per coordinate suffice by majority to recover the entire secret with probability >=1-epsilon and then remove its pad. This is an example about the proposed inference from distance alone; it is not an analysis of decoding the Fenzi-Weissenberg matrix. The remaining task is a fixed, falsifiable post-quantum reduction for the full view on the actual structured matrix, e.g. by establishing the orbit/list-decoding conditions for all compiled NO targets or finding another reduction. Hardness of worst-case nearest-codeword approximation with preprocessing supplies the frontend, not the required average noisy-secret recovery assumption or its reduction.
