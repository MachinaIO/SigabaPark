## The affine span that the issuer actually needs

Let Q be a satisfiable signed-primary 2-CNF relation. Find one satisfying assignment a, translate it to zero, and group coordinates by their occurrence patterns over the translated solution supports, dropping fixed-zero coordinates. Two coordinates have identical patterns exactly when their original bits satisfy a fixed xor in every solution. Constancy and such pair restrictions can be tested by adjoining one or two units to Q and checking 2-SAT. Consequently a does not need to enumerate solutions. These groups are precisely the equal-minimal-support blocks of the code-cover note: occurrence of i forces j and vice versa iff their patterns agree. Every translated solution is a union of whole blocks. For each block the minimal support M_i that introduces it and the union of its strict predecessors both belong to the solution family, and their difference is the block vector. Therefore the *exact affine hull* is a plus the full binary linear span of these disjoint block vectors. No higher-arity affine consequence is required to define this hull. The argument applies also to fixed coordinates and a singleton solution, where the linear part is zero. Exhaustive comparison against direct linear spans of all 4,169 nonempty four-variable 2-CNFs confirmed the formula.

For the disjunction of any collection of components compute the single binary space W spanned by (1,x) across all their satisfying assignments. An empty component adds no generator. For a nonempty one add (1,a) and one vector (0,B) per nonempty movable coordinate block B. Incremental Gaussian elimination never retains more than 129 independent rows. Alternatively any certified symbolic representation of the same space suffices. Choose a uniformly random vector r in W's orthogonal complement, by independent fair bits on a computed basis. Issue r+payload·e_0 as 129 bits. Dec simply takes its binary dot product with (1,x). It does not need a component index or a schedule. If x is valid its evaluation equals the bit with zero error. If all components are empty then W={0}, the emitted entire row is uniform on F2^129 for either bit. Even if the public deterministic bases and compiler are included, the two NO laws agree identically. The directed-implication row-per-component issuer is an alternative with less preprocessing; this construction uses at most 129 fresh bits and 129 emitted bits irrespective of the number of components.

This computation is polynomial in the *enumerated* component list, using O(n²) 2-SAT calls per component in a straightforward implementation. It does not turn a large unknown schedule family into a short input. Indeed, an exact general routine for W already decides SAT by testing whether W is nonzero. On restricted inputs with a known implicit union its augmented span can be much cheaper to compute than its list. For a union specified by a common affine system Hx=d, solve that system directly for an offset and nullspace; no coset enumeration is needed. In particular, the 2^92 charts below admit one such shared key, and inconsistent variants of that system give the exact same uniform NO law. This mechanism allows all satisfying witnesses to read the one row even when an individual syntactic schedule would not contain their neighbors.

## Exact cover of the BCH-type example

With field elements encoded in the polynomial basis modulo X^7+X+1 and checks (1,t,t³), the following is an explicit disjoint partition by nonzero codewords. Each of the first twenty supports has size six and the last has size eight.

To check the certificate, sort the concatenation and compare to 0..127; for each support xor its element indices and independently xor their cubes in the specified field, obtaining zero both times. Gaussian elimination on the fifteen binary checks gives rank 15. Thus the code dimension is 113 and the packing number is exactly 21 by minimum distance six. The smallest original-coordinate 2-CNF cover has *exactly* 2^92 components, with the upper given by the cosets of the span of the 21 disjoint words. This does not assert those specific components are generated by the syntactic routing algorithm. The 129-bit common-row construction uses the linear specification rather than materializing these cosets.

## Raw schedules versus semantically sound components

There is a connected 4096-gate example needing still more raw schedules. For every input compute n_i=NOT x_i and u_i=NAND(x_i,n_i), form the AND of the 128 u_i and append 3586 NOTs. All assignments satisfy the result. Every genuine fixed schedule nevertheless pins each input: in its local NAND for u_i the two parents are complementary and the selected one must be zero for the comparison to hold. Thus one fixed schedule admits at most one primary assignment. Exact coverage by explicit schedules needs 2^128. Randomized menus independent of the eventual witness satisfy the corresponding averaged coverage bound. This exceeds 2^100 even for one bit of explicit data per schedule. A compact algorithm that computes a schedule from the witness, a decision procedure that recognizes the tautology, and an unrestricted semantic chart have no such size requirement. The entire tautology is one semantic component and its affine hull is the whole space.

These results distinguish three requirements: preserving an exact menu of raw histories, representing their satisfying union exactly by literal charts, and giving every genuine witness a correct reading of one ciphertext. The last permits overacceptance on YES. Outside a class admitting computable certified spans, the compact issuance and its concrete machine cost remain open.

## A consequence of a fixed public reader even under computational NO privacy

Suppose every public ciphertext yields one 129-bit affine row and a genuine witness x reads the payload by dotting with (1,x), irrespective of the fresh coins. The following test does not require that the issuer explicitly knows W. Generate N=256 independent uniformly labeled encryptions of the same circuit and check binary linear feasibility of all 256 equations in the 128 common unknown bits. On YES one fixed valid x passes except with probability at most 256 epsilon, where epsilon is the per-ciphertext correctness error. On an exactly private NO the labels are independent of the rows, and a union bound over 2^128 potential x gives probability at most 2^-128 of feasibility. For computational NO privacy replace each sample by an encryption of an independent bit, one at a time. Up to N times the relevant single-ciphertext distinguishing bound the same conclusion holds, including the public cost of generating all the other samples and solving the systems. This yields a scalable decision consequence, not an unconditional finite-machine lower bound.

There is a version directly for a packet-derived public row over an arbitrary field and a fixed witness feature vector of dimension d, such as the 8257 quadratic primary features. Suppose reading the bit is an orthogonality test, returning zero on a zero pairing and one on a nonzero pairing. Compute the span A of all rows in a labeled sample carrying zero labels. Accept the SAT decision if every one-labeled row lies outside A. A genuine common witness feature annihilates A and pairs nontrivially with each correctly labeled one-row. Conversely for any fixed ordered list of N rows, any labeling passing this test must mark zero exactly on all sample points in the span of its zero rows. Each such subset is uniquely determined by a basis of at most d sample positions. Thus conditioned on independent fair labels the chance of passing is at most 2^-N sum_{j=0}^d binom(N,j), independent of field size and without searching for a satisfying witness vector. With d=8257 and N=33028 the integer numerator has 26788 bits, giving a probability strictly below 2^-6240. The standard union bound loses at most N epsilon on YES, and the computational comparison costs N appropriately sized single-ciphertext hybrids on NO. Testing 256 lists of four binary two-dimensional rows confirmed the corresponding finite count bound. This argument applies to a single extractable row per packet and a common fixed feature, even if the feature includes a witness-dependent schedule determined by the statement alone. It does not apply when obtaining the row for each sample already requires the unknown schedule or when the final test uses a nonlinear hidden selector. In particular, the bounded semantic compiler above indeed has an efficient public decision procedure on its recognized inputs.
