## The exact integer and parity optimization problems

Give each vertex of a finite graph three integer masses with sum one. Give each oriented edge six integer entries on the unequal ordered pairs of colors; require the row and column marginals to be the assigned masses at its first and second vertex. Charge the square of every coefficient once. For V vertices, M edges and minimum monochromatic edge count u, the exact minimum is D+2u, where D=V+M. Parallel edges and loops are permitted. The theorem Exact proves both inequalities constructively. Round each vertex by the first odd coordinate (default to the third). The edge's odd entries connect its rounded marginals if there is just one. Otherwise at least three entries are odd, since their sum is one modulo two. Each odd integer costs at least one. Conversely fill a proper edge with a unit. For a monochromatic (a,a), use (a,b)+(c,a)−(c,b) with the other two colors b,c. This has both correct signed marginals and costs three.

The theorem Parity proves the same rounding and attainment even if the vertex and consistency equations only hold modulo 2. The attaining tables use 0/1 coefficients, hence this is also the exact minimum Hamming weight in the binary affine syndrome. For any modulus q≥4 the optimum of the minimum centered squared representative is again D+2u. A table of energy at most two has integer sum between −2 and 2; if the sum is 1 modulo q it must equal one and the table is a positive unit. Such a table forces both endpoint residues to be the indicated one-hot vectors. Round any other vertex arbitrarily. The signed lift attains the bound at every q≥4. The modular claim here is a paper proof; the Lean nodes check the integer and the parity statements.

For a general allowed-pair relation the minimum edge energy with two prescribed one-hot marginals is the distance from the specified left vertex to the specified right vertex in its bipartite support graph. Orient all arcs left-to-right. The marginals make the table a unit flow with possibly negative coordinates. Distance to the sink (capped at the source-sink distance, constant on other components) changes by at most one across an edge. Summation by parts gives d≤sum |flow|≤sum flow² for integral flows. The simple shortest path with alternating signs attains d. If the terminals are disconnected the component divergence makes the task infeasible. A direct tensor power of three-color inequality gives no extra penalty: any proper coordinate has a length-three walk by backtracking, and any improper coordinate has a length-three walk by the signed repair above. Their simultaneous three-step walk always works.

## A faithful circuit-to-graph compiler

Use a palette triangle with colors named false, true and base relative to its own coloring. Put each primary input and each gate output next to base. For terminals in the two Boolean colors, an auxiliary triangle with its three vertices individually attached to three given terminals is colorable exactly when these terminals are not all equal: assign the three distinct colors, avoiding each of the three forbidden colors. For z=NAND(x,y) use separate triangles for (x,z,false), (y,z,false), (x,y,z). The first two rule out a false z simultaneous with a false operand; the third rules out (true,true,true), while its excluded all-false triple was already ruled out. Read uncomputed/self/future parent indices as true, as in the registered evaluator, and identify wire zero with true. Finally pin the last output to true with one edge to false. The theorem GateTrace verifies this witness relation for every list of gates against SigabaNeo.evalGates, including its initial true result for the empty list. Vertex sharing implements the Boolean wire equality; all auxiliary triangles are fresh. A graph coloring reads back the original witness relative to its palette.

With n=128 and g=4096 the counts are V=3+n+10g=41091, M=4+n+19g=77956, D=119047. On any input, including the all-false assignment, evaluate forward and give every triangle its first legal permutation. Only the final pin can fail. Thus every false circuit has exactly u=1, and its nearest signed and binary vectors are public, with energy 119049. A satisfying witness gives energy 119047. For two raw registered encCircuit instances, we generated all 4096 pairs, encoded them as the concatenation of frame(frame(unary(a)) ++ unary(b)), decoded the entire word, then checked all graph edges and signed marginals. The first 4094 pairs used a seeded pseudorandom list including future references. Both circuits set the penultimate gate to NAND(true,true); the final inputs were (0,0) or (4223,0). The false and true controls had respectively 51913172 and 51930064 raw bits, with SHA-256 prefixes cade2148eda51b80 and 656a840a606aa967 when each bit is stored as one byte. The observed energies were 119049 and 119047. The executable checks also covered all eight local NAND triples and all mass-one edge tables in the box [−2,2]^6.

## An actual fixed lattice instance and its short witnesses

Let H have three coefficients per vertex-normalization row and three per per-color incidence row (two local arcs minus the endpoint variable). The syndrome t is one for normalization and zero for incidence. This gives 508827 rows, 591009 columns and 1526481 signed nonzero occurrences. Over any prime q≥5, choose the public near vector z0 above (or the corresponding proper vector if the fixed all-false input happens to accept). Use w=z0 mod q as the word and A as a public column generator of ker H. The full-rank lifted q-ary lattice is the inverse image of ker H under reduction mod q. Then the squared Euclidean distance of w to it is exactly D+2u. A supplied satisfying witness produces its short vector z and solves As=w−z over the field for the projective witness s. In the false case s=0 already leaves a public residual of norm sqrt(D+2). The normalization rows allow 2V vertex degrees of freedom. For each edge its connected bipartite six-cycle supports all pairs of equal-total margins (solve along a spanning tree), with one extra cycle degree of freedom. Thus the kernel dimension is 2V+M=160138. Sparse parametrization uses the two vertex coordinates, incident tree flows and one cycle variable per edge. This is a public structured generator, with no established equivalence to a fresh lattice cryptosystem key.

For the base graph the distance ratio is just sqrt(119049/119047)=1.0000084000084. Uniformly duplicating the whole graph scales both squared distances equally. Even a hypothetical perfect-completeness gap reduction with NO u≥δM would give ratio sqrt(1+2δM/(V+M)), bounded by sqrt(3); no PCP, concrete blowup, or certified gap beyond u=1 is assumed in these counts. The odd-cycle alternative gives a different geometric lower bound: for target C_l, delete a uniformly chosen cycle edge and two-color the remaining path. Round each vertex to the parity of the total mass on one side. An edge is monochromatic precisely when its total mass across the deleted pair of directed arcs is odd. Summing yields E≥V+max(M,l*u2), where u2 is the minimum uncut edge count. However a homomorphism to the odd cycle gives unit planar vectors with every edge inner product at most −cos(π/l). If that SDP is feasible, random-hyperplane rounding has expected uncut count at most M/l. A promise NO gap above M/l by inverse-polynomial slack relative to M admits a polynomial-time SDP distinction for fixed l, and so cannot silently supply the desired hard promise. This does not rule out other relations or other soundness arguments.

## A concrete directional projection and full-view test

For binary H,t, let the issuer sample uniform s, error e, and publish P=Hᵀs+e and T=β+〈t,s〉. A valid projected witness z reads T+〈z,P〉=β+〈z,e〉 whenever Hz=t. With independent Bernoulli(p) noise and ρ=1−2p (0≤p≤1/2), a weight-d witness has correlation ρ^d. In the NO case the public forward evaluation above yields a readout of correlation ρ^(D+2) against the *whole* published header and body. With parameters fixed across both cases, single-projection YES correlation c implies NO correlation c^(1+2/D); for c=1/2 it is greater than 0.49999. Thus statistical or computational smoothness at these parameters already fails on the accessible statistic. This is an evaluation of this proposal alone. The probability difference between payloads for that statistic is the correlation; the deviation from an independent fair bit is half that number.

More generally let the error law be exchangeable over all coordinates with a common law at these parameters, and suppose a D-subset parity has error at most ε. For any D+2 subset U pick distinct i,j in U, C=U minus {i,j}, K any D−1 points of C. Then U=C symmetric-difference (K union {i}) symmetric-difference (K union {j}). Each of those three sets has size D. A union bound gives error at most 3ε for the explicit NO readout with no independence assumption. Circuit-dependent shaped noise may fall outside this argument.

A positive exact special case exposes the needed geometric information. For a public coordinate set J take e uniform on the coordinates in J and zero elsewhere. If a witness z has zero restriction to J it recovers the bit exactly. If there is no z with Hz=t avoiding J, there exists δ with (Hᵀδ) off J zero and 〈t,δ〉=1, by finite-field duality. The involution (s,e) → (s+δ,e+Hᵀδ) preserves the issuer's uniform coins, fixes every bit of P and flips T. The joint NO law is therefore exactly equal. This applies for example to binary list-coloring with permutation edge constraints and unary pins: parity vertex mass one on two labels is a one-hot Boolean vector, and the permitted matching forces the edge relation. General graphs with three available labels admit the three-entry repairs above. Moreover testing whether the restricted linear system has a solution is public Gaussian elimination. A polynomial-time compiler producing such J with both exact guarantees on all circuits would itself give a polynomial-time SAT decider. The exchangeable-noise failure and restricted-mask positive case do not prove a lower bound against all noise laws.

## Comparison to lattice SPHF

Bettaieb et al., arXiv:2209.04149v1, sections 3 and 5, use an MP-style trapdoor setup with shifted A, a bounded short-error encryption language and a grey zone. Their Definition 4 draws a discrete Gaussian hash vector h, publishes Aᵀh, and rounds phases from h against the word and Aᵀh against its witness. Their full projection also contains the ECC correction data formed from the individual hashes. Correctness applies to ciphertexts in the short-error language with witness s; smoothness applies on the outer complement defined by trapdoor decryption, including the explicit rejection threshold. The honest radius B and outer B'' have different roles. Benhamouda et al., ePrint 2017/997, February 2019 revision, section 5.3 and Appendix C.3, Definition C.1 and Theorem C.2, formulate the corresponding relaxed WE for honestly witnessed inner words and the complement of a larger trapdoor-dependent language. Their statistical soundness is for those setup-dependent ciphertext languages. Neither theorem asserts smoothness for the structured kernel H above from a worst-case CVP norm certificate. [Bettaieb et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.04149) [Benhamouda et al.](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/997)

In particular, a public translator on a sampled MP public matrix which sends all YES to the inner zero language and all NO outside the zero-decryption region gives the following decision procedure. Run TrapGen, retain the secret trapdoor, run the translator on its actual public key and the circuit, then test whether decryption returns zero. Its cost is one setup + one translation + one trapdoor decryption plus comparison and encoding; its failure probability is bounded by the translation error on this setup plus decryption error. Statistical distance η of the public key law costs at most η for events depending only on the public key, translated word and public distances; trapdoor-dependent guarantees must additionally be proved for the recorded conditional trapdoor or derived from uniform geometric implications. Computational key indistinguishability alone cannot transfer a hidden-trapdoor event. This is a conditional algorithm, not an impossibility result at one fixed finite size. Keeping the worst-case lattice avoids this particular fresh-trapdoor inference but gives up the cited setup theorem. A constructive alternative still needs a statement-independent joint-law reduction which works on the resulting nonrandom generator, or a different translation whose NO privacy follows from another property. No new hardness assumption, registered native hash cost, or general WE security proof is asserted.
