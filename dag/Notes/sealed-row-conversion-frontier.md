At the scalar matrix sizes below, every split of the literal bitwise circuit conversion exceeds the strict aggregate budget of 4,294,967,296. A conversion that first aggregates scalar ciphertexts keeps the original dense affine tape law. With 127 left bits and one right bit it reduces 393,216 protected scalar decryptions to 3,072. Its specified fields cost at least 1,827,954,689 encryption steps after mandatory output framing. Writing one dense result per recovered bit on a single work tape already pushes the total to 5,099,708,417. With two perfectly parallel writes per step the necessary subtotal drops to 3,463,831,553. Conventional per-gate materialization still exceeds the cap even with fourteen tapes. With three fresh bits per scalar sampler coordinate, even the aggregated conversion fails on encryption output alone at all 129 splits. There is no certified end-to-end parameter row.

### Layout and accounting assumptions

Use the sealed issuer of [PR 129](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/129). Its protected program checks a NAND trace, issues the residual-policy key from tape Mx+r, and decrypts a vector of one-bit ABE ciphertexts. The audited conversion follows the polynomial-depth bootstrap in Goyal, Koppula and Waters: an LHE encryption of the tested circuit is part of the obfuscation. Fix a public circuit layout with a literal private input slot per affine seed bit. In the direct version each slot occupies one dense binary-gadget GSW ciphertext. We even grant the remaining wiring and trapdoor slots for free in the direct lower bound. This is a choice of compiler and serialization. The GKW theorem does not mandate GSW, this layout, or these dimensions. [GKW, FOCS 2017, §I-A, p. 617](https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2017-Papers/3464a612.pdf).

* Use a bits on the left, b bits on the right, a+b=128, and t=256 lock bits. A scalar BGG ciphertext has one m-residue A lane, b+1 attribute lanes of k residues each (including the constant lane), and one target residue. Fixed-width fields of ell bits and lexicographic array offsets give the exact right payload T(b)=2^b t ell [m+(b+1)k+1]. Reconstruct each address from its position. An explicit b-bit label per column would add b 2^b. Independent encryption per component is retained.
* Take scalar BGG dimension 128, binary gadget k=128·8=1024, m=2k=2048, ell=8. For the literal sequential short-preimage sampler on [A | B_f-G], take a fixed randomness block of at least one bit at each of m+k scalar coordinates. Its tape width h is at least 3072. We price just the first 3072 rows. Actual Gaussian precision and guard bits increase the width. This floor applies to the specified coordinate sampler. It is not an entropy bound on every sampler. All exact payload examples fix h=3072, the optimistic floor.
* The outer dense GSW matrix has d+1 rows and (d+1)ell' columns over ell'-bit residues. Its size per private bit is W=((d+1)ell')². Set d=128 and ell'=8, giving W=1,065,024. The input and output lattice systems are independent. These deliberately small widths define an encoding benchmark. They carry no claim of correctness at the required depth, smoothing, or registry strength. Increasing the dimensions componentwise preserves the direct obstruction.
* WE.cost adds both clocks and both description lengths. A supported nonempty ciphertext of length L requires at least 2L+1 encryption steps. The registered encoder returns one framed output variable and writes at most one bit per step. The theorem output_cost starts at WE.enc, recovers the singleton encoding from its decoded support, and applies the machine output bound. For a lower bound on Dec work, count explicit bit writes over all work tapes, including rewrites. A step offers at most one write per work tape. A decoder with 15 or more tapes already exceeds the cap in its description alone. The remaining write slots are at most 14 times the cap less the Enc clock, with subtraction before multiplication (decoder_write_budget). An implementation performing operations implicitly needs a separate analysis.

### The literal conversion fails even at its best split

The direct ciphertext contains at least T(b)+(129-b)hW bits. With h=3072 the exact global minimum of the resulting framed clock floor is 803,576,610,817 at b=8. The table fits in the mandatory output frame exactly for b≤6, with T(6)=1,208,090,624 and T(7)=2,684,616,704. At the original illustrative b=12 it costs 128,857,407,488 bits. For variable per-bit expansion W, **some** split fits this two-field subtotal exactly when W≤5418 (witness b=1). Minimizing at the chosen large W and maximizing admissible W are different optimization questions. The Lean theorem encoded_frontier checks both across every split, endpoints included.

Even the pre-existing ring-layout depth-64 example in the XWE note has degree 2^20 and 8003-bit residue slots. A single ring element already exceeds the 32-bit output cap at fixed width. Changing the split alone cannot rescue those published rows. This observation uses the earlier note's supplied dimensions only.

For comparison, embed the a-bit address injectively into GF(2^h), possible here since h≥a. Uniform field slope and offset require 2h bits. At two distinct addresses their evaluations form a uniform pair by pair_seed_bijection. Conversely a deterministic s-bit seed whose two outputs have full support on pairs of h-bit strings must satisfy 2^s≥2^(2h), by counting images. A single address needs just h bits. Thus this substitution is optimal for the two-address support requirement. For the direct bitwise conversion the exact short-seed threshold is W≤348,500 (witness b=0). At the chosen W even the optimal two-row seed alone costs 6,543,507,456 bits. This substitution changes the full program distribution: over GF(8) on four addresses, field-affine slopes and offsets give 64 whole tables versus 512 for a dense affine map on two binary address bits. Pairwise correctness cannot justify the setup switch.

### Compact input conversion with the original dense seed

Use an independent inner binary secret s of 128 bits. Encode each private bit u by a scalar pair (A,c) with 128 uniform residue coefficients and c=〈A,s〉+(q/2)u+e modulo q. Include these scalar ciphertexts and 128 outer GSW encryptions of the bits of s. The public evaluator can homomorphically decode the scalar inputs inside the protected computation. This sketches a modified GKW input compiler. The inner LWE secret/error distribution and its joint auxiliary-input security require proof. No auxiliary public encryption key is needed for the symmetric scalar encryption step. The other private inputs of the sealed program still require protection.

At q=256 the seed block costs 1032 bits per private bit plus 128W. At b=1 the block and right table take 558,903,296 bits. The mandatory frame costs at least 1,117,806,593 steps at b=1. All splits b=0 through 6 pass the framed output filter. However, a literal dense decoder that writes one full outer matrix for **each original seed bit** consumes more write slots than any capped machine has at every one of those splits (compact_input_frontier). Transferring expansion to the decryptor fails on the same aggregate metric.

To reduce real conversion work, keep sampling exactly the original dense M and r, choose q=32768 and assume |e|≤63 for every scalar sample. For each output-tape row include its intercept ciphertext and the columns selected by the supplied x. Sum both parts of these scalar ciphertexts modulo q using public arithmetic. Only then evaluate one protected scalar decryption on the sum. The dot product with s is linear. The remaining phase is 16384 times the integer sum of selected bits, plus at most 129·63=8127 error in magnitude. Adding 8192, dividing by 16384 with floor, and taking parity gives exactly the XOR. This holds simultaneously for every x for a bounded-error draw, even with repeated calls. selected_phase_parity permits arbitrary integer representatives. With modulus 16384 and the same worst error, zero plaintext can round to one. Choosing q=32768 costs 1935 bits per scalar ciphertext.

This change removes a factor of a+1 in the **number of protected scalar decryptions** in a padded full-seed decoder. It exchanges them for public modular additions. At b=1 an all-ones left address needs 3072·127 additions of 129-residue vectors, apart from reading and selecting them. Their tape clocks still matter. The outer program must enforce its trace and use the selected row tape. Any proof of security must cover the actual compiled bytes, maliciously supplied inputs and modified public preprocessing, and the correlated right table. The arithmetic only establishes honest functionality of the tape transform.

The unframed fused subtotal is T(b)+(129-b)·3072·1935+128W. Among splits with both halves nonempty, b=1 minimizes it. No split survives the framed output plus one serial dense result write per recovered bit on one tape. With two work tapes the corresponding optimistic subtotal passes exactly through b=4. A decoder performing **eleven full dense writes per recovered row bit on average** fails at every framed output-feasible split even with fourteen tapes writing in parallel. This obstruction counts explicit writes of the specified representation in any order. For example, a fixed-schedule schoolbook inner decryptor that folds 128 public-coefficient terms separately for each of the 3072 rows and materializes even one full outer matrix after each binary addition already performs 127 such writes per row. No setup-switch proof can repair that implementation. A packed evaluator, streaming arithmetic avoiding intermediate writes, or a different bootstrap needs its own bound. See fused_conversion_frontier for the exact tests.

### The sampler precision cutoff

Allow h to vary in the fused conversion and keep the other widths fixed. Some split passes the **framed output alone** if and only if h≤8052. The boundary is attained at b=1. For the coordinate sampler this allows at most two fresh bits per coordinate with uniform fixed-size blocks: 3072·2=6144, whereas 3072·3=9216. At three bits the best split is again b=1 and already needs 4,871,446,529 encryption steps. The integer cutoff and the all-splits three-bit floor are part of fused_conversion_frontier.

This becomes an obstruction to a concrete sampling algorithm under an explicit approximation requirement. Suppose a fixed-block, sequential Gaussian sampler must approximate a scalar target conditional on its incoming state within total variation less than 1/3, uses only its fresh block at that step, and uses the same block width at every coordinate. If the target has maximum atom at most 1/6, two fresh bits give at most four outputs and hence cover target mass at most 2/3. The missing mass forces at least three bits per block. For the conventional untruncated integer Gaussian with weights exp(-π(z-c)²/s²) and s≥16, eight integers within distance four of c each have weight greater than 3/4. The normalizer exceeds six, while the maximum weight is at most one. This last Gaussian argument is an analytic sufficient condition; the verified arithmetic uses h directly. A sampler that pools coins, varies widths, or only needs a different law requires a fresh analysis. The fixed-witness WE correctness condition alone does not impose conditional Gaussian accuracy.

### What is still required

The bounds certify serialized field lengths and numerical exclusions. An affirmative row needs an instantiated sampler with its real h, a secure inner noise law and modulus, an outer evaluator and obfuscator that meet their correctness depth, explicit tape machines with clocks and descriptions, and the registry-strength and simulation-loss calculations. The first implementation question is whether protected rounding and the subsequent sampler can stay within the measured write-slot ceiling while processing the public scalar arrays. No complete upper bound is available even before the correlated setup switch. The modified compiler also needs its own joint-view security reduction. The calculation does not resolve the registered 2^100 problem or rule out every sealed issuer at 2^32.
