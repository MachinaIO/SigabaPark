For the bank interface of [the first-fault graph task](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/231), a registered three-work-tape machine samples shared keys and masks and emits all fresh Ring-LWE headers and masked outputs. With 548 rows of degree 1024, at most 171 key/mask words, two published starting keys, and up to 8192 public routing bits, its declared clock is 3,104,682,541 and its canonical transition description costs 390,044,160 bits. Their sum is **3,494,726,701**. Inputs are public graph operations selected by the issuer, including its payload-dependent choice of whether to complement a terminal mask. Compiling these operations from the raw registered NAND statement, and evaluating a complete witness path from the emitted ciphertext, are separate remaining costs.

### A four-tick multiply-accumulate

Write w=4N. A ring word uses consecutive little-endian nibbles, starting with the constant coefficient. The two-tape row machine reads concatenated fixed-size records, each consisting of one source key and one additive-offset polynomial. It publishes one fair independent ring element A and B=A K+e+offset per record, with fresh CBD(1) errors. It samples one coin per A bit and two independent coins per e coefficient. The output frame contains a one-bit marker before each raw bit and ends in zero. In deterministic product mode the same machine instead reads pairs (K,A) and emits only raw product nibbles. N is positive and even for the loop termination test; the official costs specialize to N=1024. A malformed or overlong private layout has no promised interpretation.

The first pass over K writes the negative nibbles to the key tape: from the low bit, copy through the first one, then complement later bits. Rewind the input by w, then write the positive copy immediately after the negative copy. The result stores K indexed from -N through N-1 with K_j=-K_(j+N) for negative j. Sample or read A to a separate tape while rewinding the key head by w positions. Rewind A to its first bit; three moves put the key head on bit three of K_0. The coefficient at output j is the sum over i of A_i K_(j-i). The signedWindow proof identifies this formula with the coefficient of the core Negacyclic product using the standard library's schoolbook coefficient theorem.

For even j, scan A from first to last while scanning K backwards. For odd j, reverse both scans. When the A head is at bit b of coefficient i, the key head is at address 4(N+j-i)+3-b. The finite control collects one bit of each operand in the proper order per step. After four steps it replaces the four-bit accumulator by (acc+A_i K_(j-i)) mod 16. Pending digit pairs at depths 0,1,2,3 require 1+4+16+64=85 indices per carry and direction. This is a table of explicit native transitions, reused for every pair. At the end of a forward scan move the key head right five cells to start the reverse scan of the next window; after a reverse scan move it right three cells. At the final reverse endpoint it sees the right-hand blank on the key tape, detecting the last coefficient without a 1024-way controller. The walk proof checks both address changes. A coefficient boundary takes thirteen ticks including detection, offset addition, two independent noise coins, marked output and head shifts. Thus the row time exactly equals 8w+4+N(w+13) on the stipulated layout, plus a single final end check per batch. The bulk machine has 27,878 states and 40,646,124 description bits. For 337 and 548 rows its time is 1,429,010,757 and 2,323,732,625 respectively. An 86-product deterministic batch has time 364,673,369 and total cost 405,319,493.

### Shared-state preparation and serialization

The bank machine reads four-bit opcodes, most significant bit first. It prepares the private row stream on tape three. Its other two tapes are then reused for the row machine. Opcodes 0 and 1 allocate a uniform ring key or a uniform one-bit class mask stored as the constant polynomial 8p. Allocate at the end of a contiguous store. Opcodes 2 and 3 seek one whole word left/right. Opcodes 4,5,6 copy the current word into the private stream, respectively unchanged, multiplied coefficientwise by 4 mod 16, or with the constant coefficient's high bit complemented; each advances the key head by one word. Opcode 7 emits a starting key and advances the head. Opcodes 9 and 10 emit a framed public routing bit zero or one. Opcode 8 ends preparation, erases the temporary key store, rewinds the private stream, and enters the row kernel with the original input head parked. This is one integrated Machine with 72,960 states and three tapes. It does not duplicate the multiplier description per row. All emitted material uses the same single outer frame. The registry's *challenge* format, which expands ring elements into a module matrix, is different; reading that format is a reduction obligation.

For each graph edge u to v, prepare two records (K_u,4K_v), (K_u,K_v). For a terminal, the second field is the shared class mask with an optional payload complement on the tail. For beta zero no complement is needed. For beta one complement *all* tail occurrences of the relevant mask, including repeated classes. Fresh headers and errors are generated only after the shared key stream has been prepared; no header or noise is reused, even for repeated source or destination keys. The graph plan need not be sent to the adversary. Its public routing bits carry the symbol-to-edge information. For example 42 layers, two parser states, and eight possible symbols take 1344 bits at two bits per choice when deterministic per-state edge ordering provides three choices and a reject code. Any added metadata must fit the budget charged to the instance.

For R rows, V pool words and P public routing bits, the actual clock expression is
`V(w+4)+2(2w+4)+2R(w+4)+(2R+2)V(w+4)+5P+2Vw+2wR+16+R*rowTime(N)+1`.
The terms charge allocation, two starting keys, two copies per record, full inter-word seeks in the worst order, public bit emission, temporary-store erasure and stream rewind, fixed control, row arithmetic and the final frame bit. This envelope assumes each copy or key publication is reached by a monotone seek, no extra instructions, and exactly one finish. Key-store erasure includes a scan to the right-hand blank. Seek count is at most V per access, including after allocation when the head is one past the store. Commands have their four parsing ticks charged. A 128-node pool plus 43 masks permits V=171. Different shapes can be priced by the same formula.

At V=171, P=8192 the 337-row bank costs 2,300,681,921. The 548-row bank and a separate 86-product deterministic arithmetic machine sum to 3,900,046,194, leaving 394,921,102 below 2^32. These masses include each machine description once. They do not constitute a combined Enc/Dec bound until an actual compiler and receiver implement raw input parsing, adaptive key recovery, ciphertext access and native composition within the remainder. The bank alone fits through 689 rows at these parameters; 690 does not fit this specified table and clock. Including the separate 86-product batch moves that frontier to 617 versus 618. These thresholds are for this implementation and this 2^32 subbudget, not the goal's broader 2^100 cap.

### Other schedules and verification boundary

The width-32, length-64 Nussbaumer transform over (Z/1024)[Y]/(Y^32+1) can pretransform a repeated secret and uses 15,552 ten-bit scalar multiplications per independent product if its 64 inner products use five Karatsuba levels. The folded inverse has exact factor 64, so dividing residues modulo 1024 gives the desired residues modulo 16. With a pretransformed key, the remaining forward and inverse transforms contain 24,576 ten-bit coefficient additions/subtractions before rotations, folding, copies and the inner-product additions. The per-row threshold for this implementation is 4,240,388 actual ticks including all product and row preparation moves in the two-tape body. A transformed schedule that spends even 100 ticks per scalar multiplication still has about 2.68 million ticks left for the other work before reaching this threshold, so the counts alone do not rule out a speedup. Independent A must still be sampled in the coefficient domain or through a proven exact equivalent map. The ten-level pure Karatsuba schedule has 59,049 four-bit scalar products plus its own linear work. Neither arithmetic count is a native clock. The present direct scan wins a concrete upper bound by overlapping the movement of both operands with the multiply-accumulate transitions.

Exhaustive small address checks and full-degree samples exercised both head directions, all carry and error signs, repeated keys, both edge scales, mask complements, output framing and multirow resets. Extracting the Lean machines' actual transitions, then replaying with a separate interpreter and comparing every coefficient with independent schoolbook arithmetic, gave exactly the declared row clocks at 337/548/86. The integrated 171-word tests took 1,485,849,335 and 2,447,555,655 transitions at 337 and 548 rows; the latter test has 252 edges with paired 4,1 rows and 44 terminals and includes a cycle. It exercises this record count, not a raw NAND compiler. The native cost numbers, signed ring formula, key negation, scan addresses and local nibble transition test have Lean obligations. The all-degree tape invariant, full fair-coin sampler law, semantics of every program in the stated grammar, raw-language compiler and registered WE reduction remain open.
