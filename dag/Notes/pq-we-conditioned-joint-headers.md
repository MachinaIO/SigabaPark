**Registered verifier.** There are 128 witness bits and 4096 NAND gates. Wire zero and forward/self references read one; each gate's earlier-output references are resolved at its own position. Normalize these first. Replace a NAND diamond with XOR only after checking its four exact gates, including that both inner operands actually precede the shared first NAND. Reversing the two pins is harmless. Traverse backward from the output after substitution, retaining a shared helper whenever some other live use needs it. Distinct nonconstant operands at each remaining nonlinear NAND form an edge on wire IDs. An endpoint cover meets every edge; a maximal matching followed by redundant-vertex deletion suffices. Equal-operand NAND is a NOT. A constant operand is handled directly. This public cover can be conservative; k denotes its actual size.

For each bit label z of the k cover wires, propagate affine forms of the original witness. When a NAND has a covered operand, plug in the corresponding z bit *in the product*. Keep the independent expression for the actual output of every covered wire, including internal wires, and impose that it equals the guess. Add the equation requiring the final gate to output one. This yields A_z w=d_z with at most k+1 rows. Under all these equations, a gate-order induction identifies each affine form with the actual evaluation; conversely a witness with the correct label satisfies them. This authentication precludes freely choosing a false internal guess. The accompanying replay executes the whole parser, residual compiler and rank check on registered wire numbering, including constants and unavailable wires.

**One hidden target everywhere.** Fix W=528 and retain the circuit-independent search instance, 24 syndromes, latent target X, fresh parity vector and final bit from [PR 188](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/188). In each branch z let v_j be a basis of the columns of A_z. Sample fresh independent W-bit words r_z, one per equation. Publish the words v_j dot r_z and T_z=d_z dot r_z xor X; positions, basis algorithm and equation ordering are public. To decode, evaluate the raw circuit on w to find its correct z. On a satisfying witness A_z w=d_z, so expressing A_z w in the basis gives coefficients that recover d_z dot r_z from the corresponding words. This recovers X exactly and hence the parity bit. Failing branches need not decode correctly.

On a false circuit, d_z lies outside the column space in every branch. For each label a dual u_z kills all columns and satisfies d_z dot u_z=1. For **any fixed X**, translate each equation mask by the word X wherever the corresponding bit of u_z is one. These coordinatewise translations simultaneously preserve every branch basis field and move all terminals to the identical zero-target experiment. Conditional on the entire correlated challenge, provided the branch coins are fresh product-uniform, the map is a measure-preserving bijection. The simulator samples the zero-target branch packets and does not need X, its conditional law, or even the duals. What this proves about the full packet depends on handling the original challenge fields: PR 188 gives a specified simulator for M,t and S with a free planted tail. An arbitrary pre-existing unknown tail/syndrome and extra fields correlated with the branch coins still require their own proof.

If both simulated full-view bit worlds are within total variation eta of the intended worlds, their signed gap changes by at most 2 eta. Thus the inherited single-decode recovery bound uses the retained good-block and bounded-sampler factor from PR 188 times max(0,|Delta|-2 eta)^2 (using conditional-on-success eta). For independent or sequentially conditionally close row samplers with per-step errors eta_j, eta<=sum eta_j by coupling; close individual marginals alone do not suffice. Replacing fresh words with PRG outputs additionally needs a computational hybrid per replaced seed under the actual auxiliary information. The sum of the two worlds' losses must be charged at each simulator's runtime. That substitution is optional and is not a consequence of the exact uniform identity. The quantum transform uses the local distinguisher and its inverse, after generating the shared *classical* header once.

**A common mask: necessity as well as sufficiency.** Fix a common ambient binary space and an anchor b (e.g. one of the d_z). Let K span all columns of all A_z and all differences d_z+b. Sample a uniformly random linear map R from that space to W-bit words. Retain its evaluations on a basis of K and the single word R(b) xor X. These records represent all R(A_z columns) and translate every branch terminal using R(d_z+b). On YES a satisfying w expresses b=A_z w+(d_z+b) in K. More generally b in K makes X publicly recoverable irrespective of existence of a witness. If b is outside K, a linear functional u vanishes on K and takes one at b. Then R'=R+uX is a permutation of all masks that preserves the basis data and flips the required word. Conversely, if the complete headers for two distinct centers have equal laws then b must be outside K, since otherwise a public deterministic linear combination recovers the center. The finite fiber theorem separately identifies equality of *counts* of a homomorphic uniform observation and its translate with membership of the displacement in the image. It covers arbitrary finite abelian mask groups. The anchor theorem builds the functional by dualizing the quotient V/K. The product theorem covers independent branches for a common displacement. Given a public basis these are exact, full classical transcript laws, and therefore survive arbitrary postprocessing.

Equivalently, a group of branches is shareable in these fixed row coordinates iff their separator sets S_z={u: A_z^T u=0, d_z dot u=1} have a common point (for anchor b chosen as a group rhs). Repeated identical systems may share. Pairwise compatibility does not suffice: in dimension m even, take m column-free systems with d_i=e_i and a final column-free system with rhs the all-ones vector. Every proper subfamily has a simultaneous separator, but all m+1 together do not. Conversely any unshareable family in m row coordinates has an unshareable subfamily of at most m+1 branches: stack the scalar constraints on the m unknown coordinates of u. A maximal linearly independent subset of coefficient rows has at most m members, and at least one other row has an inconsistent RHS when expressed in this basis. Keep those branches supplying the basis and that row. The minimum number of independent common masks, ignoring different basis sizes and limited to this representation, equals the least number of separating functionals hitting all S_z. An exhaustive two-row check on 256 ordered pairs of single-column systems found 175 satisfiable, 45 securely mergeable NO pairs and 36 NO pairs that expose X when merged.

**Moving row coordinates changes that obstruction.** A concrete family uses 63 input selector bits z and 64 data bits w, leaving one input unused. Compute v_i=w_i+z_i w_(i+1) for i<63 and v_63=w_63. The linear map F_z:w↦v is bijective for each fixed z by back substitution. Write l_z(v)=sum_{i<63} z_i v_i. For fixed public t in 64 bits and b0 in {0,1}, take native residuals with column map w↦(l_z(v),v) and target d_z=(b0+l_z(t),t). The verifier can implement v=t and l_z(v+t)=b0. At b0=1 every branch is false, with *unique* native normalized separator (1,z,0). Its uniqueness follows from the codimension-one graph of l_z and d_z outside that graph. Thus no two of the 2^63 false branches share a mask in these native coordinates. If one also retains explicit equalities for the 63 designated primary inputs, the separator must vanish on their independent identity columns and the conclusion persists. When branching directly on these primary inputs, their values are available to the honest decoder without trusting any guessed internal wire.

Use the branch-dependent public shear L_z(a,v)=(a+l_z(v),v), an involution. Its cost is at most 63 binary selections and parity additions and it sends every native residual to (0,F_z w) and every rhs to (b0,t). Their joint column span is exactly the 64-dimensional last-coordinate space, known symbolically without enumerating z. One common packet of 65 words (34320 bits) suffices. For YES, F_z^{-1}t may depend nonlinearly on z as a function of the full witness. For NO flip only the anchor-coordinate mask. Implementing the verifier in raw NAND uses at most 6*63 gates for v, at most 64 negations to form v+t, at most 2*63 for its selected terms, 4*62 for their parity, at most 65 negations and 2*65 further gates for equality/conjunction; padding and two final NOTs fit 4096. The result concerns a publicly generated or checked template, not discovery of that template in an arbitrary obfuscated circuit.

A second triangular example uses k=42 with 2k+1 native row coordinates and basis [e0,c1,...,c_(2k)]. Each column has its own leading one; the first k columns have lower e0 coefficient z_i. Each of the next k columns includes its predecessor direction with coefficient 1+z_i. The normalized false-case dual has first k non-anchor entries z and a final entry product_i(1+z_i), whose dense multilinear ANF has 2^42 terms. All 2^42 native separators are distinct. Expressing a vector in this bidiagonal basis instead costs O(k) row XORs and sends the anchors and columns to fixed directions. This stresses the distinction between dense expansions, fixed-coordinate sharing, and efficient changes of frame. Universal algebraic claims follow from triangularity; randomized full-size replays only exercise specific values.

**Accounting.** A branch costs (rank(A_z)+1)W public data bits and at most (k+1)W fresh bits in the uncompressed row sampler. Across all labels the bound is W(k+2)2^k. At k=18 this is 2768240640 bits; at k=19 the corresponding upper bound exceeds the local 2^32 target. This arithmetic does not supply a bound on total cryptographic cost under the registered 2^100 cap. The 4096-gate x_0 AND parity(x_1,...,x_127) fixture gives 126 recovered XORs, a cover of size one, branch ranks 1 and 2, and just 5 words or 2640 bits. Framing, search-instance headers, the syndrome/terminal, source-circuit encoding and program descriptions are extra. Host integers in the replay are not one-step machine operations.

For a direct compiler on n inputs, s gates and k covered wires, a normalization, diamond detection and backwards reachability sweep can inspect O(s) fixed-size wire records, with index parsing and memory accesses separately priced. Greedy cover selection followed by naive redundant-vertex deletion takes O(s^2) wire comparisons in the worst case. Per branch process up to s live gates on n+1-bit forms, transpose at most k+1 rows, and eliminate n columns of length k+1. A straightforward bit-operation ledger allows O(s(n+1)+n(k+1)^2) for these Boolean forms/eliminations, O(W(k+1)^2) for basis and rhs mask evaluations, and O(W(k+1)) input/output/random bits. Multiply the per-branch costs by 2^k in the explicit simulator, and charge memory addressing, tape movement, indexing, layouts and descriptions separately. The decoder can recompute just the selected branch, but random access to its serialized records is not free on a sequential tape. The normalized structured family has a directly known basis, avoiding branch enumeration entirely. No complete trusted machine clock is claimed.

If each NO circuit is guaranteed private and each YES has a decodable branch, a publicly computable basis permits a public SAT decision by checking b in K, or the OR of these checks across a polynomial list of groups. Thus this part of the construction is an efficiently decidable family when the compilation and list really are polynomial. For fixed 128/4096 parameters this is an algorithmic consequence rather than a complexity-class separation. Shared bases computed only after exponential enumeration, or a larger guessed superset K that exposes b, do not deliver a polynomial-time WE reduction. A general issuer under a sufficiently strong registered post-quantum assumption and complete machine costs remain open.
