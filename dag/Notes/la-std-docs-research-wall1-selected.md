# Symmetry of a selected prefix

Equivariance determines the angle to an external direction for a rotation-invariant input. The angle between two marks of the same lattice is a different random variable. Even selecting the prefix by exact reduction of an independent horizontal lattice leaves a competing-vector condition.

## What equivariance determines

Let an orthogonal group act on lattices and let a probability kernel select a primitive rank-$k$ prefix $W$, with $0<k<d$. Suppose $K(gL,gB)=K(L,B)$ for measurable sets of prefixes $B$. If the input law is invariant, then $(gL,gW)$ has the law of $(L,W)$. Indeed the probability of a rectangle is the integral of the kernel over its first coordinate, and change of variables by $g$ preserves that integral. Rectangles determine the joint law. The same proof includes a planted mark if its joint input law is invariant and the algorithm uses only the lattice and fresh randomness.

Write $P$ for projection onto the orthogonal complement of the selected span. For the full rotation group and any deterministic unit vector $z$, the exact law is
$$
 z^TPz\sim {\rm Beta}((d-k)/2,k/2).
$$
To see this, rotate the pair by an independent Haar rotation. Conditional on the original pair, the inverse rotation sends $z$ to a uniform unit vector, distributed as a standard normal vector divided by its norm by spherical symmetry. In axes adapted to $P$, the squared projection is the ratio $X/(X+Y)$ of independent chi-square sums with $d-k$ and $k$ degrees of freedom respectively. Changing variables from $(X,Y)$ to their sum and this ratio in their gamma densities gives the stated beta law. The argument also applies conditional on an independent external direction.

For the full signed permutation group, sign flips force every off-diagonal entry of $\mathbb E P$ to vanish, and permutations equalize its diagonal. Its trace gives $\mathbb E P=(d-k)I/d$. No beta law follows. On the invariant input $\mathbb Z^d$, select $k$ coordinate axes uniformly (equivalently choose among the shortest unoriented lines). For $z=e_1$ the fraction retained is Bernoulli with mean $(d-k)/d$. For symmetries preserving coordinate classes, independent flips and within-class permutations only give a scalar diagonal block per class on the coordinates so acted upon. The distinguished embedding coordinate must be treated separately. Left multiplication of a parity-check matrix by an invertible field matrix leaves its kernel literally identical, so this row symmetry supplies no extra Euclidean rotations.

For a concrete lattice-dependent mark, take the shortest unoriented line as mark and select it as rank-one prefix, breaking ties uniformly and using the same selected line as mark in a tie. Its retained fraction is zero for every input. In particular this holds on Haar input with the shortest line uniquely determined almost surely. Indeed in matrix coordinates a tie for distinct integer vectors $a\ne\pm b$ imposes $\|ga\|^2=\|gb\|^2$, a nonidentical polynomial equation (positive definite forms separate $aa^T$ and $bb^T$). Its zero set is Lebesgue null on invertible matrices, and homogeneity transfers nullity to the determinant-one slice by scalar decomposition. A countable union and passage to the lattice quotient preserve nullity. Rotating both marks preserves their angle pointwise. Thus interpreting an externally fixed vector as a short vector sampled from the input would invalidate the beta calculation.

## Independent horizontal reduction

Write $L=L_0+\mathbb Z(x,1)$ in horizontal and vertical coordinates. A primitive horizontal prefix chosen from $L_0$ using fresh coins is independent of $x$ when $L_0$ is independent of $x$. Conditional on this selection the length and multiplicity bounds of [Part 9](wall1_planted_prob.md) apply. There is even zero loss in the quotient determinant for a *common* horizontal prefix. If $Y$ is the horizontal projection of $L_0$ off the prefix and $p$ the corresponding projection of $x$, adjoining $(p,1)$ to a basis of $Y$ adds height one. The two quotient covolumes agree exactly. Their ranks differ by one.

There is no universal multiplicative comparison of the first exact reduced lengths. With $L_0=T\mathbb Z^N$ and $x=0$, the fully shortest-first lengths are $T$ horizontally and $1$ after adjoining the plant, for every $T>1$. Conversely, even an independent exactly reduced horizontal prefix, with a unique shortest ambient plant, can lose the terminal shortest-vector comparison. In the horizontal plane take a triangular lattice generated by $(s,0),(s/2,s\sqrt3/2)$ with $s=11/10$, and adjoin the orthogonal unit planting ($x=0$). Every nonzero horizontal squared length is at least $s^2$, since its squared length divided by $s^2$ is the positive integer $i^2+ij+j^2$. Hence the plant generates the unique ambient shortest line. Select any shortest horizontal line. For the first displayed generator the projected horizontal spacing is $s\sqrt3/2<1$. The same holds for every shortest line by area divided by its primitive length. The plant retains length exactly one and multiplicity one, yet the terminal lattice has a shorter horizontal vector. This is an exact horizontal reduction with a deterministic, hence independent, planting.

These examples obstruct a deduction from equivariance, independence and covolume. They impose no lower bound on the block size of a particular random-input reduction. For the plain shape with $m=188$, [Part 9](wall1_planted_prob.md) records dimension 701 and the heuristic comparison 406. The missing estimate is the probability, under the actual horizontal selection, that some $y\in Y\setminus\{0\}$ and integer $j$ satisfy $\|y+jp\|^2+j^2\le 1+\|p\|^2$. This follows exactly by expanding the norm of each off-line quotient vector. Even the term $j=0$ needs the selected horizontal minimum. No numerical block size follows from the symmetry and independence hypotheses alone.
