# A characteristic-two bilinear full-view gate issuer

For a public group of Boolean gate rows let S be a nonempty affine binary projection on its n categories, all of odd parity, with all correct choices present as unit vectors. Let R=span S, and M have a public basis of R for its rows. S consists precisely of the odd vectors in R: an odd linear combination of elements of S is an affine combination in F2. For each category j choose independent k-bit vectors U_j,V_j. Issue all M projections of their k+k individual bit columns and one more column M((<U_j,V_j>+b)_j), over F2. At a unit row an evaluator subtracts the dot product of the two known vectors to recover the local share b. Split the message by XOR across all groups using independent uniform shares conditioned on total parity. This is an explicit finite ciphertext; no opening oracle is present.

## Arbitrary group sizes

For c in R of odd weight, define d(c)=|supp c|-dim(R intersect F2^supp(c)). Fourier characters of the public packet pull the first two sets of k columns back to alpha_l,beta_l in R, and the final column to c in R. A change of b gives zero difference unless c is odd. The one-lane Walsh average over independent u,v is zero unless supp(alpha_l),supp(beta_l) are contained in supp(c); otherwise its absolute value is 2^(-|supp c|), by factoring the binary sums over coordinates. Squaring and counting the admissible alpha,beta across all k independent lanes gives 2^(-2k d(c)). Parseval and Cauchy-Schwarz for the total variation of the two public bit-string laws consequently give

    TV <= min(1, sqrt(sum_{odd c in R} 2^(-2k d(c)))).

If R has no unit vector then d(c)>=2 for every odd c. Codimension zero on the support would contain all its units. A codimension-one subspace avoiding all units must have the all-ones normal on that support and hence contains only even vectors, contradicting membership of c. Thus TV <= 2^((rank M-1)/2-2k). If R does contain a unit then the exact distance is one. The bound concerns every simultaneously published coefficient. Across independent group shares with one XOR constraint the full ciphertext distance is the product of the group distances.

## Four categories

The only odd non-unit vectors have three coordinates. Four such triples, being translates of the four units, are affinely independent. Hence any nonempty affine projection avoiding units is either one triple or two triples (the latter intersect on a pair of coordinates). For any one triple the discrepancy between its reported dot-product bit and the dot product of the summed U,V has bias 2^(-2k), independent of the summed U,V. For instance condition on the two pairwise differences of the three U vectors: the discrepancy is their crossed inner product with the corresponding independent V differences. It is uniform unless both U differences vanish. Thus the full-packet distance is at least 2^(-2k). For a pair of triples, additionally reveal all private values outside their common pair. Both published triples now supply the same sum vectors for that pair and the same masked sum of their dot products. Given the two sums the latter bit is uniform unless both sums vanish, because it is affine in one independently uniform pair of vectors. This exceptional event has probability 2^(-2k), proving the matching upper bound. The same upper argument applies to a single triple after revealing its third category. All unit-free four-category packets therefore attain exactly 2^(-2k).

## A joint check that both separate checks miss

Require a=NAND(x,y) and b=NAND(x,NOT y) both zero. This is the output-one condition of an AND of their negations, allowing a sound front-end that pins both outputs. Each NAND considered separately admits its own true row within the global binary affine relaxation, with the other gate allowed non-unit weights. Thus two separately released share packets expose their XOR. If the two gates share one table over (x,y), the four legal rows have (a,b)=(1,1),(1,1),(1,0),(0,1). Pinning both to zero and normalizing the sum constrains the last two row weights to one and the first two to sum to one. Exactly two distinct triples remain. For k=65 the full packet has 2*(2*65+1)=262 bits and the bit laws are 2^-130 apart. That count is for this single group's masked row coefficients. The registered 128-input 4096-NAND fixture uses seven live gates with padding; its contradiction alone is publicly recognizable. Exhaustive enumeration checked all 51 nonempty affine projections of the four-category odd hyperplane at k=1, plus both relaxations for this fixture. The supplementary Lean nodes certify the scalar Walsh cell, the crossed triple identity, the odd-support quotient rank bound, and the scalar recurrence for repeated columns. The finite-distribution arguments, fixture encoding, and runtime discussion in this note are mathematical derivations and executable checks. A registered machine proof remains open.

## An exact shortcut for repeated columns

All categories with the same nonzero column m of M contribute to the packet only via the XOR aggregates A=sum U_j, B=sum V_j, C=sum <U_j,V_j>. For multiplicity t>=1, A,B are independent uniform. By Fourier inversion, conditional on A,B, the expectation of (-1)^C for odd t is 2^(-k(t-1))*(-1)^(<A,B>); for even t it is 2^(-k(t-2)) times the indicator A=B=0. This follows since E[(-1)^(C+<alpha,A>+<beta,B>)] equals 2^(-kt)*(-1)^(t<alpha,beta>). Independent replacement of C by a fair bit costs 2^(-k(t-1)-1) TV for odd t, or 2^(-kt-1) for even t. Thus for t>=2 the error is at most 2^(-2k-1). Draw one aggregate per distinct column, multiply these by m, add the deterministic b*M1, and sum. The public cost of obtaining column identities, their parity for M1, and distinguishing singleton from multiple categories must still be accounted for. This does not provide a succinct enumeration for arbitrary NAND groups.

For a group whose column on an assignment is the sink label of an explicitly available ordered read-once branching program, propagate (min(2,count),count mod 2) at each state. Both entries compose under addition, including repeated destinations. At the end merge equal sink labels. This supplies every ingredient of the approximate repeated-column sampler in at most twice the number of reachable nonterminal layer states in transition additions. For the existing 91-input broadcast example, labels 1 and 2 according to the output both have odd multiplicity at least two, computed from 894 transition additions; this census was checked against exhaustive maps on 100 random small branching programs. It assumes the branching program is already available. For an arbitrary circuit-specified column m(w)=F(w) of one bit, even sampling the parity sum of the masked independent U_w has value identically zero if F is unsatisfiable, and is uniform if F is satisfiable. An efficient approximate sampler for this fixed, padded, unnormalized row on all inputs, with small constant total variation error, would itself give a randomized satisfiability test (repeat with independent coins). Low column dimension alone cannot justify a uniform low-cost construction.

## Taking a census of genuine observation columns

Let L specify the parity checks on the row weights. Equal columns of the observation map M mean e_i+e_j belongs to R-perp. Equal columns of L instead mean e_i+e_j lies in its kernel, hence in the translation space when the constraints and odd normalization are consistent. These conditions are different. For example a single parity normalization on n coordinates has equal constraint columns, yet the span of the odd vectors is the whole space and M may be the identity. Any compiler invoking a compact label circuit must exhibit the actual basis observation columns. There are at least rank(M) distinct nonzero types. Serialization of this basis packet charges rank(M)*(2k+1) bits, regardless of private coin aggregation.

If the actual column function is given by designated output wires of an acyclic NAND circuit, a frontier dynamic program can construct the ordered branching census. Normalize registered constants and unavailable parents to their actual default before scheduling. Fix an input order, give each gate the maximum input index among its transitive inputs (zero for constants), and after each input evaluate the gates of that level in original topological order. Track a wire until its last consumption, retaining the designated output bits through the final grouping. Start with count one. For a read, send the count to both successor assignments, even when projection makes them coincide. A gate sends each count once. Project expired wire values and add counts with equal remaining assignments. Induction on operations gives exact prefix multiplicities. At the last step group by label. Counts up to 2^128 need 129 bits at the registered size, or use cap-at-two plus parity. For frontier width f there are at most 2^f entries and at most two transitions per entry per operation. Building the schedule, storing live wires, computing labels and writing all final types are additional charges. Neither arbitrary basis construction nor small f is guaranteed. For an exact coin sampler, implementing bias 2^(-k(t-1)) may require k(t-1) bits in a fixed-clock algorithm. Approximation separates that time from the distance bound. Dropping repeated-type biases costs at most N*2^(-2k-1) per world with N repeated nonzero types, by independent coupling and pushforward. For a comparison of bit worlds both approximation errors enter.
