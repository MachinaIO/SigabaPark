## Candidate and honest evaluation

Fix a binary tree with k=2^d equal chunks of b=128/k bits, 1≤d≤7. Give each internal vertex v its own independently generated private two-input FE master and key-generation randomness. Slot identifiers are ordered by left/right position. Allocate parameters top down. Each child must handle ciphertexts for the already parameterized parent instance. Use fixed-length canonical encodings for each typed slot, fixed coin-tape lengths for the encryption algorithm, and common padded shapes for the two root circuits. The ciphertext contains all node function keys, a tagged leaf table for each position, and the framing and public parameter material needed by the evaluator. It contains no plaintext masters.

For a leaf, publish an honestly and independently randomized encryption of each b-bit value in its proper parent slot. For a nonroot internal v with parent p, issue under v's master the deterministic function

    (x,y) -> Enc_{p,side(v)}(msk_p, x || y ;
                      R_v XOR PRF_{K_v}(v,side(v),x,y)).

Here R_v is an independent uniform mask as long as this slot's entire coin tape, and the independent PRF output has that same length. Use an unambiguous injective address and distinct keys for distinct vertices. Provide a common default on malformed inputs or restrict all domains by type. The root function takes its two ordered substrings and returns beta AND eval(phi,x||y). Given a witness, select one ciphertext per leaf, apply the one designated function key at each internal vertex bottom up, and read the root bit. All repeated evaluations of the same ordered tuple use the same coins. No per-call nonce or mutable cache is required. Different addresses may coincide in their emitted bitstrings. The construction does not require injectivity of ciphertext encryption on coins.

For each fixed witness the ideal input ciphertext at any edge has the correct plaintext substring. For each internal edge its prescribed coin tape is exactly uniform because of the fresh node mask, independently of the parent setup. The two ideal children of each node have independent coins, independent of that node's setup. The function issued at that node depends only on independent parent secrets, node PRF seed/mask and public inputs, except at the root where it depends on the public statement and the bit. Induct on subtrees and couple actual decryptions to these ideal ciphertexts. The first differing evaluation is a local correctness failure on its honest ideal input. If each applicable two-input FE has error at most epsilon on independent setup, key and honest encryption tapes for fixed typed functions and inputs, the probability of a wrong result for this fixed witness is at most (k-1)epsilon. Take deterministic FE decryption as required by the registered evaluator. For the concrete k=16 case, a common local error at most 2^-132 suffices for the registered per-witness error 2^-128. The proof can union-bound unconditional ideal-node failures even when neighboring failures are dependent. This is per-witness correctness. To obtain simultaneous success on all possible witnesses by the same argument, charge all distinct reachable node inputs or use perfect correctness. A uniform mask gives the marginal claim even if the PRF is only computationally secure. It does not give information-theoretic independence across two addresses at one vertex.

Honest Enc uses k-1 two-input setups, k-1 calls to two-input KeyGen and k·2^b calls to two-input Enc. Dec makes k-1 two-input decryptions. For k=16 these are 15, 15, 4096, and 15. This tree is different from the serial growing-arity transformation examined in PR #139 and from the commitment-based tree encoding examined in the referenced DAG note.

For a fixed base construction, let H_v and J_v be setup and key-generation times including circuit input, E_{v,i} slot times, and D_v the time to decrypt its *actual* natively string-valued or explicitly compiled key. Then the core Enc time is the sum of H_v+J_v over internal v and 2^b E_{p,i} over leaves; the core Dec time is the sum of D_v. Add tape framing, selection in the supplied serialization, routing and generation of real constants, all intermediates, and both machine description lengths for the registered cost. These symbols are counts to instantiate, not unit prices. At an emitter, circuit size includes the parent master constants, PRF and a circuit for the parent's complete slot encryption including coin handling; its output length is the parent-slot ciphertext bit length. Recursively substituting actual parent parameters matters. The left and right encryption algorithms need not cost the same.

In Brakerski, Komargodski and Segev (BKS), *Multi-Input Functional Encryption in the Private-Key Setting: Stronger Security from Weaker Assumptions*, §3, the two-input master comprises inner and outer one-input masters. Encryption in slot one performs ephemeral setup, issues an aggregator function key, and emits this key plus an outer ciphertext. Slot two emits an inner ciphertext. Each two-input key is a base key. The displayed Dec comprises three one-input decryptions, with the invoked circuits and potentially long intermediate strings included in their runtimes. Thus for k=16 the ciphertext directly stores 4096 base ciphertexts, 2048 aggregator keys for left leaf slots, and 15 further base function keys, and Dec makes 45 direct base decryptions. These counts apply specifically to the selective-message scheme of §3, Theorem 3.1. Section 4's adaptive compiler has different algorithms and costs. If all same-plaintext slot queries are predetermined full chunk domains, selective-message timing suffices for those queries; the static keys and their dependencies still need the hybrid argument below. These public object counts omit metadata and cannot certify a bit or time bound. An internal slot-one emitter also computes its fresh setup and key generation from its designated tape. String-output capability itself needs accounting in the underlying FE. [BKS, §§2.3 and 3](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/158.pdf)

## First failed hybrid and the cost of an extensional repair

The conditional security question is joint privacy of the entire output for fixed false phi and either bit. Requesting the root function pair in isolation satisfies the two-input game's exact equality condition on every pair of admissible input queries: both output zero. The challenge master, however, is generated by the root challenger. In the real construction it is a constant inside both child programs that issue the root's slot ciphertexts. Running the two child KeyGens requires specifying actual circuits. An oracle answering classical private encryption queries with its own fresh randomness does not supply the master, deterministic chosen-tape encryption, or a circuit to call that oracle after the challenge. Slot separation in this interface is logical and does not itself supply an independent client capability. The secrets graph is acyclic: a key under a child master contains issuer material from its **parent**. This prevents a literal cycle and still leaves the simulation failure.

At a lower node, independently reencrypting the same plaintext is not an admissible switch between function keys: BKS asks for equality of the two *output strings* on the whole rectangle of queried left and right messages. Equal decrypted substrings or equal distributions are insufficient. For two independent uniform two-bit nonces, the strings' nonce fields agree with probability 1/4, while the deterministic real emitter agrees with itself on every repeat. An adaptive recipient has the public keys and may evaluate any cross-pair and reuse ciphertexts. A proof allowing only one externally tracked honest witness path omits these operations. Quantum use also requires a precise restriction on coherent access or an appropriate quantum proof.

One expensive repair is to tabulate each emitter on its full typed domain, from the leaves upward. First replace the emitter by a lookup program for the exact same ciphertext strings, using exactly the real deterministic tapes. Every queried cross-pair has identical output. After removing the seed and mask from that node's released program, replace the explicit table's PRF outputs by independent tapes via an appropriately secure PRF hybrid. A single common XOR with a uniform mask preserves independence for a truly random table. In later games the child table can therefore be built by fresh root-challenger encryption queries without containing that master. For each switch where the child master is challenged, the two *lower* issuers for this child must already be simulated as leaf/table oracle queries; its parent is still generated locally. This order explains what the expensive repair buys. It needs function privacy for equal-shape admissible pairs, compatibility of all auxiliary keys and cut queries, concrete PRF and FE losses, and size-hiding or padding. Literal enumeration can vastly increase the key circuit class/length. An unpadded small real program and a huge truth table may be distinguishable by their key sizes alone. This is a conditional accounting of a repair, not a security reduction for the published small candidate.

At the root cut, each of the two 64-bit slots needs 2^64 rows, giving 2^65 explicit oracle answers in this proof strategy. The Cartesian product covers 2^128 pairs, but their outputs need not each be queried: falsity of phi establishes the root equality on the entire rectangle. Lower levels contribute sums of 2^(number of bits below v) over emitting nodes. These are reduction/runtime costs; the small honest algorithm does not write these tables. The registered cap bounds honest cost at 2^100; the task's optimization target is 2^32. Separately, a challenger running an explicit-table reduction needs hardness for the composed time and advantage. For growing witness length the displayed reduction is exponential. At the fixed instance it still needs concrete inequalities. The theorem exact_issuer_queries gives a matching coverage result for one precisely specified oracle model. Fix a run of an offline deterministic compiler, including any private coins. Let its queried addresses depend on the oracle answers, and require its static emitter to be determined by those answers and to reproduce every arbitrary deterministic oracle at every address. Changing the answer at a missing address preserves the compiler output and contradicts exactness. Thus each root issuer requires every address in this model. This does not cover computational distributional simulation of keyed FE encryptions, coherent query algorithms, algebraic representations or a safe delegated handle.

### Public, asymmetric and punctured issuer interfaces

If the root challenger supplies safe public slot encryption algorithms supporting chosen tapes, the reduction generating both issuer keys can embed those public algorithms and independent seeds. It need not know the root master. The root game must still hide the two gated keys against arbitrary public encryptions and all auxiliary information. With both slots public this already gives direct WE: publish the root public parameters and its gated key; a witness holder encrypts the two halves directly and evaluates. If instead only one slot is public, choose a b-bit nonpublic slot, publish its 2^b honest ciphertexts, and let the witness holder encrypt its remaining bits in the public slot. For the original equal-split root b=64 this still needs 2^64 rows. A different, deliberately short hidden slot can avoid that table cost. In these direct variants make WE.Dec deterministic by including independent public slot coin tapes sampled by WE.Enc, then encrypting the chosen input with the supplied tape. For a fixed witness the coins are uniform and independent of setup, and the assumed public-slot game must tolerate arbitrary encryption tapes. The standard private BKS interface does not promise either asymmetric public issuance or security in the presence of a released master.

There is an important difference between the exact gated family and general-purpose public function privacy. Suppose one slot supports public encryption of arbitrary x and an obfuscator can include an honest fixed dummy ciphertext for the other slot. For arbitrary equal-size function circuits g,h with identical behavior, form keys for (x,dummy) ↦ g(x) or h(x) ignoring the second input. The dummy ciphertext, public encrypter and chosen key are an evaluable encoding of the unary function; general function privacy for all such equivalent keys gives indistinguishability obfuscation (subject to the corresponding polynomial overhead/correctness). This route is excluded by the goal. Nothing here establishes that hiding the gated root pair only implies general iO. That restricted joint privacy is a new obligation, and assuming it with public encryption gives essentially the WE security sought in the first place.

A handle restricted to well-formed honest half-assignments has no support restriction at the root: every 64-bit string on either side is a well-formed half. A secret or authenticated provenance token could constrain *which ciphertexts may be issued*, but then its issuing keys must be produced under the challenge without knowing that master. Puncturing one address lets a simulator hardwire one correct parent ciphertext in a child program while its complement still needs evaluation capability. Replacing the master on that complement creates mixed labels. With ordinary same-master Dec and one lane per value, covering all pairs (x,y) forces lane_L(x)=lane_R(y) for every x,y, hence all lanes agree. With two inputs on each side and binary labels, exactly two of sixteen assignments work. Multiple lanes per value or constrained aggregation may be possible; they require derivation of the handle from allowed fixed assumptions, cross-pair coverage and joint hiding of all such handles. Exposing a master is outside the BKS game.

## A conditional lower bound for independent single-bit replication

One literal compiler for L-bit output replaces an FE instance by L independent single-output instances and copies the child slot message into every instance. Let the ciphertext for every m-bit slot in these instances have fixed length at least m+s. For one root-to-leaf path let m_j=b·2^j, j=0,...,d-1, be slot lengths from leaf to root. Producing one parent ciphertext requires at least m_j+s separate output bits at level j. Induction from the root's single bit therefore gives at least the product of the d factors m_j+s in the public leaf length for each possible chunk entry (d-1 instance replication factors, and the final actual leaf-slot bit length). Summing the k·2^b entries gives

    2^d · 2^b · product_{j=0}^{d-1}(b·2^j+s) bits.

For s=8, the proved minimum over all seven allowed d is at least 4,529,848,320 bits, above 2^32 even with no other fields. Depth 4 attains it; at that depth s=7 gives 3,912,929,280, below 2^32 in this leaf term alone. The node replica_floor checks the seven-depth inequality. It does not impose such a cost on native string-output FE, shared many-key setups, different tree shapes, compressed/correlated ciphertexts or the registered 2^100 cap.

The +8 hypothesis admits a separate conditional justification. For a uniformly random message among M possibilities, a setup s of normalized weight w_s, a fixed-length ciphertext alphabet C, conditional encryption probabilities p_{smc} and decoder read_s(c), suppose the average probability of recovery is ≥3/4 and that two independent encryptions of the same message collide with average probability ≤1/512. Then |C|≥256|M|. The node collision_capacity proves the finite weighted inequality by summing 512p≤65536p²+1 on the chosen decoding column and bounding that column's p² by the sum over all possible messages. It permits independent auxiliary setup data such as fixed decoder randomness. For full bit alphabets this gives a ciphertext length at least m+8. This bound concerns worst-case fixed widths.

Here is one sufficient way the hypotheses can arise in a general-purpose private FE supporting bit projections on this slot. For the recovery calculation alone sample all m projection keys under the setup, fix honest independent sibling inputs/ciphertexts and decryption randomness as auxiliary setup, and read all m output bits. A marginal per-projection error epsilon gives recovery at least 1-m·epsilon. No collusion privacy for these keys is needed for this counting argument. To bound collisions, consider two same-slot LR encryption queries with no function key queries and compare equality of the two outputs. On the left both plaintexts are x; on the right they are x and x with a fixed bit flipped. Distinct plaintext collision is at most 2epsilon by coupling both decryptions with one distinguishing projection key, sibling ciphertext and identical decryption randomness. If the equality experiment has probability gap at most delta, repeated-plaintext collision is at most delta+2epsilon (normalize a concrete advantage definition accordingly). Uniform x gives the bound above whenever m·epsilon≤1/4 and delta+2epsilon≤1/512. Concrete parameters meeting these inequalities, including applicable slot domains and FE games, are assumptions for this implication. A narrowly specialized FE without projections need not satisfy this proof.

## Remaining conditions

Compact joint root issuance has not been constructed from the registered decision LWE, Ring-LWE, Module-LWE or circular Ring-LWE assumptions. The final finite guarantee needs 2^128 times squared distinguishing advantage at most adversary cost, for every false statement and registered classical Machine. Native full-string FE costs, query/loss and security-strength accounting, the two registered machines, and the honest aggregate cost remain open. In particular BKS is a transformation from suitable function-private one-input FE and PRFs; by itself it supplies neither the registry instantiation nor its post-quantum concrete security. The three Lean nodes verify the depth inequality, finite probability bound and offline oracle coverage. The candidate, its hybrid audit, and the FE-specific interpretations above are paper arguments.
