# Reverse Minkowski on arbitrary projected blocks

The proposed exact-SVP dichotomy fails on both branches. The obstructions already occur for Euclidean lattices regarded as whole blocks. Use the radii, shell width and counts of [§3.1](provable_sieving.md). Write $n$ for the block dimension and $R^*=\kappa_n^{-1/n}$ for the volume-one ball radius.

## What density certifies

Fix determinant one and $0<\delta\le1$. Either some proper positive-rank sublattice has root determinant below $\delta$, or every positive-rank sublattice has root determinant at least $\delta$. This follows by negation, since a full-rank sublattice has positive integral index. In the first case Minkowski's first theorem in the subspace gives $\lambda_1(B)\le\lambda_1(H)\le2\kappa_k^{-1/k}\det(H)^{1/k}$ for a rank-$k$ witness $H$. Exact reduction to a primitive $H$ is valid if $\lambda_1(H)\le\lambda_1(\pi_{H^\perp} B)$, since every vector outside $H$ has a nonzero projection of no greater length.

Even the first canonical filtration piece can miss every shortest vector. Take an orthogonal sum of a hexagonal plane of minimum one and area $D=\sqrt3/2$, a line of spacing $b=0.96$, and a line of spacing $1/(Db)$. Its determinant is one and its shortest pair is on the second summand. The successive root determinants are $\sqrt D<b<1/(Db)$. Each quotient is semistable at its scale (every line in the plane has spacing at least one). Hence the partial sums give the canonical filtration by the semistable-quotient and strictly increasing root-determinant characterization. In particular even identifying the minimum-slope piece for free would not justify returning its shortest vector. The first two summands may both be scaled by any factor below one and the last spacing adjusted to restore determinant one, so an arbitrarily small absolute density threshold does not fix this inference. [Canonical filtration and reverse Minkowski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05979)

## What the Gaussian bound certifies

On the second branch apply reverse Minkowski to $B/\delta$. For $t=10(\log n+2)$ the mass with weight $\exp(-\pi t^2\|x\|^2/\delta^2)$ on $B$ is at most $3/2$. Thus the number of **nonzero** points in a centered radius-$r$ ball is at most $\tfrac12\exp(\pi t^2r^2/\delta^2)$: subtract the origin and lower-bound every retained summand by its boundary weight. Shells and either type of lens are subsets of the outer ball of their defining centered shell (for $b$ count the variable $c$). This gives an absolute count; at $r=(1+o(1))\sqrt{4/3}R^*$ its right-hand side for $\delta=1$ is $2^{\Theta(n\log^2 n)}$. This elementary estimate is not a running time, nor the sharpest ball-count corollary in the source. The following examples rule out the desired constant-times-volume estimate independently of that loss. [Regev–Stephens-Davidowitz, Annals 2024](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2024/199-1/p01)

## Exponential violations at the prescribed radii

**Construction.** A unit upper triangular real matrix applied to $\mathbb Z^n$ gives a semistable determinant-one lattice. Indeed, in the exterior product of $k$ independent integer columns the lexicographically last nonzero minor is a nonzero integer. Upper unitriangular action on the exterior power preserves that coordinate, so the Euclidean norm of the wedge is at least one. In particular we can use orthogonal coordinate blocks of this kind. Write $P(s)$ for a two-dimensional shear block containing a distinguished vector of squared length $s\ge1$: use columns $(1,0),(\sqrt{s-1},1)$ and take the second column. A block with many vectors of squared length $s$ uses $P(s-h)$ and $m$ integer axes, where $h=\lfloor s\rfloor-1$. Append any signed indicator of $h$ axes to the distinguished vector. This realizes $2^h\binom mh$ distinct choices when $0\le h\le m$.

**S1 upper.** Apply the many-vector construction with $s=R_m^2$, using $n-2$ integer axes (here and below the subscript on $R_m$ is the terminal level). Stirling gives $s/n\to q=2/(3\pi e)$. The base-two exponent per dimension of this lower bound tends to $H_2(q)+q>0.46$, with $H_2$ binary entropy. In contrast the terminal shell volume is $2^{(\log_2(4/3)/2+o(1))n}$ by scaling the volume-one ball and taking the difference of the two boundary volumes. Thus even subexponential slack in S1 upper fails.

**S2 upper, both fibres.** Put $R=R_{m-1}$ and $D=\gamma R=R_m$. For $b_L(d)$ choose $d=2v$ with $v$ distinguished in $P(D^2/4)$. In orthogonal coordinates give $z$ squared length $R^2-D^2/4$ by the many-vector construction, using all remaining axes. Every $c=-v+z$ has $\|c\|=\|d+c\|=R$. For $a_L(u)$ use three orthogonal blocks: choose $v,w$ of squared lengths $R^2-D^2/2,D^2/2$ in separate shear planes, and give $z$ squared length $D^2/2$ by the many-vector construction. Fix $u=v+w$ and vary $c=v+z$. Then $\|c\|=\|u\|=R$ and $\|u-c\|=D$. In each construction $h/n$ tends to a strictly positive constant less than one ($3q/4$ or $q/2$), so the specified fibre contains $2^{\Omega(n)}$ points. Its reference volume is at most $2^{o(n)}$: the equal-radius lens is contained in the midpoint ball of squared radius $R^2(1+n^{-2})^2-D^2/4$ by the parallelogram identity. Dividing that radius by $R^*$ gives $1+o(1)$, and $\alpha_R=\gamma^n\beta_R\le\beta_R$. These are separate examples for the two quantified assertions, valid for every sufficiently large dimension.

**S1 lower.** For $B=\mathbb Z^n$ squared norms are integers. Along adjacent even dimensions with $\ell=\lceil\ln n\rceil$ constant, the increment of $R_m^2$ tends to $2q\in(0,1)$. Indeed Stirling gives $(R^*)^2=(n+\log(\pi n)+O(\log^2 n/n))/(2\pi e)$, and the remaining factor is $(4/3)e^{2/\ell}$. Both squared shell intervals have width $O(1/n)$. If both contained integers their center increment would approach an integer, impossible. Such adjacent pairs exist arbitrarily far out, proving infinitely many empty terminal shells of positive volume. The same argument applies to **every draw at any fixed prime index** in these dimensions after selecting the empty subsequence for that prime: before normalization the squared shell center is $p^{2/n}R_m^2$, whose increment still tends to $2q$ (expand $p^{2/n}=1+2\ln(p)/n+O_p(n^{-2})$). Every sampled lattice before normalization is contained in the integer lattice.

**S2 lower.** Empty target domains alone would give vacuous pointwise assertions. Here are nonvacuous finite examples on $\mathbb Z^n$ at the final transition. In dimension 168 the squared inner-level center $D^2$ lies in $(18.99978,18.99980)$ and $R^2$ in $(27.35968,27.35971)$. The $D$ shell contains a vector with 19 unit entries and the $R$ shell contains no points, so $b=0$. In dimension 1542 the respective intervals are $(155.42194,155.42196)$ and $(203.00009,203.00010)$. The $R$ shell contains a vector with 203 unit entries and the $D$ shell is empty, so $a=0$. The stated enclosures can be reproduced with rational interval arithmetic: for even $n=2j$, use $D^2=4 e^{2/\ell}(j!)^{1/j}/(3\pi)$ and $R^2=D^2/(1-1/\ell)^2$, bound $\pi$ between 3.14159265358 and 3.14159265360, sum the exponential Taylor series through degree 16 with error at most $2(2/\ell)^{17}/17!$, and check endpoints by raising positive quantities to the $j$th power. Compare integer norms with the resulting centers times $(1\pm n^{-2})^2$. Both continuum lenses have positive volume since the central radii form a nondegenerate triangle and the shells have positive thickness. These examples refute universal lower inequalities in the indicated dimensions; the empty-terminal-shell argument already prevents asymptotic worst-case T1.

## Rerandomization, domination and costs

For index $p$, the exact one-point expectation is a weighted sum of the parent and $pB$ counts in the dilated region. Its weights carry no volume-discrepancy guarantee, as the fixed-prime empty-shell example demonstrates even on a semistable parent. A growing input-dependent prime is a different question. The tuple formula tracks modular rank; obtaining simultaneous lens control and success conditional on retaining a shortest vector requires additional estimates. A fixed primitive shortest vector survives with probability $(p^{n-1}-1)/(p^n-1)$, costing order $p$ independent oracle calls for constant survival probability in the unique-pair case. [Finite kernel moments](rerandomization.md)

Remark A′ gives a one-sided distribution bound conditional on successful moves. It needs a lower bound on actual acceptance to control the normalization at each stage. An empty terminal shell forces the last transition's acceptance to zero and makes coverage S3′ impossible whenever a shortest vector is in the target ball (as it is here for large $n$). Even positive average acceptance would leave the shortest vector's representations to control. Thus neither this relaxation nor T1 yields an all-input SVP time exponent here. This conclusion does not require failure of basis initialization S5. [T1 and Remark A′](provable_sieving.md)

For an unconditional algorithm one may independently run ADRS in randomized time and space $2^{n+o(n)}$. Hhan's 2026 preprint claims $2^{n/2+o(n)}$ time and space. The November superlattice preprint reports worst-case classical time $2^{0.409n+o(n)}$ and space $2^{0.259n+o(n)}$ using random shifts and a midpoint test. These are external algorithms; the preprint bounds are reported from the authors' abstracts. No transfer of the $0.292$ heuristic exponent or the conditional $0.415$ exponent to arbitrary BKZ blocks follows. [ADRS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.7994) · [Hhan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02764) · [Hhan–Kim–Kim–Laarhoven](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2761)

## Formal scope

`SigabaNeo.RevMink.card_le_of_gaussian_mass` proves the finite count conversion for arbitrary exponents bounded above on a finite set, given its Gaussian mass budget. The reverse Minkowski theorem and the geometric counterexamples above are paper inputs and arguments. The axiom audit reports `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.
