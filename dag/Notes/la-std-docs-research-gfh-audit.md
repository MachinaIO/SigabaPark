# Fixed-pair coverage and traversal in GFH

**Verdict.** Theorem 4.2 and the nonempty-list application of Proposition 4.3 hold in the intended exponential-size, exact real-RAM regime. There is a minor literal exception to (19) on empty input. The search construction in P′ already handles it. The geometric and algorithmic arguments below are paper proofs. The machine-checked part is the passage from independent failures within a block to the global conditional miss bound. [GFH, September 12 revision, pp. 16–19 and 43–46](https://eprint.iacr.asia/2026/1844.pdf)

## Theorem 4.2

* **(16), size.** Count tuples of sample indices. There are exactly $B^m$, without a size-concentration assumption. In the asymptotic regime $b=d/m\ge2$, repeated draws have probability zero. Rounding a proposed block size $B_*\ge1$ upward changes the count by at most $2^m$. Rounding the ambient dimension to a multiple of the block count costs $O(\log d)$ dimensions. A rotation acts in the padded space.
* **(i), restricted enumeration.** Rotate the query back once. Sort the permitted scores within each block and precompute suffix sums of their maxima. Return immediately for an empty factor. A prefix passes exactly when extending by those maxima reaches the threshold, including equality. Thus every visited feasible prefix has an actual output descendant. At each depth charge it to the first such descendant. Each parent also tests at most one failing child, since the scores decrease. This gives $O(m(1+N))$ visits for $N$ outputs, with one path and the score arrays live. For early stopping after $t$ outputs, completed subtrees charge to those outputs and at most one current path remains. Initialization costs $O(\mathrm{poly}(d) B)$ including sorting when $\log B=O(d)$, as in P′. The printed proof uses $m\lceil\log_2 B\rceil=O(d)$ in D.2. For unrestricted block sizes keep the sorting logarithm and the actual depth.
* **(ii), expectations.** Conditional on all factor samples, each particular tuple has norm one. Its Haar image has the spherical law. Sum indicators over tuples to get both identities, including identical or antipodal query directions. Two tuples can share all but one block. Treating the number of hits as binomial would lose that dependence.
* **D.1, constants.** In projected inner-product coordinates the squared radius is $(u^2-2\alpha uv+v^2)/(1-\alpha^2)$. At $(r,s)$ both partial derivatives are positive by the activity margins. Convexity gives the minimum there. For $Δ=1-R$, radial integration of the two-coordinate density gives the exact enclosing tail $Δ^{(b-2)/2}$. The square $[r,r+1/b]\times[s,s+1/b]$ in inner-product coordinates has projected area $b^{-2}/\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}$; its squared radius increases by at most $O_\delta(1/b)$. Since $Δ\ge\delta$, its density throughout is at least a constant times $bΔ^{(b-4)/2}$ for large $b$. These estimates give the claimed uniform logarithmic error. Outside the activity margins the minimizing point can lie on a single face. GFH explicitly correct this domain issue in BDGL's unequal-threshold estimate.
* **D.2, (75).** For a fixed unit direction the squared length in one block is distributed as $X/T$, where $X$ is a sum of $b$ squared standard normals and $T$ the sum of all $d$. Chi-square tails at $t=K\log^2 d$ bound the relative error of each sum by $O(\sqrt{t/b}+t/b)$ with exceptional probability at most $2e^{-t}$ per sum. Apply this to the two directions and their normalized sum. Polarization recovers the block cross term, since the squared norm of the sum is $2+2\alpha$. A union bound over the three directions and $m$ blocks gives (75). The block lengths sum in square to one, so an independence argument for these lengths would be invalid.
* **D.2, (76).** Writing the error above as $ε$, rescaling lengths gives $r_i,s_i=\tau+O(ε)$ and normalized correlation $\alpha_i=\alpha+O(ε)$. By compactness the activity and radial margins persist with a smaller constant. A block hit contributes at least $\tau/m$ to each global inner product. Bounded derivatives of $\log Δ$ cost $O_\delta(bε)$ in log mass. The errors from D.1 in the two dimensions add $O_\delta(\log b+(\log d)/m)$. This yields the stated $L_d$.
* **D.3, (77) and (17).** Fix the entire regular rotation before sampling factors. If the subwedge masses are $w_i\ge w=W_d^{1/m}2^{-L_d}$, the probability all blocks hit is exactly $\prod_i[1-(1-w_i)^B]$ at this rotation. Average the uniform lower bound over regular rotations to obtain (77). Conditioning only on the indicator of regularity can correlate the hit events. For (17) even inter-block independence is dispensable. A union bound gives $m(1-w)^B\le m e^{-Bw}$. With $H_d=m L_d+3m\log_2 d$, $B w\ge d^3$. Add the irregular-rotation probability. The expectation identity alone would not imply this saturation estimate.

## Proposition 4.3 and P′

Algorithm 1 draws the rotation and all factor samples once per batch (line 1). For lines 4–8 an overflow consumes more than $S$ distinct insertion records in that cell. At each depth the cells partition disjoint filter indices, hence at most $Z_A/S$ cells overflow. Each generates two children. Depth is at most $D=m\lceil\log_2 B\rceil$, and a singleton has load at most $|A|\le S$. At most $1+2D Z_A/S$ cells are initialized. Insertion outputs across all depths cost at most $(D+1)Z_A$. Lines 10–15 query each retained leaf once, giving $Z_B$ outputs and exactly $P_{\rm bucket}$ inner-loop iterations, including equal-vector tests. A filter's unique retained leaf proves completeness separately for every sharing filter. Aborting can remove a true pair. The probability of a common filter is unconditional on completing the traversal.

Depth-first processing discards the overflowing table before either child. Store factor index ranges on the stack; keep one decoder and at most $S+1$ records. A filter identifier fits in an index word addressing the product code. Ordered tables with comparable word keys incur logarithmic overhead absorbed by the polynomial for exponential-size lists. The optional cap counts the charged operations (line 2); code generation and any additional application work need their own charges for an end-to-end cap. With both lists empty (19) has zero right-hand side even for positive $S$, while line 1 still draws the code. With $S=0$ its quotient is undefined. Return immediately for either empty list and allow constant dispatch cost. For nonempty input $S\ge1$ and setup is absorbed.

All probability guarantees quantify over a pair fixed before the batch coins. Lists may repeat indexed entries or depend on each other. Count records by occurrence and apply the desired predicate at line 13. Dependencies among different pairs do not affect linearity or a union bound. Independent repetitions refresh both rotation and codebooks. Reusing a realized code for a pair selected from its observed failures has no such guarantee. A batch needs only one shared rotation per repetition, essential for matching filter IDs.

At half threshold and limiting target correlation $1/2$, the wedge base is $2/3$; at $1/2-1/\ell$ its log differs by $O(1/\ell)$. The base increases with correlation. P′ sizes the code at the lower endpoint and its $d^{2/3}$ log slack eventually dominates $H_d$. Thus the filter-count exponent is $\tfrac12\log_2(3/2)$; repetitions suppress a fixed-pair miss to $e^{-Cn}$ for any fixed $C$ eventually. This exponent for *total work* still requires bounds on the input-dependent sum of wedge masses. For example, repeating a common direction on both sides gives cap-sized joint mass for every indexed pair.

BDGL already derive fixed-pair product-code coverage using subwedges and rotation concentration. Their heuristic sieve exponent additionally models the evolving list. GFH supply uniform margins, restricted decoding and a space-bounded traversal; none of those steps supplies the lattice/list statistics needed elsewhere in stage 3. [BDGL, Theorem 5.1 and Appendices B–C](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/23692/23692.pdf)

## Formal boundary

`SigabaNeo.Lattices.Search.productCodeMiss_le` proves the bound $m q^B$ on a fixed probability space from measurable within-block independent failure events, individual failure probability at most $q$, and containment of the global miss event. Apply it at a fixed regular rotation with $q=1-w$. The Lean statement leaves the Haar law, wedge estimates, geometric containment, integration over rotations, and decoder correctness and cost outside the formalization. The real-RAM estimates give no finite-precision implementation. The target build reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` for this theorem.
