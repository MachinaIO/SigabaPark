title: Exact and approximate consistency guards for repeated witness reads

Consider a fixed order of h binary occurrences of witness variables. We want a matrix product on independent branch choices whose scalar output suppresses inconsistent choices before the terminal message is encoded.

**Exact cost for the guard alone.** At a cut let l be the number of variables occurring on both sides. Store their first-read values as a partial assignment; compare a reread, discard a mismatched path by a zero transition, and forget values after the final occurrence. Each branch matrix is a rectangular partial deterministic transition, with a single initial and final state. The cut width is exactly 2^l for this implementation. It is optimal for exact 0/1 products over Z/(2^k): fix occurrences of noncrossing variables to zero, and on each side repeat the assignment to its l crossing variables consistently. The prefix-suffix matrix of guard values is the identity of size 2^l. Factorization through the cut followed by reduction modulo two forces at least this width. Tensoring this guard with a bounded-width verifier multiplies their widths and gives exact zero on inconsistent paths. Variable order matters; the lower bound concerns the guard separately, and a verifier may itself kill some of the inconsistent paths.

**Polynomial-width approximate guard for every order.** Form a binary matrix H with a row comparing each repeated occurrence to its first occurrence. Then Hx=0 precisely on consistent branch strings x, and there are s=h-(number of used variables) rows. Sample D independent uniform strings u_j in F2^s. Give coordinate j at occurrence i and branch b the diagonal sign (-1)^(b u_j^T H_i), where H_i is column i. Initialize and terminate with all-ones vectors. The integer product is

    g(x) = sum_j (-1)^(u_j^T Hx).

Every consistent path has g=D exactly. For a nonzero syndrome the summands are independent fair signs. Thus D >= 2 ((s+1) ln(2) + ln(1/delta))/epsilon² ensures |g|<=epsilon D simultaneously on all inconsistent paths except with probability delta over the compiler coins, by the two-sided Hoeffding bound and a union bound over syndromes. No checking loop over paths is required. These are the parity biases studied by [Naor and Naor](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~naor/PAPERS/bias_abs.html); the bound here uses direct independent sampling. One can choose D odd by rounding up. The program has width D, h layers, and two sign changes per syndrome row suffice to generate the public signs for each coordinate by accumulating at its two occurrence positions. Tensoring with a verifier of scalar output 0 or 1 yields a score of D on a consistent accepting path, zero on a rejecting path, and absolute score at most epsilon D on an inconsistent path on the good event. With q>2D the signed scores have unambiguous lifts. This is a polynomial-size threshold separation for inverse-polynomial epsilon and polynomial log(1/delta), independent of live-variable count.

**Why the current WE endpoint still fails.** For odd D, every score of the guard is odd, including on inconsistent paths. It is a unit modulo every dyadic q. Attach the zero-versus-uniform scalar terminal key of the preceding note by multiplication. Even an inconsistent accepting path of small score multiplies the uniform key to a uniform residue, provided the terminal draw is independent of the guard. On the two-read example one can select (0,1) with scalar verifier output one. If centered product error is at most B except with probability beta per message on this fixed path (including abort), the same threshold test gives gap at least 1-2 beta-(4B+1)/q. Choosing an even D only changes the valuation issue: for any fixed nonzero score a with valuation v<k, a uniform key yields uniform mass on the 2^(k-v) multiples of 2^v, so any single output has probability 2^(-(k-v)). Small real amplitude does not give small modular leakage.

For the noiseless linear endpoint this yields a distributional obstruction even if one changes the two terminal-key laws: multiplication by an odd score preserves their total variation exactly, and with known score its inverse also preserves efficient distinguishability up to the modular inversion cost. To benefit from the approximate guard one would need an encoding that hides the joint view of nonzero low scores while decoding the high score. Publicly rounding only the final decoder output leaves the unrounded public path products available. No such joint-view encoding is supplied here.

**Even-score lower bound with an efficient witness.** More generally suppose every diagonal score is a fixed a with v=v2(a)<k. At a cut of width w<2^l choose any w+1 distinct crossing assignments and evaluate their square of prefix/suffix scores. Some off-diagonal score b must satisfy v2(b)<=v (give zero infinite valuation). Otherwise modulo 2^(v+1) the square is 2^v times the identity. Its image has 2^(w+1) elements killed by two. Yet the image is contained in the image of the left factor with only w columns; by Smith form that containing module is a direct sum of at most w cyclic groups, with at most 2^w elements killed by two. This contradiction locates an offending entry in at most (w+1)^2 public evaluations. Since a=c b modulo q has an efficiently computable solution c, an observer of the noiseless scalar b K can reconstruct a K for *any* terminal key law. This rules out repairing the isolated guard with a different even score or key distribution at subexponential width in l when all such mixed paths reach that scalar interface. It does not cover verifiers that reject the offending mixed paths, nonpublic transition data, or noise amplified by c.
