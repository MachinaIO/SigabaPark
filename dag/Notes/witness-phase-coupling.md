The theorem counts complete honest random tapes producing an arbitrary decidable event. Write e for the permutation converting the seed of front zero into a seed of front one with exactly the same public front. The phase in either world can be any function of the corresponding seed. For each coordinate choose a dual vector v_i with R_i(v_i)=0 and T_i(v_i)=1. Translate the i-th private mask by (h₀(x)_i−h₁(e x)_i)v_i. This preserves every R_i, sets every transmitted h_i+T_i(mask) equal, and is invertible together with x ↦ e(x). Its restriction to any observable event yields equal finite cardinalities. Finite uniform sampling therefore gives identical joint laws, including for tests comparing several fields. The theorem does not assume the phase is independent of the front or require a simulator to sample it under somebody else's secret. The algebra is over an arbitrary field, with the two-bit application taking F₂.

The 318-bit fixed-header example supplies the front permutation by fixing s₀ and the noise, then varying its ten uniform header coordinates and dedicated private mask coordinates. In native order the four outer header coordinates giving the four feedback bits are unchanged, and adding four selected top-message shifts to the independent inner coordinates is invertible. For each resulting header add ten fixed offsets to ten independent uniform private coordinates. This is paddedFront. Composing the payload-zero front normalizer with the inverse of the payload-one normalizer gives the permutation required by the theorem. The phase is obtained from the two sentinel headers and the noisy front's actual key. A false Mv=c supplies the required v_i by duality, so the theorem applies to the entire 60-bit front plus the 258-bit affine release. Conditioning on s₀ and the noise can then be removed because their law is equal across payloads.

For the test layout, write ten pairs of three-bit coordinates and three-bit bodies, least-significant bit first, then two 128-bit masks and their tags. Three thousand full serialized packets with random 128-bit witnesses, random consistent 12-equation systems and random two-bit Feistel round functions all decrypted. Every offset translation of a single dedicated secret pad was also enumerated at modulus eight. These checks exercise the host-level serializer and decryption. A parser for the registered NAND statement and a proof of its machine clocks remain open.
