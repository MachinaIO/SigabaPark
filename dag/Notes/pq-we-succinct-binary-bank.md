# Generating functions for implicit columns: sampler and public unit recovery

Let M have r binary rows and N=2^n columns. Assume public field evaluation of the vector G(x)=Σ_j M[:,j] x^j in K=GF(2^s). This is a substantially stronger interface than individual-column evaluation. This note derives both an issuance algorithm and the limitation of giving its evaluator to the public.

## Issuance without expanding N entries

Use independent private uniform t,z,v,h in K for one lane. Set U_j=Tr(zt^j), V_j=Tr(hv^j). Each sequence has bias at most ε=(N−1)/|K| against every nonzero linear parity: average first over z, then count roots of the nonzero coefficient polynomial. This is the standard small-bias field construction; see Alon, Goldreich, Håstad and Peralta (https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.3240030308). The measured linear columns are Tr(zG(t)) and Tr(hG(v)), entrywise. The measured product column is the sum over i=0,...,s−1 of Tr(zh^(2^i) G(t v^(2^i))). This follows by expanding the second trace. Thus s+2 vector evaluations replace enumeration. Use independent seeds for each of k lanes; sum the product columns and apply the share shift with G(1)=M1.

For any Walsh character of the three r-bit outputs its sign is of the form (−1)^(aU+bV+U·DV), D diagonal. Replacing V by uniform costs at most ε in expectation conditional on U. Averaging the resulting exact uniform V leaves a signed affine-subspace indicator in U, namely (−1)^(aU) times [DU=b]. It has Fourier coefficient absolute sum at most one by the character expansion of the affine constraints. Replacing U costs at most ε more. Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz give one-lane total variation at most ε sqrt(2^(3r)−1); the tensor hybrid and deterministic postprocessing give k times this bound for the whole share packet. This bound is for a fixed matrix independent of the private seeds and for this public view only. Correlated side information must be included in a new bound. Real and ideal unit decoding are exact because each uses its own internally consistent U,V.

Example: index 128-bit columns by w and give them the 129 coordinates [w≠0],w_0,...,w_127. All columns are distinct. The generating functions use products of (1+t^(2^i)) and prefix/suffix products, including at t=1, without division. For s=571,k=65 the abstract sampling distance is below 2^-243 using 148460 fair bits and 37245 vector evaluations, plus G(1) if not precomputed. The Python check uses the small field F8 to verify equality of the two algorithms, not this security parameter or a tape cost. This example illustrates succinct evaluation only and encodes no arbitrary SAT instance.

## Public evaluation also gives a public test

Assume alpha in K has multiplicative order at least N. On polynomials of degree <N the dilation T:f(X)↦f(alpha X) has distinct eigenvalues alpha^j and corresponding eigenvectors X^j. Form V=span_K of the row polynomials. Starting with W=V, replace W by {p in W : T p lies in W} until stable, in at most r strict decreases. The stable subspace is the largest invariant subspace contained in V. It is spanned by exactly the monomials in V: projection onto each eigenspace is a polynomial in T by Lagrange interpolation, so an invariant space contains each individual monomial in each member's support. For the proof the interpolation polynomial can be huge, but computing the stable subspace needs only r rounds of linear algebra. Extension of scalars preserves membership of a binary coordinate unit in a binary row space (or apply a binary linear functional on K mapping 1 to 1). Its dimension therefore *counts* the Boolean unit vectors in the original row space.

Implement without coefficient enumeration. Draw 2r independent uniform x_i. Compute both G(x_i) and G(alpha x_i). With probability at least 1−2r(N−1)/|K|, evaluation embeds the entire space V+TV faithfully. Indeed, if its dimension is h≤2r, the determinant of an h-point evaluation matrix for a polynomial basis is nonzero as a polynomial in the h independent coordinates (linearly independent polynomials admit some full-rank evaluation when |K|≥N). Its total degree is at most h(N−1). Apply Schwartz–Zippel once; the same embedding suffices for all adaptive subspaces since they lie in this fixed sum. Keep original row-basis coefficients throughout elimination to evaluate T on later W using the second sample matrix. The operator is injective, so on a stable space it has a square matrix. A nonempty stable space yields an eigenvector by factoring its square-free split characteristic polynomial over the finite field and solving a linear system. The represented polynomial is cX^j. Normalize by c=P(1) via G(1), then apply a binary linear functional mapping 1 to 1 to each coefficient of its representation in the original rows. This gives binary coefficients yielding the desired unit. Recover the corresponding measured columns without identifying j or computing any discrete logarithm.

For r=129 the index test needs at most 516 vector evaluations, plus G(1) for normalization. At N=2^128, s=571, its embedding failure is below 2^-434. These are field-query counts, not gate or tape costs. Honest generation and unit discovery both benefit from the same public G. The result rules out treating the implicit exponent count as a hardness argument for this interface, subject to charging the actual extraction algorithm against the security budget. It says nothing comparable for a public interface that only evaluates M[:,w] at a presented witness and provides no generating-function oracle. For example, if a row is the truth predicate of a circuit, testing whether G is nonzero by polynomial identity testing already decides satisfiability with small one-sided error. Neither a generic efficient generating-function evaluator nor full WE follows here.

## Degrees, recovery, and oracle requirements

For substitution of the first biased mask, the Fourier coefficient of a nonzero parity differs from the ideal zero by at most epsilon. Substitution in a zero parity incurs no error. The indicator of a consistent affine system is the average of the signed parities of its defining row span, and is zero for an inconsistent system. Multiplication by a parity permutes these coefficients. Thus the second substitution costs at most epsilon as well. Each nontrivial output character has absolute difference at most 2 epsilon; Parseval in normalized counting convention has a factor 1/2 for total variation. For k independent lanes this gives the stated bound before summing product columns. For comparisons of two real bit worlds, both real-to-ideal errors must be charged.

The dilation algorithm uses evaluation of the polynomials represented by the original row list. Preserve a row-combination representation in every intersection. Once a step is stationary the preimage condition implies invariance. Every invariant subspace was contained at every step. To isolate exponent j in an invariant subspace multiply the diagonal operator factors (T-alpha^e I) for e other than j. They preserve the subspace; on a vector with nonzero j coefficient only that coordinate survives. This argument proves existence and is not an algorithm charging N projections. The corresponding two abstract facts and the trace-product identity are Lean-verified.

One may choose alpha uniformly from the field (zero counts as a failure). The extra failure probability is at most (1+N(N-1)/2)/q because every element of order below N is a root of X^d-1 for some 1<=d<N. A guaranteed high-order element avoids this loss. A nonempty stable space can be split without factoring: in a field basis beta_1,...,beta_s, take the operators H_l = sum_{i<s} (beta_l T)^(2^i). On eigenvalue a, H_l acts by the binary trace Tr(beta_l a). The trace pairing is nondegenerate, so after iteratively retaining a nonzero 0- or 1-eigensubspace all surviving eigenvalues are equal. On the true monomial space they are simple. Precompute the s repeated matrix squares of T, and form s linear combinations; straightforward elimination in the restricted spaces is polynomial in r and s. For binary recovery normalize the represented polynomial at 1 first, then project its (possibly extension-field) row coefficients by any binary linear map sending 1 to 1. All masked lane outputs use the original binary rows. Query counts omit bit-cost for the field representation, scalar arithmetic, evaluating G, linear algebra, parsing and tape movement.

### Independent random multiplicative triples suffice

Suppose a sample gives G(x), G(y), G(xy) for independent uniform x,y. Neither a fixed dilation oracle nor caller-selected x,y is needed for the dimension test. For the row polynomial space V form the formal linear diagonal substitution Delta P=P(XY). Then

    U = {P in V : Delta P belongs to V(X) tensor V(Y)}

is exactly the span of the monomials in V. Indeed the X^j coefficient slice of a tensor decomposition lies in V(Y); for Delta P this slice is p_j Y^j. Conversely every supported unit contributes its own simple tensor. The slice implication has a Lean proof. The dimension of the space spanned by the r diagonal rows and the r^2 pairwise product rows is at most H=r+r^2. Evaluate at H independently sampled pairs. A full-rank basis determinant, nonzero as a polynomial in the independent sample variables, has total degree at most 2H(N-1). Evaluation faithfully embeds that fixed space except with probability at most 2H(N-1)/q, assuming q>=N. Compute the intersection and retain coefficients in the row space by ordinary elimination (discard dependent original rows). Each sample costs three vector values. On the good event one recovers the tensor coefficients of Delta on U in a basis of U tensor U. Contracting one factor with evaluation at a fresh x produces a diagonal endomorphism with eigenvalues x^j; for at most r supported exponents, collision probability is at most binom(r,2)*(N-1)/(q-1) for uniform nonzero x. With distinct eigenvalues, the trace-coordinate splitting procedure recovers a supported exponent's row combination. Use G(1) to normalize, or a separately verified normalization. Dimension testing by itself needs neither fresh x nor normalization. Single-point samples or local column queries alone do not implement this triple oracle.

Choosing issuer coins is another route to recovering full field values: if a public program accepts directly selectable independent t,z and exposes all Tr(zG(t)) coordinates, holding t fixed and cycling a binary basis of z gives G(t) by the nondegenerate trace pairing. An API that merely omits G while retaining this ability does not remove the dilation test. See the accompanying quadratic-packet label test for a restriction that persists even when these direct samples are unavailable.
