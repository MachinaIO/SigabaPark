Let q=dp with p dividing d. In the negacyclic ring over Z/q, write the coefficientwise uniform secret as s=a+dt with independent low digits a modulo d and t uniform modulo p. Since d squared is zero modulo q, Taylor expansion gives h(s)=h(a)+(s-a)h'(a) for every public polynomial in the ring argument. This works for h=-g S f(S) and h=g f(S), the top and bottom ring GSW phases of a desired plaintext f(s).

For each independent ordinary Ring-LWE challenge pair (A,B) modulo p, sample H uniformly among lifts of A modulo p. Compute the body aH+dB+U with independent centered U uniform modulo d in every coefficient. A real challenge B=tA+E yields sH+dE+U, because d times a multiple of p vanishes. For fixed s, H is uniform and independent of fresh e=dE+U. Put L=h'(a) and K=h(a)-aL, and publish (H-L,aH+dB+U+K). Its phase is e+h(s). Conditional on s the source header translates bijectively to the appropriate native column coins, including the top column's gadget shift. Fresh independent per-column coins therefore give the full joint native law for the stated lifted error distribution. For an ideal challenge, dB+U is uniform modulo q at every coefficient, independently of the lifted header conditional on a. Translations use only a and public inputs. Thus the entire ideal public matrix is independent uniform. This is a single ordinary Ring-LWE challenge with all columns counted.

The source parameters are dimension 1024, twenty samples, modulus 2^19, uniform secret and CBD(1) errors. At q=2^38 and d=p=2^19, each lifted error coefficient is between -786432 and 786431. Radix 16 requires ten digit columns per side, giving 1556480 raw matrix bits before framing. The registered estimator returns 158 bits for the source. These figures do not price a registered sampler or encode the usual CBD law at q.

For binary nearest rounding, a zero plaintext with constant perturbation one is represented by the right operand (0,1). Its radix digits select exactly the unit bottom column. With an encrypted full uniform secret s the resulting phase is s+e. At each fixed coefficient, uniform s and independent e give a uniform residue modulo q. A half-turn-compatible binary decoder partitions residues into pairs separated by q/2, with opposite decoded bits. The probability of zero is exactly one half. This diagnostic applies to this chosen valid small-noise operand; a separate correctness theorem would be needed for a specified random right-operand sampler. Also, an exact Boolean phase obtainable from a known affine header translation is constant modulo d on each a-coset. For d>1 it cannot extract a bit that varies within that coset.

Small negacyclic rings independently reproduced 7506 conditional secret/message/gadget/side cases. The Taylor reduction supplies full-secret polynomial plaintexts. It gives neither a high-digit selector nor a witness-conditioned release. Full external-product correctness, a registered clock and transition description, and a complete capped WE construction remain open.
