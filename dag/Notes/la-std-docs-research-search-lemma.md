# Indexed shell search at the sieve exponent

The counterexample at cosine 0.45 refutes Hypothesis P in §3.9. It does not apply to DL26. The repair below specializes an existing search theorem. The lattice input enters its running-time application through Lemmas H and I.

## What the papers require

DL26, Appendix A.1, Lemma 19(ii), says “Every fixed pair with correlation $\zeta\in[1/2-1/\ell,2/3]$ shares a filter except with probability $\exp(-\Omega(\log^2 n))$.” The larger interval in (50) controls the cap integral approximation. Equations (59)–(60) size the code using the **lower endpoint of the recall interval**. Appendix A.2 retains occurrence indices and applies exact geometric tests. Thus the repair already appears in DL26 itself. [DL26 v1, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37482v1#A1)

GFH Theorem 4.2(ii) states “Every codeword is marginally uniform”. Its two expectation identities concern the numbers of incident and shared codewords. Part (iii), equation (17), requires $M W_d(\tau,\alpha)\ge 2^{H_d}$, with $H_d=O_\delta(\sqrt d\,\mathrm{polylog}\,d)$ on the admissible parameter box. Algorithm 1 and Proposition 4.3 stream shared-filter pairs with repetitions, subject to the optional abort budget. Corollary 4.4 combines traversal and fixed-pair coverage. GFH does not promise coverage based on membership in the parameter box alone. [GFH, §§4.2–4.3 and Appendix D](https://eprint.iacr.asia/2026/1844.pdf)

## P′ and proof

Fix two indexed lists of nonzero vectors of lengths at most $M$, independently of the **fresh search coins**. The lists may depend arbitrarily on each other, and values may repeat. Use the randomized real-RAM with exact arithmetic, comparisons, square roots and independent standard normal draws (equivalently for this construction, efficient exact Haar rotation and spherical samples). Space counts words. Let $c_0=1/2-1/\ell$, $\ell=\lceil\ln n\rceil$. For fixed lists, a batch search streams candidates with the following guarantees.

* Each required pair with cosine in $[c_0,2/3]$ is visited with miss probability at most $e^{-Cn}$ for any fixed $C$ and all sufficiently large $n$. Apply the sieve predicate to the original vectors.
* With $F=(3/2)^{n/2+o(n)}$ implicit filters per code, expected candidate **multiplicity** for any indexed pair is at most $nF K(x,y)$. Expected total incidence count is at most $2MnF C_n$.
* Including traversal and setup, expected time is at most $2^{o(n)}[M+MF C_n+F\sum_{ij} K(x_i,y_j)]$ for nonempty lists. Working space is $(M+1)2^{o(n)}$, excluding any retained output. These are batch bounds. Individual vectors need no worst-case incidence cap.

For the construction pad by zeros to $D=kb=n+O(\log n)$ with $k=\lceil\log_2 D\rceil$ (round up to a multiple, adjusting $k$ if a power of two is crossed). In each of $k$ independent blocks draw $B$ independent directions on the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^b$. Scale blocks by $1/\sqrt k$, take their implicit Cartesian product and apply one independent Haar rotation. Gaussian normalization and QR with positive diagonal implement the draws at polynomial cost per sample. Write $W_D(c)$ for the half-threshold wedge at cosine $c$. Choose

$$ B=\left\lceil [2^{2D^{2/3}}/W_D(c_0)]^{1/k}\right\rceil,\qquad F=B^k. $$

Uniformly on $[2/5,2/3]$, GFH Lemma D.1 gives $\log W_D(c)= (D/2)\log(1-1/[2(1+c)])+O(\log D)$. The derivative of the log base is bounded there. Hence $\log F=(n/2)\log(3/2)+O(n/\ell+D^{2/3}+\log n)$ and $B=2^{O(n/\log n)}$. To implement the choice without a wedge oracle one may substitute the explicit base to power $D/2$ for $W_D(c_0)$: the polynomial discrepancy leaves more than $2^{D^{2/3}}$ saturation slack eventually. Integer powers and comparisons suffice to round the block size using polynomial work (round the slack exponent up).

Wedges increase with correlation: in bisector and difference coordinates the two inequalities read $\sqrt{(1+c)/2}\,u\ge 1/2+\sqrt{(1-c)/2}\,|v|$. For $c\in[c_0,2/3]$, GFH's margins hold with $\delta=1/8$ for large $n$, since $\tau=1/2$, $\tau(1-c)\ge1/6$, and the wedge base is at least $9/14$. Consequently $FW_D(c)\ge2^{H_D}$. GFH Appendix D proves this criterion by concentrating the block Gram matrices after rotation. Except with probability $\exp(-\Omega(\log^2 D))$, each block has a subwedge of mass at least $W_D(c)^{1/k}2^{-O(\sqrt D\,\mathrm{polylog} D)}$ whose products lie in the global wedge. Given the rotation, missing any block costs at most $k e^{-D^3}$ with the chosen slack. Thus one code misses with probability at most the sum of these two bounds. Run $n$ fresh codes sequentially to obtain the stated exponential guarantee.

Marginal uniformity and linearity count every report, even from dependent filters. Project a uniform padded direction onto the input space. Its direction is uniform and its length at most one, so satisfying both positive thresholds in padded space implies satisfying both in the original space. This proves the upper bounds for **all** correlations, including degenerate ones.

Use one label and depth-first GFH Algorithm 1 with incidence budget $M$. Stream each point's accepted codewords within a Cartesian subcode via Theorem 4.2(i) at polynomial cost times $B$ plus output size. Store the first list's incidences; on overflow split a factor in half and retry both children. Otherwise probe with the second list. Each filter belongs to one leaf. At any depth, overflowing cells each consume more than $M$ disjoint incidences, so their number is at most $Z_A/M$, where $Z_A$ counts first-list incidences. Depth is $O(\log F)$. Summing the point initializations over these cells and their children costs $\mathrm{poly}(n)B(M+Z_A)$; redecoded incidences cost $\mathrm{poly}(n)(Z_A+Z_B)$ and leaf cross products are the candidate multiplicities above. Empty lists return immediately. The stack stores factor ranges and one live table. Use occurrence indices as payload and apply the predicate after streaming, including the appropriate zero-difference rejection. Distinct directions were never required for decoding or the split count. This proves P′ using the cited GFH decoder.

## From indexed reports to Theorem J

The shell law of cosines gives forward cosine $1-\gamma^2/2+O(w)$ and reverse cosine $\gamma/2+O(w)$ with $w=O(n^{-2})$, $\gamma=1-1/\ell$. Both exceed $c_0$ eventually. On the terminal minimum-length event of the audit, all nonzero catalogue differences in range have cosines between the cutoff-derived lower bound of §3.9 and $5/8+o(1)$. For exact SVP alone the sought shortest pair has cosine $5/8+o(1)$. Completeness is needed only on these predicates. Expose the ideal exhaustive transcript and its coins first, then couple the filtered computation to it until first disagreement. Across subexponentially many batches there are at most $(4/3)^{n+o(n)}$ indexed tests. The union bound gives exponentially small miss probability. Per mover, keep distinct verified indices in an ordered set, saturating at one above its rejection threshold. Both thresholds are $2^{o(n)}$ by the lens and list-size parameters of §§3.2–3.4. Sort indices for the same uniform-choice coupling. At termination a shortest-vector scan can ignore repetitions; for a catalogue keep distinct output values subject to the catalogue size bound.

On the good shell-statistics event, charge forward and terminal cross products by Lemma H for the two independent ideal lists. For reverse work apply Lemma I's proposal bound *before* averaging over the bank and use fresh codes after fixing proposals. For $j'\ne j$ its displayed calculation applies to expected multiplicity with kernel $nF K$, yielding $2^{o(n)}M^2F C_n^2$. For the generating index $j'=j$, §3.9's “at most one” only bounds distinct indices. There is at most one such index per proposed query and its expected multiplicity is at most $nF C_n$, even conditional on the proposal. All generating indices together cost at most $MnF C_n$. Incidences and cell initializations also fit, since $MC_n=2^{o(n)}$ and $M<F$. Thus the total expected charge on the ideal transcript, including all merges, is $F2^{o(n)}$. Coupling controls the actual algorithm up to first miss; use an online work cap to bound the exceptional executions as well. Markov with a factor $e^{n/\ell}$ over the established expectation gives timeout probability at most $e^{-n/\ell}$. This argument needs no conditional expectation bound after a miss.

The capped time exponent is $\tfrac12\log_2(3/2)=0.29248125036\ldots$, with $o(1)$ added, and the space exponent is $\tfrac12\log_2(4/3)+o(1)$. This recovers the search step under Theorem J's static lattice and sampling hypotheses, including the audited q-ary applications where their counterparts to H and I hold, in lattice dimension. It neither establishes those hypotheses for other ensembles nor supplies a bit-model implementation. The formal result in Reduction/Search.lean is the finite-transcript union bound. The construction and running-time analysis above are paper proofs.
