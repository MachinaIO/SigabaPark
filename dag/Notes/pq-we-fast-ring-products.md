[PR 152](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/152) uses K=2048 independent seed bits per wire root, N=512, q=256 and the packed response As+e. Each secret and error coefficient is the difference of two bits. This note changes only arithmetic inside an honest expansion. Public A, every output byte for every seed, and all challenge distributions are preserved. Independently implemented reference kernels, differential tests, and benchmark code are in the companion replay note.

### A dyadic transform with exact division

Let N=LM, L a power of two dividing M, D=2L and Q=256D. Group coefficients by residue modulo L, so z represents X and y=X^L lies in S=(Z/Q)[y]/(y^M+1). Two degree-<L polynomials in z have a product of degree <2L-1, which can be computed as a cyclic length-D convolution without wrap in z. Multiply each frequency in S and fold the high block z^L to y at the end. Thus a wrapped y shift has a minus sign, including at the last coefficient. A D-th root for the block transform is w=y^(M/L), where w^L=-1 and w^D=1.

For 0<k<D the sum of (w^k)^j, j=0,...,D-1, is zero. Split a doubled sum as (1+x^m) times its first half. For odd k the multiplying factor is zero. For even k=2a the induction hypothesis applied to w² kills the first half. This proof uses no inversion of D, and applies even to rings with zero divisors. The Lean theorem orthogonality verifies all nonzero frequencies for arbitrary dyadic orders. Hence the unnormalized inverse gives D times each coefficient. Knowing a multiple of D modulo 256D suffices to divide by D and recover the desired residue modulo 256. An unlifted modulus would lose log₂D bits in this step.

There is also an exact local normalization. Work initially over the integer negacyclic ring, where w is still a monomial root. Write the true unwrapped block product as F_t and its spectrum as H_k. At inverse recursion size m=2^j, the child with frequency-index offset r transforms H_(r+hD/m), h=0,...,m-1. The normalized output at position i is the sum of F_t w^(rt) over t congruent to i modulo m. Expand the DFT and use the character sum above to verify this expression after multiplying by m; coefficientwise integer equality then allows cancellation. Thus the two terms of each inverse butterfly have an even sum and an even difference after applying its twiddle. The local normalized butterfly divides these outputs by two. If inputs are already reduced modulo 2p, ((2t mod 2p) div 2)=t mod p, including negative t. The Lean signed-decoding lemma proves this last step. Inputs at stage j have precision 8+log₂D-j+1 bits, outputs one less. This property applies to actual multiplication spectra. It need not hold on arbitrary random vectors. If the small negacyclic products are recursively evaluated by another lifted transform, its precision increment must be added on top of the current outer precision before its own divisions. The replay uses exact packed small products instead.

For L=16,M=32 the zero-padded forward ternary transform has an initial (s_i,0) level: its two outputs can alias the one input. At the next four levels each coefficient is a signed sum of at most 2,4,8,16 ternary inputs, respectively. Signed widths 3,4,5,6 suffice and the forward result can be interpreted in S at the point multiplication. For each of the 32 spectra choose the public representative in [0,8191], offset the private vector of coefficients by +16 to [0,32], pack as radix 2^23 integers and multiply. Ordinary convolution coefficients are bounded by 32·8191·32 < 2^23. Subtract the upper digit from its lower partner, then subtract 16 times the precomputed public vector times the all-ones polynomial. The j-th coefficient of that correction polynomial before scaling is twice the inclusive prefix sum minus the total sum. Reduce modulo 8192. The 736-bit packed operands and their products are real costs. The shared public packed factors and correction vectors can be precomputed per machine. If all 32 degree-32 products instead use separately written 13-bit accumulators for each of their 32² elementary MACs, the accumulator writes alone cost 425,984 bit writes per expansion.

### Exact Fermat-prime transform without changing the challenge

Represent public bytes as integers in [-128,127]. Toggling the top bit sends each a_i to a_i+128 if negative and a_i-128 otherwise. The two absolute values sum to 128. Choose B to be the representative (original or all bytes toggled) with smaller ℓ₁ norm, choosing the original on ties. The two vector norms sum to 65,536. Consequently ||B||₁≤32,768. For any ternary s, every *signed negacyclic* integer coefficient of Bs is in [-32,768,32,768]. Lean checks the general bound 2|(Bs)_i|≤128N using the core negacyclic ℓ₁–ℓ∞ inequality.

Use p=65,537 and a primitive 1024-th root r=3^64 mod p, with r^512=-1. Twist by r^j, do the length-512 cyclic transform with w=r², multiply spectra, inverse-transform and untwist, including the inverse-512 factor. Centering each residue returns the precise integer coefficient of Bs, including both endpoints. In Z/256 the difference between A and B, on the toggled branch, is the constant-coefficient vector 128J, J=1+...+X^511. Every shift of 128J equals 128J because its negation is itself. The correction at every output position is 128 times the parity of the sum of s. Accumulate this single parity bit while decoding s, and toggle the output MSBs when both it and the public flag are set. Add e *after* exact multiplication; the norm estimate need not hold after adding e. The public matrix remains uniform; no restriction of its distribution, acceptance loss, second prime, binary convolution or tail probability is needed. The theorem public_matrix_recovery proves the full modular correction with the core multiplication on both sides for every flag, A, s and output index. A single small prime applied without folding and correction can wrap, even though a distributional tail analysis can sometimes bound its probability.

The benchmark uses a radix-two NTT. Multiplication by a power of 2 modulo the Fermat prime is a signed shift with a wrap correction, not a free tape rotation. The subgroup generated by 2 has order 32, so most 1024-th roots are outside it. A different mixed-radix layout with time index a+32b and frequency c+16e does 32 inner twisted transforms of size 16 and 16 outer cyclic transforms of size 32; their internal twiddles are powers of two. The intermediate diagonal is r^((2c+1)a). There are 32 exponent classes modulo 32, each occurring 16 times; a=0 gives the 16 literal identities. A model in which signed shifts are free and each constant has its own addition chain gives class costs 0,1,2,3,4 with multiplicities 1,1,1,15,14, yielding 1664 additions per intermediate diagonal. The cruder independent-signed-shifts method takes 2160. These counts omit rotation, carries, signed reductions, memory movement, the point products with the A-dependent spectrum, and the two small-transform families. They do not give canonical machine ticks. Public A can absorb fixed factors in pointwise multipliers only if the adjoining transforms and final scale are adjusted consistently.

### Explicit serial cost screens

Use the same four-row, independent, full-width wire-pad forest as PR 152: G=4096, I=128. The issuer needs at least 24,320 expansions. For the selected evaluator two K-bit pads per gate and at most I+G active roots give at least G-I=3968 more, for 28,288 products total. This upper bound on available roots includes even unused ones. In addition, a separately serialized four-row table of one (K+1)-bit record per cell costs 33,570,816 output bits. These disjoint passes leave at most 150,643 serial ticks per product *on average* under a strict 2^32 total, before the rest of WE. The direct pair-visiting double loop would require 7,415,529,472 visits. Using shortened on-wire keys would require a separately proved seed encoding, revised trie capacity, target strength and concrete losses.

* **Dyadic serial passes.** Ordinary full-width stores after five forward and five inverse stages at 1024 13-bit coefficients consume 133,120 writes before the pointwise products. A more economical schedule writes the four small forward levels (18,432 bits), the 32 point products (13,312), the first four inverse levels at widths 12,11,10,9 (43,008), and the fused final folded output (4096). This counts 78,848 serial writes, including the point outputs. Fold can compute the two last valid half-butterfly results and add a one-position signed rotation, omitting an intermediate output plane; it must still handle the boundary and access the shifted coefficient. The lower ledger with the final table is 2,264,023,040, leaving at most 71,795 ticks per product on average for *all other costs*, about 2243 per small product before any other work. Substituting the separately written schoolbook MACs gives 14,314,258,432. Packed integer multiplication evades this MAC floor, but no certified 736-bit multiplier and tape-layout clock fitting that remaining aggregate are supplied here.
* **Fermat serial passes.** If every coefficient is stored in a uniform w-bit word after all 9 forward stages, the first 8 inverse stages, and point multiplication, and the last inverse stage directly emits 8 output bits, there are (17+1)·512w+4096 separately written bits per call. Even before twiddles, at w=17 the combined ledger is 4,581,376,000; at w=16 it is 4,320,673,792 (the latter would require a different prime or representation). These are lower bounds only for these explicit passes. At 65537 the first local *twisted* length-2,4,8 transforms on ternary inputs may use 10,14,16 signed bits: their coefficient magnitudes are bounded by 257,4,369,21,845, because their local roots are signed powers of 2. This requires a negative-transform schedule with the twist pushed to the recursion. Omitting the first stage stores by recomputation and using the smaller next two levels reduces the tally by 10,752 bits per call. The remaining ledger is 4,277,223,424, with just 627 ticks per product for everything else. A separate scan of K input bits for each product already exceeds this remainder. Fusing scans with writes or moving a word on several heads can change that conclusion. The mixed-radix transposes and actual lookups also require accounting.
* **Multiple tapes.** The core encoding charges 6s·3^k·3^ops·(4+4k+4ops+size(ops)+size(s)) bits for s states and k Boolean work tapes. At zero oracle channels, 15 tracks and a single state already cost 5,596,073,730 description bits; even 14 tapes permit at most two states before the strict cap, or 13 at most seven. On eight tapes there is room for at most 2272 states from description alone. Thus one bit per work head per tick can be legitimate, but adding a separate track per 17-bit coefficient or a giant finite-state multiplication table invalidates a small description claim. Simultaneous input reads and work writes must not be added as if they were separate serial passes. Separate Enc and Dec cannot be credited for one another's private preprocessed tables; either rebuild locally or actually transmit and read a fixed representation.

For the single two-input normal carrier plus zero-header selector in PR 152, the physical example needs 10 issuer and 5 evaluator expansions, one shared 4096-bit public A, 8200 garbled cell bits and 6147 selected-opening bits when physically delivered. Both exact accelerators implement those expansions for all seeds. The optimized dyadic serial-write component for 15 calls is 1,182,720 bits, before local small products, extension tables, preprocessing and key transport. In CPython 3.12.3 on an x86-64 Ryzen 9 7950X3D, comparing the same 30 degree-512 products after setup, median online wall times were 0.903 ms (Fermat), 1.490 ms (dyadic with packed point products), and 17.145 ms (direct). Setup for the first two was 1.672 and 0.988 ms. These measurements include interpreter arithmetic and list movement; they do not count canonical steps or guarantee a time limit. The replay also checks 6762 locally divided products with a varied public operand and all ternary vectors at degrees 4 and 8. Original unnormalized-reference tests checked 365 products; separate Fermat tests checked the inclusive endpoint and both parity flags.

The full four-row WE component is excluded for the displayed serial full-store schedules at the strict exploratory threshold. The optimized packed/streaming variants, complete honest clock, recursive WE wrapper, and the registered 2^100 cap remain open. Faster arithmetic does not change Ring-LWE.H or discharge the cryptographic reduction.
