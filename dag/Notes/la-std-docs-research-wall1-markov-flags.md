# Two obstructions to mixing on integral flags

Fix a lattice and record the primitive sublattices spanned by successive basis prefixes. Two distinct issues arise before using a mixing estimate. The proposed transition must descend from bases to flags, and its reference law must be a probability on the state space where the estimate is used. Both issues already occur for the sole proper prefix in dimension two.

## Forgetting the basis can change the transition law

In the lattice with basis $(e_1,e_2)$, consider one random update of an ordered basis $(x,y)$. Keep $y$ and replace $x$ by $x+Sy$, where $S$ is a fair random sign. Each of the two updates has determinant one. Start alternatively from

$$
B=(e_1,e_2),\qquad C=(e_1,e_1+e_2).
$$

Both have prefix $\mathbb Z e_1$. From $B$ the next prefix is generated by $(1,1)$ or $(1,-1)$. From $C$ it is generated by $(2,1)$ or $(0,-1)$. The two supports are disjoint even after identifying opposite generators. Their total variation distance is exactly one. Hence no single transition kernel on prefixes describes this update for every initial basis. The same example applies to the full unoriented flag, since rank two has just one nontrivial proper rank. A specific initialization might still induce a Markov process. The calculation rules out strong lumpability, the property that all representatives of a state give the same next-state probabilities.

## A symmetric walk can lack a stationary probability

For comparison, consider a walk that *is* defined directly on primitive unoriented lines in $\mathbb Z^2$. In fixed ambient coordinates apply one of $U,U^{-1},V,V^{-1}$ with probability $1/4$ each, where $U(a,b)=(a+b,b)$ and $V(a,b)=(a,a+b)$. The kernel is symmetric because each move is paired with its inverse, counting multiplicities. It is irreducible. Repeated additions or subtractions of one coordinate from the other implement Euclidean division and reduce any primitive pair to a signed coordinate vector. The two coordinate lines communicate via $(1,0)\to(1,1)\to(0,1)$.

Suppose a stationary probability exists. Its point masses attain a positive maximum $M$, since only finitely many masses can exceed half their positive supremum. By symmetry and stationarity, a maximizing mass equals the average of the masses at its four neighbors. Each must therefore equal $M$. Irreducibility propagates this equality to every line, including the infinitely many distinct lines generated by $(j,1)$ for integer $j$. This contradicts total mass one.

Counting measure is invariant here. Every finite-time law from a deterministic start also has total variation distance one from the rotation-invariant probability on real unoriented lines: the countable set of rational lines has probability one under the walk and zero under the angular law. This argument concerns total variation; weak convergence is possible, but to the walk's Furstenberg stationary measure, which need not be the angular law.

## Scope for Wall 1

The first update acts on basis columns and the second acts on ambient coordinates. The second example therefore gives no stationarity conclusion for a reduction tour. Neither example excludes a stationary distribution for a specified tour with a metric-dependent reduction step. Even given such a distribution, transferring a fixed-prefix estimate would require control of the joint law of the prefix and the planted lattice. Part 9 gives concentration under a horizontal fixed-prefix hypothesis. Part 8 additionally requires a nonzero primitive projected plant and strict separation from every competing line for recovery of the projected vector up to sign. Mixing of the prefix marginal alone supplies none of these conditional estimates. The success probability and block-size threshold for the selected quotient remain open.
