## The zero predicate

Pin wire zero and the final output to true, and read each unavailable parent as true. A gate has first-trace parent bits a,b and complemented output t. The independently supplied second trace supplies its second parent u and complemented output s. On ordinary integer representatives put

    H = (a-2t-4b+2)u + (-2a-4t+4)s + 2b + 3t.

The five coordinates need not agree across traces. In the 32 possible local patterns, 0 ≤ H(a,b,t,b,t) ≤ H(a,b,t,u,s) ≤ 6. The diagonal value is zero exactly when t=ab. Summing therefore gives a value between 0 and 24576 at 4096 gates, with an attained zero iff the left trace can be the trace of an accepting input. In particular every zero's left trace is valid. Conversely copying any accepting trace attains zero. Per-trace wire coordinates must be reused at each occurrence. The bounds include duplicate and forward parents. TwoTraceEnergy.independentTrace gives the aggregate theorem for arbitrary port maps from a trace type. TwoTraceEnergy.registeredRun directly relates two independently supplied traces with all local costs zero to SigabaNeo.evalGates, including its initial and empty-gate conventions. Its existential right primary input may even have arbitrary length; the forward implication holds for arbitrary initial right wires, and the reverse uses the same left input. Nonnegative summands make the zero-run and sum tests equivalent for the pinned traces.

For real coordinates in the unit cubes, independently round whole trace coordinates to Boolean values with their specified expectations. Bi-affinity equates expected energy to the energy at the means, so every false-instance pair still has energy at least one. A 15-bit unsigned integer energy suffices on the honest cubes. With ordered affine coordinates [1,x],[1,y] each of length 4224 the energy is a single inner product [1,x] M [1,y]^T; only constantly many entries per gate are added. It can also be coefficient zero of a negacyclic product at dimension 8192 by lifting indices i to i and -i on the respective sides. These are clear computations, not public encrypted zero tests.

## Integral lifts and actual local squares

Let Q4 = H + 4 sum_i v_i(v_i-1), v=(a,b,t,u,s). Write v=c+d where c is its coordinatewise Boolean clamp and d points outwards. Every integer has this representation. The verified bound is 2(Q4(v)-H(c)) ≥ sum_i d_i^2. For the proof, the mixed term is au-2as-4bu-2tu-4ts in the displacements. Twice this expression plus seven times the sum of all squares is nonnegative by expanding squares along the five edges. At each clipped corner the gradient contribution plus four times the displacement's absolute value is nonnegative coordinatewise. Thus any non-Boolean used coordinate incurs a strict integral cost. Clamping commutes with complemented output wires. A tighter coefficient three passes an independent finite check on [-5,6]^5; outside this box the mixed block with Gram [[21,6],[6,20]] has norm ≤sqrt(27), yielding Q3-H(c) ≥ (3-sqrt(27)/2)||d||²-sqrt(5)||d|| > 0 for norm at least six. Coefficient two fails at five minus ones. Only the coefficient-four estimate is formalized here.

One exact geometric implementation charges the squares intrinsically. With mixed block M=((1,-2),(-4,0),(-2,-4)), create |Mij| rows e_i+sign(Mij)e_(3+j) for each nonzero entry. Pad each of the five column squared norms to eight with singleton rows. Shift by -1 on the a+u row and on respectively (3,2,1,1,0) singleton rows. The resulting 27-by-5 matrix D and shift h satisfy D^T h=(-4,-2,-1,-2,0), ||h||²=8 and ||Dv+h||²=8+2Q4(v) by exact Gram expansion. Concatenate gate blocks then substitute actual wire variables and pinned values: dimension 110592. The left and right affine lattices each have mutually orthogonal nonzero wire columns; same-side port columns are already pairwise orthogonal within each gate. An integer coefficient can be arbitrary in either lattice basis. Their squared minimum separation is exactly 32768 on YES and exactly 32770 on NO. To attain the latter, evaluate on any input and flip only the false final gate output to true on both traces, incurring the diagonal cost one there. This genuinely binds the squares to actual differences when the quantities entering the norm are lattice points with those coefficients. Permitting free ambient vectors, revealing a distance-dependent mask, testing modular norms without a lift, or taking the caller's claimed square as an additional coordinate implements another interface. No fixed registry distribution issues hidden distance-test capabilities for this pair of instance-dependent lattices in this argument.

## A static issuer from sign-switchable energy graphs

Assemble the constant, unary and mixed coefficients of the Boolean sum, merging duplicate edges after wire substitution. For fixed right bits, it is an affine nonnegative function of all left bits. Its zero set is empty or a single face: minimize each coefficient independently by choosing zero for positive slope and one for negative slope; a minimum of zero admits arbitrary zero-slope bits. This gives a witness from every nonempty such face. A cheap exact case enumerates just the k left bits whose slope takes *both* signs as right bits vary (its extrema are explicit sums of positive or negative coefficients). Fix all other left bits to one of their universally minimizing values, enumerate the k bits, and minimize the remaining right affine form in one pass per leaf. This tests existence without a table of faces. The arithmetic cost is O((n+g)2^k) word operations after coefficient assembly, with no noise, keys or secret setup.

A second case preserves many arbitrary signs. Create a signed edge for each nonzero mixed coefficient. Complementing a variable reverses its incident coefficient signs. Find vertex parities making all coefficients nonpositive by graph traversal; an inconsistent parity cycle rejects this fast mode. More generally choose a deletion set of k vertices whose removal leaves consistent parities, branch on those k bits, and use the same parities for all branches (substitution only modifies unary costs). A uniformly constructible choice is to propagate parities on a spanning forest and pick one endpoint of each inconsistent remaining edge; remove duplicates. This is an efficiently verifiable deletion set with no optimality requirement. On each branch replace -hxy by a directed cut term h x(1-y) minus h x, and represent every signed unary by a source or sink edge and a constant. Minimizing this integral cut plus its constant gives the exact global minimum. Extract a left witness from a zero cut. The signs here concern the actual bi-affine energy, unlike cut formulations for choosing how to partition a formula. A single gate with five separate free ports already contains the frustrated four-cycle on a,t,u,s; a universal small deletion set does not follow.

The raw l1 sum of a gate's displayed polynomial coefficients is 24; bit substitutions and all polarity switches combined increase it by at most a factor four (expand each original monomial once). At g=4096 a branch uses at most 8446 variables and 20480 mixed edges. Unary plus arc capacities are at most 192g in total because converting a mixed term adds a unary of equal magnitude. Integer augmenting paths therefore need at most 192g augmentations; charge also a failed search, graph construction, and each edge scan and reverse edge. This is a loose RAM bound times 2^k, not a registered tape clock or a claim to meet the cap. The format's unary wire indices and decoding them are paid separately. For any subclass on which iteration and parsing fit a prescribed clock, the static issuer emits the message bit on recognized satisfiable circuits and a fixed zero on recognized unsatisfiable circuits; decryption reads one bit. For every recognized NO the whole public transcript is pointwise identical for both payloads. Every valid witness on recognized YES succeeds exactly. There are no issued keys, no noise, and no cryptographic reduction loss for this restricted mode. Bounding search then emitting a fixed symbol on exhaustion retains full NO secrecy but leaves correctness for unrecognized YES open. A universally valid fallback with its own complete ciphertext law is needed for the registered target.

Explicit enumeration of all right-fixed faces can grossly overpay. Put one NOT copy of each primary input among 128 internal gates, fill unused gates, and end with a constant-true gate (e.g. NAND of an available fixed false wire with itself). Every assignment is accepting, and changing any input also changes an internal wire. No edge of the augmented left trace cube has both endpoints valid: changing a gate wire alone also violates determinism for the fixed input. Hence each nonempty right-fixed zero face is a singleton. Covering all valid left traces by listing such faces takes at least 2^128 entries for this family. Decision, accepting-witness recovery, a different compiler, and preprocessing this public tautology are outside this enumeration bound.

## Why the numeric public-row interface still leaks

Consider a direct two-input public numeric release T(v,w)=Delta*message+r*H(v,w)+error over any modulus q with a single shared hidden slope. This includes an implementation that first encrypted coefficients under a fixed distribution but then exposes these arithmetic outputs through public decryption. On a particular registered false circuit, let the first gate be NAND(x1,true); all remaining gates use NAND(true,true) so the final pinned output is wrong. On both sides use the actual trace on x1=0 with only the last output flipped. Htotal=1. Vary only the left primary bit, keeping all gate bits fixed. The first gate changes from (0,1,0,1,0) to (1,1,0,1,0), adding one. Then 2T1-T2=Delta*message+2e1-e2. No inverse, injectivity, or independence of errors is needed. With q even, Delta=q/2 and each signed error less than q/12, nearest-half decoding is exact. The same test applies to slopes depending solely on the fixed right selection. For slopes depending solely on the fixed left selection, use its mirror-style fixture: first gate NAND(true,x1), first local tuple (1,0,1,0,1) or (1,0,1,1,1) by changing only the right input. Retain the same fixed-true terminal and zero other costs; totals are 2,3. The combination 3T2-2T3 cancels the slope and noise below q/20 per response decides the bit. This is an interface-level comparison of two permitted numeric responses on NO. A zero-only primitive or new jointly rerandomized evaluator needs an actual complete public-key distribution and a proof for arbitrary mixed evaluations.

Even omitting noise, a single bi-affine scalar form on two *unrestricted* affine vector spaces over a field is zero-free only when it is constant nonzero. If a mixed or linear slope is nonzero, choose the other side to give a nonzero coefficient and vary the corresponding coordinate to obtain the entire field. Thus a small-modulus unrestricted field interface cannot replace the box predicate merely by selecting a larger field. Reporting the squares independently as extra vector coordinates makes that relaxed expression bi-affine again. One algebraic alternative over every Z/q with q>1 is a vector of simultaneous zero equations: enforce x_i=y_i on each duplicated wire, x_i(y_i-1)=0 on every primary bit, and NAND outputs 1-x_a y_b; pin the output. Any zero solution reduces modulo any prime divisor of q to a Boolean primary assignment followed by its uniquely determined Boolean gate outputs, hence gives a valid witness. Copying an honest assignment solves all equations even over the composite ring. This binds products without range checks; it replaces a scalar hidden zero test by a joint vector hidden zero test. A simple public compressed scalar with freely chosen same-field coefficients again encounters the preceding surjectivity fact. Enlarging the field and encoding multiple checks in a basis requires proof that selector scalars are confined to its base field.

None of the four lattice games in the registered definition has been reduced to a public false-instance simulator for independent encrypted lattice-point selection and a zero-only terminal. The squared distance gap and numerical-test examples specify interfaces to test, without proposing any extra hardness assumption.

## The same NO has a modular zero for every modulus, even with genuine squares

Use 4096 gates all equal to NAND(true,true), with only the final output pinned to true. At the otherwise correct diagonal traces the total Q4 is one. Select three unused intermediate gates. Change their left complemented outputs from 1 to 1+d_i and leave the right complemented outputs equal to one. Expanding the true squared terms gives the exact global value

    1 + f(d_1) + f(d_2) + f(d_3),    f(d)=4d²+d.

For every q>1 some residue choices give zero modulo q. Over 2^k the map f is bijective since f(x)-f(y)=(x-y)(1+4(x+y)); use just one coordinate. For an odd prime p, multiplying the zero condition by 16 gives sum_i (8d_i+1)²=-13. Choose the last square root equal to one and represent -14 as a sum of two squares in the prime field: the set of squares and -14 minus that set each have (p+1)/2 elements, so they intersect. The last square root has unit derivative 2, allowing one base-p correction digit to lift the solution at each higher power. The map d to 8d+1 is invertible there. Combine the prime powers by CRT. This also handles a simultaneous test at any finite family of moduli on unrestricted shared integer selectors by using their least common multiple. A direct enumeration of f-images and their sumsets checked every modulus from 2 through 300.

Even mildly extended coordinates can wrap at the Boolean modulus 32768. On 2978 unused gates choose left t=2, right s=0, giving Q4=11 each. On three more choose t=0, s=1, giving three each. Keep the remaining nonterminal gates correct. Including the terminal gives 2978*11+3*3+1=32768. Every source/output value in this fixture belongs to [-1,2]. This explicit computation tests only the proposed scalar modular predicate. It neither breaks ordinary LWE nor excludes a guarded evaluator. Over the reals, setting both traces' free coordinates to 1/2 in 4095 nonterminal gates NAND(x1,x2) and a false constant terminal gives squared distance 4095*7/2+10, already below the YES radius squared. Thus even a continuous cube restriction is insufficient after the quadratic correction.

## A single exact semilinear scalar for unrestricted field selectors

There is a constructive way to bind both domains when Frobenius-linear maps are available. Choose K=GF(2^8192) and a fixed F2-basis of K. Decode each independently supplied field scalar x into its absolute field trace T(x) in F2. This is a surjective F2-linear map even at even degree: its nonzero polynomial has degree 2^8191 below |K| and T(x)^2=T(x). Pin bit one directly (T(1)=0 here); to supply bit one as a free selector, choose any fixed field element of trace one. In a normal basis all basis vectors have trace one, and T(x) is the parity of all coordinates. Normalize unavailable wire addresses before reading the symbols.

For gate i, write A,B,Z for its left projected inputs and output and U for its second input on the right. Form the two F2 residuals (1+Z)+A*U and B+U. Multiply the 8192 residual bits by distinct basis vectors and add to get one element R of K. All full-field selections satisfy R=0 iff the left trace is accepting and each right second parent agrees where used. Right outputs unused by any second parent require no constraint. Every honest accepting left trace has a matching right trace by surjectivity. Each term is affine separately over F2 in the original field selectors, even when the two sides use different representatives. This is a single scalar with unrestricted mixed queries. It escapes the earlier field-surjectivity statement precisely because the input projections are only F2-linear. Packing costs 4096 bit products and 8192 output coordinates plus the input projections and XORs. If both sides supply all 4223 free wire scalars explicitly, this naive interface has 69,189,632 input bits; a real witness can instead build only zero or the fixed trace-one representative. Selection input vs ciphertext bytes must be charged separately.

The output bit nonzero(R) can be computed by the field norm R^(2^8192-1). With u_1=R and u_(2l)=u_l times u_l^(2^l), thirteen sequential multiplications and thirteen Frobenius powers suffice. Frobenius is a fixed F2-linear coordinate rotation in a normal basis; multiplication and basis conversions are still charged. This closure works at zero too. A mathematically exact payload release is message*(1+nonzero(R)). On every NO it is constant zero for all public selections, including noncanonical field inputs. Exposing only that black-box functionality is sufficient even for coherent oracle queries; an explicit program with the message or the field-norm arithmetic visible as coefficients has not been simulated. A generic public circuit is not an implementation of the required program privacy. In particular the plaintext ring F2[X]/(X^8192+1) in ordinary power-of-two cyclotomic RLWE has nilpotents and is not K, so its usual product cannot be substituted directly. Simulating K arithmetic on bits remains possible but an honest evaluation clock, keys for the released result, and joint security on all evaluations remain necessary. This supplies a precise successor mechanism without a new assumption.

A compatible linear input projection does exist for the registered cyclotomic plaintext arithmetic. In R2=F2[X]/(X^N+1) for a power of two, pi(f)=f(1) is a ring homomorphism, f^N=pi(f), and S f=S pi(f) for S=1+X+...+X^(N-1). For an ordinary ring response b=a*s+e+(q/2)m, q even and integer coefficient errors bounded by B, publish/use the computable pair S*a, coeff0(S*b) as a scalar LWE response under the signed coefficient expansion of the same s. Its plaintext is the parity pi(m), with error at most NB and exact half-modulus rounding whenever 4NB<q. The sign changes from negacyclic multiplication disappear mod two. An alternative stores all trace bits as ordinary binary coefficients of a single ring element. Multiplication by the signed monomial X^(-i) and extracting coefficient zero selects coordinate i with error at most B, without the expensive parity conversion. Extracted responses from one ciphertext are deterministic postprocessing of the same ring sample; they are not independent LWE samples. This handles the linear input operation. Encrypted products, Boolean OR of the residual coordinates, the secret-key distribution at public payload release, and full security and machine accounting remain open. In clear bit arithmetic an OR tree of 8192 flags takes 8191 binary ORs instead of a large-field norm.

Small exact replays covered 5,308,416 unrestricted GF(16) selections over 81 two-gate circuits, obtaining precisely the accepting primary-input set in every case. A separate branch-and-cut implementation matched the true minimum on 625 two-gate fixtures and 100 four-gate fixtures, including forward references; deletion-set sizes ranged from zero to three. These checks and the composite-modulus examples are finite replays, distinct from the three Lean theorems.
