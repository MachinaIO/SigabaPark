title: The joint public view of two reversed projected layers

Work over Z/(2^k). The independent uniform keys A0,A1 have shapes n0 by m0 and n1 by m1. Fix terminal targets Y with t columns. Publish Gaussian conditional preimages D1 with A1 D1=Y. For each of b labels a, publish m1 preimage columns D0a satisfying A0 D0a=S_a A1+E_a, and publish R=P A0. P has full row rank mod two. All target, transition and error data here are fixed independently of both keys; one may average over a common external sampling law. The inner preimage draws use fresh coins conditional on the keys and targets. Let D0 concatenate the D0a horizontally. Require the single-layer reversal bounds with t columns at A1 and b m1 at A0, with respective errors delta1, delta0.

**Eliminating both keys.** Draw ambient Gaussian matrices D1,D0 independently and abort if either fails its column pivot test. Fix a canonical pivot set I for D1 and let J be the remaining rows. Stack the P S_a vertically into B, and the P E_a vertically into F. Let W denote the vertical stack of R D0a. The following procedure gives the successful reverse law:

1. For every j in J sample W_j uniformly and independently in the coset F_j + image(B), using the subscript for a column.
2. Solve uniquely for W_I in W D1 = B Y + F D1 using the invertible pivot minor.
3. Reshape W into the horizontal target bank [W_a]_a. Complete R uniformly subject to R D0=[W_a]_a, by free columns and pivot elimination.

Indeed, conditional on D1, the reverse key A1 has independent uniform columns at J. Hence W=B A1+F has exactly the free-column law and pivot equation above. Conditional on this bank and D0 the projected-parent result gives step 3. To justify the two replacements in the forward experiment, first reverse the outer sampler conditional on A1,D1, then reverse the inner sampler and apply the already reversed outer kernel. Uniformity of the outer bound in its targets and contraction of total variation give loss at most delta0+delta1. Integer preimages may be retained throughout. No secret completion key is needed in this simulator.

**What the two layers expose.** The public matrices recover every W_j via R D0a and recover W D1 by multiplication. Conditional on successful D1,D0, the affine support of W is exactly the set described by steps 1 and 2, with uniform weights. For a common image(B), equality of all free-column cosets and equality of the pivot-equation right side are necessary and sufficient for equality of the two conditional public laws. A single unequal coset or right side makes their supports disjoint. These assertions concern fixed data and fixed successful D1,D0; averaging over hidden data can hide the difference. In particular, comparing only path products can miss a public constraint. At q=2 take one label, m1=2, t=1, D1=(1,0)^T, P=1, S=0. Offsets (0,0) and (0,1) give identical path products and disjoint free-column views. Conversely if B is surjective, all free-column cosets disappear: the simulator only needs B Y+F D1, the stacked path products, besides public dimensions and the ambient preimage draws.

**Cost and applicability.** Keeping the two pivot errors small by the thin-block bound requires m1 >= n1+t+slack1 and m0 >= n0+b m1+slack0, with pivot terms at most 2^(-slack_i). Thus repeating this particular pooled independent-column reversal through h binary layers requires outer width at least 2^h times the terminal width even before statistical slack. The result supports a short suffix or bounded branching. It does not give polynomial size for a full binary depth proportional to the witness length. Improving this recurrence requires a different sampler or dependencies among the branch columns, with a new joint-law proof. For this two-layer design the remaining security object is the distribution of the affine constraints above, including the cosets when B is not surjective.

**A construction variant for dependent label families.** Suppose the design admits public integer coefficients c_aj and r basis transitions T_j with S_a=sum_j c_aj T_j. Generate only r independent basis preimage blocks H_j for targets T_j A1+G_j, where the basis errors G_j are sampled independently of the keys. Send H_j; a witness selecting a uses sum_j c_aj H_j modulo q. This gives the required equation exactly with E_a=sum_j c_aj G_j. The proof above now applies directly to the r published basis blocks, with B and F stacked over j. Public expansion costs no statistical distance. The outer preimage payload is r m0 m1 coefficients, and the width condition replaces b by r. An entry bound L on all basis errors or preimages becomes L max_a sum_j |c_aj| on the corresponding effective entries, which must enter correctness accounting. Public coefficients must be common to the compared message experiments, and exposing basis blocks means the simulator must account for their full views. This changes the joint error and preimage law of the design. For example a label choosing one of T1,T2,T1+T2 uses two basis blocks, with at most twice the entry bound. This saves a factor in a structured alphabet; for generic binary layers the rank can still be two at every depth.
