title: Smoothing the low-digit GSW lift to a prescribed discrete Gaussian

The exact finite-uniform dither in the low-digit lift replaces the printed fresh error law by a staircase. Independent *Gaussian* dither gives a quantitative statistical bridge when target and source widths permit. This is a distribution calculation, not a new assumption.

Use the convention D_s(z) proportional to exp(−πz²/s²) on the integers. Let q=d m, hidden source-LWE error E drawn from D_σ at modulus m, and dither U independently from D_τ. Keep the same uniform low digits a, uniform header lift and public-key transformations as the exact construction. The real integer error is d E+U. Set α=dσ, S²=α²+τ², r=ατ/S. Its probability at z is proportional to

    exp(−πz²/S²) · sum_{x in d Z} exp(−π(x−α²z/S²)²/r²).

This follows by completing the square before normalizing. By the Poisson summation formula for the Gaussian, the last factor divided by r/d lies between 1−η(r/d) and 1+η(r/d), uniformly in the center, where

    η(x) = 2 sum_{j≥1} exp(−πx²j²) ≤ 2 exp(−πx²)/(1−exp(−πx²)).

Consequently the error is within η/(1−η) total variation of D_S for η=η(r/d)<1. Indeed, after dividing by the target distribution's mean of the factor, every likelihood ratio differs from 1 by at most 2η/(1−η). Reduction modulo q cannot increase variation. In the ideal challenge d L is uniform on the subgroup of multiples of d. The mixture dL+U is uniform inside each coset with coset distribution U mod d. Applying the same Gaussian sum with width τ to each residue coset bounds its distance to uniform by η(τ/d)/(1−η(τ/d)). The translations by the known digits and GSW gadget do not affect either estimate. The bounds hold conditional on the common low digits. With N independent challenge columns they incur at most N times the respective errors; later public deterministic or randomized postprocessing cannot enlarge these distances. If a bounded feedback sampler is used afterwards, compare the full pool before acceptance and account for its success factor separately.

Thus one fixed lower-modulus LWE challenge with uniform secret and D_σ errors also justifies GSW encryption of arbitrarily selected functions of *all* residues s mod d under standard uniform s at q, with target honest D_S errors, up to the above losses. The observable view may include arbitrary functions of a and independent side data. For a prescribed S, r≤S/2 and r=S/2 at α=τ=S/√2. Small S/d can make this proof useless. No claim about a registry distribution with non-Gaussian source errors follows. Sampling D_τ in a finite machine introduces its own tail and precision costs.

For illustration, d=2, σ=9, τ=18 yield target width S=18√2, r/d=9/√2. For N=2,632,016 columns (512 standard GSW bit blocks at n=512, ceil(log2 q)=10, plus 5,456 feedback columns) the sum of the two pool variation bounds is less than 2^(-160). To check without trusting rounded exponents, use π>3.14 and ln 2<0.694 to get π(9/√2)^2/ln 2>183; τ/d=9 gives exponent >366. Writing a=exp(−πx²), the variation bound is at most 2a/(1−3a)<(5/2)a for these values. N<3·2^20, hence the joint loss is below (15/2)·2^20·(2^(-183)+2^(-366))<2^(-160). The 512 explicit GSW blocks alone occupy 13,474,252,800 bits and use 2,626,560 native columns. These are serialization and distribution numbers. They establish neither source LWE hardness at those parameters nor an affordable evaluated tree nor a negligible final gate-error probability. With q constrained to a power of two, a 10-bit modulus encoding can for example use q=1024 and m=512.

In particular the bridge does not provide GSW encryptions of arbitrary high secret digits or the CRS-dependent master. An actual full-tree application must justify the decomposition of its phase, the public CRS programming, all jointly correlated outputs and a structured NO simulation.

More generally, a public matrix B with n rows and k columns can specify the remaining hidden key directions. Assume B modulo m has a left inverse. Choose a uniform canonical coset representative a of (Z/q)^n modulo dB(Z/m)^k; s=a+dBt is exactly uniform for t uniform mod m. For example, take B to be k columns of a supplied invertible basis over Z/q. In that basis reveal all omitted coordinates and only the low d-digits of the retained coordinates. For each source header H sample A uniformly in the fiber BᵀA=H mod m, using the public change of basis or a right inverse and a uniform kernel element. Since Bᵀ mod m is onto, the unconditional A is exactly uniform. Set Y=dL+⟨a,A⟩+U with the same two dither choices. The preceding real and ideal proofs hold, allowing GSW encryption of any function of the sampled representative a. For a fixed coefficient F, predicting the full master phase from a with certainty now requires BᵀF=0 mod m. Its remaining support size is m/gcd(m,(BᵀF)_1,...,(BᵀF)_k). The public basis can depend on an independently sampled public setup and statement, but not inadvertently on the source secret or fresh challenge body. This permits choosing expensive hidden directions outside a publicly identified coefficient span at the cost of lowering the source LWE dimension to k. A standard LWE compiler for all tree coefficients and correlated CRS has not been constructed.

There is also a necessary low-digit statistical tradeoff for any independent dither in this lift. Let ε_R be its real error's distance to the prescribed D_S and ε_I its ideal error's distance to uniform. Both real dE+U and ideal dL+U have exactly the same residue U mod d. Data processing and the triangle inequality imply TV(D_S mod d,Uniform_d) ≤ ε_R+ε_I. For d>1 this lower bound is strictly positive at each finite S: the first cosine Fourier coefficient of D_S mod d is the ratio of the positive Gaussian sums sum_j exp(−π S²(j−1/d)²) and sum_j exp(−π S² j²), by Poisson summation. The uniform coefficient is zero, so TV is at least half that ratio. Thus the two Gaussian/native and uniform/ideal endpoints cannot both be exact for this mechanism. This argument concerns classical exposed additive noise distributions; the exact finite uniform-dither construction targets a different native distribution.

The CyclicProgrammedLaw Lean node verifies the square-completion polynomial identity, the integer inequality used for the stated N-column loss, and the odd/even half-period remainders. The Gaussian Poisson step and probability couplings are mathematical arguments in this note.
