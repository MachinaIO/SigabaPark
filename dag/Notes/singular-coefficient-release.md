## Concrete box and its guarantee

For each statement let a public compiler produce a square h-by-h matrix M(x) over K=GF(2^k) on N Boolean variables. A valid witness must efficiently furnish an input making M singular (in the simplest case x is the witness). On NO every input to the public evaluator must make M invertible. Write D(x)=det M(x). Suppose additionally that on NO the set E of distinct determinants has at most m elements. No bound on the size or frequency of any value on YES is necessary. The set E and its enumeration are not supplied to the issuer. These are structural hypotheses on the compiler, not computational assumptions.

The issuer samples an independent uniform coefficient c_j in K for each j=1,...,m, defines P(z)=sum c_j z^j, and publishes **only the canonical square-free Boolean coefficient vector** of

    Y(x) = b XOR Tr(P(D(x))).

Tr is the absolute field trace to F₂. There is no exceptional branch or separately stored pole value. Decryption evaluates the published polynomial at the witness input. At D=0 its value is b with certainty, even on a circuit with many singularities. A fixed coefficient layout (or a deterministic encoding uniquely determined by the Boolean function) matters. Serializing the unnormalized syntax for b+Tr(P(D)), the polynomial P, its random tape, or an uncleared zero-coefficient record can disclose the bit. The statement, compiler and all fixed public parameters are identical across message worlds.

On NO define S(z)=1−product_{a in E}(1−z/a). Then S(0)=0, S(a)=1 at every reachable determinant, and deg S≤|E|≤m. Pick gamma in K of absolute trace one. Replacing the coefficient vector by that of P+gamma S permutes the uniform bounded-degree coins; the two global Y functions, hence every serialized bit and every subsequent evaluation, coincide under this permutation after flipping b. The issuer does not compute S. The Lean node checks its degree and values, finiteness of the actual mask space and the observation-event bijection. The associated solution proves equality of finite counts for each complete observable event. This exact law also permits quantum postprocessing of the sampled classical packet; a quantum game and a registered machine are not formalized here.

For K=F₂ one coin r gives Y=b+r·det M, since every regular determinant is one. An alternative full-matrix interface chooses a matrix R(x) with each entry uniform in the space of square-free polynomials of degree at most e(h−1), where each entry of M has Boolean degree at most e. Publish the normalized coefficients of M(x)R(x)+bI. On NO, R↦R+adj(M) is a legal coin translation and M adj(M)=I at every Boolean point. On a singular witness choose a nonzero binary left null vector u. Its multiplication into the matrix yields b u, which reveals b. If every singular left kernel has a vector nonzero at a fixed index, the corresponding single output column suffices. The scalar construction avoids the h² output multiplier. The verified matrix identities are in the same definition node.

## Encoding and costs

Let a be an upper bound on the Boolean degree of D. One may use a=min(N,eh) but exploit a smaller certified bound when available. In characteristic two, on Boolean inputs D(x)^(2^j) raises only its square-free coefficients to the 2^j-th power. Its degree remains at most a. Consequently D(x)^v has degree at most a·popcount(v) and the entire output degree is at most

    B=min(N, a floor(log₂(m+1))).

This argument uses the canonical Boolean relation x_i²=x_i; the raw polynomial over formal indeterminates may have larger degrees. Applying trace is binary linear. The term tr(c z^(2v)) equals tr(c^(2^(k−1)) z^v). Repeatedly halving even exponents folds all coins into the odd exponents, with exactly the same law since each original odd coefficient is independent uniform. Thus k·ceil(m/2) random bits suffice. For m greater than the total possible regular image use m≤2^k−1 instead. Alternatively, taking the entire regular image always permits the known norm mask r D^(2^k−1), with one fair bit and degree ≤ka, without relying on a small NO image.

Let S_N(B)=sum_{i=0}^B C(N,i). A deterministic complete ANF serializer evaluates Y on indicator vectors of every subset of size at most B and inverts the Boolean subset-sum transform. In-place induction over coordinates (subtract the value at the set with that coordinate removed) recovers the coefficients. There are exactly sum_i i C(N,i) such additions when subsets containing each coordinate are enumerated directly. No high-weight coefficient can survive by the degree bound. The resulting payload has S_N(B) bits, all of which may be postprocessed jointly. For an affine pencil formed from public coefficient matrices each evaluation can use at most B additions of h-by-h matrices, O(h³) field operations for elimination and O(m) more for evaluating the univariate polynomial. Account separately for addressing subsets, field representation, statement generation, public randomness, framing and decoding; these are not registered tape-clock theorems.

At N=128 the largest degree giving fewer than 2^32 coefficient bits is 5 (275,584,033 bits), and the analogous degree for the registered 2^100 cap is 31 (707,105,980,394,692,860,071,225,225,273 bits). At N=4224 when issuing a full purported trace as a Boolean argument the corresponding degrees are 2 (8,923,201 bits) and 10 (494,170,943,468,367,161,395,006,733,841 bits). A registered witness has 128 bits; any extension into trace or kernel coordinates and its cost must be provided by the application.

## A smaller public evaluation method on the same promise

For odd j≤m take the k binary linear coordinates dual to multiplication and trace on D(x)^j. Form the feature row v(x)=(1, these coordinates), of length r=1+k ceil(m/2). At any singular input the row is v*=(1,0,...,0). On NO the separator gives a binary functional that vanishes on all v(x) but takes one on v*, so no span of genuine rows contains v*. This also gives an exact criterion for the packet model: the uniform linear mask image must contain the constant function; otherwise the two canonical coefficient distributions have disjoint supports. If the public coefficient maps are computed this condition is a linear-system test. Equivalently, a counterexample to a separator is an odd binary dependence among evaluations with all nonconstant features summing to zero. Reachable image cardinality is sufficient, not necessary.

There is a concrete one-sided public decision-and-release procedure. Sample T independent uniform Boolean x's and take the linear span of the resulting publicly computed rows. Emit b if it contains v*, otherwise emit zero; the decoder reads the one bit. On NO it always emits zero. On YES a valid x gives v*, whether or not the samples themselves hit that x. Whenever the current span omits v* there is a functional zero on that span and one on v*. Its evaluations form a nonzero Boolean polynomial of degree ≤B. Its nonzero fraction is at least 2^(-B), by fixing all but the variables of a maximal-degree monomial and taking its alternating subcube sum. Therefore the next sample extends the span with probability at least p=2^(-B). At most r extensions are needed; count any subsequent trial as a success after v* is present. With success count Z, induction gives E[2^(-Z)]≤(1−p/2)^T. Failure implies Z<r and has probability at most 2^(r−1)(1−p/2)^T. For T=2^(B+1)(r+κ) this is below 2^(-κ). This needs T evaluation/row insertions (O(r²) bit operations per straightforward insertion), without an N-to-the-B coefficient table. The feature-row method has ciphertext length one because its structural conditions actually give a randomized public SAT recognizer; existence of a universal small compiler meeting them is a substantive extra requirement. This abstract method does not provide arbitrary SAT decision or a universal cost certificate.

The separator degree bound can be sharp. If E contains every nonzero point of a binary t-dimensional linear subspace of K and a separator vanishes at zero, then on that subspace its Boolean function is the complement of the indicator of zero. The square-free representative has degree t. For |E|=2^t−1 this meets floor(log₂(m+1)). The proof concerns the degree of a separator in the determinant *value* coordinates. It does not lower-bound all ciphertext architectures.

## Padded 4096-gate example and actual public laws

Use K=GF(4) with alpha²=alpha+1. For t a public bit let

    M_t(x,y) = [[1,x],[t+y,1+(alpha+1)y]].
    D_t(x,y) = 1+(alpha+1)y+xy+tx.

At (0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1), D_0 gives (1,1,alpha,alpha+1), all nonzero. D_1 gives (1,0,alpha,alpha). The matching 128-input, 4096-NAND examples use 4093 dead gates NAND(true,true), then z=NAND(y,y) for t=1 or z=NAND(x,x) for t=0, u=NAND(x,z), out=NAND(u,u). Thus t=0 is false at every input and t=1 accepts precisely x=1,y=0. The matrix compiler here is explicit for this fixture family.

With m=1, the XOR of three publicly evaluated outputs on the same false circuit, at (0,0),(0,1),(1,1), equals b because 1+alpha+(alpha+1)=0. The two bit choices have four distinct ANF headers each with no shared header. At m=3 only j=1,3 are needed after folding. Exhausting all 16 coefficient pairs yields eight different complete ANF headers on NO, with identical multiplicity in both message worlds. On YES every coefficient pair gives b at the actual singular witness. The two worlds on NO are thus compared at the level of their full bit strings; no appeal to a restricted rational oracle or to independent per-trace sampling is made.

## Limits on general NAND

For a binary pencil in fixed Boolean witness coordinates with a unique accepting witness 0^128, 1+det M(x) equals product_i(1+x_i) as a Boolean function. For affine entries this forces h≥128 and its explicit expansion contains all 2^128 monomials before the constant adjustment. If there are auxiliary bits, fixing one valid auxiliary value gives the same restriction provided no other witness coordinate can become singular. Different coordinates, public preprocessing, compressed programs and larger fields are not covered by that binary-indicator argument. In particular the 1-by-1 extension-field pencil D(x)=sum_i e_i x_i in GF(2^128), for a binary basis e_i, has exactly the single zero x=0. Its field norm has large Boolean degree even though D itself is linear. The range promise here restricts NO, not YES. For witness coordinates of a genuine full compiler, an efficiently computable normalization accepting every input would still need a full cost analysis.

There is another difficulty specific to using a raw residual digest as D. On a 4096-gate false circuit one can expose nonempty independent fault sets in 1366 locations via legitimate purported accepting traces. A characteristic-two linear digest on those residual coordinates either has a nontrivial kernel (hence a spurious zero on NO), or its NO image contains all nonzero vectors of a 1366-dimensional subspace. An analogous 683-dimensional example survives two rounds of nonadjacent-wire substitution: use 683 four-NOT chains from true, followed by a final NAND of their conjunction, with one dead gate to reach 4096. At least one position per chain survives two independent-set elimination rounds. Flip one retained position on any nonempty subset of chains and recompute subsequent gates; precisely those branches become false and the final output becomes true. The residual support is the selected subset. If a generalized digest has Boolean degree d on the fault-coordinate cube, takes zero at its origin, and a separator has degree h' on the value bits, their composition equaling one everywhere else forces d h'≥683. The statement is about this residual-only representation, not a degree bound in arbitrary trace coordinates. Circuit simplification that removes the constant chains can evade it. Both this boundary and the singleton example leave the existing general WE goal open.
