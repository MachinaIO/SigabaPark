Let P=Hᵀs+e and T=cᵀs+2b over Z/4, with uniform s independent of e=z+2r and uniform binary z. Let A=H modulo two and c0=c modulo two. At a sensitive Fourier character of (P,T), averaging s imposes H lambda=c after normalization by an odd unit. Consequently the two full ciphertext laws are equal iff phi(lambda)=E i^(lambda·e)=0 at *every* such solution. A forbidden coefficient 2 test is one sufficient condition, but is much stronger when required at every frequency for every matrix, and need not be used by a circuit-specific sampler. Classical full-law equality also survives quantum postprocessing of the classical input. An unconditioned scalar TV under translation by two is |Re phi|+|Im phi|.

### One prescribed parity and the exact universal classification

On a fixed nonempty support S draw the high bits uniformly except for the parity sum r_i=f(z). Outside S one may draw independent fair binary errors. Conditional on z, a character survives the high-bit average only if its odd subset on S is empty or all of S. The all-odd case has no 2 on S, and the other case is killed by a fair low bit if it has a 2 there. Thus all forbidden frequencies vanish, for arbitrary f. At a target sign 1+2a on S, f=floor(sum z/2)+a·z modulo two cancels the carry and yields exact high-bit recovery from lambda·P−T. clearCarry proves the integer quarter-turn cancellation at every weight. More generally use f=floor(sum z/2)+a0·z+g(Lz) where L is full row rank k, k even, and g is a sum of products of disjoint consecutive pairs. For a=a0+Lᵀd, correcting by g(d) gives success (1+2^(-k/2))/2 from that scalar alone. Independence of the k uniform coordinates and the four-term bentPair identity give the sign and magnitude. The conditioned-low result below can be stronger.

For a full-row-rank binary M, draw r uniformly subject to Mr=f(z). At lambda=u+2v, the coefficient is zero unless u=Mᵀh; then the integrand left is i^(u·z)(-1)^(v·z+h·f(z)). For any fixed u, all v with some v_i=1 outside u must have zero coefficient for universally forbidden-frequency protection. Walsh inversion says this is equivalent to the displayed function without the v-twist depending only on coordinates in supp(u). The quarter-root prefactor already has this property, so the requirement is exactly that the i-th difference of f takes values in the binary span of column M_i. Indeed it is annihilated by all h orthogonal to that column. A zero column contributes nothing. Mixed differences in two coordinates with distinct nonzero columns lie in both one-dimensional spans and hence vanish. mixedDerivative proves the general disjoint-subgroup step. In the Boolean multilinear expansion of f every nonconstant term therefore uses only coordinates with one common nonzero column, and its vector coefficient is in that span. These conditions are also sufficient by taking one-variable differences. Enumeration at identity M on two bits finds exactly 16 universally supported tables out of all 256. The classification does not constrain unrestricted circuit-specific cancellations.

For another general limitation take any low-uniform law on e, including arbitrary high-bit dependence, and lambda(a)=lambda0+sum_j a_j v_j in Z/4 with independent mod-two reductions of the k directions. For independent e,e' the average over independent a bits of |phi(lambda(a))|² equals the expectation of i^(lambda0·(e-e')) times the product of (1+i^(v_j·(e-e')))/2. Each odd inner product gives modulus 1/sqrt(2), the rest at most one. The parity projections are uniform independent, so the average is at most ((1+1/sqrt(2))/2)^k. This concerns individual phase readout, not the whole packet or using public low fields with the phase.

### Signs in the same support are reusable

Choose a feasible u. Write lambda=u+2v, so A v=(c−H[u])/2 over F2, with canonical integer representatives before dividing. For two lifts with the same low support, d=v−v0 lies in ker A. Hence d·z=d·(P mod 2) is already public (kernelRead). This eliminates the nominal sign-chart penalty when a reference lift v0 is computable from public u and the issuer uses the carry-correcting parity for it. If a solution avoiding 2 is required, add the linear restrictions (v0)_i=0 off u when finding that reference. The high output for any other lift differs by precisely the public bit d·P. This recovers the payload exactly on all signs over that fixed support, no matter the sign-space dimension. A publicly chosen valid reference also makes recognition of this support a linear-algebra problem; it is not a method to discover hidden supports in arbitrary satisfiable inputs.

For a circuit-specific relaxation, fix u=Mᵀh satisfying A u=c0. If A v=d has no solution supported inside u, there is a binary j with Aᵀj zero on u and j·d=1 (here d=(c−H[u])/2). For each candidate lift, translation of the uniform z by Aᵀj leaves i^(u·z) fixed and negates the sign (-1)^(v·z). It suffices for h·f to be invariant under this one translation. Requiring invariance for all translations in row(A) supported outside u is sufficient even when the particular j is unknown. This allows arbitrary additional functions of the coset of z modulo row(A), including cross-column nonlinear terms forbidden by the universal classification. Such coset dependencies alone are publicly computable and confer no secrecy.

More precisely the observable low header (p,t)=(Aᵀs0+z,c0ᵀs0) determines a coset of K=Aᵀker(c0ᵀ), and z conditional on that header is uniform there. To sample the ordinary conditional experiment choose s0 uniformly subject to its one equation, put z=p+Aᵀs0, and generate the remaining high coins normally. Any h.f(z) depending on such a public coset is known for a given h. For a usable phase set F_lambda(z)=floor((u·z)/2)+v·z+h·f(z) mod 2. For any j with c0·j=0 put Aᵀj=k and Hᵀ[j]=k+2l mod 4. Its coset difference is

    F_lambda(z xor k)+F_lambda(z)
       = (u AND k)·z + u·l + floor((c·[j] mod 4)/2) + h·(f(z xor k)+f(z)).

Indeed u·k=0 mod two, and lambda·Hᵀ[j]=c·[j] fixes the otherwise quadratic constant floor((u·k)/2)+v·k. This is affine in u,h. A numerical replay checked the general derivative with the extra (u·k)(u·z) term for 4,200 completely sampled feasible systems. Near-perfect coset corrected single-phase readout accordingly admits a linear training test. The companion note treats a larger class of decoders without needing this explicit derivative. These are mathematical conclusions and finite tests; the four named sublemmas are the parts formalized in Lean.
