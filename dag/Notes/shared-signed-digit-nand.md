## One addressed pair with no erasure

Write c=(1,x,y,z) for public Boolean pins. The gate output NAND(NAND(x,y),z) is true exactly when z=0 or x=y=1. At q=16 choose independent fair signs and independent uniform s in (Z/16)^4. Let d_i be the four public coefficients of the affine forms

    D1(c)=4(1-x)+(1-z),      D2(c)=4(1-y)+2(1-z).

Print P=s+σ1 d1+σ2 d2 and T=c·s+8β. Given the two signs, P is independently uniform. The entire observed packet is equivalently the independent P and residual R=T-c·P=8β-σ1 D1-σ2 D2; the overall sign convention is immaterial. When z=1 and some input is zero, the corresponding term is ±4. Flipping that sign changes the residual by 8, for either sign; P can be kept fixed by translating the uniform s. This bijection identifies the complete law for both bits even on x=y=0. For z=1 and both inputs one the residual is the clean phase. For z=0, reducing modulo 8 makes ±4 indistinguishable, hence the known contributions from x,y can be subtracted. The residues ±1±2 are all distinct modulo 8, so both signs, and then the bit, are determined. This is one 20-bit addressed packet (five 4-bit residues), perfect correctness, no repetition, no computational premise. The earlier q=2^19 erasure construction used 12,160 raw bits and 128 independent trials. Ordered real outputs include every coefficient; decoding other signed linear combinations does not alter the coupling. The two internal-connected NANDs are evaluated semantically with their shared wire fixed by its parents. No independent witness pin for that wire is given. All eight pins and all four sign tapes were checked. TwoGateSupports and TwoGateFlip verify the support separation and the NO coupling of the residual, respectively. MaskTransport supplies the algebraic change of variables.

## A radix construction for all local NAND checks at one address

For k positive-width clauses of width at most three, use h_i(w) in {0,1,2,3}, the number of true literals in clause i. These are integer affine forms in the shared Boolean raw coordinates; repeated wires cause no problem. Put Q=4^k, q=4Q, a_i=4^i h_i, D_i=Q+a_i, with i starting at zero. Publish the same uniform affine header P and response T for a single fixed address w at modulus q, using one independent sign per clause. Up to reversing all independent signs the residual is

    R=2Qβ+Σ_i (1-2b_i)(Q+a_i).

Subtract R from the known center Σ(Q+a_i), reduce modulo 2Q and divide the even least residue by two. The result in [0,Q) is Σ4^i b_i h_i. Its base-four digits are exactly b_i h_i: no carry, even when several clauses repeat identical literals. At a satisfied clause its own coin is recovered. If all clauses hold this determines every coin and β exactly. At any failed clause D_i=Q, and flipping its fair sign translates by 2Q without changing the law of P. This is exact for every mask and addresses all signed or modular alternative marginal readings of the public pair. SignedLedger proves the integer identity and RadixRead verifies the one-digit nonzero test. Exponential numeric modulus costs linearly many bits per residue; these are scalar modular words, not a Ring-LWE instance.

One NAND z=NAND(x,y) contributes the three clauses (x OR z), (y OR z), (NOT x OR NOT y OR NOT z). Add a terminal unit clause. For the registered raw trace with 128 input and 4096 gate bits there are k=12289 clauses, a 24580-bit modulus word, 4225 coordinates including the constant and one response. This straightforward addressed issuer prints 103875080 raw bits. Uniform mask sampling takes 4225*24580 fair bits, plus 12289 sign bits. Bit-operation and registered machine clocks are separate. Constants and default parents are fixed literals under their interface convention. The address includes the proposed complete trace at issuance. A public ciphertext for an existential statement must handle all possible traces without being issued for one of them.

## Why a common set of signs is inadequate for an unrestricted address family

Suppose an interface releases the above residual R(w) at every permitted w with the same b_i. The low word at w reveals b_i whenever h_i(w)>0, irrespective of all other clauses. If every clause is satisfied somewhere, querying at most k suitably chosen addresses recovers all signs and then β from any one residual. The existence statement need not give an efficient procedure for an arbitrary implicitly specified address set. On the full Boolean cube, for the affine h_i a nonzero restriction is nonzero at the origin or at a unit vector, so n+1 predetermined addresses suffice. For a nonempty family, conversely, if some h_i vanishes throughout it, flipping that one coin preserves every residual under a β flip. Thus for the *vector of residuals*, existence of a common failed clause is necessary and sufficient for perfect privacy. Appending independent uniform headers preserves this conclusion; coin-dependent auxiliary material requires a separate argument. This is a distinction between a common failed clause and a failure that moves with the queried witness.

## A full joint issuer for moving faults with two dependent gates

Let A=1+z(1+x), B=1+z(1+y) be Boolean functions on raw witness w, with F2 arithmetic. Their conjunction is exactly the two-gate predicate. Indeed AB=1+z+zxy in the multilinear Boolean algebra. The local indicators may share any inputs. Generate four independent bits u0,u1,v0,v1 and define u=u0+u1 A and v=v0+v1 A. Publish the following three **normalized functions** over the actual raw witness domain:

    E0=β+u+v,       E1=A u,       E2=B v.

These are a parity header and two locally readable coin functions, corresponding to the parity and gated coin digits of the radix packet. If A=B=1 the XOR of all three evaluations gives β. If no witness satisfies both, translate u by 1+A and v by A. Equivalently flip u0,u1,v1, keeping v0 fixed. The parity header is unchanged after flipping β. The change in E1 is A(1+A)=0 and the change in E2 is AB=0 on the entire domain. The coefficient-coin translation is a bijection even when the function space degenerates. With a canonical function representation this identifies the byte strings for every tape under that bijection. Thus simultaneous evaluation at arbitrary forged or inconsistent addresses, quantum postprocessing of the public bits and all derived marginals are covered. TwoLocalCoupling verifies the per-coordinate identity under the NO hypothesis; the fixed coefficient translation works simultaneously on all coordinates. As a moving-fault example take z=1,x=t,y=1+t. Both local tests can pass separately; they never pass together. The corrected transcript has equal laws. Exhaustive enumeration over all 64 pairs of Boolean truth tables on three addresses, 16 coin tapes and both messages confirmed equality precisely on NO, and disjoint support on YES.

If x,y,z are public affine functions of n witness bits, compute normalized multilinear ANF over w by multiplying and reducing w_j^2=w_j. All released functions have degree at most three (Au is a multiple of A and Bv is a linear combination of B,AB). Compute deterministic independent bases of (1,A), (A), (B,AB) from their actual ANFs, discarding zero/dependent vectors, and publish coordinates of the three functions in those bases. These spaces have total dimension at most five, giving at most five payload bits for the one-bit message once the public statement and format are fixed. A single ANF vector has at most Σ_{j≤3} binom(n,j) bits, at most 349633 at n=128; cost of generating or recomputing bases is additional. Publicly sending coefficients in a *formal* independent (B,AB) basis can expose the coin when AB is identically zero. Storing the circuit syntax for E2 with its secret coefficients creates the same problem. Compact coordinate counts here depend on actual-domain normalization. Checking AB=0 via the same ANF also decides satisfiability for this class. Even a simpler random multiple of the normalized output could use this property; the contribution here is the shared-sign correction and its full-transcript proof on moving faults.

For more controls A_i, write prefix p_i=product of the first i indicators, p_0=1. Across bit-flip worlds shift function-valued signs by p_{i-1}+p_i for i<k and by p_{k-1} for the last. These changes telescope to 1, and multiplying by the corresponding indicator gives zero on NO (the last change gives the full product). Uniform coins from the corresponding public spans implement the shifts. Canonical normalized representations for the header and each issued gated function are necessary; local rank bounds on formal spans alone do not provide them. Large raw circuits can have dense ANF and detecting whether the final product vanishes can require solving the original NO case. This step supplies no short jointly secure representation for arbitrary NAND DAGs, no reduction to a registered lattice distribution, and no complete capped Machine submission. No new hardness assumption is introduced.
