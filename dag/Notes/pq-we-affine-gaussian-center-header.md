### A restricted NAND construction

Fix a public undirected graph G on vertices 0,...,7 with endpoints s=0,t=7. Its registered circuit reads eight 3-bit vertex names from a 128-bit input, ignoring the other 104 bits. It requires the first vertex to be s, the last to be t, and every consecutive pair to be equal or adjacent. Every connected pair has a simple path of length at most seven; repeat its endpoint to pad to eight vertices. The encryption algorithm can recover G directly from this syntactic family: prefix the circuit with 28 unused gates in lexicographic edge order, NAND(1,1) for present and NAND(0,0) for absent, using wire zero (constant true) and primary wire one. Generate a false wire with NAND(0,0) and cache negations of the first 24 input wires. Each of seven adjacency tests is a six-bit lookup with 63 multiplexers, three NANDs per mux. Conjoin the seven results and six endpoint bits. Place unused legal NANDs before the final two inversions to make exactly 4096 gates. This particular emitter uses 1402 gates before padding, including the descriptor and final inversions. The issuer can recompile and compare the public representation. The result covers this explicit family of circuit encodings.

Use the Gaussian-center one-bit payload lock of Aggarwal–Jain–Lin–Wu, Theorem 5.1, Figure 2 and Remark 5.3, [ePrint 2026/2927](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2927). Let x be the specified truncated discrete Gaussian integer vector of dimension m, in the ball X of radius B. Generate the genuine homogeneous (A,T), a uniform secret s₀, b=s₀ᵀA+xᵀ, and the independent payload sample (C,d=s₀ᵀC+e'ᵀ+floor(q/2)·beta); arithmetic in publication is modulo q. Correct payload decoding also requires the ordinary rounding tail on e', e.g. |e'|<q/4−1 suffices. The concrete parameters must pay that tail. For t_radius≥1 let f(x) move the first integer coordinate one unit toward zero, leaving zero and other coordinates unchanged. Then f(x)∈X and its Euclidean distance from x is at most one. Taking f(x)=x implements the zero-error case. The auxiliary headers below need *neither* a leakage oracle for f(x) nor any premise about obfuscating an adversarially chosen center.

In W=(Z/qZ)^m sample vertex potentials P independently uniform except that P_t := P_s + z, where z=f(x) mod q. Emit, in fixed public order and orientation, P_v−P_u for each present edge (u,v). All cycles have sum zero. A satisfying path traverses forward or negates an edge delta for reverse traversal and uses zero for a stutter. Its sum is P_t−P_s=z by telescoping (theorem walk). Interpret coordinates as centered residues; B<(q−t_radius)/2 gives the unique lift f(x). Run Figure 2's TdInvert(A,T,t_radius,b−f(x)); under the paper's correctness event this recovers s₀, which decrypts d. No connectivity decision is used during issuance.

**Joint simulator for a false input.** In a disconnected graph, let U be the connected component of t. No present edge crosses U, and s∉U. Adding the same vector a to every potential in U preserves the entire ordered public edge tuple including every cycle, and adds a to its endpoint difference. Theorem cut proves this for any edge-respecting side, with the inverse translation. For any prescribed z, unconditional independent potentials P map to Q by shifting U by z−(P_t−P_s). The map P ↦ (Q,P_t−P_s−z) is a bijection to the potentials with prescribed endpoint difference z times one free W coordinate (theorem fibers). Hence Q is uniform on the fiber, and its public edge tuple equals that of P. This holds *pointwise* for every z, including any deterministic function of the hidden center and all terminal coins. Given a challenge (A,T,b,h(x),C,d₀), the NO reduction samples unconditional independent vertex potentials and emits their differences. It never computes U, decides connectivity, queries x, or samples a trapdoor seed compatible with T. On the real branch this reproduces the full joint source and terminal law; on the ideal branch it uses the same operation on (A,T,u,h(x),C,u'). Add the payload offset to d₀ on both branches. Uniform u' hides either bit, so the two real payloads differ by at most the sum of two computational gaps and the Gaussian restriction tails. The theorem does not license arbitrary source data correlated with x. Here independence of the whole additional tuple is proved by the cut. No use of the O(log λ)-bit leakage allowance is needed by this supplier.

For independently chosen public linear transformations one may also issue bR+dS+Δᵀ(AR+CS)+h₀ with the correspondingly transformed public samples AR+CS; here d includes the chosen payload shift, and h₀ and Δ are sampled by the postprocessor. Reuse any of these matrices or offsets by actually passing the same public values on both branches. The error on a resulting real linear form is xᵀR+e'ᵀS plus the known transformed payload and offset; the generally correlated noise is not a fresh Gaussian in each column. To instantiate the same-center refresh from Remark 5.2 use b+ΔᵀA+δᵀ for each publicly selected shift δ. Retaining the same ideal u across related entries preserves all public dependencies. These are ordinary efficient postprocessing consequences, including a polynomial number of correlated entries with their actual costs. Theorem 4.2 allows a short possibly randomized h(error), polynomial q, the homogeneous trapdoor law and specified LWE dimension/noise inequalities. It does not in general supply f(x), xR, fresh independent ideal samples for correlated rows, or a secret-dependent offset chosen by the reduction. For the restricted family the simulator for *all* source entries above bypasses every such request.

### Generalization and quantitative limits

For any finite field, view vertex incidence as an explicit row-span access structure. More generally choose a public target row v≠0 and public rows M, condition a uniform matrix Z by vZ=z, and emit MZ for its entire batch. A witness with coefficients α satisfying αM=v computes z=α(MZ). If v is outside the row span, pick a column u such that Mu=0 and vu=1 solely for the proof. Z ↦ Z+u(z−vZ) preserves the emitted matrix, and together with vZ−z yields the same fiber bijection column by column. MZ from an unconditional matrix is again the exact false-header sampler, regardless of z. A family with explicit polynomial-sized M,v where all satisfying statements have row-span witnesses and all false statements have v outside the span would itself give a polynomial-time SAT decision algorithm by linear algebra. Directed reachability cannot simply use the undirected incidence representation; every public difference can be negated. For example the oriented edges s←u→t permit a public signed path from s even though no directed path starts there.

**Directed DAG alternative.** Let the public acyclic graph have source s and terminal sink t, and suppose its number of s-to-t paths h is at most 2^128. Give every nonterminal vertex v a fresh independent potential in Z/2^(128+k), set P_t to a k-bit unsigned coordinate of z, and publish P_s and all D_v=P_v−∑_{v→u}P_u for v≠t. Repeat coordinatewise with independent nonterminal potentials (k=ceil(log₂q) suffices for canonical residues). Fixing P_t, the triangular system in reverse topological order is a bijection between all nonterminal potentials and all residuals. Thus the entire residual vector is independent uniform. With h_v the count of paths v→t, set F_t=0 and F_v=D_v+∑_{v→u}F_u. Induction gives P_s−F_s=h_s P_t. If h_s=0, sample the residuals uniformly and set P_s=F_s. In fact this sampler never needs the count or the center; on every NO instance it precisely supplies the required joint header. For h>0, write h=2^a u, u odd. The canonical residue P_s−F_s is divisible by 2^a; divide it and invert u modulo 2^(128+k−a). Since a≤128, the low k bits recover the unsigned coordinate exactly, including h=2^128. Dead-end vertices distinct from t have h_v=0 and independent residuals. Computing h,F from the graph takes linear-many ring additions in the DAG size, with the corresponding operand width charged. The cost per coordinate is |V|·(128+k) header bits and |V|−1 random words. Directed paths here are public witnesses and graph reachability is efficiently decidable.

A smaller-ring randomized alternative assigns fresh public edge multipliers and performs both recurrences with those multipliers over F_q. The coefficient h_s is the polynomial sum of all directed-path monomials. In an acyclic graph with distinct edge variables, these monomials are distinct; on YES this is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most L (longest path). Schwartz–Zippel bounds failure to recover z by min(1,L/q); on NO h_s is identically zero. Repeat independent coefficient trials r times, reuse the same z with independent potential banks, and take the first nonzero coefficient. The error bound is at most (L/q)^r when L<q. All residuals in all trials are uniformly simulatable on NO jointly with the terminal. Count the r edge-weight vectors and r independent residual banks. This extension isolates the effect of modular cancellation. It does not turn a public directed graph into an encoding of unrestricted SAT.

A limitation of fixed-feature linear compression is quantitative even at finite size. Consider a path of L edges, all needed to connect the fixed endpoints. Suppose each edge has a fixed row r_i, the target is their sum, and deleting any single row must put the target outside the span of the remainder. Then every r_i is outside the span of the others, so all L are independent and the feature dimension is at least L. Equivalently any minimal linear reconstruction with L nonzero coefficients needs at least L dimensions. This concerns a fixed reusable feature representation under edge deletion. It does not rule out instance-adapted features, computationally hidden access structures, or other proofs. A literal exponential configuration graph for a general circuit does not presently yield a succinct, clocked issuer. The hard missing ingredient is private global consistency of the witness across all uses. Praxis encodes a single satisfying assignment via a rank-one witness with a rank gap on false inputs; its algebraic check does not transfer its pairing backend to this lattice construction. Tree Encodings I §4.4 explicitly discusses a separate circularity in its heuristic obfuscation route. Neither property is supplied here. See the supplied Praxis manuscript and Tree Encodings I §4.4.

The graph header takes at most 28m⌈log₂q⌉ bits in addition to the genuine terminal, plus public graph metadata when not already in the statement; P_s,...,P_7 themselves remain secret. Seven edge lookups, vector sums and TdInvert are required for decryption. Sampling uniform residues has to be clocked or appropriately approximated in a concrete Machine. Even one fixed-width bit per scalar in an explicit m×m full-rank T costs at least m². Write L=floor(log₂q)≥1. With n'≥16 and the stated asymptotic slack quantified by r≥2m, positive integer radius and t_radius≪q/(4rm), the relaxed inequalities m≥64L² and q≥8m² follow from m≥4n log₂q+1 and n' log₂q≤n−ω(log λ). The theorem dense uses just these displayed integer inequalities and q<2^(L+1), obtaining m≥73984 in its proof and m²>2^32. This rules out a literal dense table at a separate strict 2^32 local budget under those assumptions; it does not decide the 2^100 overall cap. The support or entropy of every structured encoding does not follow from that count. A short function of the challenge-derived T that suffices for evaluation may be output by a reduction, but no such representation and exact compatible evaluator have been established here. Publishing the seed of a sampler for (A,T) would require a conditional joint seed simulation or a new proved security theorem. A seed generating an inhomogeneous trapdoor does not have the law of the published homogeneous Gaussian trapdoor.

### A different local terminal for the PR 176 bank

The local independent-bank game of [PR 176](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/176) has 8 lanes of F_(257⁴) and 6 lanes of F_(769⁴), with independent nonzero t,g in each lane. Raw targets are g t^8449. Conditional on the independent bank and source headers they are uniform over a product of size K=(257⁴−1)^8(769⁴−1)^6 because multiplication by fixed nonzero t^8449 permutes the nonzero g. This reasoning is limited to the stated local view. Encode the 56 base-field coordinates canonically in F769, using integer representatives (there is no field embedding F257→F769). Pack the original coordinates injectively into 528 bits. Publish the 24 moments S_k=∑_{j=1}^{56} E_j j^k for k=0,...,23, an independent 528-bit binary seed R, and beta xor ⟨R,pack(E)⟩. This is exactly 769 extra bits.

Up to 12 erroneous coordinates are correctable. The difference of two candidate errors has weight at most 24, and a square Vandermonde submatrix on its nonzero positions is invertible. To recover algorithmically, for an actual weight d the d×d moment Hankel matrix of the errors equals V diag(nonzero magnitudes) Vᵀ and is nonsingular; every larger such Hankel has rank at most d. Solve for the error-locator recurrence, take its d roots among the 56 positions, then solve for the magnitudes. The zero error has all differences zero. An output beyond the promised radius need not be rejected. On the independent-bank endpoint the exact total variation between the two bit worlds equals the mean over H,R of the sum over syndromes of the absolute signed mass of each syndrome fiber, the signs being the binary characters of the injective encoding. Orthogonality over R, then Cauchy–Schwarz over the syndrome bins and Jensen over H, bound this by sqrt(769^24 · E_H∑_e Pr[e|H]^2). Here that collision term is 1/K. The theorem terminal_ledger verifies 769^24·2^256<K. A margin just over 128 bits at this statistical endpoint is not yet a real 128-bit concrete reduction with computational and sampling losses. Quantum postprocessing of a classical transcript cannot increase its statistical distance; preexisting quantum side information is a different setting. This corrector does not turn the bounded Hamming loss into the Euclidean Gaussian radius of the other construction.

For the specific honest product of two source labels, put Q=p⁴ in a lane. Independent uniform labels have product equal to a prescribed nonzero target with probability (Q−1)/Q². For one repeated uniform label, t^8449 is square with probability one half since 8449 is odd and the multiplicative group has even order, giving equality probability 1/Q after averaging over t. The independent nonzero g uniformizes the entire nonzero discrepancy. Consequently the coordinate weight dominates that of a uniform four-coordinate base-field word (the actual zero probability is at most 1/Q and the nonzero-conditional law is identical). For each fixed legal pair lanes are independent. For at most 8448² pairs the probability that even one response lies within 12 coordinate errors is at most

    8448² · [sum_{i+j≤12} binom(32,i)256^i binom(24,j)768^j] / (257^32 769^24) < 2^-314.

This applies also to choosing one legal pair after viewing the packet, by union bound. It does not bound combinations of multiple responses or reprogrammed source labels. It measures the missing witness-to-center property for that bank. The graph construction above supplies it for a different, explicit circuit family.

### Evidence

The edge-header histograms matched on all 48 disconnected graph/target comparisons at four vertices over F3, including cycles. Across all 64 edge masks and three target shifts, unconditional and conditioned header distributions were compared with exact multiplicities on the false instances. Twenty-four 4096-gate compiled circuits were evaluated on 504 supplied paths and on padded shortest/simple path witnesses when connected. The independent bank corrector recovered all 130 full-size fixtures at each tested weight zero through twelve collectively, checking both payload bits. The full-size honest-product integer union bound has base-two logarithm −314.9427121194. Five component proof files cover path telescoping, edge invariance, exact conditioning fibers, the dense-table parameter floor, and the 769-bit terminal arithmetic. The finite tests and proofs do not establish an end-to-end quantum or Machine-cost theorem for general witness encryption.
