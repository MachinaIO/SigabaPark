### One root column in place of a reference column, a random bookend direction, and a body

In the four-slot construction use a target rank r≥2 and set the first polynomial secret coordinate to the public encoding center Δ=floor(q/2) multiplied by the *private* message β. Only the other r−1 coordinates are independently uniform ring elements. The resulting physical mixed secret remains uniform on those other r−1 coordinates. Select column j corresponding to weight one of the first gadget block, so G[:,j]=e₁. Issue the regular input rows and only one additional polynomial c_R=B_C[:,j]ᵀs+U. Every accepting slot decodes the nearest message center to c_C[j]−c_R. PinnedBookend verifies the algebraic cancellation, leaving Δβ+e_C[j]−U. It suffices that HEW+F<(q−1)/4. No random direction d, extra scalar body, or p matrix is transmitted or reconstructed.

Use the registered ModuleLWE.decision at **rank r−1** and km samples, with uniform secret and bounded coefficient error as in the companion note. A selected group's address bits X are known to the simulator. Fill the bottom r−1 rows of A_i from the challenge matrices; independently sample each missing top row uniformly. Set B_i=A_i−X_iG. To each bottom-challenge body add the known product of its corresponding top entry and Δβ. This produces the exact honest input response in the real experiment. In the ideal experiment all translated response polynomials are uniform independent of all headers and of β. On NO choose reference c_C[j]+U from these public inputs. The shift from its honest center is now at most HEW per slot, so the group's TV is at most 4HEW/(2F+1). Across multiple groups one may use the usual hybrid changing one message at a time: other groups use their own bottom secrets, can set their top coordinates to either endpoint as required, and share exactly the programmed B_i. This conditional reduction uses the existing fixed decision law. It does not certify its estimator strength or a complete simulator clock.

This version issues 4(km+1)t row bits per four addresses with 4krmt common header bits. Slot projection uses no random projection column, and error and simulation bounds save its m factor. All independent-reference costs and remaining all-address enumeration are still material. A simulator using an exponentially large false-instance truth table as its advice is unnecessary: it needs the four public addresses for the selected group, the circuit and their four clear evaluations. Its simulation of other groups and the node-to-node matrix operations must be paid for.

### A genuine mixed secret in a compressible common-scalar-header model

Let s₀₀=x, s₁₀=y, s₀₁=z, s₁₁=y+z−c x for a known scalar c. The ordinary mixed difference is (1−c)x, which need not vanish. The signed combination of the four published rows with coefficients (c,−1,−1,1) removes B from both lanes. In the left lane the surviving gadget phase is Gᵀ(z−cx); in the right it is Gᵀ(y−cx). Once r=y−cx is recovered, subtract c times the (0,0) left row and (B_L+G)ᵀr from the (1,0) left row. The result is cGᵀx plus error, and the other secrets follow if c is invertible. ThreeMaster certifies the scalar identities. At c=2 over an odd modulus, radius-E coordinate errors grow to at most 5E in the mixed sums and 3E in the final step. For the full binary gadget the established odd-modulus phase reconstruction applies when 5E<q/6. For a dyadic modulus use any odd c, with first and second error norms at most (|c|+3)E and (|c|+1)E respectively and the corresponding dyadic recovery radius. A replay at q=31, c=2 recovered all three masters in 1000 randomly sampled grids with independent radius-one errors on every published coordinate. This result concerns the specified common scalar header and exposed lanes; the separate CRT headers evade its algebraic premise.

### General public feature spaces

Let Z be a set of distinct bit strings and let V be a publicly specified linear space of scalar functions on Z over a finite field. Each coordinate of the unknown secret S(z) lies in V. For each address-bit index i the public lanes have matrices B_i+z_iG, with B_i shared across all addresses. Assume first exact lanes and a gadget exposing its scalar argument. Let D_i denote multiplication of a function by bit z_i. Put V₀=V and iteratively
V_{l+1}={v∈V_l : D_i v∈V_l for every i}.
After round l the known data determine S modulo V_l in every coordinate. Initially this is the declared feature representation. Choose a public representative for the recovered coset; subtract its exact contribution from the original rows. For any linear relation α annihilating V_l, the corresponding sum of the residual i-lanes removes B_i and exposes Gᵀ times the secret combination weighted by α_z z_i. As α ranges over a basis of the annihilator, the common kernel is exactly V_{l+1}, by finite-dimensional double annihilation. Linear algebra now recovers the new coset. Repeat on the original lanes with the updated exact representative.

At stabilization, both choices D_i and 1−D_i preserve the remaining space. Their products isolate any one address, as formalized in IsolatePoint. Over a field, if any remaining vector is nonzero at z then the point mass δ_z itself belongs to V. Conversely, any δ_z in the original V persists through every step. Thus the only unlearned-coordinate *space left by this procedure* is the span of point masses that individually occur in V. It need not hide all of this span against other algebra or side information. At most the number of address bits of peeling suffices: membership after l steps requires membership of all products of at most l coordinate multipliers in the original space, and a complete pattern indicator expands as a linear combination of these products. An exact k-bit full-support table with no point masses therefore has no residual secret under this interface even when its ordinary mixed differences are nonzero.

For small errors the same reasoning works if, at every round, there is a spanning family of annihilator vectors whose centered integer lifts each have l₁ norm at most T, and TE is below the applicable gadget threshold. Recover the phase of each such sum before doing field elimination. Subsequent large field coefficients combine already exact values so do not amplify row noise across rounds. Generic V need not have short annihilators; no noisy recovery claim without that condition follows. The finite replay enumerated all 212 subspaces of functions on four addresses over F₃ and verified that two peeling rounds leave precisely their point-mass span.

Finally, in an algebraic SIMD implementation suppose one commutative algebra over a field contains encoded bits X_i with an evaluation homomorphism for each of the 2^k addresses, recovering each genuine bit. The products of X_i or (1−X_i) for the 2^k possible patterns have an identity matrix of evaluations. They are linearly independent, so the algebra has dimension at least 2^k. This limits a dense uniform ring-element representation of that model. Individual low-complexity elements may still have short symbolic descriptions.
