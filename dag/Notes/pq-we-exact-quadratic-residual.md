### Recover the stated predicate before counting

Fix a publicly recognized mandatory affine system in the 128 witness bits. Inconsistent rows decide NO. Otherwise row reduction gives an explicit affine bijection from n free bits onto its solution set. Restrict the original predicate to this affine space. A Boolean NAND required to be false requires *both* operands true. A repeated operand is NOT and flips the required bit. An unrepeated NAND required to be true does not assert a conjunction. Extract a list of guards only along justified splits; retain all shared wire identities. Polynomial computations take place in the Boolean squarefree ring: addition is symmetric difference of monomials and multiplication uses union of variable supports, cancelling duplicates modulo two. A support cap, unresolved guard, or surviving degree above two rejects this fast mode. Truncating high terms would change the accepted language.

One raw-front-end implementation recognizes the consecutive four-NAND XOR macro in a streaming pass (compare the two intermediate wires, the shared NAND and its parents), records semantic operations by raw ordinal, then traces only gates needed by the required guard list. Repeated operands give NOT; an unrecognized macro remains a literal NAND. Normalize wire 0 and self/forward references to true according to the registered evaluator. A backward liveness sweep and one forward symbolic evaluation share all intermediates. In this implementation a required false literal NAND is split before polynomial extraction, a required NOT is flipped, and a recognized XOR remains a single polynomial guard. At a NAND on two known polynomials multiply exactly under the cap or reject that branch. Testing polynomial guards before attempting a split is also correct and may recognize more circuits. For an affine substitution outside the cheap path, substitute a constant plus a linear form for each eliminated bit in every surviving quadratic monomial, multiply the two exact affine factors, and reduce. The two four/seven-guard controls below use no eliminated coordinate in their recovered nonlinear guards, as checked by the raw compiler. Alternative positive-control parsing first recovers the mandatory affine hull and builds a shared expression DAG for the restricted predicate.

For a certified exact residual of k equations Q_i(x)=0, Enc tests whether the count is positive and emits the single bit β AND [count>0]. Dec returns the payload bit on a valid witness. Every satisfying input is correct, and on a false statement the public bit always equals zero. Consequently the two false-statement output laws agree even against unbounded quantum postprocessing. This is a setup-free *decision backend* for a restricted public class. An unrecognized input is an explicit dispatch to another exact backend; returning zero there without a decision would fail correctness.

### Signed counts, with the pivot sign

For each mask a let G_a=∑_x (−1)^{a·Q(x)}. Then
`2^k #{x : Q(x)=0} = ∑_a G_a`.
At a single x the right inner character sum is the product over i of 1+(−1)^Q_i(x): it is 2^k on a common zero and zero otherwise. This pointwise identity, including k=0, is Lean-verified. In Gray order neighboring masks require just one form XOR; integer addition and the final divisibility by 2^k are exact.

Choose any xy term with coefficient one. Write the combined quadratic as xy+xU(z)+yV(z)+W(z), with U,V affine on the remaining variables. Summing over the pivot bits yields 2(−1)^{W+UV} for each z. The second Lean proof establishes the finite signed sum itself, including constants. The updated form stays quadratic, because the two factors are affine. Concretely for U=a+u·z, V=b+v·z, toggle the constant by ab, the linear vector by av+bu+(u coordinatewise-times v), and the off-diagonal polar matrix by uvᵀ+vuᵀ. Delete both pivot rows/columns. If no pivot remains and a linear term survives, the sum is zero by pairing that bit; otherwise the signed sum on the remaining variables is (−1)^const times their domain size. After p pivots a nonzero G is therefore ±2^(n−p). Polar elimination is an invertible change of coordinates that splits a nonsingular alternating plane and its orthogonal complement each time, giving polar rank 2p. Thus |G|≤2^(n−rank/2), and its *sign* still requires the constant and linear information.

For an arbitrary Boolean acceptance h on k quadratic features, with the unnormalized Walsh transform ĥ(a)=∑_z h(z)(−1)^{a·z}, replace G_a by ĥ(a)G_a in the divided sum. Only nonzero certified weights need evaluation. For an affine target of codimension t, testing t independent affine output combinations suffices and uses at most 2^t characters. A truth-table transform itself costs O(k·2^k) additions; an asserted sparse Fourier representation needs a proof or a checked construction. Dense Fourier support is possible already for conjunction.

The identity term can certify YES without evaluating the other signs. Partition nonzero masks by their first set index j in a fixed guard order. If all polar matrices in that coset have rank at least r_j and ∑_j 2^(k−1−j−r_j/2)<1, the absolute sum of all nonidentity terms is strictly smaller than 2^n. A concrete sufficient check restricts to coordinates on which *all later polar matrices* have zero rows and columns; compute the current restricted principal rank. For the positive 108-variable control this gives 24,24,24,24 and the bound 15/4096. One random specialization is not a coset certificate. Conversely an unsatisfiable subfamily of equations suffices for NO. The negative control's three lowest-rank individual guards have pair counts all 2^105 and triple count zero; only eight characters of that triple are needed. The three individual polar ranks are all four. Picking low-rank guards is a heuristic for search order, while zero triple count is an exact certificate.

### Common radicals and a complementary branching parameter

Let K be the common kernel of every polar matrix in the pencil. On K the derivative D(r)=Q(r)+Q(0) is a linear map from K into F₂^k, and Q(t+r)=Q(t)+D(r). Choose a linear complement T. For t∈T there are no accepting r unless Q(t)∈im D; when nonempty the accepting fiber has exactly 2^(dim K−rank D) elements. The Lean radical-fiber proof gives the additive equivalence with ker D by translation. Impose only the k−rank D independent characters annihilating im D on T. Functions represented by those characters descend to the quotient. Equivalently every character outside (im D)^perp has exactly zero Gauss sum on the full domain. Recompute the common radical after the projection; it can grow, and every nontrivial round removes at least one input dimension. Keep the product of the exact fiber multiplicities. The transformation also handles constant images and k=0.

There is an alternative even when the common radical is zero. Supply a subspace W such that B_i vanishes on W×W for every i, with h=codim W. On each of the 2^h cosets choose a representative t. Each Q_i(t+w) is affine in w, with linear part Q_i(w)−Q_i(0)+B_i(t,w); it is additive by the verified isotropic-coset theorem. Solve the resulting linear equations jointly. Add zero for an inconsistent system or 2^(dim W−rank L_t) for a consistent one. For a coordinate subspace, checking that every quadratic monomial meets the complementary variable set certifies the promise. For a general basis G, check GᵀB_iG=0 for each i; its construction and coordinate changes cost work. This gives a 2^h exact branch count rather than 2^k. A residual with 107 guards s x_i+c_i (1≤i≤107), c≠0, has no common radical. Branching on s requires just two affine systems and yields a unique solution among 2^108 assignments. We placed an invertible mix of XORs on the outputs, fixed 20 extra input bits to zero, compiled the resulting 1,022 NANDs, and padded to 4,096. Parsing this raw circuit recovered 20 linear and 107 nonlinear guards; the two-branch count remained one. Its raw witness density is 2^−128.

### Replays on the registered layout

Two 4,096-gate controls impose 20 affine checks, followed by four (YES) or seven (NO) quadratic equations in the 108 free variables. A public invertible shear makes all 108 variables active and the guard-support incidence graph connected. On YES, each of the 108 individual coordinate directions was witnessed to change acceptance. The unsheared four guards each ask that the parity of twelve disjoint products be one, on disjoint 24-variable blocks; twelve further coordinates are free. The positive count was **20,262,609,815,397,502,464,200,540,160,000**, independently equal to 2^12((2^24−2^12)/2)^4. On NO the seven-equation count was **zero** despite pairwise compatibility of the three extra guards. The corresponding four-guard nonzero-mask rank multiplicities are 4,6,4,1 at ranks 24,48,72,96.

For reproduction, number the 108 surviving coordinates by increasing original coordinate. In the four-block control replace coordinate 0,24,48,72 of the disjoint forms by its XOR with the parity of 96 through 107. For the seven-guard version use the parity of 100 through 107 and shear coordinates 0,24,48,72,96. Number the additional quartet 96,…,99 by bit indices 0,…,3. Encode each squarefree monomial by its support mask. The three additional parity lists and required outputs are (2,4,6,9,10,12)→0, (2,8,3,5,6,9)→1 and (2,6,9)→1. The twenty affine checks use the rows of the binary code control, targeted at syndrome 33026. [Code matrix and raw gate recipe](https://github.com/MachinaIO/SigabaPark/pull/155)

The cheap raw XOR front end produced 24/27 guards before removing the affine equations. It visited 1,060/1,089 live raw nodes for the two circuits. The YES run had 836 XORs, 48 ordinary NANDs and 48 NOTs besides its 128 primary variables, read 20,150 XOR operand monomials, made 96 NAND monomial products, and stored 20,518 output monomials across the live nodes. NO had 845 XORs, 58 ordinary NANDs, 58 NOTs, 20,066 XOR operand monomials, 130 NAND monomial products and 20,512 output monomials. The largest single list length in either run was 128. All numbers are counts of the explicit compiler's finite operations, not TM steps. Testing only consecutive XOR macros produced the same counts. In the alternative affine-hull route there is no permission to count the initial scan or substitution as free.

Two additional raw controls contain 2,296/2,409 construction NANDs padded to the same registered length. They shear the input and mix forty or forty-three quadratic outputs by repeated invertible XOR operations. A separate set of twenty conditions determines the other inputs from the first 108. From the raw gate lists, 60/63 guards were recovered. Repeated common-radical quotienting finished in 41 rounds, reducing respectively to zero equations on zero variables or three equations on three variables. The exact counts are **2^68** and **zero**. The first rounds removed radicals of dimensions 87/84 with image rank 21 in each. The forty pre-mixing forms have the triangular shape x_i+x_{i+1}x_{i+2}+c_i for i=0,…,39; the first forty inputs are sheared by the parity of input indices 42 through 107. The three added equations before mixing use the last three input variables x,y,z, with xy=xz=1 and yz=0. Invertible XOR row operations preserve their common-zero predicate. In the raw generator the random signs and row mixing use a public fixed seed 158. These are lower-density examples: the positive covers 2^−60 of raw assignments and 2^−40 after imposing the first twenty constraints. Uniform independent probing of the residual needs at least 2^40 trials merely to reach expected witness count one. In the four-guard positive control the residual density is instead (1−2^−12)^4/16. Bounded sampling can be competitive there, but evaluating the raw 20 extra checks without the affine parametrization loses a factor 2^20 in success probability. No density estimate substitutes for worst-case correctness.

The Gauss implementation was compared against enumeration on all 2,048 squarefree quadratics in four variables (constant, four linear, six quadratic coefficients) and 100 sampled small systems. Repeated radicals were checked on 300 systems, and isotropic branching against full enumeration on 200 more. The exact character identity, pivot sum, fiber multiplicity and coset linearization are separate Lean results. The raw parsers, fixtures, complete cost accounting and the Gaussian implementation are experimental evidence, not a proved registered machine.

### Storage, clocks and where fallback is necessary

The registered format uses 128 witness bits, 4,096 NANDs, unary gate references and true at an unavailable read. A raw list can occupy up to 103,821,312 bits before outer tuple framing. Both algorithms receive the statement, while the one-bit Dec need only navigate the framed input to its payload. Read/write, skipping, and output framing have costs even when Dec performs no SAT search. A compact parsed operand fits 13 bits and two refs plus an opcode fit 29 bits. Four consecutive NAND records fit in a fixed rolling window. A fully positional XOR lookup that repeatedly scans the operand table can be much more costly than that local recognition. Boolean liveness can be stored in at most two flags per wire for the two demanded polarities, then a backward dependency sweep for candidate guards; if arbitrary-position worklist entries are used, bound their number by two per wire and pay their sequential lookup. The cheap parser and the affine-hull scanner are alternative implementations.

A quadratic form in n variables takes N=1+n+n(n−1)/2 independent binary coefficients, or 1+n+n² with a redundant symmetric polar matrix. At n=128 there are 8,257 independent slots so a degree≤2 monomial id fits in 14 bits. Storing all YES compiler monomials in separately framed sorted lists takes 287,252 payload bits plus lengths, node indices and tape layout (NO: 287,168 payload bits). When multiplying lists, allocate and canonicalize the *true* degree≤4 intermediate supports (e.g. use 128-bit masks for these temporary candidates), then reject a surviving high degree. Bound the product length before allocating. Combining sparse records by sorted merges costs O(N₁+N₂) identifier comparisons, each costing up to the identifier width. Dense canonical forms use one fixed bit per coefficient and avoid per-monomial ids. General affine substitution can expand one old quadratic term into O(n²) coefficient updates; count the number of old terms and gates actually transformed. No claim about every raw circuit follows from the fixture list sizes.

A concrete sequential layout for a Gauss call uses row-major polar bits and separate bit rows for the linear part, selected neighbors and active-coordinate flags. At active width m, scan for an off-diagonal one, collect the two selected rows and flags, then stream a new (m−2)×(m−2) matrix while applying the two masked XORs. Rewind the neighbor vectors between output rows, and overwrite or swap the old and new matrix tapes. This uses O(m²) bit-cell visits per pivot with unary/delimited row and column marks, O(n²) workspace, and one final zero-pivot scan. A naive layout that retains and scans the original n×n matrix every time has a different clock. In `m²` scan units the shrinking dense schedule, including the final scan, totals ∑_{j=0}^{p}(n−2j)² for a mask of polar rank 2p. The worst for n=108 is 215,820 units per mask. Summed using the actual rank histograms the four-guard control needs 2,622,064 units and the seven-guard control 21,737,008. A unit measures a single matrix area before the number of sweeps, rewinds and control transitions. Even prior to any constant factor, 127 nonidentity masks may require more than 27 million such cells. The Gray update and copy of a combined form occur once per mask and the signed accumulator needs n+k+2 bits including sign. Count all guard storage kN, working copies, parser and divider/reconstruction.

For a repeated radical round at dimensions (n,k), stream kn length-n polar rows through a basis using O(kn³) bit-coordinate visits for classical elimination. If q radical directions are found and their output image has rank h, evaluating their images takes O(kqn²) coordinates and forming k−h combinations costs O((k−h)kn²), plus O(k²q) guard-space linear elimination and basis restriction. The pivot-coordinate complement can be chosen as standard coordinate vectors from Gaussian elimination. This avoids a mandatory dense arbitrary basis change at each restriction. The sums of these four *upper polynomial terms*, without constant factors, over the 41 rounds of the YES fixture are 148,953,572; 86,218,160; 59,381,972; and 333,740 respectively. On NO they are 165,045,908; 87,625,523; 73,513,511; and 358,985. For isotropic branching with a coordinate complement of h bits and d=n−h, each of 2^h branches can form k shifted linear rows by scanning the input coefficients and solve them in O(kd²) coordinate visits. Include the isometry test or graph-cover certificate in the cost. One may impose deterministic caps on each stage and select the cheapest certified candidate, provided overflow calls a correct fallback.

In a conservative sequential implementation, a table lookup in L stored bits can be made by scanning and maintaining a binary position in O(L log(L+1)) steps per indexed access; use sequential scans where identified above. At zero oracles the registered machine description is 6·states·3^tapes·(4+4·tapes+Nat.size(states)) bits. Even 500 states and 10 tapes cost 9,388,791,000 description bits before time; separate Enc and Dec descriptions are charged. Counts of rows, monomials or Python timings by themselves do not certify the strict 2^32 target, or the registered 2^100 cap for arbitrary inputs.

A parity of two disjoint cubic monomials has a genuine cubic ANF and can occur as a single positive XOR guard in a 12-NAND core. No quadratic certificate may discard its cubic coefficients. Another control is a system of m disjoint product guards in 2m variables. Its common polar radical is zero, and the sum of their polars is a nonsingular alternating form: any common isotropic space has dimension ≤m. Both raw exponents can therefore be m even when another syntactic or component solver decides the instance easily. An unconditional fallback is enumeration of the affine solution space, evaluating the original circuit; without a certified hull enumerate the raw 128-bit domain. It gives correct well-defined one-bit WE, but the worst-case clock remains outside the desired budget. A uniform sub-2^32 backend, bounded transition programs, and complete registered proofs are open.
